Macro · Rates

The newsletter covers geopolitical developments around the US-Ukraine-Russia conflict, with Washington threatening to withhold intelligence from Kyiv over missile strikes on Russian refineries, and Trump reportedly striking a diesel deal with Putin. Risk tone is mixed as geopolitical uncertainty persists alongside some tentatively improving signals on the Treasury front. Overnight action is shaped by a combination of diplomacy headlines and bond market dynamics.

Treasury selling at the long end of the curve is showing signs of fatigue, and if CPI and PPI this week come in without major upside surprises, a multi-day rally in bonds is possible. A series of supportive data points have accumulated — weak August JOLTs and PCE, soft July payrolls, Fed officials downplaying the need for consecutive hikes, and a slowdown in AI-linked debt issuance. Treasury Secretary Bessent is expected to reduce auction sizes for the 20- and 30-year bonds at the November 4th refunding announcement and shift more issuance toward bills, which would further relieve upward pressure on yields.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously constructive on rates and by extension equities, contingent on this week's CPI and PPI not coming in hot. The key catalyst to watch is inflation data — a benign print could trigger a meaningful Treasury rally that spills over positively into risk assets. Geopolitical noise around Ukraine-Russia adds an overlay of uncertainty but is not the dominant market driver today.

Sector Watch

AI infrastructure and data center spending faces a potential headwind as AI-linked debt issuance fell sharply in September, signaling market indigestion from heavy supply. This could slow the pace of capital deployment into data centers. Legacy US wireless carriers, particularly T-Mobile, are defending their competitive positioning against Starlink Mobile. Treasury and bond markets are the key sector focus this week ahead of CPI and PPI.

VK · Key Names
◆ META Meta Platforms Other

Meta held talks with Anthropic about potentially selling compute capacity to the AI startup but ultimately decided against it, at least for now. This suggests Meta is evaluating how to monetize or deploy its AI infrastructure assets but has not yet committed to a third-party compute strategy. The decision to pass keeps Meta's compute focused internally for now, with the door left open for future deals.

▲ TMUS T-Mobile Other

T-Mobile's CTO John Saw published a LinkedIn piece pushing back on fears that Starlink Mobile poses a major near-term threat to legacy US wireless carriers. Saw argued that the Grain spectrum transaction involves roughly 14 MHz of spectrum T-Mobile previously divested because it was no longer central to its strategy, and that over 70% of mobile traffic occurs indoors where terrestrial networks have a fundamental advantage. The commentary is an attempt to reassure investors that the competitive moat of traditional wireless infrastructure remains intact.
