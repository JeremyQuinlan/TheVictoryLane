MARKET OUTLOOK

S&P futures are holding up remarkably well given the overnight headlines — Iran missiles and an SK Hynix miss would normally be a recipe for a ugly open, but the resilience is telling. Iran is increasingly looking like tariffs: a persistent, background negative with periodic flare-ups rather than an acute market-breaking event, and oil knows it (Brent is well off last week's highs). The Fed hike — if it comes — would likely be net positive for equities by clearing uncertainty and signaling policy conviction. Earnings remain the real story, and the bar remains brutally high for tech; companies like STX and TER clearing it easily gives the bulls something to work with heading into META, MSFT, and QCOM tonight.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran launched a surprise ballistic missile attack on American forces in Jordan — all missiles were intercepted — breaking an unofficial ceasefire that had shown early diplomatic promise (NYT). The US and Saudi Arabia responded with joint strikes on Iran-linked militia in Iraq.

- Iran has rejected an Omani proposal to share control of the Strait of Hormuz, demanding more (WSJ). Europe has separately said it will not join any Hormuz patrol initiative until there is sustained peace (Politico).

- Iran will receive Chinese shoulder-launched missile systems within weeks, a development that risks further straining Beijing-Washington relations (Reuters).

- Washington is moving to ban imports of Chinese humanoid robots and power inverters to protect the US AI buildout (Reuters).

- Ukraine is sharpening its strike strategy against Russia, targeting critical infrastructure for lasting damage rather than symbolic hits (FT).

- South Korea called an emergency market meeting after the KOSPI collapsed nearly 6% — the worst performer in Asia Wednesday (Bloomberg).

COMPANY NEWS

V (Visa) posted FQ3 EPS of $3.32 vs. Street $3.23, with strong payment volumes, cross-border growth, and value-added services up 45% Y/Y. Full-year guidance nudged higher. Separately, Visa plans to cut roughly 7% of its workforce (~2,600 employees) to improve efficiency (Reuters).

STX (Seagate) delivered a blowout quarter: EPS $5.71 vs. Street $5.08, revenue $3.63B vs. $3.49B, GMs more than 200 basis points above plan, and FQ1 guidance of ~$7.30 EPS vs. Street $5.80.

BE (Bloom Energy) crushed it: EPS 78c vs. Street 40c, revenue $1.065B vs. $838MM, and full-year EPS guide raised to $2.55–2.85 from $1.85–2.25. Every major US hyperscaler has now approved Bloom's power solutions.

MDLZ (Mondelez) beat on Q2 organic sales (+2.2% vs. Street +0.9%) and EPS (73c vs. 68c), raising full-year top-line guidance to "at least 2%."

CAKE (Cheesecake Factory) posted Q2 EPS of $1.44 vs. Street $1.18, with same-store sales +5.8% vs. Street +2%.

F (Ford) reported Q2 EBIT of $2.5B vs. Street $2.1B, with ICE margins expanding 480 basis points to 4.4%. Full-year EBIT guidance raised to $10–11B from $8.5–10.5B.

SK Hynix missed on Q2 revenue (KRW79.3T vs. Street KRW83.85B) and operating profit (KRW60.5T vs. Street KRW64.2T) despite explosive Y/Y growth. Stock fell ~7.7% but finished off lows — with the stock already more than 50% off highs at 3–4x PE, incremental sellers are scarce.

ACGL (Arch Capital) beat Q2 EPS ($2.56 vs. $2.47) but quality was poor — driven largely by $165MM in favorable prior-year reserve developments, with core combined ratio inline and NPW down 6.9% Y/Y.

BXP beat FFO ($1.78 vs. $1.70), with occupancy up 100 basis points Q/Q to 88.4% and leasing volume jumping to 1.8M sq. ft. from 1.1M in Q1.

OI (O-I Glass) badly missed Q2 EPS (9c vs. 24c) on European weakness and restructuring disruption; 2026 EBITDA guidance cut to $1.0–1.1B from $1.125–1.225B.

UBS beat Q2 estimates, reported $36B in net new wealth management assets, and announced a $3B buyback. Deutsche Bank posted strong Q2 results with FIC revenue +36% and a €500MM buyback. Standard Chartered raised full-year guidance and announced a $1B buyback on solid Wealth and Global Banking results.

GOOGL (Google) has largely dismantled the team behind its Nobel Prize-winning AlphaFold AI system (FT).

AAPL (Apple) unveiled "Apple Upgrade," a Klarna-powered leasing program for iPhone, Watch, Mac, and iPad. Apple is also planning a fresh smart home push with new TV 4K, HomePod Mini, and a Siri AI hub (Bloomberg).
