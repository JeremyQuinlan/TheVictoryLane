RATES & FED

Treasuries caught a bid after the very weak July jobs report, with yields down 2–5 basis points across the curve. Markets are now pricing roughly 29 basis points of hikes this year, down from ~33 basis points pre-report, with odds of a September 16th move at 44%. Fed Chair Warsh still faces real inflation headwinds that likely necessitate 1–2 hikes regardless of labor softness.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The "bad is good" playbook is alive and kicking today — weak jobs data took rate pressure off and gave equities room to run, but don't confuse a relief rally with a durable trend shift. Warsh isn't done tightening, and the drop in yields is modest enough that this isn't a macro regime change. Tech is leading convincingly, housing is catching a bid alongside falling yields, and Q2 earnings wrapped on a reasonably constructive note. The Hormuz situation remains the wildcard — until that deal is officially inked, energy markets stay twitchy. Watch CPI Wednesday; that's the next real test.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran's draft Hormuz framework would bar ships from US-linked or "offending" nations from transiting the strait until financial compensation is paid — the official release, expected Tuesday or Wednesday, still hasn't materialized (CNBC).

- Iran's negotiating team is under heavy pressure from domestic hardliners to extract greater financial concessions from the US before any deal is finalized (WSJ).

- Saudi Arabia is actively preparing for "multiple coordinated attacks" from Iraqi militias and the Houthis (CNN).

- US intelligence assesses that Putin is increasingly likely to test NATO's resolve via a limited incursion against a member state (WSJ).

COMPANY NEWS

CART beat Q2 EBITDA at $313MM vs. $299MM expected, outperformed on GTV, ad revenue, and total revenue, and set Q3 guidance ahead of expectations.
