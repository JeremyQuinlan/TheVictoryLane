MARKET OUTLOOK

The AI capex narrative is cracking under its own weight, and today's Nvidia-Ohio data center story (WSJ) is the clearest evidence yet. Hyperscaler free cash flow is evaporating — Google already went negative last quarter, and Amazon and Meta are expected to follow this week — forcing the industry into circular, unsustainable financing structures just to keep data centers moving forward. Chips and AI infrastructure names are leading today's underperformance, and that's not a one-day story. The broader market is holding up because rotation into software, consumer, healthcare, and travel is absorbing the damage, but the Nasdaq's relative weakness is a warning sign worth taking seriously.

- Washington is set to lift a yearlong ban on Mexican cattle imports (suspended due to screwworm), which should begin easing beef prices (WSJ).

- A meaningful number of immigrants are losing protected status, and employers are warning of a looming worker shortage as a result (NYT).

COMPANY NEWS

NVDA (Nvidia) is reportedly in talks to provide ~$600B in financial guarantees on a massive Ohio data center alongside OpenAI — a structure described as radically unprecedented and deeply circular. There is no positive spin here; it reflects how difficult it has become to finance AI infrastructure as capital markets turn less hospitable (WSJ). Separately, Nvidia has made a "substantial" investment in Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence startup (Bloomberg).

BKR (Baker Hughes) posted a blowout Q2: EPS of $0.64 vs. the Street's $0.50, revenue of $6.7B vs. $6.5B expected, with IET orders of $7.1B absolutely crushing the $3.6B consensus estimate. Book-to-bill hit 1.57x; full-year IET order guidance is raised. OFSE EBITDA guidance nudged lower but offset by IET. The stock is spiking.

ARGX (Argenx) is acquiring FBRX (Forte Biosciences) for $77/share in cash, valuing the deal at ~$2.2B (WSJ).

KO (Coca-Cola) confirmed Fairlife has resumed the majority of production across its four U.S. facilities.

EXE (Expand Energy) announced a definitive merger agreement to acquire Twin Eagle Holdings for $1.25B.

CBRL (Cracker Barrel) announced CEO Julie Masino will be replaced by David Deno effective August 10.

HOOD (Robinhood) is in talks to bring prediction market contracts from Crypto.com onto its platform, broadening beyond its current Kalshi-reliant setup (WSJ).

UAL (United Airlines) approached DAL (Delta) last year about a merger, but talks never progressed. United had also approached American Airlines earlier this year and was rebuffed (WSJ).

BF/B (Brown-Forman): Sazerac is lobbying BF shareholders — including the controlling Brown family — for its $32/share bid, hinting the price could improve if the board engaged, but is being rebuffed (WSJ, Bloomberg).

AstraZeneca beat Q2 EPS estimates handily at $2.63 vs. $2.48 expected (+18% on an FX-neutral basis), with strength in Oncology and Rare Disease; full-year guidance reiterated. Shares outperformed in European trading. China's CXMT surged ~466% on its Shanghai IPO debut, briefly hitting a ~$550B market cap and eclipsing Tencent (FT). China has also reportedly achieved a breakthrough in immersion DUV lithography machines, a critical step toward silicon self-sufficiency (The Information).
