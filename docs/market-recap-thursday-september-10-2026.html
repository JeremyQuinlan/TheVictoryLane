RATES & FED

Treasuries sold off hard, with yields spiking 7–14 basis points across the curve as Middle East escalation sent oil surging and inflation fears intensified. The Fed is now priced for 45 basis points of hikes this year, with the odds of a move at the September 16th meeting sitting at roughly 75%. The ECB hiked 25 basis points Thursday and signaled October is live (Bloomberg).

MARKET OUTLOOK

This market is navigating a genuinely hostile macro backdrop — oil ripping higher, yields refusing to cooperate, and a Fed that looks poised to hike next week. Druckenmiller told the FT he doesn't think the Fed needs to hike, but concedes yields need to rise further, and he's worried the AI boom may be in its late stages with earnings in an unsustainable bubble. The majority of Treasury investors are now price-sensitive and need to be enticed with higher yields to absorb the flood of issuance (FT) — Bessent's buyback messaging isn't moving the needle. With Washington unserious on fiscal discipline (Trump's $5K dividend would explode the deficit per Politico) and energy markets in chaos, a sustained rally in bonds or equities is hard to construct.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The Houthis seized the port city of Mocha, tightening their grip on the Bab el-Mandeb Strait — meaning both Hormuz and the Red Sea are now severely compromised simultaneously (Pentagon can't escort ships through both). A Houthi spokesman told Reuters that commercial navigation remains safe, but the market isn't buying it.

- Iran's Pickaxe Mountain has seen a surge in construction activity suggesting nuclear program hardening; Trump warned Tehran "not to get cute" (CNN, Al Arabiya). A potential US and/or IDF response remains on the table.

- White House advisors are warning Trump the Iran war could last his entire presidency (WSJ); The Atlantic reports Pentagon planning suggests preparation for a long conflict with potential post-midterm escalation.

- Over 100 US military advisors are on the ground in Saudi Arabia supporting the Kingdom's Houthi campaign (CNN). Saudi oil production plunged in August (WSJ), adding another layer of supply shock to an already fragile energy market.

- A Trump-Xi summit (Sept 24) could produce modest tariff reductions on both sides (AP). China pre-bought ~1 million tons of US soybeans ahead of the meeting (Reuters).

- The White House has stalled on refined copper tariffs over affordability/inflation concerns (Reuters).

- Taiwan reunification odds are quietly rising as US military bandwidth is consumed by the Iran conflict and China expands kinetic capabilities (The Atlantic).

- Zelensky's plane was threatened by a drone over Moldova this week (Washington Post).

- Paul Tudor Jones called on the US and China to jointly govern AI, warning it could become "a third superpower" that dwarfs both nations (WSJ).

COMPANY NEWS

NVDA (Nvidia): Jensen Huang at the Goldman conference reiterated confidence in the 70% sales growth target and said supply constraints — not demand — are the only thing preventing >100% growth. He called cybersecurity "the next major use case of AI." Separately, the DOJ is investigating Nvidia's Groq "acquihire" for potential antitrust violations (NYT).

TSM (TSMC): August revenue exploded +53% year-over-year and more than 10% sequentially, with AI chip demand continuing to outstrip supply (Bloomberg). Clean confirmation the data center buildout remains in full swing.

M (Macy's): Q2 EPS of 63 cents beat the Street's 36-cent estimate, though 23 cents came from tariff refunds — strip that out and the beat narrows to 4 cents. Full-year guidance moves higher but Q3 EPS outlook came in light.

AVAV (AeroVironment): FQ1 EPS of 59 cents beat the 51-cent estimate; sales of $480.5MM topped the $456MM consensus (+6% Y/Y); gross margins came in at 26% vs. the Street's 23.7%; backlog jumped 37% Y/Y. Guidance merely reiterated.

COO (Cooper): Missed on sales, issued weak guidance, and scrapped plans to divest its Surgical business — stock tumbled.

AEO (American Eagle): Modest comp shortfall and trimmed operating income guidance for H2 on an ex-tariff-refund basis.

SHOE (Shoe Station): Ugly FQ2 miss and full-year guidance cut, blaming "an increasingly promotional footwear marketplace." Management noted back-to-school trends improved vs. Q2.

SPCX (SpaceX): CFO at Goldman reiterated confidence in the $100B ARR target and disclosed a new AI compute hosting deal worth ~$1.1B per month in revenue. Separately, the company is overhauling its data center buildout to prioritize reliability over speed, which could slow construction timelines (The Information) — this weighed on AI infrastructure names Thursday.

JBLU (JetBlue): Bullish Q3 demand update with RASM now guided +17–20% vs. prior +12.5–16.5%, though weather disruptions pushed CASM higher and fuel overshot guidance.

META (Meta): Star AI researcher Andrew Tulloch — recruited for over $1B — is leaving after less than a year (WSJ). Separately, Meta spends hundreds of millions per month on Anthropic's Claude but is actively working to reduce that dependency (Business Insider).

UBER (Uber): CEO purchased $10M in stock Thursday; shares rallied on the insider buy.

ET (Energy Transfer): Will become the first major company to switch its primary listing from the NYSE to the Texas Stock Exchange (WSJ).

ELV (Elevance): Reiterated full-year EPS and MCR guidance for 2026 at a sell-side conference.

EA (Electronic Arts): Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund is weighing a merger between EA and Savvy Games to create a global gaming giant (Bloomberg).
