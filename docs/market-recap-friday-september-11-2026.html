Specific index percentage changes were not provided in this newsletter. The directional takeaway is clear: bears won the week decisively. Brent surged on Middle East escalation, pulling yields higher and pressuring equities broadly across risk assets.

---

RATES & FED

Rising oil prices from the Yemen/Hormuz escalation added further upward pressure on global borrowing costs, making a sustained yield decline hard to envision given persistent inflation, fiscal recklessness, and AI-driven debt issuance. The ECB hiked this week and flagged more tightening ahead, while both the BOJ and FOMC are expected to raise rates the week of September 14th. Core CPI for August came in a touch above expectations sequentially, and the PPI — while in line — remained very hot and accelerated versus July.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

September is living up to its historically brutal reputation, and the bear case is stacking up fast — surging oil, rising global yields, a Fed that's about to hike, and geopolitical risk with no obvious off-ramp. That said, a one-and-done hike from Warsh, paired with messaging that elevated rates have already done heavy lifting, could limit the equity damage. The Trump-Xi summit on September 24th is a real potential catalyst for tariff relief (AP), and the AI capex supercycle remains fully intact per every corporate speaker at the Goldman and Citi conferences this week. Tactically, this market is stuck between a genuine earnings/AI tailwind and a macro/geopolitical headwind that isn't going away quietly.

---

GEOPOLITICAL

- The Houthis seized the port city of Mocha Thursday and captured additional territory Friday, tightening their grip on the Bab el-Mandeb Strait — meaning both the Red Sea and Hormuz are now severely compromised simultaneously (Reuters). The Pentagon cannot effectively chaperone shipping through both waterways at once.

- Saudi Arabia's oil production plunged in August, and reports Thursday night/Friday morning indicated its East-West Pipeline may have suffered catastrophic damage from Houthi strikes.

- A senior Iranian-GCC meeting is scheduled for Monday in Oman — the first since the war began in February — aimed at reaching a consensus on Hormuz traffic (FT). Iran's economy is under unprecedented financial strain, which could eventually nudge the regime toward conciliation.

- White House advisers have warned Trump the Iran war could last through the end of his term (WSJ), and The Atlantic noted Pentagon posture suggests preparation for a long conflict including potential post-midterm escalation.

- VP Vance made the unusual move of directly canvassing US military commanders worldwide for an unvarnished assessment of the Iran war — and came away with "deep concerns" (NYT).

- The Russia-Ukraine war remains on a dangerous escalatory path; weekend talks in Moscow and Kyiv produced no meaningful progress.

- The Panama Canal administrator warned daily transits may need to be cut further due to low water levels — a compounding problem as other global shipping lanes face geopolitical strain.

- The US-Canada trade war continued to escalate this week.

COMPANY NEWS

META shares surged 6.5% Wednesday on strong reception for the Muse personal AI agent. The company's Watermelon frontier model is reportedly weeks away from launch.

ORCL (Oracle) beat expectations on all major FQ1 KPIs and nudged up full-year revenue and EPS guidance, reinforcing the data center/AI demand narrative that dominated conference circuit commentary this week.

TSM reported robust August revenue, another confirmation that the AI infrastructure buildout shows no signs of slowing.

AAPL rallied ~3.5% Thursday on positive early feedback for the new iPhone Duo; price increases and a deliberate mix shift toward higher-end devices are expected to help offset component cost inflation.

ADBE, BRZE, and TTAN all saw pressure post-earnings, a reminder that not all software names are benefiting equally from the AI wave.

RH warned of approximately $50M in unexpected costs tied to Iran war fallout, though tariff refunds will partially offset the hit.

SFD issued a negative preannouncement, following TSN's recent guidance cut — two notable signals of stress in the protein/food supply chain. KMB also talked down Q3 expectations.

AEO, CASY, CHWY, KR, and SHOE all disappointed on the consumer side. By contrast, ASO, ODD, SIG, SYY, V, and MA offered more upbeat reads — Visa and Mastercard both said at sell-side conferences that aggregate consumer spending remains healthy and resilient.

SUNB's beat-and-raise quarter was flagged as an encouraging data point for the industrial economy.
