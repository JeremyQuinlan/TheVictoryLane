Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with the S&P up 49 points or 65 basis points, Nasdaq up 329 points or 110 basis points, and the Russell 2000 up 47 basis points. Asian and European markets also posted broad gains, with the Hang Seng up 1.18% and major Eurozone indices up 85 to 120 basis points. The strength is somewhat puzzling given a weekend full of negative headlines including geopolitical escalation in the Middle East and troubling AI sector news, with the primary driver appearing to be optimism around this week's UN General Assembly in New York and potential diplomatic progress on Iran.

Treasury yields are down 2 to 3 basis points across the curve, giving bonds a bid to start the week. The market is pricing in roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes across the Fed's final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting and approximately 35 additional basis points of increases priced into 2027. Fed speakers remain hawkish — Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari echoed those concerns Sunday, saying inflation is reaching all corners of the US economy.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems trace back to elevated oil prices, and even a modest geopolitical improvement at the UN General Assembly could trigger an outsized decline in energy prices. Lower oil would cascade into lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence — a powerful combination for equities. Trump has signaled openness to meeting Iran's president at the UN GA, and China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting some international pressure is building toward de-escalation. The Trump-Xi summit on Thursday could also deliver modest tariff relief and an extension of the existing trade truce, adding another incremental positive.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was overwhelmingly negative — the Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia, Iran warned of fierce retaliation if the US resumes military action, and a cascade of troubling AI headlines emerged including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic revenue projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. The geopolitical landscape remains grim with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies through Hormuz, aligned militias, and the Houthis, while oil tanker costs surge and motor oil shortages spread in the US. Diplomatic progress at the UN GA may prove to be wishful thinking, and the market is rallying into thin air on hope alone.

Market Outlook

Adam leans cautiously mixed with a short-term bullish tilt driven entirely by diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly and the potential Trump-Xi summit on Thursday. The key catalyst to watch today is any headline out of the UN General Assembly suggesting progress toward a ceasefire or diplomatic engagement with Iran, which would likely trigger a sharp drop in oil and a cascade of bullish macro effects. Without that catalyst, the rally lacks fundamental support given the weight of negative weekend newsflow.

Sector Watch

AI and data center picks-and-shovels stocks are outperforming in Europe Monday morning despite a negative weekend for the sector broadly, including a delayed Anthropic IPO, disappointing Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI projecting roughly $280 billion in negative free cash flow from 2026 to 2030, and Oracle data center debt concerns. Big Tech off-balance-sheet AI liability exposure has risen approximately $300 billion in just the last 12 months, creating a growing risk for the sector. Chinese memory maker CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, which combined with comments from Acer's CEO about Chinese memory easing component shortages, points to incremental margin pressure for Western memory players including Micron. Energy stocks face pressure as Brent crude slides 170 basis points to around $102.10 per barrel, though geopolitical risk remains the dominant overhang for the sector.
