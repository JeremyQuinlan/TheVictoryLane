Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +329 points/110 basis points, and Russell 2000 +47 basis points, despite a weekend full of negative news including geopolitical escalation and troubling AI headlines. Asian and European markets are broadly higher, with the primary driver appearing to be optimism around the UN General Assembly in New York and potential diplomatic progress on Iran and the Middle East. Brent crude is sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10, extending losses from last week, while bitcoin surges 350 basis points to roughly $83,900.

Treasury yields are down 2-3 basis points across the curve, providing some relief to risk assets. The Fed is priced for roughly 33 basis points of additional rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds of an increase at the October 28th meeting, and another 35 basis points of increases penciled in for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices. Treasury is also set to issue over $1 trillion worth of T-bills in the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of total marketable debt.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems trace back to elevated oil prices, and even a minor geopolitical breakthrough at the UN General Assembly could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices. A sustained decline in oil would lower yields, reduce inflation, ease margin pressure, and improve consumer confidence simultaneously. Trump signaled openness to meeting Iran's president at the UN GA, and China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing global urgency to resolve the crisis. The market has already absorbed a weekend full of bad news and is still rallying, suggesting resilient underlying demand.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was nearly uniformly negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia, Anthropic's IPO was delayed with underwhelming revenue projections, OpenAI is burning through cash at an alarming rate, and Oracle faces data center debt problems. Monday's rally appears entirely sentiment-driven around diplomatic hopes that may prove to be wishful thinking, as a breakthrough at the UN General Assembly is hard to imagine. The global monetary policy backdrop remains hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month and more increases expected from multiple central banks. AI off-balance-sheet liabilities for big tech have surged roughly $300 billion in just the last 12 months, creating a fresh and underappreciated risk.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish on a session basis, driven entirely by geopolitical diplomacy optionality at the UN General Assembly rather than fundamentals. The key catalyst to watch is any signal from the UN General Assembly meetings — particularly a potential Trump-Iran presidential meeting — that could trigger a meaningful drop in oil prices and unlock a cascade of positive macro effects including lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, and improved consumer confidence.

Sector Watch

AI and data center infrastructure is under meaningful pressure from a negative weekend news cycle — delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn of roughly $280 billion in negative free cash flow projected for 2026-2030, and Oracle data center debt issues. European data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming despite this, suggesting selective resilience. Energy is sliding as Brent drops 170 basis points on diplomatic optimism, but tanker costs are surging and motor oil is being rationed in the US due to Iran war-related shortages. Chinese memory supply from CXMT entering mass production with next-gen chips could pressure pricing for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron.
