GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran continues striking ships in the Strait of Hormuz while the US declines to respond kinetically, raising serious questions about Washington's endgame (WSJ). Behind the scenes, Tehran's leadership is more nervous about domestic economic fallout than their defiant public posture suggests (Reuters).

- The UAE has suspended all trade and commercial ties with Iran after Tehran fired two ballistic missiles at the Gulf state (FT). Iran is also threatening to target US military installations in Europe if Trump escalates (FT).

- The Pentagon is evaluating a smaller military footprint in the Persian Gulf and may not rebuild bases damaged by Iranian strikes — a move that would effectively elevate Tehran's regional standing (Washington Post).

- Trump paused 50% tariffs on select Canadian goods for three days while both countries work to finalize a trade agreement (WSJ).

- Trump is seeking a meeting with Kim Jong Un this year in hopes of persuading North Korea to abandon its nuclear weapons program (WSJ).

- China's youth unemployment jumped to 17.9% in July as a record graduate class entered the labor market (SCMP). Beijing is separately easing access to $1.6 trillion in housing provident funds to stimulate the property sector (Bloomberg).

- Fuel prices will remain elevated even without fresh highs in Brent, because global refining capacity has been severely reduced by the Iran and Ukraine wars, causing crack spreads to surge — "The world isn't running out of oil. It is running out of places to turn that oil into fuel" (NYT).

COMPANY NEWS

KEYS (Keysight) delivered a clean beat in FQ3: EPS of $3.07 vs. Street $2.48, revenue of $1.85B vs. Street $1.745B. FQ4 guidance is well above consensus — EPS midpoint $3.37 vs. Street $2.68, revenue midpoint $1.94B vs. Street $1.83B. Some investors are fixating on the book-to-bill of only 1.13x, but there's little to complain about here.

LZB (La-Z-Boy) missed on FQ1 EPS (43c vs. Street 49c) and sales ($475MM vs. Street $501MM), with wholesale weakness swamping retail strength. FQ2 guidance is also soft: sales of $510MM vs. Street $537MM, operating margins of 4.75% vs. Street 6.9%. Disappointing release, full stop.

TOL (Toll Brothers) reported FQ3 results roughly inline — EPS $2.97 vs. Street $2.94, delivery revenues slightly ahead on higher ASPs, gross margins of 23.9% in line, and core margins about 30 basis points ahead. Full-year guidance reaffirmed. Management emphasized "the resilience of our affluent customer base" — a luxury builder holding up in a challenged housing market.

GOOGL (Alphabet) was forced to pay close to 7% on a long-dated Australian dollar bond sale, a record cost that underscores how AI data center financing is getting materially more expensive (Bloomberg).

NVDA (Nvidia) — China has slightly eased H200 restrictions, with ByteDance and Tencent each receiving roughly 10,000 processors recently. More firms may follow, but Beijing remains committed to domestic chip development (FT). Separately, Nvidia is increasingly deploying capital financing as a competitive weapon alongside chips and software (CNBC).

PPC (Pilgrim's Pride) — JBS offered to acquire the remaining PPC stake it doesn't own at 2.086 JBS shares, equating to roughly $28.50/share, in line with Tuesday's close.

UGI (UGI Corp) received a $42.50/share takeout bid from KKR, valuing the natural gas and electricity distributor at approximately $9B (WSJ).

CCC (CCC Intelligent Solutions) shares rallied Tuesday after Bloomberg reported Copart is among suitors for the auto insurance software firm (Bloomberg).

KEYS SK Hynix announced a ~$29B buyback after the Korean close, with management stating intrinsic value "is not fully reflected in the current stock price." The company now targets returning more than 50% of free cash flow via buybacks and dividends (Reuters). The SKHY ADR is climbing in pre-market trading.

OpenAI's Q2 revenue grew only 18% quarter-over-quarter while losses deepened, disappointing shareholders and widening the gap with Anthropic (WSJ). The company is also pausing training on its most powerful new models to install additional safety guardrails after capabilities advanced faster than expected (Sources).

CBRS (Cerebras) launched the CS-4, claiming it further widens its inference speed advantage over Nvidia (Bloomberg).
