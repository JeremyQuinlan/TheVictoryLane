Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points (65 basis points), Nasdaq +329 points (110 basis points), and Russell 2000 +13.5 points (47 basis points). Asian markets closed higher across the board led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while major Eurozone indices gained 85-120 basis points with strength in industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The rally is confounding given a weekend full of negative headlines — escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine's largest drone strike on Russia, and a slew of troubling AI-sector developments — with the primary driver appearing to be optimism around diplomatic progress at the UN General Assembly this week.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. Fed Presidents Schmid and Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices, and monetary policy globally remains pointed in a hawkish direction with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is the primary driver today, with Trump signaling openness to meeting Iran's president and China and Pakistan reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis. Even a small improvement in Middle East tensions would likely trigger an outsized decline in oil prices, which in turn would lower yields, reduce inflation, ease margin pressure, and improve consumer confidence. Saudi Arabia is also moving to increase ship-to-ship oil transfers to compensate for supply shortages, showing some supply-side flexibility. The US-China trade truce remains intact through November 10th, and the upcoming Trump-Xi summit creates additional potential for modest tariff relief.

BEAR CASE

Nearly all the weekend newsflow was net negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest-ever drone strike on Russia, and a cascade of troubling AI headlines emerged including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. Geopolitical conditions remain genuinely grim with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis, while motor oil prices are surging and being rationed in the US. The UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism could easily prove to be wishful thinking, and the latest energy price spike has not yet fully worked its way through the economy and corporate earnings.

Market Outlook

Adam leans cautiously bullish for Monday's session driven entirely by UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism — specifically the prospect of even modest Iran/Middle East de-escalation, which could cascade into lower oil, lower yields, and improved margins. The key catalyst to watch is any signal out of the UN General Assembly around US-Iran contact or a Trump-Iran meeting, as even a small geopolitical improvement could trigger an outsized drop in oil and a significant market rally. However, Adam acknowledges the underlying macro backdrop remains genuinely grim and the weekend newsflow was net negative.

Sector Watch

AI infrastructure is under significant pressure after a weekend deluge of negative headlines: delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI expected to burn roughly 280 billion dollars in free cash flow from 2026-2030, and Oracle data center debt issues in New Mexico. Big Tech AI off-balance sheet liabilities have risen roughly 300 billion dollars in the last 12 months, creating a fresh risk overhang for the sector. Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are actually outperforming in Europe this morning despite these headwinds, suggesting the sellside narrative remains bifurcated. Energy remains the dominant macro sector theme — oil is sliding 170 basis points to around 102 dollars on diplomatic optimism, but tanker costs are surging, motor oil is being rationed in the US, and the Iran war continues to drive diesel price spikes. European financials are outperforming with SocGen rallying after providing bullish 2029-2030 return on tangible equity targets of 13-15 percent-plus at its analyst day. Chinese memory producer CXMT announced its next-gen platform has entered mass production, which is bearish for DRAM pricing and could pressure Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron margins.
