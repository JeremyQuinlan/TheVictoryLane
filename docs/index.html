Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +110 basis points, and R2K +47 basis points despite a weekend full of negative headlines. Asian markets closed broadly higher led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while European indices are up 85-120 basis points with strength in industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The primary driver of equity strength appears to be optimism around the UN General Assembly in New York this week, where diplomatic progress on the Iran conflict is hoped for — Brent crude is sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10 on those peace hopes.

Treasury yields are down 2-3 basis points across the curve Monday morning, providing a modest tailwind to equities. The Fed remains in hawkish territory — the market is pricing roughly 33 basis points of additional rate hikes over the final two FOMC meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and another 35 basis points of increases penciled in for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed's Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that elevated inflation is broad-based and not solely driven by oil prices. Treasury is also set to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months as Bessent leans heavily on short-term debt, pushing bills above 24% of marketable debt versus the department's 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is driving the morning bid, with Trump signaling openness to meeting Iran's president and reportedly pulling back from Houthi air strikes at the last minute. Any credible de-escalation in the Middle East would cause oil to drop sharply, unlocking a cascade of bullish macro effects — lower yields, easing inflation, margin relief, and improved consumer confidence. China and Pakistan are reportedly pressing Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting even adversaries recognize the danger of further escalation. The Trump/Xi summit Thursday could also deliver modest tariff reductions, adding another incremental positive.

BEAR CASE

Nearly every piece of weekend news flow was net negative — the Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest-ever drone strike on Russia, and a string of troubling AI headlines hit after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, disappointing Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI's massive cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. The Middle East geopolitical situation continues to deteriorate with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias, and the Houthis, while oil tanker costs are surging and motor oil is being rationed at US retailers. UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism could easily prove to be wishful thinking, and monetary policy globally remains firmly hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously mixed with a diplomatic-optimism-driven bid that feels fragile. The key catalyst to watch today and this week is whether the UN General Assembly produces any tangible de-escalation signal on the Iran/Middle East conflict — even a small step toward diplomacy would likely trigger a sharp drop in oil and a cascade of bullish macro effects including lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, and improved consumer sentiment. Without that, the weekend's negative AI headlines and geopolitical deterioration remain overhangs.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe Monday morning. AI infrastructure broadly is under pressure from a cluster of negative weekend headlines including Oracle debt problems, OpenAI's projected negative free cash flow of roughly $280 billion from 2026-2030, and the Anthropic IPO delay. Energy is a critical sector to watch — Brent at $102.10 and sliding on Middle East diplomacy hopes, with motor oil being rationed at US retailers and oil tanker costs surging; a sustained oil decline would be a major positive for margins across the economy. European financials are outperforming with SocGen rallying after guiding to 13-14% return on tangible equity in 2029 and above 15% beyond 2030 at its capital markets day.
