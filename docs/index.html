VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTH Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO has been pushed from October to November, and its projected year-end ARR of greater than 100 billion dollars to greater than 110 billion dollars disappointed bulls who were hoping for 120 billion dollars or more. The company is considering launching a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture momentum lost to OpenAI's Astra, and plans to have 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027, though OpenAI appears to be taking meaningful token share.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times report on efforts to offload loans tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center facility in New Mexico, with those efforts described as having 'hit a wall.' The debt price action on the Oracle-linked facility is deteriorating, raising questions about the risk profile of the company's aggressive AI infrastructure buildout.

▲ PSKY Paramount Global M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit blocking its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. Under the potential settlement, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central, clearing a meaningful regulatory hurdle for the deal to close.

▲ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Warner Bros. Discovery's pending merger with Paramount is moving closer to resolution as Paramount enters advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general. The potential concessions — CNN editorial independence and divestiture of select cable channels including Comedy Central — appear manageable and could clear the path for deal completion.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. This raises concerns about franchisee financial health and the potential for unit closures or operational disruptions within the Wendy's system.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk M&A

Novo Nordisk is facing shareholder pressure to diversify away from its heavy GLP-1 concentration by pursuing acquisitions. While this reflects confidence in the company's balance sheet capacity, it also signals investor unease about over-reliance on the GLP-1 franchise for future growth.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in active talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on ten or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, a meaningful potential contract win that would validate the program after its troubled history and provide a revenue stream to compete with SpaceX.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment, positioning the firm as a key enterprise partner in the growing AI governance and compliance space. This is an incremental positive for Accenture's AI services revenue.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores nationwide as the grocer pushes back against supplier price hikes, signaling increasing retailer leverage and willingness to remove major brands over pricing disputes. This is negative for Red Bull's distribution but reflects broader grocery sector dynamics around margin protection.

▲ NSCL Nscale Other

London-based neocloud firm Nscale filed its S1 publicly on Friday afternoon and plans to list on the NYSE under the ticker NSCL. The IPO adds to a growing pipeline of AI infrastructure plays coming to market and will be closely watched as a read on investor appetite for the neocloud sector.

Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically ratcheted up missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities, raising fears of further energy supply disruption, while Ukraine launched a massive drone strike on Moscow including an oil refinery. The Trump/Xi summit scheduled for Thursday September 24th offers a potential modest positive catalyst, with discussions underway on tariff reductions covering roughly 30 billion dollars in goods and eliminating Chinese tariffs on American LNG.

Global monetary policy remains firmly hawkish, with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. The BOJ's latest move was slightly more dovish than expected but paradoxically spooked markets by pressuring the yen lower and rekindling fears that Japan could sell Treasuries to defend its currency. Markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over the coming weeks, and Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small improvement in Middle East geopolitics could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. The UN General Assembly running September 22nd through the 28th brings together officials from major Middle East nations including Iran, creating a real if uncertain window for diplomatic progress. The Trump/Xi summit could deliver modest but tangible tariff reductions, and China leaving its Loan Prime Rates unchanged signals stability rather than emergency stimulus. Pentagon data showing Hormuz oil shipments at a six-month high also suggests supply chains are more resilient than feared.

BEAR CASE

The Houthis are dramatically escalating attacks on Saudi infrastructure, Iran is warning of fierce retaliation if the US resumes military action, and the Pentagon is described as stretched too thin to respond to multiplying fronts — all of which points to sustained or worsening energy price pressure. The AI industry faces a cluster of negative developments including OpenAI's staggering projected negative free cash flow of roughly 280 billion dollars through 2030, rising off-balance sheet AI liabilities for Big Tech up approximately 300 billion dollars in the last year alone, and a slightly disappointing Anthropic ARR outlook. Energy price spikes are beginning to work through the system with motor oil rationing already occurring at US retailers, and it is hard to imagine this does not eventually trigger demand destruction and margin compression across the economy.

Market Outlook

Adam's overall lean is cautious to bearish heading into the week. The weekend news flow was net negative across both geopolitics and AI — the two biggest macro drivers right now — with no obvious near-term catalyst to flip sentiment. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday is the key event to watch, as even modest tariff relief could provide a short-term bid, but the escalating Iran/Houthi situation and mounting AI cost concerns are the dominant headwinds.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a cluster of headwinds including surging off-balance sheet liability exposure up roughly 300 billion dollars in the past year, OpenAI's projected negative free cash flow of 280 billion dollars through 2030, and a modestly disappointing Anthropic ARR outlook — collectively creating a more cautious setup for the sector. Energy is the dominant macro driver with the Iran conflict pushing diesel and motor oil prices sharply higher and US retailers already rationing motor oil supplies. Autos face dual pressure from Chinese OEM competition and a challenging global demand environment, as evidenced by Volkswagen's large downside margin preannouncement. Memory components are seeing some price relief as Chinese supplier CXMT ramps production, a modest positive for PC and server supply chains.
