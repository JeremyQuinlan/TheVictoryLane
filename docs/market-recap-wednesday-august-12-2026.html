RATES & FED

Treasuries were quiet Wednesday, with yields flat to down 2 basis points across the curve. The market is pricing roughly 27 basis points of hikes across the final three meetings of the year, with about 40% odds of a move on September 16. The July CPI came in right inline with consensus, modestly nudging down the probability of a September hike without removing it entirely.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Tech is doing the heavy lifting again — and the psychology has completely flipped back to bull mode, with investors once again chasing the AI pick-and-shovel names after strong hyperscaler results from AMZN and MSFT anchored sentiment over recent weeks. The real event cluster to watch starts August 26: July PCE in the morning, Nvidia earnings that night, and Warsh's Jackson Hole speech on the 28th — that three-day stretch will define the tape into Labor Day. A September hike framed as "once-and-done" could actually be a net positive, bolstering Fed credibility and helping cap long-end yields. Bears are right that inflation remains elevated in absolute terms and that fiscal deterioration is real — the US federal deficit exploded to -$432B in July vs. -$291B in June — but Iran is increasingly a "who cares" for equities as long as enough oil clears the Strait.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran is adopting a Carter-era playbook — obfuscate, delay, stall, move the goalposts — as Washington bets that economic pressure will force Tehran back to the table (WSJ). The regime is actively restructuring into a "survival economy" and hardening against sanctions.

- Houthis killed 6 people aboard a cargo ship in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait on Tuesday, the first reported fatalities from Red Sea shipping attacks in over a year (CNBC). Hormuz remains materially impaired as a commercial lane.

- The IEA's latest report warns oil supply could fall 4.3M barrels per day in 2026 due to renewed Middle East hostilities, before rebounding 8.3M BPD in 2027; demand is also seen falling 1.6M BPD as high prices bite (IEA).

- Trump is weighing a call for capital gains tax cuts and home sale exemptions as a midterm political catalyst (Bloomberg). Wisconsin Dems pushed back the progressive wing Tuesday, with DSA-backed Francesca Hong suffering a primary upset (Politico).

COMPANY NEWS

CRWV (CoreWeave) revealed a sharp jump in its revenue backlog in the opening weeks of Q3 and raised full-year guidance for revenue, operating income, and capex. The bull and bear case both got reinforced — cash consumption remains ravenous, with capex running multiples of revenue, plugged for now by capital markets, prepayments, and vendor financing.

NBIS (Nebius) similarly delivered strong bookings while highlighting improving capex ROI dynamics — the key pushback from skeptics remains the same sustainability question around the capex funding stack.

SMCI (Supermicro) had already preannounced strong FQ4 bookings and margins; the guide was strong and confirmed data center demand is firmly intact.

LITE (Lumentum) delivered very strong FQ1 guidance, though expectations coming in were elevated.

CAVA reported Q2 comps of +9%, roughly 150 basis points above consensus with guest traffic up 5.3%; EBITDA came in at $54.7MM vs. the Street's $53MM, and management reaffirmed the full-year guide.

EAT (Brinker) posted a slight EPS miss ($3.07 vs. $3.09 consensus), but Chili's comps beat (+5.6% vs. +5.55% expected), management said July momentum has accelerated, and F27 EPS guidance mid-point of $13 tops the Street's $12.50.

NVDA (Nvidia) — the $500B+ financing consortium announced Monday was framed as entirely third-party capital, but Nvidia may backstop up to 25% of originated projects (The Information). The structure exists because many customers simply can't afford the chips (WSJ).

TRMB (Trimble) beat Q2 EPS and revenue ($0.86/$972MM vs. $0.80/$950MM), raised full-year EPS guidance to $3.60–3.70 from $3.47–3.64, and announced a new $1B buyback, though ARR was roughly inline and Q3 EPS guidance midpoint was slightly light.

KTB (Kontoor Brands) posted Q2 EPS of $1.06 vs. $1.04 expected on a 710 basis point jump in adjusted gross margins, raised full-year EPS guidance to $5.25–5.35, and announced a $400MM accelerated share repurchase.

PFGC (Performance Food) missed FQ4 EPS slightly ($1.59 vs. $1.61), with F27 guidance midpoints light on both sales ($72.75B vs. $72.79B) and EBITDA ($2.175B vs. $2.19B).

ORCL (Oracle) is preparing another round of layoffs as infrastructure buildout costs escalate (Business Insider).

WEN (Wendy's) — a Trian-led consortium is preparing a takeout bid (FT).

MAT/WBD — hopes for a Barbie sequel are dimming amid a compensation standoff between talent and Warner Bros. (Variety).

PSKY/WBD — Paramount is discussing an editorial board structure for CNN to address independence concerns around the merger (WSJ); California gubernatorial frontrunner Xavier Becerra said the deal should settle with state AGs suing to block it (Bloomberg).

T (AT&T) says open-weight AI models now account for 25% of internal token use and expects that figure to reach 70–80% (WSJ).

HD (Home Depot) — Chair and CEO Ted Decker is taking a temporary medical leave of absence; the company expects his return within months.

Los Angeles Lakers — sold to Josh Kushner and Bob Iger for ~$12.5B, up from the ~$10B valuation when Mark Walter took majority control in 2025 (FT).
