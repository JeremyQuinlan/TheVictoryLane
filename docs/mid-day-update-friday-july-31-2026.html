RATES & FED

Treasuries are under meaningful selling pressure, with yields rising 5–6 basis points across the curve — a problem that isn't going away. The three hawkish Fed dissenters made their case publicly Friday morning, and the view here is that markets would be trading considerably better had they won Wednesday's argument. Anxiety that the Fed is falling behind on inflation is a primary driver of the relentless rise in borrowing costs.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The headline indices are flattering a market that's actually quite sick underneath — equal-weight S&P is down 45 basis points while the Russell 2000 is off sharply, and that tells you everything. The yield problem is getting worse, driven by a toxic cocktail of sticky inflation, AI capex-fueled debt issuance, resilient growth, and eroding Fed credibility. Amazon's blowout AWS print is genuinely impressive, but the fact that its free cash flow went deeply negative and capex guidance jumped to $220B underscores the fundamental tension in the AI trade: the cash drain is enormous and the payoff horizon keeps extending. With AAPL weak, software names rolling over on earnings, and yields still climbing, the path of least resistance for equities looks lower until rates stabilize.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Japan's BOJ held rates steady but delivered a hawkish warning — flagging for the first time that inflation could overshoot its target (Reuters). Separately, Japan and South Korea conducted a rare coordinated FX intervention, buying their currencies possibly with US support (Reuters).

- China's NBS PMIs for July missed badly: manufacturing came in at 49.2 vs. 50.1 expected (down from 50.3 in June), and non-manufacturing hit 49 vs. 50 expected (CNBC).

- Eurozone CPI for July: headline +2.9% inline, but core came in at +2.5% — a tick above the +2.4% consensus and up from June (Bloomberg).

- TSLA: WSJ reported Tesla has structured its China business for easy separation amid geopolitical tensions — potentially useful for a SpaceX merger scenario too. Musk called the report "absurdly fake news" (WSJ, Reuters).

COMPANY NEWS

AMZN delivered blow-out Q2 AWS revenue and margins with very bullish AI demand commentary, but free cash flow was deeply negative and full-year capex guidance was raised 10% to $220B — the cash problem is real (results).

AAPL posted underwhelming FQ3 results with particularly soft Services revenue, and FQ4 guidance missed (sales +~10% vs. Street +12%) due to FX headwinds and supply chain constraints.

ETN crushed Q2 estimates with EPS of $3.15 vs. $3.08 expected on 14% organic growth vs. the Street's 9.7%, and raised full-year organic sales guidance to +11–13% from +9–11%, with EPS now $13.40–13.60 vs. prior $13.05–13.50 — data center demand is a clear tailwind.

NVT was the blowout of the morning: Q2 organic sales surged +47% vs. the Street's +26%, EPS hit $1.45 vs. $1.15 expected, and full-year organic growth guidance jumped to +32–34% from +21–23%.

MPWR reported Q2 EPS of $6.50 vs. $5.87 expected on sales of $980.6MM vs. $903MM, driven by the Enterprise Data unit; Q3 guide of $1.14–1.16B dwarfs the $986MM consensus.

CBOE posted Q2 adjusted EPS of $3.56 vs. $3.47 and boosted its organic revenue growth target to 15–19% from 11–15%.

RDDT actually beat on EBITDA ($343MM vs. $295MM) with strong ARPU-driven sales and an above-consensus Q3 guide — but the stock is tumbling anyway, likely on valuation and sentiment.

RBLX missed on DAUs (123MM vs. 128.7MM) and hours engaged (29B vs. 30.8B), with bookings at the low end and a soft Q3 guide.

COIN badly missed on EBITDA ($208MM vs. $322MM expected) and revenue ($1.22B vs. $1.29B) as crypto spot volume fell 25% Q/Q and volatility dried up.

XOM narrowly missed on Q2 adjusted EPS ($3.52 vs. $3.54 expected) with weakness in Energy Products, though free cash flow of $17.2B and production both beat.

CVX delivered solid Q2 adjusted EPS of $6.06 vs. $5.65 expected, with Downstream driving the beat and robust cash flow.

LYB posted EBITDA of $2.12B vs. $1.78B expected, benefiting from favorable execution and supply chain disruptions stemming from the Iran conflict — alongside DOW, a clear geopolitical beneficiary.

CRWV had to pile on sweeteners to close a debt deal tied to Anthropic, signaling waning investor appetite for AI-linked credit (FT).
