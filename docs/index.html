▲ DIS Disney Other

Disney's parks business is outperforming peers, many of whom are seeing traffic headwinds, thanks to strategic price promotions and package deals. ▼ NVO Novo Nordisk Guidance Cut

Novo Nordisk shares are slumping in European trading after the company failed to provide sufficiently granular financial targets at its Monday analyst meeting, disappointing investors who were hoping for more concrete guidance. The stock is underperforming the broader European market rally. This is a near-term negative as the company touts future blockbuster launches without specific numbers to back them up.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times report over the weekend focusing on poor price action in debt tied to its massive data center in New Mexico known as Project Jupiter, with efforts to offload loans tied to the facility having hit a wall. This adds to a broader set of concerns about AI off-balance-sheet liability exposure for large tech firms rising roughly 300 billion dollars in just the last 12 months. The headline is a near-term negative for Oracle and the broader data center buildout narrative.

Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with the S&P up 49 points or 65 basis points, Nasdaq up 329 points or 110 basis points, and the Russell 2000 up 47 basis points. European indices are up 85 to 120 basis points with strength in industrials, banks, and tech, while Asian markets also closed broadly higher led by Korea up 165 basis points and Hong Kong up 118 basis points. The rally is somewhat confounding given a weekend full of negative headlines — escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine's largest drone strike on Russia, and a slew of troubling AI-sector news — with the primary driver appearing to be optimism around diplomatic progress at the UN General Assembly this week.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2 to 3 basis points across the curve. The Fed remains in hawkish mode — markets are pricing in roughly 33 basis points of additional rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds on a hike at the October 28th meeting, plus another 35 basis points of increases penciled in for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that inflation is running hot well beyond just energy prices, while the Treasury plans to issue over one trillion dollars in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is the primary driver of today's strength, with markets hoping for progress on Middle East tensions that could bring oil prices meaningfully lower. A sustained decline in oil would unlock a cascade of positive macro effects — lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing international alarm that could accelerate a diplomatic resolution. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday could also yield modest tariff reductions and a trade truce extension, adding another incremental positive.

BEAR CASE

Nearly every piece of weekend news was net negative — Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalated dramatically, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia, and a string of troubling AI headlines hit after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. The geopolitical landscape remains grim with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis, while oil tanker shortages are adding fresh cost burdens. UN General Assembly optimism may prove to be wishful thinking, and the aggregate tariff rate on Chinese imports is expected to be hiked back to pre-SCOTUS IEEPA levels in the coming weeks regardless of the summit outcome.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously mixed with a diplomatic-optimism-driven bid that could easily reverse. The key catalyst to watch today is any signal out of the UN General Assembly regarding US-Iran diplomacy — Trump reportedly called off Houthi airstrikes at the last minute Sunday and signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, and even a small de-escalation signal could trigger an outsized oil sell-off with a cascade of bullish macro effects.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe this morning despite a weekend full of negative AI headlines. AI off-balance-sheet liability exposure for large tech firms has risen roughly 300 billion dollars in the past 12 months, creating a fresh source of sector risk. Energy is under pressure with Brent sliding 170 basis points to around 102 dollars and 10 cents as diplomatic optimism around Iran weighs on crude; oil tanker costs are surging and motor oil is being rationed in US retail, compounding margin pressure across energy-exposed industries. European banks outperform on the back of SocGen's bullish capital markets day targets including a return on tangible equity of 13 to 14 percent in 2029 and above 15 percent thereafter.
