Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points (65 basis points), Nasdaq +329 points (110 basis points), and Russell 2000 +13.5 points (47 basis points). Asian markets posted broad gains led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while major Eurozone indices are up 85-120 basis points with strength in industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The rally is confounding given a weekend full of negative headlines — escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine's largest drone strike on Russia, and a wave of troubling AI news — with the primary driver appearing to be diplomatic optimism ahead of the UN General Assembly in New York.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The Fed remains in hawkish mode — the market is pricing approximately 33 basis points of additional rate hikes across the final two meetings of the year, with roughly 50/50 odds of an increase at the October 28th meeting, and about 35 basis points of further tightening priced for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that inflation is running too hot even without oil, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari echoed those hawkish sentiments Sunday, noting inflation is reaching all corners of the economy. Treasury is also set to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt and above the Treasury's own target of approximately 20%.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is the primary bull driver — Trump signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, and even a marginal de-escalation in the Middle East would likely send oil sharply lower. A sustained drop in oil prices would be a powerful multi-vector positive: lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, improved consumer confidence, and margin relief for corporates. The Trump-Xi summit on Thursday could also yield modest tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion in goods and an extension of the existing trade truce, adding another layer of macro relief.

BEAR CASE

Nearly all the weekend news flow was net negative — Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia dramatically escalated, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia, and a cascade of troubling AI headlines hit after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, disappointing Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. Monetary policy globally remains pointed hawkish with multiple central banks expected to hike in the coming weeks, and core inflation is running hot beyond just energy. The UN General Assembly optimism may simply be wishful thinking, and any failure to deliver on diplomatic progress could quickly reverse today's gains.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously mixed with a near-term bullish tilt driven by geopolitical optionality — specifically the hope that the UN General Assembly produces even modest Middle East diplomatic progress, which would cascade into lower oil prices, lower yields, and better margins. The key catalyst to watch is any signal from the Trump-Iran or Trump-Xi diplomatic track this week, as even a small positive development on energy supply could trigger an outsized bullish reaction across markets.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe Monday morning. AI-linked off-balance-sheet liabilities for Big Tech firms have surged approximately $300 billion in the last 12 months alone, creating a fresh and underappreciated risk for the sector. Chinese memory giant CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, which could further pressure pricing for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Acer's CEO noted Chinese memory supply is helping ease component shortages and capping prices. Energy and utilities are underperforming in Europe. The Iran war is the primary driver of diesel price spikes and motor oil shortages, with retailers in the US now rationing motor oil supplies. Oil tanker costs are surging, adding further strain to already tight energy markets.
