Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +110 basis points, and R2K +47 basis points, despite a weekend full of net negative news including geopolitical escalation and troubling AI headlines. Asian and European markets are broadly higher, with the UN General Assembly driving diplomatic optimism around Middle East tensions and a potential Iran meeting with Trump. Brent crude is sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10 while bitcoin surges 350 basis points to roughly $83,900.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The Fed remains in hawkish territory — markets are pricing in roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28 meeting and an additional 35 basis points of increases expected in 2027. Kansas City Fed's Schmid and Minneapolis Fed's Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that elevated inflation extends well beyond energy prices. Treasury also plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

The primary bull driver is UN General Assembly optimism — if even marginal diplomatic progress occurs on the Iran/Middle East front, oil could drop sharply and unleash a chain of favorable macro effects including lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing global pressure for de-escalation. Trump called off air strikes against the Houthis at the last minute and indicated openness to meeting Iran's president, which markets are reading as a meaningful de-escalation signal. The global equity tape is remarkably resilient given the weight of the negative weekend news flow.

BEAR CASE

Nearly every piece of weekend news was net negative — the Houthis launched a brazen missile and drone attack on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine hit Russia with its largest drone strike of the war, and a string of troubling AI headlines emerged including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic revenue projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. The geopolitical situation is genuinely deteriorating with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supply via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis, while Russian refining capacity continues to be taken offline. Today's diplomatic optimism could easily prove to be wishful thinking, and the recent energy price spike has not yet fully worked its way through corporate earnings and consumer spending.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously mixed with a slight bullish tilt for today driven purely by diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly and the prospect of a Trump-Iran meeting — not fundamentals. The key catalyst to watch is any headline out of the UN General Assembly suggesting diplomatic progress on the Middle East or the Iran war, which could trigger a sharp drop in oil and a cascade of bullish macro effects.

Sector Watch

AI and data center infrastructure stocks face headwinds from a cluster of negative weekend headlines including delayed Anthropic IPO, disappointing Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn forecasts, and Oracle data center debt problems — yet data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe this morning, suggesting the market is selectively digesting the bad news. Energy is under pressure as Brent slides toward $102, though sustained geopolitical tension keeps a floor under oil prices. Chinese memory producer CXMT announcing mass production of its next-gen platform is a modest headwind for memory pricing broadly, with Acer's CEO noting Chinese supply is already helping ease component shortages and cap prices.
