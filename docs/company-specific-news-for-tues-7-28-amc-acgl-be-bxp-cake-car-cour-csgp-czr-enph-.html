MARKET OUTLOOK

The overriding theme of this earnings season is that the bar is extraordinarily high for tech — modest beats simply aren't moving stocks. That said, standouts like STX and TER are delivering numbers too good to dismiss. Outside tech, consumer and industrial results are more mixed, with pockets of real strength (Ford's ICE margins, Cheesecake Factory comps) sitting alongside genuine disappointments (O-I Glass, Caesars).

COMPANY NEWS

F (Ford) delivered solid Q2 EBIT of $2.5B versus the Street's $2.1B, with ICE vehicle margins surging 480 basis points to 4.4% (Street was 3.1%), though Pro margins disappointed at 9.7% versus the 10.4% expected. Full-year EBIT guidance raised to $10–11B from $8.5–10.5B.

CAKE (Cheesecake Factory) beat on Q2 EPS ($1.44 vs. Street $1.18) with comp sales of +5.8% at the flagship brand versus the Street's +2% estimate, citing menu innovation and rewards program momentum.

CAR (Avis) topped Q2 EBITDA estimates ($286MM vs. Street $257MM) on higher fleet utilization and lower per-unit fleet costs, though total revenue of $2.998B missed the Street's $3.1B forecast.

CZR (Caesars) missed on Q2 adjusted EBITDA ($920MM vs. Street $960MM), with Vegas and Digital dragging against regional gains. No earnings call will be held given the pending acquisition by Fertitta Entertainment announced May 28, 2026.

MDLZ (Mondelez) posted Q2 organic sales growth of +2.2% (Street +0.9%) and EPS of 73c (Street 68c), raising full-year top-line guidance to "at least 2%" from the prior 0–2% range on volume growth and share gains.

ACGL (Arch Capital) beat Q2 EPS ($2.56 vs. Street $2.47), but quality was low — the upside was largely driven by $165MM in favorable prior-year reserve developments, while core combined ratio was inline and net premiums written fell 6.9% year-over-year.

BXP posted Q2 FFO of $1.78 versus the Street's $1.70, with the beat driven by higher occupancy and lower expenses. Leasing volume hit 1.8M square feet (up from 1.1M in Q1) and total portfolio occupancy rose 100 basis points quarter-over-quarter to 88.4%.

CSGP (CoStar) beat Q2 EBITDA ($184MM vs. Street $174MM) but trimmed full-year revenue guidance; Q3 EBITDA guidance of $200MM fell well short of the Street's $222MM.

V (Visa) beat FQ3 EPS ($3.32 vs. Street $3.23) on resilient payment volumes and value-added services growth of +45% year-over-year, with full-year guidance nudged higher. One watch item: US payment volume growth linearity appears to have dipped in July versus June.

LSTR (Landstar) missed Q2 EPS slightly ($1.44 vs. Street $1.48) due to unfavorable development of prior-year insurance claims, though truck revenue per load jumped approximately 17%.

OI (O-I Glass) badly missed Q2 EPS (9c vs. Street 24c) on European weakness — competitive pricing pressure, elevated energy costs, and restructuring disruption — and cut full-year EBITDA guidance to $1–1.1B from $1.125–1.225B.

PPG (PPG Industries) posted a small Q2 EPS miss ($2.23 vs. Street $2.25) and merely reiterated guidance — a meaningful contrast to Sherwin-Williams' recent beat-and-raise.

BE (Bloom Energy) crushed Q2 EPS (78c vs. Street 40c) on sales of $1.065B (Street $838MM) and margins roughly 200 basis points ahead of plan. Full-year EPS guidance raised to $2.55–2.85 from $1.85–2.25. All major US hyperscalers and over a dozen neoclouds have now approved Bloom's solutions for AI factories.

COUR (Coursera) announced a $100MM strategic equity investment in LearnVector Inc., a new AI-native learning company founded by Andrew Ng, AI luminary and Coursera co-founder.

ENPH (Enphase) reported Q2 EPS/revenue essentially inline at 46c/$292MM versus the Street's 47c/$292MM, with Q3 guidance midpoints also tracking close to consensus.

KLAC (KLA Corp.) delivered FQ4 results roughly inline — underwhelming given the high bar — and FQ1 guidance of $1.16 EPS/$4B revenue is only modestly above the Street's $1.10/$3.94B. Nothing broken, but likely not enough to drive near-term buying.

LOGI (Logitech) beat FQ1 EPS hard ($1.85 vs. Street $1.33), aided by tariff refunds boosting gross margins, but FQ2 guidance disappointed with sales of $1.185–1.22B versus the Street's $1.23B.

MANH (Manhattan Associates) beat Q2 EPS/sales ($1.39/$298MM vs. Street $1.32/$288MM) with RPOs up 20%, and raised full-year EPS growth guidance to 8–9% from 5–6%.

NXPI (NXP Semiconductors) posted modest Q2 upside ($3.61 EPS/$3.49B revenue vs. Street $3.54/$3.46B) with OK Q3 guidance — but like much of tech this season, "not bad enough to sell, not good enough to buy" sums it up.

OMC (Omnicom) delivered strong Q2 organic revenue growth of +6.1% versus the Street's +3.9% and raised full-year organic revenue guidance, with EPS roughly inline.

QRVO (Qorvo) crushed FQ1 EPS ($1.64 vs. Street $1.10) on sales of $785MM (Street $743MM) and gross margins of 52.8% — roughly 380 basis points above plan. F27 EPS guidance raised to "above $7" from "approaching $7."

STX (Seagate) is one of the season's clearest standouts: Q2 EPS of $5.71 (Street $5.08), sales of $3.63B (Street $3.49B), margins more than 200 basis points above plan, and FQ1 guidance of ~$7.30 EPS absolutely blowing away the Street's $5.80.
