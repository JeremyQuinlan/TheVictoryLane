Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

US equity futures are in the red Thursday morning with the S&P down 44 points, Nasdaq off 105 basis points, and broad weakness across European and Asian markets. The big story is the lack of progress on US-Iran diplomacy, which is sending Brent crude spiking 275 basis points back to around $106 after briefly dipping below $100 just 36 hours ago. The Trump-Xi summit is expected to be long on pageantry and short on substance, with the Busan Agreement extended only two months — a deeply underwhelming outcome that markets are digesting negatively.

The 10- and 30-year Treasury yields are climbing 2 to 3 basis points, extending gains from Wednesday's blowout flash PMIs, while 2-year yields are flat. Markets are pricing in 38 basis points of additional Fed hikes this year with odds of an October 28th hike above 70%, plus another roughly 55 basis points in 2027. The Japanese yen continues to weaken back toward the 160 level versus the dollar, and Fed Governor Barr on Wednesday became the latest official to warn that further tightening will be needed.

Market Outlook

Adam is leaning bearish for Thursday's session. The twin headwinds of rising oil prices due to stalled Iran diplomacy and climbing yields from hot PMI data are the dominant pressure points. The key catalyst to watch is the Trump-Xi White House meeting — but the base case is status quo, meaning no meaningful positive surprise, which leaves the market vulnerable to further selling.

Sector Watch

Energy is the standout sector to watch — Brent back at $106 on stalled Iran diplomacy is bullish for energy stocks but a major headwind for the broader market via yields and inflation. AI and tech are under pressure as the Meta Connect keynote disappointed and broader AI news flow over the past week has been net negative; Raspberry Pi's CEO also noted memory prices are at or near their peak, which could weigh on semiconductor sentiment. European industrials, autos, tech, and banks are underperforming this morning. Nicotine and tobacco names could get a lift from reports that the FDA may ease restrictions and allow faster product launches.

VK · Key Names
◆ META Meta Platforms Product Launch

Zuckerberg's Meta Connect keynote Wednesday night was at best inline with expectations. While AI narrative remains positively focused on Muse's momentum, the broader AI news flow from the industry over the past week has been net negative. The keynote did not deliver meaningful upside surprise.

▲ GOOGL Alphabet Product Launch

Google is nearing the release of its next-generation flagship frontier model, Gemini 4, according to the head of its DeepMind unit. This is a significant product milestone in the escalating AI model race and signals Google is closing the gap with competitors.

▼ BA Boeing Other

Boeing is unlikely to receive an incremental plane order from China during the Trump-Xi summit this week. The company is instead focused on finalizing the 200-unit order announced back in May, and Reuters reports hope for a new deal has largely faded. This is a missed opportunity for a meaningful positive catalyst.

◆ GRAL Grail FDA Approval

An FDA advisory committee recommended approval for Grail's multicancer blood test, though support was somewhat tepid regarding effectiveness and overall benefits. The outcome was largely expected following the earlier publication of briefing documents, limiting the upside surprise.

▲ KNF Knife River Activist Entry

Knife River is facing activist pressure from Starboard Value, with the hedge fund pushing the construction company to consider a sale. This type of activist entry typically puts a floor under the stock and could attract strategic interest.

▼ MCD McDonald's Guidance Cut

McDonald's shares slumped after the company's analyst meeting revealed Q3 comparable sales are tracking slightly below consensus. Free cash flow conversion will also fall short of prior guidance due to higher capital spending, though the company did provide a 2030 operating margin target that came in ahead of expectations.

◆ MGM MGM Resorts M&A

Barry Diller on Wednesday withdrew People Inc's proposed takeout bid for MGM Resorts, removing a near-term acquisition premium from the stock. However, Diller said he remains open to and interested in a strategic transaction with the casino giant, keeping some optionality alive.

◆ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is looking to raise equity to bolster its balance sheet as the Warner deal moves closer to completion, and the company is reportedly considering tapping Elon Musk for an investment. Equity dilution risk is a near-term overhang even as the strategic combination with Warner advances.

▲ FUL HB Fuller Earnings Beat

HB Fuller reported modest upside on EPS and EBITDA — $1.52 and $187 million versus Street estimates of $1.46 and consensus — driven by margin expansion with gross margins up 120 basis points and EBITDA margins up 80 basis points as pricing increases offset raw material inflation. Sales were inline at $938 million and guidance was left approximately unchanged with the new EPS midpoint at roughly $4.77 versus the prior $4.75.

▲ LLY Eli Lilly Partnership

Eli Lilly struck a research collaboration and licensing agreement with China's InnoCare Pharma worth up to $3.35 billion. The deal expands Lilly's pipeline access and reinforces its aggressive push into new therapeutic areas.
