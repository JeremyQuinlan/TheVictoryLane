Macro · Rates

Wednesday morning brought a notably cool August PCE print, with core inflation coming in at +0.2% month-over-month versus the Street's +0.3% estimate, and +3.0% year-over-year versus the +3.3% consensus. Q2 GDP was revised sharply higher to +2.2% annualized from a prior reading of +1.5%, reflecting broad-based upward revisions to investment, consumer spending, and government spending. ADP's September jobs report also came in slightly ahead at +90K private payrolls versus the 75K forecast, rebounding from a weak +36K in August.

The cooler PCE print is dovish and bullish for rates, particularly coming on the heels of dovish remarks from Fed's Williams on Tuesday. However, important methodological changes were implemented starting with the August PCE reading that had a downward mechanical impact on price momentum, which may limit the market's enthusiasm for the softer number. Markets are now focused on September price data, with the next CPI print not due until October 14.

Market Outlook

Mildly bullish lean on the back of soft PCE and solid ADP, but conviction is limited given the PCE methodology caveat. The key near-term catalyst is Friday's BLS September jobs report, where markets would prefer a soft but not disastrous number — something in the 50–75K range is seen as ideal for yield relief.

Sector Watch

Construction, manufacturing, education/health services, and leisure/hospitality showed strength in ADP's September report. Financial activities and professional/business services were areas of weakness, shedding jobs in the month.

Calendar
Oct 3 — BLS September Jobs Report (current forecast: +90K total, +80K private)
Oct 14 — September CPI
