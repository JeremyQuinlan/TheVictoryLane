RATES & FED

The relentless rise in yields is becoming a genuine equity problem, driven by elevated inflation, the AI capex boom, resilient growth, oil prices, sovereign debt supply, and growing Fed credibility anxiety. St. Louis Fed President Musalem — a non-voter — said the yield spike signals the market is questioning the Fed's commitment to fighting inflation and suggested he would have voted for a rate hike (FT). Separately, Chair-designate Warsh is reportedly floating a reduction in annual FOMC meetings from eight to four — the legal minimum — according to sources cited by both the NYT and WSJ.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The yield surge is the market's dominant headache right now, and the bear case on rates remains intact as long as Warsh sounds more dovish than Hammack, Kashkari, or Logan — the three dissenters who, frankly, had the better argument last week. If Warsh pivots hawkish, long-end yields start falling and equities get relief; until then, valuations stay under pressure. The Iran de-escalation removes a near-term tail risk and should ease some oil-driven inflation anxiety, but don't mistake a paused airstrike for a resolved conflict. The AI capex story is a double-edged sword — $2.4 trillion in hyperscaler spending commitments is breathtaking, but free cash flow at Meta, Google, and Amazon is essentially zero or deeply negative, meaning capital markets will increasingly be asked to fund the gap (Bloomberg). The jobs report Friday is the week's biggest macro binary.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran: Trump announced Saturday night he was calling off an imminent, Israel-coordinated airstrike campaign against Iranian targets — including energy infrastructure — after a vague "deal" was reached on Hormuz and Iran's nuclear program (Vital Knowledge analysis). The attack's cancellation reflects military reality more than diplomacy: months of strikes haven't changed Iranian behavior, U.S. interceptor stockpiles are dangerously depleted, and Gulf states including Saudi Arabia pressured Washington to stand down. The prior MOU signed June 17 collapsed within weeks after Iran attacked commercial ships in Hormuz — skepticism on this latest arrangement is warranted.

- OPEC+: Agreed to a further ~188,000 BPD production increase — the sixth consecutive hike — which fully unwinds the 1.65 million BPD cut from 2023. Largely symbolic given Middle East disruptions (Bloomberg, WSJ).

- FX intervention: The U.S. Treasury intervened in currency markets Friday to support the yen — selling euros to buy yen via the NY Fed — the first such action in nearly three decades (FT).

- Ukraine: Zelensky warned partners that interceptor supplies are nearly exhausted as Russia continues ballistic missile barrages on Kyiv (Washington Post).

- Trump polls: Approval ratings are at or near record lows for his presidency — the most unpopular second-term president since Nixon (NYT).
