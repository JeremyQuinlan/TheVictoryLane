Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points (65 basis points), Nasdaq +329 points (110 basis points), and R2K +13.5 points (47 basis points). Asian markets posted broad gains led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while European indices are up 85-120 basis points with strength in industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The primary driver appears to be diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly in New York this week, despite a weekend full of negative geopolitical headlines including escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and Ukraine's largest drone strike on Russia.

Treasuries have a bid with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The market is pricing in approximately 33 basis points of rate hikes over the Fed's final two meetings of the year, with roughly 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of increases priced in for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks recently, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just energy prices, while the Treasury plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months as Bessent relies increasingly on short-term debt.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems trace back to elevated oil prices, and even a small geopolitical improvement at the UN General Assembly could trigger an outsized drop in energy, with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. Trump signaled openness to meeting with Iran's president at the GA, and China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis — signs that global pressure for de-escalation is building. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday could also produce modest tariff reductions and help sustain a constructive risk tone through the week.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape remains grim — Iran continues to tighten its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis, who dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia over the weekend. Motor oil prices are surging and being rationed in the US, oil tanker costs are spiking, and the Iran war is driving a significant diesel supply loss. Corporate earnings have held up over the summer but the latest energy price spike will begin working through the system, threatening demand destruction, and UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism may simply be wishful thinking.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish on diplomatic optionality — the key catalyst to watch is whether any meaningful progress emerges from the UN General Assembly, particularly around Iran and the Middle East, which could trigger an outsized decline in oil and a cascade of bullish macro effects. However, the strength feels fragile given the overwhelmingly negative weekend news flow, and the bear case remains very much alive if diplomacy disappoints.

Sector Watch

AI and data center stocks face a challenging backdrop after a deluge of negative weekend headlines: Anthropic's IPO has been delayed, its 2027 ARR projections were described as underwhelming, OpenAI is expected to generate approximately $280 billion in negative free cash flow from 2026 to 2030, and Oracle's Project Jupiter data center debt is struggling to find buyers. Despite this, European data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming this morning. Memory and PC component prices may face a lid as Chinese memory maker CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, which Acer's CEO noted is helping ease component shortages. Energy sector is a key watch — Brent crude is sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10 as diplomatic optimism tempers the geopolitical risk premium, but oil tanker costs are surging and supplies remain strained. Financials: SocGen shares are outperforming in Europe after providing bullish targets at its analyst day, including ROTE of 13-14% in 2029 and above 15% in 2030 and beyond.
