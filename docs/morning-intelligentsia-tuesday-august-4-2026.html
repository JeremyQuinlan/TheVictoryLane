Treasuries are selling off Tuesday morning with yields up 2–3 basis points across the curve, pressured by rising oil and resilient risk appetite. MARKET OUTLOOK

AI is the economy right now — full stop — and the WSJ piece out this morning makes clear this isn't hyperbole. The manufacturing ISM for July was net constructive, with growth metrics broadly improving even as inflation remains hot in absolute terms. Earnings season is delivering enough beats to keep bulls engaged, but elevated yields, stretched inventories, and Iran tail risk are real overhangs that the market is choosing to look through for now. CAT before the open and AMD/SPCX after the close are today's key read-throughs. The path of least resistance is modestly higher unless yields break out again.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran: Tehran denied Monday that any talks with Washington are even taking place, while Trump simultaneously shifted back toward hawkish rhetoric — yet nothing has structurally changed. There is no viable US military option, and the most likely outcome remains an MOU increasingly skewed in Iran's favor, potentially granting Tehran tacit control of the Strait of Hormuz (NYT). Bears note US forces in the region are stretched thin, global oil inventories are depleted after serving as a buffer early in the conflict, and Trump's pattern of threats-then-retreat is eroding negotiating leverage.

- US Tariffs: More than 20 Democratic state AGs filed suit against the Trump administration over the latest round of tariffs tied to alleged forced labor violations (FT).

- AI Oversight: The White House is hosting a group of AI executives Tuesday to review the recent industry oversight framework (The Information).

- G7 Debt: Interest expense now exceeds defense spending for most G7 countries, per Scope Ratings (Bloomberg).

- Japan FX: The yen will struggle to sustain any meaningful rally absent a significant BOJ policy shift; skepticism around the durability of recent intervention is widespread (WSJ).

COMPANY NEWS

PLTR (Palantir) delivered standout Q2 results: EPS of 41c vs. Street 35c, revenue $1.935B vs. Street $1.808B, operating margin 62% vs. Street 60%. Full-year sales guidance raised to $8.15–8.158B from $7.65–7.662B.

SNAP posted very strong Q2: revenue $1.598B vs. Street $1.539B, EBITDA $249.6MM vs. Street $192MM, DAUs 493MM vs. 488MM, free cash flow $121MM vs. Street $10.4MM. Q3 EBITDA guide of $325MM is roughly inline.

AMD and SPCX report after today's close — primary focus for the market tonight.

ON (ON Semi) Q2 EPS 74c vs. Street 71c; revenue $1.604B vs. Street $1.587B; GMs 39.3% vs. Street 39%. Q3 guidance mid-points ahead of plan.

GOOGL/AVGO: Google and Broadcom reportedly structured a $200B financing scheme for Anthropic to build data centers and purchase TPUs (FT).

HSBC Q2 profit before tax surged 60%, buyback initiated, cost-cutting and NII guidance raised — but expectations were already lofty going in.

VRTX Q2 EPS $4.73 vs. Street $4.72; full-year revenue guidance nudged to $13.1–13.2B from $12.95–13.1B.

CLX FQ4 EPS $1.66 vs. Street $1.64; F27 EPS guide mid-point $5.85 vs. Street $5.95. Management flagged "continued cost volatility and a value-seeking consumer."

WHR Q2 EPS -21c vs. Street -5c; full-year EPS cut to $2.50–3.00 from $3.00–3.50 due to higher interest expense. Margin improvement is happening, just not fast enough.

PLD (Prologis) formally agreed to acquire UK's Segro for $18.8B (WSJ).

BP reported Q2 adjusted net income of $5.7B vs. Street $5.0B and confirmed plans to sell its $4B US biogas business (FT).

CXMT: HP, Asus, and Acer have begun using CXMT memory in limited quantities for non-US products amid a global shortage (Nikkei). Separately, CXMT is preparing high-end smartphone memory chips competitive with SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron by year-end (Bloomberg).

WMB (Williams) agreed to acquire Momentum Midstream in a Haynesville-focused deal valued at up to $5.5B.

BWXT Q2 EPS $1.07 vs. Street $1.05; full-year EPS raised to $4.75 from $4.68. Also selling its medical business to Nordic Capital for up to $800MM to focus on nuclear national security and commercial power.

AZN/BMY: AstraZeneca shareholders pushed back on the potential Bristol-Myers acquisition, noting BMY's patent cliff exposure and that AZN can hit its $80B/2030 sales target without M&A (FT).
