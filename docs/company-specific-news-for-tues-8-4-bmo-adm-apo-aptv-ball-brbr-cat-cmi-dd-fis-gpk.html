MARKET OUTLOOK

The earnings tape this morning is unambiguously constructive — beats are broad-based, guidance is mostly moving higher, and the industrial/machinery complex (CAT, CMI, ROK) is showing genuine demand acceleration, not just cost-cutting. Tariff refunds are adding a one-time tailwind that partially flatters margins, so quality-of-beat matters. Still, when backlogs are exploding and healthcare names are raising numbers, the macro backdrop is clearly more resilient than the bears want to admit.

COMPANY NEWS

ADM delivered a strong Q2 beat ($1.84 EPS vs. Street $1.49) and raised the full-year EPS outlook to $5.15–$5.60 from $4.15–$4.70, citing broad-based segment profit growth, a constructive biofuels environment, and Nutrition momentum led by Flavors.

BRBR (BellRing) missed on EBITDA due to inventory charges and input cost pressure, trimming the full-year EBITDA guide — though sales edged ahead of plan and the full-year sales outlook was nudged higher.

KMB (Kimberly-Clark) beat on EPS ($2.12 vs. Street $2.00) via tariff refunds, productivity savings, and FX tailwinds, but organic revenue was flat vs. the Street's +1.4% forecast, and full-year guidance was trimmed.

MCD beat on Q2 EPS ($3.38 vs. Street $3.32) while aggregate comps of +1.3% barely missed the +1.4% consensus, with US comps at +0.8% and negative comparable guest counts. The company is installing Skye Anderson as new US business head to "raise the bar and accelerate performance."

W (Wayfair) posted Q2 EPS/revenue upside (95c/$3.51B vs. Street 92c/$3.47B) and strong free cash flow ($301MM vs. Street $267MM), with US revenue growth described as "the best we've seen in the entire post-COVID period, with nearly 9% year-over-year revenue growth."

APO (Apollo) missed slightly on adjusted net income per share ($2.11 vs. Street $2.15) but FRE beat and inflows of $60B crushed the $51.1B consensus estimate.

FIS (Fidelity National) reported essentially inline Q2 EPS/revenue ($1.48/$3.377B vs. Street $1.47/$3.38B) but trimmed full-year EPS guidance to $6.15–$6.24 from $6.22–$6.32.

HSIC (Henry Schein) beat Q2 EPS ($1.27 vs. Street $1.24) and raised full-year EPS guidance to $5.29–$5.39 from $5.23–$5.37.

MRK (Merck) posted a smaller-than-feared EPS loss (M&A charges the culprit) with sales of $16.6B beating the Street's $16.3B; Keytruda showed impressive upside, and the full-year sales guide was raised.

PFE (Pfizer) delivered a solid Q2 beat (77c/$15B vs. Street 68c/$14.4B), with Eliquis revenue well ahead at $2.42B vs. Street $2.09B. Full-year EPS and sales guidance raised, though the EPS range now absorbs a 10-cent acquisition charge.

RVTY (Revvity) beat on Q2 EPS ($1.41 vs. Street $1.22) and organic revenue (+3% vs. Street +1.76%), raising full-year EPS guidance to $5.30–$5.40 from $5.20–$5.30. The company is redeploying tariff refunds into incremental business investment.

WAT (Waters) edged ahead on organic revenue (+7% vs. Street +6.8%) and EPS ($3.05 vs. Street $3.01), raising all components of full-year guidance, citing BDX deal synergies and broad end-market improvement.

APTV (Aptiv) trimmed full-year EPS guidance to $5.60–$5.80 from $5.70–$6.10, blaming a "dynamic macroeconomic landscape" and unfavorable customer mix in automotive.

BALL (Ball Corp.) posted a few cents of Q2 EPS upside ($1.03 vs. Street $0.99) and reiterated prior full-year guidance.

CAT (Caterpillar) delivered a blowout Q2 ($8.17 EPS vs. Street $6.17) on stronger MP&E sales of $19.58B (+25%) vs. Street $18.1B, with Construction up 35% to $8.3B leading the charge. Adjusted op. margins of 21.9% surged 430 basis points year-over-year. Backlog hit $72.1B, up $9.4B sequentially and $34.6B year-over-year. Full-year sales guidance raised to mid-to-high teens growth from low double-digits. Caveat: $392MM in IEEPA tariff refunds and a $700MM construction dealer inventory build inflate the quality of the beat — and the standout segment was Construction, not the data-center-linked Power & Energy unit.

CMI (Cummins) beat on Q2 sales with inline EBITDA margins and raised full-year guidance to sales +10–13% (from +8–11%) and EBITDA margins of 18–18.5%.

DD (DuPont) beat Q2 EPS ($1.88 vs. Street $1.76) on better organic revenue (+4% vs. Street +3%), margin expansion, lower interest expense, and a lower tax rate. Full-year EBITDA guide raised modestly to $1.75–$1.77B.

GPK (Graphic Packaging) posted modest Q2 upside but warned EBITDA will land at the low end of prior guidance due to elevated inflation.

ROK (Rockwell Automation) beat FQ3 on EPS ($3.49 vs. Street $3.39) and organic growth (10% vs. Street 6.7%), raising full-year organic growth guidance to +7.5–9.5% and EPS to $13.00–$13.30. Strength in semiconductor, data center, and warehouse automation was the primary driver.

IT (Gartner) beat Q2 EBITDA ($466MM vs. Street $427MM) on higher revenue ($1.675B vs. Street $1.648B) and margins nearly 200 basis points above plan. Full-year guidance raised despite FX headwinds.

LDOS (Leidos) beat Q2 EBITDA ($631MM vs. Street $596MM) with margins of 13.8%, roughly 40 basis points ahead of plan, and guided toward the upper end of prior EPS/
