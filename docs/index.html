Macro · Rates

This is a company-specific earnings newsletter focused on Wednesday October 21 before-market-open reporters. There is no broad index or overnight macro commentary included in this edition. Risk tone and broad market context are not addressed.

No Fed, rates, or dollar commentary is included in this newsletter. However, M/I Homes explicitly flags higher mortgage interest rates as a key headwind depressing housing demand and margins.

Market Outlook

No explicit directional market call is made. The earnings slate is mixed — healthcare and industrials are broadly constructive, financials are nuanced, housing is a clear pressure point, and AT&T's competitive overhang from SpaceX adds an uncertainty layer to telecom.

Sector Watch

Healthcare devices are showing a pattern of weakness — both J&J and Abbott's Medical Devices segments have now missed, warranting caution on the broader med-tech group. Housing remains under acute pressure from elevated mortgage rates and margin compression, as evidenced by M/I Homes' large shortfall. Telecom faces a structural competitive threat from SpaceX entering the wireless market. Industrial and life science tools names like Wabtec and Thermo Fisher are showing resilient execution with raised guidance.

VK · Key Names
◆ PM Philip Morris Earnings Beat

Philip Morris beat Q3 EPS at $2.36 versus the Street's $2.30 and delivered organic growth of 7.1% versus the expected 5.4%. Despite the beat, management only reiterated full-year FX-neutral EPS guidance of $8.11–$8.26, citing geopolitical headwinds and energy-related cost inflation from the Middle East conflict pressuring Q4 input prices.

▼ MCO Moody's Earnings Miss

Moody's missed Q3 EPS at $4.14 versus the Street's $4.27 and revenue at $2.023 billion versus the expected $2.076 billion. The company reiterated its prior full-year guidance, offering limited incremental positive catalyst to offset the top- and bottom-line shortfall.

◆ NTRS Northern Trust Earnings Beat

Northern Trust posted a large headline EPS beat at $3.40 versus the Street's $2.88, but approximately $50 million in one-time pre-tax tailwinds flattered the number. Core fee revenue missed at $1.373 billion versus the expected $1.405 billion and the Q4 revenue outlook of plus 6–7% fell short of the Street's 7.4% estimate.

◆ ABT Abbott Restructuring

Abbott reported Q3 EPS of $1.42 and revenue growth of 7% — essentially inline with consensus — and reiterated full-year guidance. Nutrition and Diagnostics outperformed while Medical Devices fell short, marking the second consecutive miss from a major device company after J&J. Abbott also announced plans to spin off its Nutrition business — home to Ensure, PediaSure, Glucerna, and Similac — into an independent public company by end of 2027.

◆ DHR Danaher Earnings Beat

Danaher delivered solid Q3 EPS upside at $2.11 versus the Street's $1.92 with core revenue growth of 3% at the high end of guidance and 110 basis points of adjusted operating margin expansion. Full-year EPS guidance was nudged higher to $8.57–$8.62 from the prior $8.45–$8.60, but the Q4 core revenue growth guide of 3% fell well short of the Street's 5.1% expectation.

▲ ELV Elevance Guidance Raise

Elevance posted a headline EPS beat of $6.99 versus the Street's $4.68, though $1.85 of that was a non-operating tailwind, leaving adjusted EPS of $5.14 — still a solid beat. The medical cost ratio came in at 90.6%, about 75 basis points better than consensus, and full-year EPS guidance was raised to at least $29 from at least $27, with the 2026 baseline lifted by 75 cents and 2027 EPS implied above $30 versus the Street at $29.60.

▲ TMO Thermo Fisher Guidance Raise

Thermo Fisher delivered strong Q3 upside with EPS 15% above the prior year at $6.68 versus the Street's $6.41 and organic revenue growth of 6% versus the expected 4.2%. Full-year EPS guidance was raised to $25.13–$25.43 from the prior $24.64–$25.12, a clean beat-and-raise quarter.

▼ MHO M/I Homes Earnings Miss

M/I Homes reported a large Q3 EPS miss at $2.56 versus the Street's $3.26 as higher mortgage rates weigh on demand and inflation pressures margins. Management explicitly cited increasingly difficult market conditions including weaker demand and growing economic uncertainty, painting a gloomy picture for the near-term housing outlook.

◆ WAB Wabtec Guidance Raise

Wabtec beat Q3 EPS at $2.83 versus the Street's $2.74, driven by strong margin expansion of 190 basis points to 22.9%. Full-year EPS guidance was raised to a midpoint of $10.95 from $10.75, though revenue fell slightly short at $3.12 billion versus $3.2 billion expected and the 12-month backlog declined sequentially.

◆ T AT&T Guidance Raise

AT&T beat Q3 EPS at 65 cents versus 60 cents expected and revenue of $32.3 billion versus $31.7 billion, achieving its best service revenue growth in over four years, while EBITDA of $12.5 billion also topped estimates. Full-year EPS guidance was raised to $2.40 from the prior $2.25–$2.35 and long-term capex was nudged lower to $22–$23 billion per year. However, SpaceX management reiterated plans to be a major wireless market competitor, keeping a significant competitive overhang on the stock.
