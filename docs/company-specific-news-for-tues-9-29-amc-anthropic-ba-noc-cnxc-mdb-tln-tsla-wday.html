Company-specific news for Tues 9/29 (AMC) - Anthropic, BA/NOC, CNXC, MDB, TLN, TSLA, WDAY

Sep 29, 2026 · 05:55 PM ET

Here's your after-the-bell recap for Tuesday, September 29th. This edition is all company-specific news — no broad macro commentary tonight, so let's get straight into the names.

Starting with defense. Boeing officially won the Navy's next-generation F/A-XX fighter jet contract after the close. Boeing was already the slight favorite here — they build the F-18 Super Hornet, which is the predecessor, and they won the F-47 last year. This is a major contract win. The loser is Northrop Grumman, which some thought had a real shot given Pentagon concerns about concentrating too much fighter jet supply with a single contractor. That thesis didn't hold — Northrop walks away empty-handed.

On the AI infrastructure front, Reuters published details from the Anthropic IPO prospectus tonight. The big takeaway — Anthropic routed nearly fifty percent of its 2025 revenue through Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud. The cloud giants collected sixteen cents of every dollar in Anthropic revenue sold on their platforms. This underscores just how dependent the leading AI labs are on big tech's cloud infrastructure, and it's a quiet positive for both Amazon and Alphabet.

Concentrix reported fiscal third quarter results. The earnings beat was solid — two ninety-two versus the Street's two seventy-one — with thirty basis points of year-over-year margin expansion and free cash flow of two eighteen versus estimates of one eighty-five. But the fiscal fourth quarter guidance missed on both earnings per share and revenue. The stock carries enormous existential AI risk to its core business model, but the valuation is already pricing in a lot of pain — we're talking roughly two times earnings and four times EBITDA, with free cash flow yields approaching forty to forty-five percent. It's a value trap debate, not a growth story.

MongoDB added one billion dollars to its buyback authorization, bringing the total to one point three five billion. Clean bullish signal from management on valuation.

Talen Energy made three moves at once — CEO succession with Terry Nutt taking over January first of next year, a one point five billion dollar accelerated share repurchase, and an upsized three billion dollar total repurchase program. That three billion figure is roughly twenty percent of the current market cap. That is an aggressive capital return statement.

Tesla filed an eight-K disclosing approximately thirty billion dollars in new loans and credit lines. The company is clearly preparing for a major capital spending cycle. That's a big number and it raises near-term free cash flow questions — worth watching how the market digests this tomorrow.

Finally, Workday announced a two point five percent workforce reduction while reiterating fiscal third quarter and full year twenty twenty-seven guidance. The cut is small in absolute terms, but it muddies the Silver Lake takeout speculation that's been circulating. Some will read this as management signaling they're not preparing for a sale. Others may just see it as routine cost discipline. Either way, the deal chatter gets more complicated tonight.

Bottom line for tomorrow — watch Boeing on the contract win, watch Tesla on the financing headline, and keep an eye on how Workday's restructuring news interacts with any M&A developments. No clear macro catalyst on the docket yet — it's a stock-picker's tape.
