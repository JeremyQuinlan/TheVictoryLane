RATES & FED

Treasury yields are flat to up 1 basis point as Brent's push through $100 keeps inflation fears alive and investors abandon hope for meaningful Treasury buybacks (the announced $6B ceiling disappointed those hoping for $10B+). Markets are pricing 37 basis points of hikes this year with 60% odds of a move at the September 16 FOMC — a decision that now hinges entirely on today's PPI and Friday's CPI (per Nick Timiraos, WSJ). The author favors a "one and done" September hike to restore Fed credibility without materially burdening growth.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The September 16 decision is the near-term fulcrum: a hike here is actually constructive for equities because it shores up central bank credibility and, paradoxically, should help compress long-end yields. Staying on hold just kicks the "will they or won't they" debate to October 28 and December 9 — that overhang is the last thing this market needs. Tech remains a bright spot with virtually every Citi and Goldman conference presentation bullish on AI/data center demand, and TSMC's August revenue up 53% year-over-year confirms the cycle is intact (Bloomberg). Fiscal recklessness — Trump's $5K "dividend" pledge, a debt ceiling fight looming by mid-2027 — keeps structural upward pressure on rates regardless of what the Fed does. Watch CPI Friday above all else.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran war durability: Trump suggested the conflict might end after the midterms, but senior advisors are now openly discussing it lasting through the end of his presidency (WSJ). Iran's government told Bloomberg it has "enough missiles for a lengthy conflict" — and crucially, Iran only needs one successful strike while the US requires a 100% interception rate. Pentagon munition stockpiles are materially depleted.

- Pickaxe Mountain: Construction activity at Iran's potential underground nuclear refuge surged this year; Trump warned Tehran "not to get cute" (CNN, Al Arabiya).

- Houthis/Saudi Arabia: The Houthis launched their biggest attack on Saudi Arabia in years, compromising Red Sea shipping. Pakistan delivered a stern Saudi warning to Tehran to rein them in (FT, Reuters).

- Russia-Ukraine: CIA Director Ratcliffe is taking a larger role in negotiations — a development welcomed in Europe, which hopes for a more forceful US posture toward Kyiv (FT). Separately, a drone nearly struck Zelensky's plane over Moldova (Washington Post).

- Taiwan: As the US dials back its Asia footprint and China expands kinetic capabilities, odds of a Taiwan reunification deal are rising (The Atlantic).

- AI governance: Paul Tudor Jones is calling on the US and China to jointly address AI's rapid advancement, warning of "a new superpower...unknowable and unending" (WSJ). A bipartisan AI safety bill could land in Congress as soon as next week (Semafor).
