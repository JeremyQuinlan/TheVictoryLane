Company-specific news for Wed 9/30 (BMO) - AAPL, CAG, CALM, CI, FDS, GIS, HPE, HRL, JBL, NCLH

Sep 30, 2026 · 07:55 AM ET

Good morning. Here is your briefing for Wednesday, September 30th. This morning's newsletter is a pure earnings and company news edition — there is no fresh macro commentary or index-level directional call from Adam today, so let's go straight to the names.

Starting with Apple. The company confirmed it will launch its new smart home device on October 13th. This is a long-anticipated product in a category Apple considers strategically critical. The launch date is now locked in — clean bullish catalyst.

Conagra delivered a strong fiscal first quarter earnings beat — 41 cents versus the Street at 29 cents — driven by adjusted operating margins of 11.5% against a 9.2% estimate. Sales were roughly inline. The problem: full-year guidance was left unchanged. Adam's read is that the beat doesn't carry forward, implying a softer second through fourth quarter outlook. Mixed signal.

Cal-Maine is the ugly one this morning. EPS came in at negative $1.26 versus the Street's negative 77 cents. Revenue crashed 41.5% to $539 million. Conventional egg sales collapsed nearly 60% year-over-year with a negative 35.2% operating margin. And because EPS was negative, the variable dividend gets suspended entirely. Clearly bearish.

Cigna reaffirmed 2026 guidance and laid out a 10 to 14% earnings per share compound annual growth rate target through 2030. The midpoint implies 2030 EPS of roughly $48, which beats the Street's $45.82 consensus. Modest positive.

FactSet beat fiscal fourth quarter EPS at $4.52 versus $4.37 and posted 7.1% organic revenue growth versus a 5.75% estimate. But fiscal 2027 guidance midpoints on both organic ASV growth and EPS came in slightly below consensus. Adam flags this guidance shortfall as the likely driver of downward pressure on the stock today.

HP Enterprise raised its fiscal 2027 Networking segment revenue growth guidance to high teens to low 20% year-over-year, up from 14 to 17%. Juniper synergy targets moved to $800 million from the prior floor of $600 million. Data Center Networking is targeting a low-to-high 50% compound annual growth rate through fiscal 2029. Clearly bullish.

Jabil is the most nuanced read this morning. The company blew past fiscal fourth quarter estimates — $4.40 EPS versus $4.07, revenue of $10.6 billion versus $9.7 billion — and forward guidance on both fiscal first quarter and full fiscal 2027 is well above the Street. The Intelligent Infrastructure AI segment grew 56% to $5.85 billion. But Adam's key point: the bar for AI-linked names is extremely high. A massive beat on paper may simply register as inline in the market's eyes. Watch the price action carefully.

Hormel announced a $1.055 billion acquisition of Brakebush Brothers, a value-added chicken company, expected to be earnings per share accretive starting in fiscal 2028. And Norwegian Cruise Line said third quarter results will beat prior guidance with the full 2026 outlook affirmed — the second positive cruise print in two days after Carnival reported Tuesday.

Bottom line for today: the tape is mixed. You have genuine strength in AI infrastructure names and cruise, an ugly agricultural read from Cal-Maine, and a nuanced set of beats where guidance or quality of the beat matters more than the headline number. AI names in particular trade on a high bar — do not chase the Jabil headline without watching how the market actually responds at the open.
