▲ DIS Disney Other

Disney's parks business is outperforming peers, many of whom are seeing traffic headwinds, thanks to price promotions and package deals. ▼ NVO Novo Nordisk Other

Novo Nordisk shares are slumping in European trading after the company failed to provide more granular financial targets at its Monday analyst meeting, disappointing investors who were looking for greater specificity on the path to future blockbuster launches. The lack of concrete guidance is being interpreted as a negative signal. Shares are underperforming in Europe on Monday.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article over the weekend focusing on poor price action in debt tied to a massive Oracle-linked data center in New Mexico known as Project Jupiter. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have reportedly hit a wall, raising concerns about off-balance sheet data center liability exposure. This adds to the broader negative AI infrastructure sentiment from the weekend.

◆ PARA Paramount Global M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California to resolve a lawsuit against its Warner Bros. Discovery merger deal. As part of the settlement, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central, though not all state attorneys general involved are on board. The outcome remains uncertain and the path to deal closure still faces regulatory friction.

Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +329 points/110 basis points, and the Russell 2000 +13.5 points/47 basis points, despite a weekend full of negative headlines. Asian and European markets are broadly higher, with the UN General Assembly kicking off this week as the primary driver of optimism around potential Middle East diplomatic progress. Brent crude is sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10, gold is down 60 basis points, and Treasuries have a bid with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve.

Fed expectations are holding fairly steady with the market pricing in roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, including roughly 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28 meeting, and approximately 35 basis points of additional increases in 2027. The BOJ's recent rate hike was more dovish than anticipated, but monetary policy globally remains hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks, warning that elevated inflation is broad-based and not just an oil story. Treasury plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing bill supply above 24% of marketable debt and above the Treasury's own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro headaches trace back to oil, meaning even a small diplomatic breakthrough at the UN General Assembly this week could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices. Lower oil would cascade into lower yields, eased inflation pressure, margin relief, and improved consumer confidence. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, signaling growing global alarm and potential pressure toward de-escalation. The Trump-Xi summit on September 24 could also yield modest tariff reductions, adding another incremental positive.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was nearly uniformly negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia, and a deluge of troubling AI headlines hit after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, weak Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. Inflation remains hot and broad-based with multiple central banks in hiking mode, and US-China trade truce extension talks have stalled. Any optimism around the UN General Assembly could easily prove to be wishful thinking, and the geopolitical landscape is deteriorating on multiple fronts simultaneously.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously mixed — the equity strength feels confounding given uniformly negative weekend news flow, and the rally is almost entirely driven by diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly. The key catalyst to watch is whether any tangible progress on Middle East tensions or Iran diplomacy emerges this week, as even a modest improvement in the geopolitical picture could trigger a meaningful drop in oil prices with powerful favorable knock-on effects across the entire macro landscape.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in European trading Monday morning. AI infrastructure sentiment broadly is under pressure after a wave of negative weekend headlines including OpenAI's projected negative free cash flow of roughly $280 billion from 2026 to 2030, a delayed Anthropic IPO, and Oracle data center debt concerns. Big Tech AI off-balance sheet liability exposure has risen approximately $300 billion in the past 12 months, flagged by the Financial Times as a growing risk. Chinese memory giant CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, which Acer's CEO says is helping ease component shortages and place a lid on memory prices — a headwind for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron pricing power. Energy and oil tanker costs are surging as the Iran war strains global supply, pressuring margins across transportation and consumer-facing sectors. Motor oil prices are surging in the US with retailers rationing supplies.
