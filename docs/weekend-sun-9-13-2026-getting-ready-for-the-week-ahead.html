No Friday close data provided in this newsletter — weekend edition focused on positioning for the week ahead. Watch for Monday pressure on Nasdaq vs. equal-weight outperformance given the AI pacing news.

---

RATES & FED

The recent spike in yields has been driven in part by AI compute spending mania and widening fiscal concerns — Trump's $5K dividend proposal (which Hassett confirmed the president is "very serious" about) rattled bond markets this week. The FOMC is now all but certain to hike on Wednesday 9/16, and as Nick Timiraos flagged in the WSJ Friday, the Fed rarely stops at one — so investors should begin mentally pricing a tightening cycle, not a one-and-done. The Aug fiscal deficit came in at -$168B, better than the -$211B consensus, but the YTD deficit is running ~$1.9T and Washington shows zero appetite for restraint.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The single biggest macro variable for equities right now is the Middle East — if Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, and the East-West Pipeline situation begins to resolve, the macro picture clears dramatically and fast. The AI frontier pacing story is the second major swing factor: a collective slowdown in model development would hit pick-and-shovel names hard (data center, power, semis), though hyperscalers might quietly welcome the capex relief and Treasuries would benefit from reduced AI-driven yield pressure. Monday sets up as equal-weight S&P outperforming Nasdaq with a long-end rally — that's the directional lean heading into a week stuffed with central bank decisions. The Fed hike Wednesday, if delivered cleanly and framed as credibility-preserving rather than cycle-starting, could actually be a modest positive for stocks. But the bear case — a proper tightening cycle layered on top of an energy shock — is not trivial.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Hormuz remains severely compromised and the Iran-Oman understanding does not provide for an immediate reopening of the waterway (Al Arabiya). Saudi Arabia has only 5–7 days of oil storage at Yanbu to sustain exports if the East-West Pipeline stays offline (Reuters). The Houthis have expanded territorial control in Yemen, tightening their grip on Bab el-Mandeb and Red Sea shipping lanes.

- Iran's chain of command is fractured — the commercial ship attacks that blew up the summer MOU were carried out without authorization from the IRGC head, infuriating moderates, while the Supreme Leader remains in hiding (NYT). Hardliners effectively sabotaged the peace deal.

- A critical GCC–Iran meeting is scheduled Monday in Oman — the first between these parties since the war began in February — aimed at managing Hormuz traffic. Outcome uncertain; Tehran still demands the US return to the MOU and the White House has been reluctant (FT).

- US intelligence links Chinese satellite imagery to Iran's July strike on a US base in Jordan that killed three troops (WSJ).

- BRICS countries pushed for Middle East peace at their annual summit in New Delhi (FT).

- Trump urged Zelenskyy to stop striking Russia's diesel refineries (Bloomberg). Separately, Trump said a US–Canada trade deal is coming "fairly soon" (Bloomberg), while Mexico and the US are sprinting toward a bilateral deal before mid-term elections (Reuters). Trump also said he'd be open to Chinese automakers building vehicles in the US (Reuters).

- ECB's Lagarde warned the current inflation shock will be longer-lasting than expected (Bloomberg).
