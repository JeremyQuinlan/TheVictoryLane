RATES & FED

The 2-year yield finished up roughly 6 basis points after an initial Treasury bid reversed post-Fed decision, while 10- and 30-year yields were essentially unchanged. The market is now pricing ~32 basis points of rate hikes across the final two 2026 meetings and ~40 basis points more in 2027 — notably more aggressive than the Fed's own dots, which implied one 25 basis point hike this year and none in 2027. Fed Chair Warsh's 2:30pm presser struck a hawkish tone that offset a net-dovish initial statement, sending equities lower into the close.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The Fed is telling you one thing, the market is pricing another, and that divergence doesn't resolve cleanly. Warsh's hawkish posture combined with structurally sticky inflation from two active war theaters makes the bull case increasingly dependent on geopolitical relief that isn't assured. August retail sales came in strong — +1.2% M/M vs. +0.8% expected, with the ex-fuel read still a healthy +1.1% — but transport companies are already warning of demand destruction risk as fuel costs metastasize through the supply chain. The secular upward pressure on yields (resilient growth, AI-linked issuance, fiscal imbalances) isn't going away, and the approaching US mid-terms — where Dems look on track to take the House at minimum — add a political overhang the market hasn't fully priced.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The US is burning through interceptor stockpiles at an unsustainable rate as Iran's missile attacks grow more sophisticated, while success rates remain well below 100% — new photos obtained by CBS News show widespread damage to US military positions across Kuwait and Saudi Arabia from Iranian strikes over recent months (WSJ, CBS News).

- AMZN's AWS cloud facilities in Bahrain and the UAE remain offline more than six months after Iranian drone strikes — a stark illustration of the conflict's real economic toll (CNBC). Americans have spent ~$107B more on fuel than they would have without the Iran conflict (WSJ).

- Half of Russia's top diesel-producing refineries have cut output following Ukrainian drone attacks, while China may reimpose refined fuel export restrictions amid plunging domestic inventories — both developments tighten global diesel supply further (Reuters, Bloomberg).

- Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline — knocked offline by Tehran-aligned militias — could be partially restored within days, per Bloomberg. Libya's oil output has returned to normal after a brief Tuesday outage. A US-Houthi ceasefire from 2025 remains nominally intact per Reuters, though the real concern is Houthi pressure on Saudi Arabia and Red Sea shipping, not the US-Houthi bilateral.

- The Trump/Xi summit is set for Thursday September 24 in Washington; the US and China are discussing tariff reductions on select goods ahead of the meeting, and Treasury Secretary Bessent will meet his Chinese counterpart this weekend (Bloomberg, Reuters). Separately, US-Mexico trade talks could resume as soon as next week, with potential relief on steel, aluminum, and auto tariffs — Washington is pressing Mexico to reduce Chinese content in tech/AI hardware (WSJ).

COMPANY NEWS

AAL management at the Morgan Stanley conference described the current revenue environment as the best outside post-9/11 and post-pandemic recoveries, with strength across corporate, leisure, domestic, and international — though fuel headwinds were acknowledged. LUV and UAL echoed the same demand-strong/fuel-costly message at the same conference; UAL called Q4 bookings "tremendously strong" with no visible demand destruction despite fare increases.

AXP at the Barclays conference reiterated 2026 guidance with QTD billings tracking +8% — slightly below Q2's +9% pace and a touch under the Street's +8.7% Q3 forecast — while management pushed back on macro fears, citing broad cardholder spending strength (Barclays conf.).

BA shares fell after management at the Morgan Stanley conference said 2026 free cash flow (guided $1–3B) is unlikely to reach the top of the range given slower-than-expected deliveries, and flagged "drags" on 2027 FCF growth.

CIEN held an analyst meeting projecting ~30% revenue CAGR from F26 to F29, implying ~$14.1B vs. Street $12.85B, with operating margins of 32–35% in F29 vs. Street's 30.2%.

COF at Barclays described consumer spending as healthy and resilient, and noted the Discover integration is on track — new Discover originations have fully migrated to Capital One's technology platform as of this month.

DOW guided Q3 EBITDA to $1.5–1.6B (midpoint $1.55B vs. consensus $1.58B) due to polyethylene price pressure and weakness in consumer, building/construction, and auto end markets (Morgan Stanley conf.).

GS noted FICC has been "a bit softer" while equities remain "very strong" (Barclays conf.).

HBAN cut its 2026 EPS target to $1.75–1.83 from $1.90–1.93, citing weaker NIM outlook from elevated deposit costs, lower loan yields, and reduced loan volume forecasts (Barclays conf.).

JBHT warned of Q3 earnings shortfalls driven by fuel cost headwinds; NSC's CFO described diesel prices as something out of "science fiction." Both companies plan to introduce surcharges but expressed concern about how much demand can absorb.

OSCR pre-announced upside for 2026, raising operating earnings guidance to ~$700MM from ~$600MM, driven by a 50 basis point improvement in the medical loss ratio.

DHI raised its F26 buyback target from $2.5B to $3.25B and added $5B to its total repurchase authorization (Tuesday night).

INTC / SKHY: Reports indicate SK Hynix is in talks to build memory chips at an Intel facility in Ohio, potentially in partnership with hyperscalers seeking greater memory access (Reuters).

WMT is partnering with SCAN Health Plan to launch a co-branded Medicare Advantage plan in two states, targeting more than two million Medicare enrollees.

OpenAI is in talks to raise a fresh funding round at a $1.2–1.5T valuation ahead of a planned 2027 IPO, per NYT. Separately, OpenAI's nascent ad business is struggling — growth is slower than hoped and ad effectiveness has been mixed (WSJ). Rival Anthropic will have its Claude Science model tested by Novo Nordisk for R&D workflows.
