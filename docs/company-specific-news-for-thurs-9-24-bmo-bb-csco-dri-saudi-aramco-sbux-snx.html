Company-specific news for Thurs 9/24 (BMO) - BB, CSCO, DRI, Saudi Aramco, SBUX, SNX

Sep 24, 2026 · 07:17 AM ET

Good morning. This is the company earnings note for Thursday, September 24th. No broad macro or Fed commentary in today's edition — it's all about the earnings tape, and the tape is mixed but leans bullish on the tech side.

Let's start with the big beats. BlackBerry — yes, BlackBerry — delivered a massive fiscal second quarter. Revenue came in at $163 million, up 26% year-over-year, smashing the Street at $146 million. EBITDA hit $47 million versus the consensus at $28 million. The QNX division is the driver here, and the company raised its full-year EBITDA guidance to a range of $141 to $158 million, up from $119 to $139 million previously. This is a real beat-and-raise and the stock should react well.

TD SYNNEX also had a monster quarter. Fiscal third quarter EPS came in at $5.68, up nearly 59% year-over-year, against a Street estimate of $4.70. Revenue surged 37.7% to $21.6 billion versus expectations of $18.95 billion. Operating margins expanded 39 basis points year-over-year. Gross margins did miss — down 61 basis points to 6.61% versus the 7.09% the Street expected — but the fourth quarter guidance is bullish, with EPS and revenue mid-points of $5.90 and $22.2 billion, both well above the Street at $4.87 and $19.5 billion.

Cisco didn't report, but its president gave a notable interview worth paying attention to. He said AI agents performing the same tasks as humans consume 450% more network bandwidth. That's a direct demand driver for Cisco's infrastructure. He also said 70% of Cisco's software products will be fully AI-written by end of next year, and the long-term goal is zero legacy code. This reinforces the AI infrastructure trade.

On the bear side, Darden Restaurants came in essentially in-line on the headline — aggregate comps of plus 3.1% and EPS of $2.05, both matching estimates — and full-year guidance was reaffirmed. But Olive Garden comps missed at plus 1.1% versus the Street's plus 1.6%, and LongHorn was also light at plus 6.2% versus plus 6.4%. Adam flags those two brand-level misses as likely to drive a negative stock reaction, even though the overall numbers were on target.

Starbucks announced it's closing an additional 1% of its North American portfolio and cutting global net new store openings to roughly 440, down sharply from the prior guidance of 600 to 650. That's a meaningful pullback in expansion and signals real caution on the consumer.

Saudi Aramco's CEO pushed back on Middle East supply disruption fears, saying the infrastructure is redundant and any interruption could be fixed within days. That's a modest positive for oil supply sentiment.

Bottom line for today: the AI infrastructure trade is getting fresh validation from multiple angles. The consumer picture is more challenged. Watch BlackBerry and TD SYNNEX on the open for follow-through. Darden and Starbucks are likely to face selling pressure. Stay focused.
