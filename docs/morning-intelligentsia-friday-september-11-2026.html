MARKET OUTLOOK

The geopolitical relief trade is real — the Oman meeting is a genuine catalyst — but don't mistake a one-day Brent pullback for structural resolution when Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb are both compromised. Yields aren't going sustainably lower with oil elevated, fiscal policy reckless (Trump's $5K dividend would balloon the deficit further), and the Fed still hiking. Druckenmiller's FT warning that we may be in an unsustainable AI-driven earnings bubble deserves serious attention, especially as ADBE's tepid guide and DeepSeek's HBM efficiency claims introduce fresh doubt on the AI capex supercycle. The FOMC decision Wednesday and BOJ Thursday are the week's pivotal macro events.

COMPANY NEWS

ORCL (Oracle) posted solid FQ1 upside across all major metrics and nudged full-year guidance higher, though the EPS/revenue bump wasn't dramatic. Separately, Oracle announced a new product that increases competitive pressure on PLTR (Palantir).

ADBE (Adobe) delivered a few cents of EPS upside in FQ3 ($6.13 vs. Street $6.08) with total sales +13% to $6.76B, ARR of $27.5B, and creative freemium MAUs crossing 100 million (+70% Y/Y). However, RPOs were light at $22.16B (+8% FXN) and the FQ4 sales guide of $6.80–6.85B missed the Street's $6.85B. The AI overhang remains unresolved.

RH posted FQ2 EBITDA of $178.5MM — well above consensus — though tariff refunds were a significant factor; ex-refunds, EBITDA was $123.5MM vs. the Street's $114MM. Sales rose 2.6% to $922.2MM. Critically, the company flagged ~$50MM in unplanned supply chain cost increases tied to the Middle East war and elevated oil prices. Q3 guide disappointed (+5.5% vs. Street +9.5%), but Q4 accelerates sharply (+16–21%).

MSFT (Microsoft) plans to triple its compute footprint to 38GW by 2032 from 12GW today to meet surging AI demand (Bloomberg).
