RATES & FED

Treasuries are under mild selling pressure, with yields flat to up 2 basis points. The market is pricing 23 basis points of rate hikes across the Fed's final three meetings of the year, with roughly 32% odds of a move on September 16. Cleveland Fed's Hammack pressed for immediate hikes despite July's modest inflation cooling, while Chicago's Goolsbee conceded the data has been "a little better" (Reuters).

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

AI remains the dominant market force by a wide margin, and nothing out of AMAT, CBRS, COHR, or CSCO this week changes that — though the sell-the-news reactions confirm the bar for tech is extraordinarily high right now. The trio of catalysts on the calendar starting August 26 — July PCE, Nvidia earnings, and Warsh's Jackson Hole speech — is what truly matters. Iran is increasingly a "who cares" issue for equities so long as enough oil keeps flowing, but bears are right that Trump has no clean exit and depleted inventories leave little cushion for escalation. Rates remain structurally biased higher given AI-driven debt issuance, sticky inflation, tariffs, and fiscal imbalances — Fitch's AA+ affirmation with a "Stable" outlook shouldn't inspire much comfort given debt running more than double the AA median.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The Pentagon says it can sustain an indefinite naval blockade of Iran; Treasury Secretary Bessent pledged "economic isolation like the world has never seen before" alongside the Hormuz blockade (Reuters, CNBC). The USS George Washington is being dispatched to relieve the Abraham Lincoln, which has been on an extended deployment (NYT).

- The White House announced drone tariffs: 100% on large drones, 25% on smaller ones, framed as a national security and supply chain measure (White House).

- China's auto export volumes are overwhelming global shipping capacity — there simply aren't enough vessels to handle the pace (WSJ).

- Japan's BOJ is highly likely to hike rates in September and may accelerate tightening thereafter as inflation and yen weakness intensify (Reuters).

- Fitch affirmed the US credit rating at AA+ with a Stable outlook but flagged debt at more than double the AA median and persistent fiscal deficits averaging 7% of GDP since 2022.

COMPANY NEWS

AMAT (Applied Materials) delivered FQ3 EPS of $3.50 (+41% Y/Y, vs. Street $3.42) on revenue of $9.115B (+25% Y/Y, vs. Street $9.02B). Gross margins came in at 50.4% (+150 basis points vs. Street 50.1%) and operating margins hit 34% (+330 basis points vs. Street 33.6%). FQ4 guidance of $4.02 EPS/$10.25B revenue blew past the Street's $3.72/$9.61B. Management flagged accelerating DRAM and leading-edge foundry demand and said 2027 is shaping up as another strong growth year. Stock is seeing a sell-the-news response in pre-market.

GLOB (Globant) missed Q2 EPS at $1.40 vs. Street $1.50, with operating margins down 180 basis points Y/Y and sales roughly inline. Full-year guidance was cut to EPS of $5.75–$6.15 from $6.10–$6.50.

KLC (KinderCare) cut full-year guidance to EBITDA of $200–$220MM (prior $215–$235MM) and EPS of $0.05–$0.15 (prior $0.15–$0.25).

TSN (Tyson) is further shrinking its beef processing footprint as a significant cattle shortage keeps industry fundamentals bleak (WSJ).

WDAY (Workday) — Silver Lake is in active talks to acquire Workday in what would rank among the largest software buyouts in history (Reuters).

BABA (Alibaba) is nearing a $1.5B+ sale of its video game unit to Trustar Capital (Bloomberg) and is separately partnering with Apple to train AI models for the Chinese market (Reuters).

CHTR (Charter) received its final state approval for the $21.9B Cox deal (WSJ).

GS (Goldman Sachs) has begun investor outreach for Nvidia's $500B+ AI chip financing initiative after landing the lead role (Reuters).

TROW (T. Rowe Price) warned it will take several more years to stem asset outflows and is considering M&A to accelerate the turnaround (FT).

C (Citigroup) is acquiring credit card rewards startup Kard Financial (Barron's).

UBER gets a positive mention in Barron's — autonomous driving fears have pushed valuation to a compelling level (Barron's).

RDDT (Reddit) will join the S&P 500, replacing AVB, before the open on August 18.

INTC (Intel) is exploring entry into the memory business as CEO Lip-Bu Tan hints at new architecture initiatives (Barron's).

OpenAI's Chief Revenue Officer Denise Dresser is departing after less than a year (WSJ). Anthropic and OpenAI are in a price war on frontier models, driven by Chinese open-source competition (FT).
