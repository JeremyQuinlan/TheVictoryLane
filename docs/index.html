VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTH Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO has been pushed to November from October, with year-end ARR projections of greater than 100 billion dollars per the NYT and greater than 110 billion dollars per the WSJ — below some bull expectations of 120 billion dollars or more. The company is considering launching a powerful new model before the IPO to recapture momentum lost to OpenAI's Astra, and plans to have 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027. The delayed IPO reflects volatile AI stock trading, the safety and alignment debate, questions about Meta-driven ARR inflation, and OpenAI's recent sharp growth inflection.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focused on poor price action in debt tied to its massive data center facility in New Mexico known as Project Jupiter, with efforts to offload loans tied to the facility having 'hit a wall.' This raises fresh concerns about Oracle's off-balance sheet data center financing exposure and the risk of a broader repricing of hyperscaler infrastructure debt.

▲ PSKY Paramount Global M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a state-led lawsuit against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. Under the potential settlement, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central. Resolution would clear a meaningful regulatory obstacle for the deal to close.

▲ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Warner Bros. Discovery's pending merger with Paramount moved closer to resolution as Paramount enters advanced settlement talks with California's AG. A deal could require divestiture of cable assets like Comedy Central and commitments around CNN editorial independence. Clearing this regulatory hurdle would be a positive step toward deal closure.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. This raises concerns about franchisee financial health across the system and potential near-term headwinds to royalty revenue and unit economics.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk Activist Entry

Novo Nordisk is facing growing shareholder pressure to conduct M&A to diversify away from its concentrated GLP-1 exposure. This signals investor concern about the company's dependence on a single drug class and could accelerate deal activity, though M&A execution risk is real at Novo's scale.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA to use its Starliner spacecraft on ten or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, representing a potential commercial lifeline for the troubled program. A contract win here would be incrementally positive for Boeing's space business after Starliner's high-profile setbacks.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture announced it will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment. This is a modest but visible win for Accenture's AI professional services positioning, aligning it with the highest-profile AI lab ahead of Anthropic's IPO.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores nationwide as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. This reflects broader retailer pressure on consumer staples pricing and is a negative signal for Red Bull's distribution reach in the US market.

▼ VOWG Volkswagen Guidance Cut

Volkswagen issued a large downside preannouncement Friday, cutting its 2026 operating margin guidance to up to 1% from prior guidance of 4 to 5.5% and versus the Street's 4.1% estimate. The miss is driven by a goodwill impairment charge at Porsche, a challenging auto market especially in China, and additional restructuring expenses — though adjusted for special items, margins would be roughly 4% and essentially in-line.

Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. Houthis dramatically ratcheted up missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities, raising fears of further energy supply disruption. The geopolitical backdrop is broadly risk-off heading into the week, with the Iran conflict continuing to mutate and the Pentagon stretched thin across multiple fronts.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction — the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiked this month, and markets are placing greater than 50% odds on additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over the coming weeks. The BOJ's latest hike was slightly more dovish than expected, which paradoxically pressured the yen and rekindled fears about Japan potentially selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid made hawkish comments Friday, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small improvement in Middle East geopolitics could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices, with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday could produce select tariff reductions on roughly 30 billion dollars worth of goods, providing a tangible trade-war relief catalyst. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing international pressure for de-escalation. Saudi Arabia is also increasing ship-to-ship oil transfers to help compensate for supply shortages, offering some near-term cushion.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape is deteriorating rapidly, with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via the Strait of Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and an increasingly aggressive Houthi campaign against Saudi Arabia. Energy prices spiking through the system will eventually trigger demand destruction and pressure corporate earnings, which have so far held up well through the summer. The AI sector faces a cluster of fresh headwinds including a delayed Anthropic IPO, disappointing ARR projections relative to bull-case expectations, OpenAI's massive projected negative free cash flow of roughly 280 billion dollars through 2030, and rising off-balance sheet AI liability exposure for Big Tech firms up roughly 300 billion dollars in just the last year. Global monetary policy is still tightening with multiple central banks expected to hike in the weeks ahead, leaving little room for a valuation-driven rally.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bearish into the week open given the net negative weekend news flow — escalating Middle East conflict, AI sector headwinds, and a hawkish global rate backdrop. The key catalyst to watch is the Trump/Xi summit on Thursday September 24th, which could deliver modest tariff relief and a positive risk surprise, while the UN General Assembly running Monday through Sunday offers a long-shot opportunity for Middle East diplomatic progress.
