VK · Earnings Whispers
▼ ANTHROPIC Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO was pushed to November from October, and its anticipated year-end ARR of over $100-$110 billion disappointed bulls who were hoping for $120 billion or more. The company is considering releasing a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture momentum from OpenAI's Astra, and plans to have 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027. Safety and alignment concerns, Meta's potential shift away from Anthropic tools, and OpenAI's renewed growth momentum are all headwinds into the listing.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focused on troubled debt tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center in New Mexico. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have 'hit a wall,' raising concerns about Oracle's data center financing strategy. This adds to the broader theme of Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liability risk.

◆ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California to resolve a state attorney general lawsuit against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. A potential settlement could require Paramount to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central. Progress here could clear a meaningful regulatory hurdle for the deal to close.

◆ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Warner Bros. Discovery's pending merger with Paramount faces the same California attorney general lawsuit, and Paramount's advanced settlement talks could resolve a key obstacle to deal completion. The potential divestitures and editorial independence commitments are the price of clearing the regulatory path. Outcome remains uncertain but talks are described as advanced.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's came under pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Franchisee financial stress signals potential unit closures, royalty revenue risk, and broader consumer spending pressure at the QSR level. This is a meaningful negative read for Wendy's near-term fundamentals.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. The move highlights ongoing retailer-supplier tension over pricing power and signals Kroger is willing to use shelf space as leverage. Broadly negative for Red Bull and potentially a template for further supplier pressure across consumer staples.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk Activist Entry

Novo Nordisk is facing shareholder pressure to diversify away from its heavy GLP-1 concentration through acquisitions. The activist pressure reflects concern about over-reliance on a single drug class and the need for a broader pipeline. This could lead to M&A activity but also signals investor unease about the current business mix.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on ten or more new flights into low-Earth orbit. This would be a meaningful commercial recovery for Starliner after its troubled crewed mission saga, and represents a direct competitive response to SpaceX's dominance in the segment. Still early-stage discussions but directionally positive for Boeing's space business.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture announced it will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor safety and alignment, a move that positions the firm at the center of the rapidly growing AI governance and oversight market. This is a relatively small but strategically notable partnership that extends Accenture's AI services footprint. The deal also validates the growing enterprise demand for third-party AI oversight.

▼ VW Volkswagen Guidance Cut

Volkswagen issued a large downside preannouncement for 2026, now guiding operating margins to 'up to 1%' versus prior guidance of 4 to 5.5% and Street estimates of roughly 4.1%. The miss was driven by a goodwill impairment at Porsche, a challenging auto market especially in China, and additional restructuring charges. Adjusted for special items margins would be roughly 4%, but the headline guidance cut is a significant negative signal for the European auto sector.

Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a cluster of disappointing AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically ramped up missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities, raising fears of further energy supply disruption. Ukraine also launched a massive drone strike on Russia including Moscow, adding to geopolitical risk heading into the week.

Global monetary policy remains in a hawkish posture, with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all having hiked this month. The BOJ's latest move was slightly more dovish than expected, pressuring the yen and stoking fears that Japan could sell Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that inflation is running too hot even excluding oil, reinforcing the hawkish Fed narrative.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

A geopolitical improvement, even a small one, could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with powerful knock-on effects — lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer sentiment. The Trump/Xi meeting on Thursday could deliver select tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion in goods, a modest but real positive. China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting international alarm is building toward a potential resolution. The UN General Assembly brings key Middle East leaders together, creating at least a forum for incremental diplomacy.

BEAR CASE

Iran continues to tighten its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, Iraqi militias, and the Houthis, who just escalated dramatically against Saudi Arabia. The energy price spike is already working its way through the system and demand destruction looks increasingly hard to avoid. AI sentiment took multiple hits over the weekend — Anthropic's IPO slipped to November, ARR projections disappointed the highest bulls, OpenAI faces $280 billion in negative free cash flow through 2030, and Big Tech's AI off-balance sheet liabilities surged roughly $300 billion in the past year. The combination of hawkish global central banks and worsening geopolitics leaves the macro backdrop fragile.

Market Outlook

Adam leans cautious/bearish heading into the week given the net negative weekend newsflow — escalating Middle East conflict, disappointing Anthropic metrics, and rising AI off-balance sheet liabilities. The key catalysts to watch are the Trump/Xi summit Thursday and the UN General Assembly, which carry modest potential for geopolitical relief but face long odds of a breakthrough.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face mounting headwinds from rising off-balance sheet liabilities (up roughly $300 billion in 12 months), IPO timing slippage, and disappointing growth projections. Energy sector is elevated on Middle East supply risk, with diesel and motor oil prices surging and Iranian proxies tightening the grip on global supply routes. European autos are pressured by Volkswagen's dramatic margin guidance cut and continued China market weakness. Consumer and QSR names face franchisee stress and retailer pushback on supplier pricing. Memory/PC components are seeing some relief as Chinese supply from firms like CXMT eases shortages.
