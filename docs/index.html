Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +329 points/110 basis points, and Russell 2000 +13.5 points/47 basis points despite a weekend full of net negative headlines. Asian and European markets are broadly higher, with data center pick-and-shovel stocks outperforming in Europe. The primary driver behind the strength appears to be optimism surrounding this week's UN General Assembly in New York, where diplomatic progress on the Iran conflict and Middle East tensions is being hoped for. Brent crude is sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10, gold is down 60 basis points, and bitcoin surges 350 basis points to roughly $83,900.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. Fed expectations remain fairly hawkish — the market is pricing in roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes over the year's final two meetings, with about 50/50 odds of an increase at the October 28th meeting, plus roughly 35 basis points of additional increases in 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari echoed those hawkish sentiments Sunday morning. Meanwhile, Treasury plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing bills above 24% of marketable debt and well above the Treasury's own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is the key bull catalyst — if even modest progress is made on Middle East tensions, oil could drop sharply, unleashing lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. Trump called off planned air strikes against the Houthis at the last minute Sunday night and signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, suggesting a window for de-escalation exists. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, indicating broader international pressure building toward resolution. Saudi Arabia is also moving to increase ship-to-ship oil transfers to compensate for supply shortages, providing some near-term energy market relief.

BEAR CASE

Nearly all the weekend news flow was net negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia including Moscow, and a series of troubling AI headlines emerged after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt issues. The geopolitical backdrop remains extremely fragile, with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis, while Russia's refining capacity continues to be taken offline. On the trade front, the US-China truce extension was not reached on Sunday, and Washington is expected to hike aggregate tariff rates on Chinese imports back to pre-SCOTUS IEEPA levels in the coming weeks, creating a fresh macro headwind.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for today's session, driven almost entirely by diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly and hopes for a de-escalation in the Middle East that could bring oil prices sustainably lower. The key catalyst to watch is any concrete signal of progress on Iran diplomacy — Trump has indicated openness to meeting Iran's president at the General Assembly, and even a modest improvement in tensions could trigger an outsized oil selloff with significant positive knock-on macro effects. However, the underlying news flow remains net negative, so this rally could fade quickly if diplomacy disappoints.

Sector Watch

AI and data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe Monday morning despite a flood of negative weekend AI headlines including delayed Anthropic IPO, weak Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn of roughly $280 billion projected through 2030, and Oracle data center debt concerns. Big Tech AI off-balance sheet liability exposure has surged roughly $300 billion in just the last 12 months, creating a fresh risk overhang for the sector. Energy is sliding as Brent crude falls 170 basis points to around $102.10, with oil tanker costs surging and motor oil prices spiking due to Iran war-driven supply shortages. Banks in Europe are outperforming, partly driven by SocGen's bullish analyst day targets including ROTE of 13-14% in 2029 and above 15% in 2030 and beyond.
