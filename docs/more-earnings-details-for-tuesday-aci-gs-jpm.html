More earnings details for Tuesday: ACI, GS, JPM

Oct 13, 2026 · 07:40 AM ET

Here's what you need to know heading into Tuesday's session. This note is purely an earnings update — no macro tape commentary, no Fed speak, just three important reports that dropped this morning and here's what matters. Starting with the biggest one: Goldman Sachs absolutely crushed it. EPS came in at seventeen dollars and one cent versus the Street's twelve eighty-two — that's a massive beat. Revenue surged twenty-three percent year-over-year to eighteen point six seven billion, well ahead of the sixteen point seven six billion consensus. The headline grabber is Equities Trading, which exploded seventy-two percent to six point four four billion versus the Street's five point one billion. That was driven by prime financing, portfolio financing, and derivatives — essentially the prime brokerage and structured products engine firing on all cylinders. Investment Banking fees were also strong, up twenty-one percent to three point two two billion. The one soft spot was FICC trading, which came in roughly flat year-over-year at three point four seven billion, a touch below the Street's three point seven two billion estimate. The efficiency ratio of sixty point five percent was several hundred basis points better than consensus, with expenses growing three hundred basis points less than revenue — that's operating leverage. This is a blowout print and should reprice Goldman and likely the broader capital markets complex higher at the open. Next, JPMorgan raised its 2026 net interest income guidance to one hundred and six point two billion from one hundred and five point five billion. Expenses also ticked up to one hundred and eight point six billion from one hundred and seven point five billion — so it's not a clean raise, but the key number is the preliminary 2027 net interest income ex-markets outlook of one hundred and seven billion, which is ahead of the Street's one hundred and four point five billion. That's a durable earnings signal for the bank and a positive read-through for the sector. Finally, Albertsons posted a modest beat — EPS of thirty-six cents versus thirty-three cents expected, EBITDA of seven eighty-four versus seven seventy-one, and comps of negative zero point eight percent, better than the feared negative one percent. But the company pulled its full fiscal twenty-twenty-six outlook entirely and won't provide updated guidance until a comprehensive business review wraps up in Q4. That uncertainty is the story there and will likely weigh on the stock. Directionally, the session leans bullish on the back of Goldman's outsized beat and JPMorgan's constructive forward guidance. Watch financials closely at the open.
