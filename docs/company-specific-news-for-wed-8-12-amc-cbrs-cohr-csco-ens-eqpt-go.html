MARKET OUTLOOK

The AI infrastructure buildout continues to drive broad-based earnings upside across networking, photonics, and equipment — but the bar is high and any misses at the margin get punished. The competitive dynamics in AI chips are intensifying fast, with Nvidia, Google, and Amazon increasingly weaponizing financial scale (think $500B+ packages) alongside technical superiority to lock up deals. That's a structural headwind for smaller players trying to compete on merit alone. Watch for broadening demand signals beyond hyperscalers — Cisco's print suggests the networking supercycle is real and spreading across geographies and customer types, which is constructive for the broader market.

COMPANY NEWS

CBRS (Cerebras) posted Q2 core revenue of $209.9MM (+103% Y/Y) against the Street's $194MM, with gross margins of 41% well ahead of the consensus 36.7%, and operating margins of -16% beating the expected -30.8%. Full-year guidance was lifted to $885MM from $860MM. The problem: most of this was already in the price, and the RPO of $25.4B barely budged from Q1's $25B. More concerning longer-term is the competitive moat question — when Nvidia is assembling $500B+ customer packages, it's hard for CBRS to compete on financial firepower alone.

COHR (Coherent) delivered FQ4 EPS of $1.74 versus the Street's $1.62, on sales of $2.046B (+33.8% Y/Y) against expectations of $1.99B. Gross and operating margins expanded 215 basis points and 381 basis points Y/Y, respectively. FQ1 guidance came in well ahead — EPS of $1.95 versus $1.79 expected, sales of $2.3B versus $2.15B. Management called out the industry's structural shift from copper to optical connectivity in AI datacenters as a multi-year tailwind for Coherent's photonic portfolio. The one blemish: the industrial segment fell short of expectations.

CSCO (Cisco) turned in an impressive FQ4, with EPS of $1.22 beating the Street's $1.17, on sales of $17.3B (+18% Y/Y) and operating margins of 35.9%. Full-year F27 guidance was notably bullish — EPS of $5.08 versus consensus $4.84, and sales of $72.8B versus $69.1B. Hyperscaler orders hit $4B in the quarter, and total product orders surged 35% (25% ex-hyperscalers), with double-digit growth across every geography and customer market. Management is calling it a "networking supercycle," and it's hard to argue with that framing given the breadth of demand.

ENS (EnerSys) posted a large FQ1 EPS beat at $3.66 versus the Street's $2.86, but the quality of the beat matters — it was driven entirely by Advanced Manufacturing Production Credits and tariff refunds rather than operational strength. Sales of $936MM were only marginally ahead of the $927.8MM consensus, and the FQ2 sales guidance midpoint of $975MM was merely in line.

EQPT (EquipmentShare) reported strong Q2 numbers with sales of $1.44B crushing the Street's $1.142B estimate, and EBITDA of $531MM well above the $374MM consensus. Crucially, the core rental segment — the higher-quality revenue stream — drove much of the beat, with rental revenue jumping 39% to $908MM versus the expected $822.4MM. Full-year EBITDA guidance was raised to $1.946–2.058B from $1.883–1.995B. Management cited consolidation of customer spend and an expanding mega-project pipeline as key drivers.

GO (Grocery Outlet) beat on EBITDA at $65.7MM versus the Street's $56.7MM, with comps declining only 0.3% against expectations of -1.45%. Transaction count rose 1.8% even as average ticket fell 2.1%, suggesting traffic is holding up well. Gross and EBITDA margins both came in ahead of plan. Full-year EPS guidance was tightened and lifted to 51–55 cents from 45–55 cents, with comps now expected flat-to-down 0.5% versus the prior flat-to-down 2%.
