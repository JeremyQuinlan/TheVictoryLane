RATES & FED

Treasuries are largely steady across the curve. Fed hike expectations edged dovish overnight — 37 basis points priced for the year (down from 40) with ~61% odds of a move at the September 16 meeting. MARKET OUTLOOK

Tech earnings Wednesday night were broadly positive but mixed against elevated expectations, and futures are holding their gains. The real macro thread to watch is the JPY — a sustained yen rally would theoretically relieve upward pressure on global yields, which could be a meaningful tailwind for equities. Friday's jobs report (+55K consensus vs. -23K prior) is the next big test. The Iran situation remains the wild card: oil flows through Hormuz are recovering but refined products — especially diesel — remain dangerously tight. Markets are too complacent about the post-midterm escalation risk flagged by Reuters.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Roughly 17M barrels left the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, rising to 18M Tuesday — not far from pre-war levels of ~20M — as the Pentagon systematically degrades Iran's ability to control the waterway (CNN). Syria is playing an increasingly important role routing Iraqi oil around Hormuz via pipeline (Washington Post).

- Trump has discussed with aides declaring the Iran war over and has indicated he favors the idea, though the Pentagon is simultaneously extending Middle East troop deployments into 2027 (WSJ).

- Trump aides fearing a midterm rout are pushing to keep the Iran conflict quiet for now — with the implication that kinetic activity could escalate sharply after the election (Reuters).

- The BOJ is said to favor a 25 basis point hike at its September 18 meeting and could accelerate tightening thereafter, driving a surge in the yen and a historic reversal of capital outflows from Japan back home (Bloomberg, Reuters).

- The Dutch central bank moved 78 tons of gold from New York to London citing "increasing geopolitical unrest" (FT).

- Switzerland's August CPI came in hot at +0.8% year-over-year versus the +0.5% consensus, fastest in two years (Bloomberg).

- China's Caixin services PMI for August beat at 51.4 versus 50.6 expected (Bloomberg).

COMPANY NEWS

AVGO (Broadcom) posted FQ3 EPS of $3.32 versus the Street's $3.23, with total revenue up 86% to $29.6B (AI accelerators +221% to $16.7B). The FQ4 guide was slightly light at $34.8B versus $35.05B expected. The bigger story is F2028 — management's outlook was genuinely bullish. Anthropic could become AVGO's largest XPU customer with OpenAI at #2 and Google still a significant buyer. Notably, SpaceX/xAI is the only major hyperscaler without a custom chip, having gone exclusive with Nvidia — which raises serious questions about who is financing SpaceX's 10GW+ compute ambition and how much of that underpinned Nvidia's recent 70% sales guide.

AGX (Argan) delivered blowout FQ2 results: EBITDA of $70M versus $49M expected, revenue of $384M versus $300M, with EBITDA margins up 200 basis points year-over-year. Backlog slipped to $2.51B from $2.77B the prior quarter.

FIVE (Five Below) crushed FQ2: EPS $1.68 versus $1.39 expected, comps +14.1% versus +10.6% — impressive given store count grew 8.8% to 2,022 locations. Full-year comp guide raised to +10-12% from +6-8%.

SNOW (Snowflake) beat on FQ2 sales (+35% to $1.55B versus $1.485B expected), net revenue retention of 126%, and operating margins of 15.3% versus 12.6% expected. Full-year product revenue growth guide raised to 36% from 31%.

NTAP (NetApp) posted strong FQ1 upside: EPS $2.58 versus $2.12, revenue $2.025B versus $1.832B. Full-year guidance moves higher.

COST (Costco) reported August US core comps (ex-gas and FX) of +5.6%, a modest miss versus the +6.0% Street estimate and down from +6.9% in July.

VRNS (Varonis) shares spiked Wednesday afternoon on a report that Thoma Bravo-backed Proofpoint is in acquisition talks (WSJ).

MSFT (Microsoft) will restructure financial reporting starting F2027, adding greater transparency into Azure and M365 Cloud — the two most important businesses.

PVH reported FQ2 EPS of $3.70 versus $3.08 expected, though $1.80 of the beat came from tariff refunds. Sales fell 3% on an FX-neutral basis, with strength at Tommy offset by Calvin weakness. Full-year guidance only reaffirmed.

F (Ford) is targeting 100,000 first-year sales for the Ford Fathom, a new ~$30K EV pickup — an ambitious goal by any measure (WSJ).

TSLA (Tesla) Cybercab launch event Thursday will generate plenty of coverage, but the broader Robotaxi initiative remains very early-stage.
