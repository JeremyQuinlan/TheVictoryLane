RATES & FED

Treasuries were essentially flat across the curve. Markets are pricing roughly 30 basis points of Fed hikes by year-end, with ~55% odds of a move at the September 16th meeting. Fed's Schmid cautioned that the inflation problem extends well beyond energy and that tighter policy will be needed to hit 2% (WSJ).

MARKET OUTLOOK

Wednesday's price action told you everything — a strong open, a fade to unchanged, and an equal-weight index that was red most of the day. The bulls have a credible story on travel, healthcare, and pockets of infrastructure tech, but the AI capex/ROI debate is back with a vengeance after SpaceX posted a capex-to-revenue ratio of 6.3x in Q2 and Bloomberg revealed ~70% of Microsoft's AI revenue flows from OpenAI. Hyperscaler off-balance-sheet commitments now total $2.7 trillion (Barron's) — a number that will only grow. Until the market gets a cleaner answer on AI monetization beyond the hyperscaler echo chamber, tech leadership will remain fragile. Friday's jobs report is the next major catalyst.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran and Oman reached a framework to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but the terms expand Tehran's control — all inbound traffic would hug the Iranian coast, with tolls likely after a 60-day grace period. Substantive US-Iran talks remain absent, and nuclear program differences loom as the next hurdle (Reuters).

- The Houthis claimed a strike on a Saudi tanker in the Red Sea and threatened to escalate (multiple wires). The IDF launched strikes in Southern Lebanon after Hezbollah violated the ceasefire. The overall conflict is settling into a pattern of manageable-but-persistent risk — think tariffs or Ukraine — rather than a fatal market overhang, but that calculus can shift fast.

- Washington has issued roughly $100 billion in IEEPA tariff refunds, approximately 60% of total collections to date (FT). Shark Ninja noted a 15-cent EPS tailwind from tariff refunds in its guidance update.

COMPANY NEWS

LLY (Eli Lilly) delivered a blowout Q2: EPS of $8.38 vs. the Street's $6.00, revenue of $23B vs. $20.6B expected. Mounjaro came in ~$1B above estimates; Zepbound, Trulicity, and Jardiance all beat. Full-year guidance raised.

DIS (Disney) posted FQ3 EPS of $2.06 vs. $1.86 expected, op. income of $5.55B vs. $5.23B. Entertainment and Experiences drove the beat; Sports disappointed. FY26/27 guidance reiterated, though management flagged Moana underperformance and a softer domestic SVOD ad environment heading into Q4.

SHOP (Shopify) impressed across the board: GMV of $115.5B vs. $112.1B, free cash flow of $654MM vs. $550MM. Q3 revenue guide of +31-33% crushed the Street's +26.7%.

SPCX (SpaceX) reported Q2 sales of $7.8B (+92% Y/Y) vs. $6.8B expected and adjusted EBITDA of $3.5B vs. $2B, but free cash flow was deeply negative on a capex/revenue ratio of 6.3x. The conf. call confirmed exclusive use of NVDA (Nvidia) chips and a compute ramp from ~1.4GW to ~10GW by end-2027 — sending Nvidia shares higher. SpaceX also flagged direct consumer wireless ambitions, hammering tower stocks AMT, CCI, SBAC and carriers T, TMUS, VZ.

AMD (Advanced Micro Devices) Q2 was essentially inline; Q3 revenue guide of $13B topped the $12.5B estimate but gross margins of 56% missed by ~25 basis points. Stock fell.

ANET (Arista Networks) Q2 EPS of $1.02 vs. 89 cents, sales +37.7% Y/Y to $3.03B vs. $2.82B. Q3 guide also ahead at $1.07/$3.3B vs. $0.92/$2.95B.

CVS (CVS Health) beat significantly: Q2 EPS $2.85 vs. $1.85, revenue $106B vs. $100B. Medical cost ratio of 87.4% was ~200 basis points better than expected. Full-year EPS raised to ~$8 from ~$7.40.

GOOGL (Alphabet) shares fell after a sweeping AI leadership shakeup at DeepMind reinforced fears the company is falling behind OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta (Reuters).

MSFT (Microsoft) drew fresh scrutiny after Bloomberg reported ~70% of its AI revenue derives from OpenAI, raising questions about the durability and breadth of its AI franchise.

BKNG (Booking Holdings) delivered across all KPIs: bookings $51B vs. $49.7B, EPS $2.54 vs. $2.43. Management said Q3 demand remains resilient.

BLMN (Bloomin' Brands) Q2 EPS 39 cents vs. 29 cents expected. Full-year EPS guide raised to $0.90-$1.00 from $0.75-$0.90.

BWA (BorgWarner) Q2 EPS $1.42 vs. $1.28. Buyback authorization boosted by $1B; full-year EPS guide lifted on higher buybacks.

CPRI (Capri Holdings) FQ1 EPS 67 cents vs. 40 cents; sales $769MM vs. $752MM; op. margins 3.6% vs. 1.4% expected. Full-year sales trimmed to $3.4B on Michael Kors weakness; EPS outlook maintained at $2.15 via cost cuts.

UBER Q2 EBITDA and revenue were essentially inline; Delivery and Freight bookings modestly outperformed but free cash flow of $2.8B missed the $3.2B estimate.

ETD (Ethan Allen) faces activist pressure from DGB Investments, which has built a stake and is pushing for full board replacement (Bloomberg).

SHAK (Shake Shack) is in Starboard Value's crosshairs; the activist wants significantly more US franchising (Bloomberg).

BMY/AZN: Reuters confirmed there are "no discussions" between Bristol-Myers Squibb and AstraZeneca regarding a deal.
