▼ ORCL Oracle Other

The Financial Times published a negative article about Oracle focused on poor price action in debt tied to a massive Oracle-linked data center in New Mexico known as Project Jupiter. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have reportedly hit a wall, raising concerns about Oracle's massive AI infrastructure bet and its associated financing risk.

▲ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California to resolve a lawsuit led by state AGs against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. Under the potential settlement, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central, clearing a key regulatory hurdle for the deal to close.

◆ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

The Paramount-Warner deal faces a potential California AG settlement that could require Paramount to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest cable assets including Comedy Central. Progress on this regulatory front would represent a meaningful step toward deal closure.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure on Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. This raises concerns about franchisee financial health and potential near-term headwinds for Wendy's unit economics and royalty streams.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on 10 or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, a potential meaningful contract win following the vehicle's troubled history. This would represent a significant rehabilitation of the Starliner program and renewed revenue opportunity for Boeing's space division.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk M&A

Novo Nordisk is facing shareholder pressure to diversify away from its heavy GLP-1 exposure through mergers and acquisitions. While this reflects concern about concentration risk, it could also signal potential for value-accretive deal activity ahead.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators to monitor safety and alignment at Anthropic, marking a notable new partnership in the AI safety space. While a modest contract, it signals Accenture's growing role in enterprise AI governance and its relationship with one of the most anticipated IPOs in the market.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. This reflects broader consumer and retailer pressure on premium-priced goods and could signal a tougher environment for branded consumer staples companies relying on elevated pricing.

▼ VW Volkswagen Guidance Cut

Volkswagen issued a large downside preannouncement on Friday, now guiding for operating margins of up to 1% for 2026 versus the Street's estimate of approximately 4.1% and prior guidance of 4 to 5.5%. The miss was driven by a goodwill impairment charge at Porsche, a challenging auto market especially in China, and additional restructuring expenses; adjusted for special items operating margins would be roughly 4%, essentially in line with consensus.

◆ NSCL Nscale Other

Nscale, a London-based neocloud firm, publicly filed its S-1 on Friday afternoon and aims to list on the NYSE under the ticker NSCL. The filing brings another AI infrastructure company to public markets at a time when investor sentiment toward the sector faces pressure from rising capex concerns and OpenAI's massive negative free cash flow projections.

Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia over the weekend, with Iran warning against any US military resumption, while the State Department flagged potential for unforeseen escalation. Ukraine also launched a massive drone strike on Russia including Moscow, adding further geopolitical risk heading into the week.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction, with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. The BOJ's latest rate hike was slightly more dovish than expected, but it triggered macro concern by pressuring the yen and rekindling fears about Japan potentially selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over the coming weeks, while Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that elevated inflation is driven by more than just oil.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all the market's macro problems trace back to oil, and even a small geopolitical improvement could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The UN General Assembly brings together officials from Middle East countries including Iran, creating a real if slim window for diplomatic progress. The Trump/Xi summit could yield select tariff reductions on roughly 30 billion dollars worth of goods, and China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, signaling growing global alarm that could accelerate a resolution.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape is quite grim, with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis in Yemen now escalating attacks on Saudi Arabia. The economy and corporate earnings have held up over the summer, but the latest spike in energy prices will begin working through the system quickly and risk at least some demand destruction. The weekend AI dump — including a disappointing Anthropic ARR figure, OpenAI's staggering negative 280 billion dollar free cash flow projection through 2030, and rising Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liabilities — adds a fresh layer of sector-specific risk on top of the macro headwinds.

Market Outlook

Adam leans cautiously bearish heading into the week. The weekend news flow was net negative across AI and geopolitics, with no clear near-term catalyst to reverse either headwind. The key catalysts to watch this week are the Trump/Xi summit on Thursday September 24th — which could deliver modest tariff relief — and the US flash PMIs on Wednesday September 23rd. Any Middle East diplomatic progress at the UN General Assembly is possible but seen as wishful thinking.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a cluster of negative weekend headlines: rising off-balance sheet AI liabilities up roughly 300 billion dollars in the last 12 months, OpenAI's projected negative 280 billion dollar free cash flow from 2026 to 2030, and Anthropic's IPO pushed to November with ARR projections coming in below the most bullish expectations. The energy sector is front and center given Houthi escalation against Saudi Arabia, Iran's grip on Hormuz, and a diesel price spike driven primarily by the Iran war. Chinese memory supply from CXMT entering mass production could weigh on pricing for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. The auto sector remains challenged globally, as illustrated by Volkswagen's dramatic margin preannouncement miss.
