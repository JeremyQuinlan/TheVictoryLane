Macro · Rates

This is a company-specific earnings digest for Thursday October 15th, focused on pre-market reporters. There is no broad index or overnight macro commentary provided in this edition. Risk tone is mixed given the blend of beats, inline prints, and one major negative preannouncement.

No explicit Fed or rates commentary is included in this newsletter. However, PNC notes that residential and commercial mortgage income fell due to fallout from high rates, suggesting the rate environment remains a headwind for rate-sensitive businesses.

Market Outlook

No explicit directional market call is made by Adam in this edition. The overall earnings tone is modestly positive for financials, with several EPS beats, but FMC's aggressive negative preannouncement is a notable drag on the industrials/materials space.

Sector Watch

Financials are broadly solid this morning with multiple EPS beats across custody banking, regional banking, and brokerage — BNY, PNC, Schwab, and Marsh all topped estimates, while USB was inline and FHN's restructuring is strategically sound. Agricultural chemicals are under pressure following FMC's large negative preannouncement around channel inventory and pricing, which could reprice the ag-chem complex lower. The staffing sector remains in a murky inflection with Manpower's in-line print and slightly soft Q4 guide.

VK · Key Names
◆ KMB Kimberly-Clark Earnings Beat

KMB preannounced mixed Q3 results with adjusted EPS of $1.70 beating the Street's $1.60, but organic sales fell 1.3% versus the consensus estimate of -0.7%. Full-year guidance was reiterated, offering no incremental upside. The volume softness is a modest negative offset by the earnings outperformance.

▲ PAG Penske Auto M&A

Penske Auto will be taken private by Penske Corp. and Mitsui at $212 per share in cash, valuing the deal at $16.3 billion. This represents a significant premium and removes PAG from the public market. The deal is straightforwardly bullish for existing shareholders.

▲ BNY Bank of New York Earnings Beat

BNY delivered strong Q3 results with EPS up 24% to $2.37 versus the Street's $2.27, driven by revenue growth of 12% to $5.676 billion and 180 basis points of pre-tax margin expansion year over year. Issuer services fees surged 43% and FX revenue rose 19%, highlighting the strength of its fee-based business model. Management flagged a constructive but uneven operating environment.

◆ FHN First Horizon Restructuring

FHN reported an EPS loss of 20 cents in Q3 due to a deliberate balance sheet restructuring, though adjusted EPS of 53 cents was in line with consensus. The company agreed to sell approximately $4.5 billion of lower-yielding consumer mortgages, a move expected to be accretive to earnings and free up capacity for future loan growth. The headline loss masks a strategically constructive repositioning.

▲ MRSH Marsh Earnings Beat

Marsh reported Q3 EPS of $2.03, a few cents above the Street's $1.96, with aggregate organic revenue growth of 5% beating the 4% consensus. Consulting segment delivered standout 8% organic growth versus the 4.8% estimate, though Reinsurance was weak at -6%. Overall a modestly positive print driven by the consulting outperformance.

◆ PNC PNC Financial Earnings Beat

PNC beat on Q3 adjusted EPS at $5.09 versus the $4.96 consensus, aided by better efficiency at 57% and significantly lower provisions of $189 million against the $246 million estimate, though revenue of $6.562 billion slightly missed. The 2026 guidance update is mixed — revenue growth guidance moved up to 13.7% from 13% but expense growth also increased to 9.5% from 8.5%. Mortgage income fell 6% sequentially and 16% year over year due to the high-rate environment.

▲ SCHW Schwab Earnings Beat

Schwab posted Q3 EPS of $1.69, up 20% and a few cents above the $1.65 estimate, with revenue of $7.17 billion in line and margins expanding 350 basis points to 54.8%. Total client assets rose 15% to $13.33 trillion and core net new assets surged 38% to $190.4 billion, while margin loan balances jumped 14% sequentially to $187.9 billion and daily average trading volume rose 44% year over year. NII and NIM were slightly light but the asset and engagement metrics are clearly strong.

◆ USB US Bancorp Guidance Raise

USB reported Q3 EPS of $1.34, essentially in line with the $1.33 consensus, as better efficiency and lower provisions offset slightly weaker revenue of $7.97 billion versus the $8.01 billion estimate. Fee revenue accelerated to 14.9% growth, though ex-BTIG the underlying trend cooled to 7.6%. Full-year revenue guidance was raised to up 6-7% ex-BTIG with operating leverage expanding to approximately 400 basis points from the prior 300-plus basis points.

▼ FMC FMC Corp. Earnings Miss

FMC issued a major negative preannouncement for Q3, citing weaker-than-expected demand and elevated inventory of higher-priced FMC products at channel customers limiting replenishment. The company is aggressively slashing prices on select products and modifying its US selling and pricing model to clear the channel. This is a significant bearish catalyst for the stock and potentially for the broader agricultural chemicals sector.

◆ MAN Manpower Other

Manpower reported Q3 EPS of $1.01, essentially in line with the $1.02 consensus, with organic growth of 6% slightly above the 5.75% estimate. The Q4 EPS guidance midpoint of $1.08 is just below the $1.10 Street estimate, a modest miss. Management called 2026 an inflection point for the business citing sequential improvement in Experis and Talent Solutions, but the guide doesn't fully confirm that conviction.
