RATES & FED

The July jobs report came in weak enough to take some upward pressure off yields, but with core CPI expected at +2.5% and headline at +3.4% for July — still well above the Fed's target — the relief may be short-lived. Trump renewed his threat to fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook on Friday, another chip off the foundation of Fed credibility (FT). Bears argue the soft labor print won't fix inflation driven by AI, tariffs, and war fallout, leaving one to two hikes still on the table.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The Iran situation is the dominant market variable right now, and the weekend news flow was unambiguously worse than expected — Tehran moved the goalposts again with a fresh slate of demands, and another commercial vessel was struck in Hormuz. That said, the author's view is that the situation isn't necessarily catastrophic: if the White House simply recommits to the MOU terms and formally acknowledges Iran's traffic-control role in Hormuz, commercial activity could normalize fairly quickly. The bigger structural problem is that the US has no credible military leverage left — depleted stockpiles, exhausted airstrike effectiveness, and a Joint Chiefs chairman privately pushing for an off-ramp (CNN) — which means Iran knows it can drag this out. CPI on Wednesday is the key domestic catalyst; a hotter-than-expected print would be a meaningful headwind for risk assets.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran's Supreme National Security Council chief issued a new round of sweeping demands over the weekend, insisting they be met before Hormuz reopens — a significant setback after last week's optimism about a deal (NYT, WSJ). A commercial ship was struck in the waterway over the weekend.

- General Dan Caine, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, has been privately urging White House officials to find an off-ramp, arguing escalatory options are exhausted and airstrikes have run their course (CNN).

- US weapons stockpiles have fallen to "troubling" levels — emboldening Iran at the negotiating table and raising alarm bells about Beijing and Moscow (NYT). The Pentagon is now pressing defense contractors to dramatically accelerate production (Washington Post).

- Iran is explicitly seeking a major reduction in US military presence in the Gulf as part of any settlement (WSJ). Tehran faces growing economic pressure — no tankers have loaded at Kharg Island in over a week due to the US blockade — giving it some incentive to eventually deal (FT).

- Yemen is sliding back toward civil war as the fragile truce between the official government and the Houthis collapses, while the Houthis continue striking Saudi Arabia (NYT).

- Saudi Arabia's new defense pact with Turkey and Pakistan signals the Kingdom is hedging against a weakening US security umbrella and has no near-term plans to join the Abraham Accords (WSJ).

- Turkey is restricting Black Sea ship traffic amid a surge in Russia-Ukraine attacks (Bloomberg).

- China's July inflation undershot expectations: PPI came in at +3.5% vs. +3.9% consensus (down from +4.1% in June), CPI at +0.5% vs. +0.8% consensus (down from +1.0%) (Reuters).

- The Senate passed a stopgap spending bill 90-6 to fund the government through December 11, though House passage remains uncertain (NYT). Crypto legislation stalled despite a $200M+ lobbying push for the Clarity Act (FT). Canada and the US are reportedly in tentative talks on trade concessions in exchange for tariff relief (Reuters).

COMPANY NEWS

BRK/B (Berkshire Hathaway) posted Q2 operating income of $12.983B, up 16.3% year-over-year, crushing the consensus of roughly $5 per Class B share with ~$6 in EPS. Revenue hit $101.8B, up 10% Y/Y and well ahead of the $94.8B Street estimate. The quality asterisk: a $1.274B "other" income contribution (vs. $32M a year ago, largely FX-driven) flattered the headline. GEICO was a notable drag, with insurance underwriting down 45% on higher claims frequency and severity. Offsetting that, BNSF grew 6%, BH Energy surged 27%, and Manufacturing/Service/Retailing jumped 24%. Cash ended the quarter at ~$365B, down ~$30B sequentially after ~$20B of net equity purchases (including the $10B Google private placement) and ~$4.5B in buybacks — the latter at the low end of expectations.

AAPL is testing memory chips from China's CXMT across iPhone and Mac product lines and is seeking White House approval to use the components for China-sold devices; HP and Acer are already using CXMT memory outside the US (WSJ).

AVAV (AeroVironment) jumped Friday after Bloomberg reported the US Army will purchase at least $400M of counter-drone lasers from the company.

CELH (Celsius) surged after Rockstar Energy founder Russ Savage disclosed a ~4.7% stake and publicly called for a management overhaul, including installing himself as CEO (CNBC).

ASH (Ashland) spiked Friday on a Bloomberg report that management is exploring a sale after receiving takeover interest.

PSKY/WBD — Paramount has made written commitments to major theater chains to release 30 theatrical films per year if the Warner Bros. Discovery merger closes, a concession that could also help settle state AG litigation against the deal (Bloomberg).

SPCX (SpaceX) — SemiAnalysis argues SpaceX can legitimately hit 10GW+ of compute capacity by end of 2027 as Elon projected, though the math requires $300-500B in 2027 capex with heavy vendor financing from Nvidia; Microsoft is cited as a potential anchor buyer. Separately, SpaceX could close its acquisition of Cursor (the AI coding tool) as soon as this week (The Information).

NVDA (Nvidia) is investing $2B into Lancium, the power developer behind the OpenAI/Oracle Texas data center, with the potential to put in another $1B (The Information).

OpenAI has paused development of its next-generation model Astra after it exhibited "critical cyber capabilities," per the company's own disclosure.
