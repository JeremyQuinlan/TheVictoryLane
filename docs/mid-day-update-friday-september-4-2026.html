RATES & FED

The hot August jobs report pushed 2-year yields up 3.5 basis points, though 10- and 30-year yields held relatively steady — a sign the bond market isn't panicking. The Fed is now priced for ~36 basis points of hikes across the final three meetings of the year (vs. ~33 before the report and ~40 post-Jackson Hole), with a 9/16 move now ~60% odds. Not a dramatic repricing, and frankly the jobs data didn't warrant one.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The knee-jerk blame on the jobs report for today's equity weakness doesn't fully hold up — yields are well off their intraday highs and yet the SPX keeps drifting lower, which suggests something else is driving the selling. The read here is that today's number was actually constructive: strong job creation, no excess on unemployment or wages, the two metrics the Fed cares most about. Iran-related geopolitical noise isn't the culprit either given Brent is flat. More likely this is positioning ahead of a week packed with the ECB decision, US CPI/PPI, and the Apple event — and the market is choosing to lighten up rather than lean in.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The White House has told Middle East intermediaries it won't return to the MOU with Iran, instead pushing for a broader deal that addresses the nuclear issue — a longer negotiation timeline, which markets don't love (FT).

- Iran's economy is buckling under US sanctions and the ongoing blockade, with financial pain described as "growing increasingly difficult to withstand" — which could ultimately push Tehran toward the table (Reuters).

- Europe has formally joined Washington's "Operation Economic Outcast" against Iran, piling on additional financial pressure (CNBC). South Korea is weighing naval deployments to the Strait of Hormuz at US request (SCMP).

- Republicans have abandoned hopes the Iran conflict ends before the midterms and are recalibrating expectations accordingly (Politico).

- Ukraine is planning drone swarm campaigns to shut down Russian commercial airspace (WSJ).

- Norway's $2T+ sovereign wealth fund is proposing to reduce sovereign bond holdings, which would cut its Treasury exposure — worth watching for duration demand implications (FT). Offsetting that, Chinese banks have been actively buying Treasuries given favorable yields vs. domestic alternatives (Reuters).

- Chinese rare earth firms are halting some US shipments over geopolitical concerns — the trade friction isn't fully resolved (Reuters).

- Global food prices hit their highest level since late 2022 in August, driven by geopolitical fallout and extreme weather (Bloomberg).
