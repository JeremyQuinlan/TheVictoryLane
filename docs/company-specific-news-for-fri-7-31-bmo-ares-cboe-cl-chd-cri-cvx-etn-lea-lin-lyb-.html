MARKET OUTLOOK

The Friday BMO earnings slate is broadly constructive — beats in industrials, energy, and autos suggest the real economy is holding up better than the macro bears want to admit. The data center buildout is clearly still accelerating (ETN and NVT both screaming higher guidance), and even the consumer names are finding their footing. Tariff refund noise muddies some of the consumer beats, but organic trends are quietly improving underneath. The one watch item: NVT's orders lagged revenue hard and the backlog slipped — that's a yellow flag worth monitoring as the cycle matures.

---

GEOPOLITICAL

- Middle East/Hormuz disruptions remain an active operational risk — XOM management explicitly flagged that ongoing Iran-war-related disruptions could weigh on Q3 production, compounding a contractual shift in Guyana that will reduce net entitlement volumes by roughly 100K BPD.

- LYB and DOW are cited as primary beneficiaries of supply chain disruptions stemming from the Iran conflict, which helped drive LYB's significant EBITDA outperformance.

COMPANY NEWS

LEA (Lear) delivered decent Q2 upside with EPS of $4.28 versus the Street's $3.94, and raised the midpoint of most 2026 guidance ranges.

MGA (Magna) posted a strong Q2, with EPS of $1.86 versus the Street's $1.51 (+29%), driven by margin expansion to 6.2% (Street: 6.0%) and revenue of $10.98B versus $10.76B expected. Full-year EPS guidance lifts to ~$7 from $6.75, though the sales outlook is trimmed on FX and divestitures.

CL (Colgate) beat on EPS at 99c versus the Street's 95c; organic revenue was inline at +2.5%. Full-year EPS guidance now calls for ~+5% growth versus the prior +1-5% range.

CHD (Church & Dwight) crushed on organic sales (+5.8% versus Street +3.4%) but EPS landed inline at 89c as higher marketing and SG&A offset the top-line strength. Full-year EPS now +6-8% (prior: +5-8%), but Q3 EPS guidance of 89c trails the Street's 93c.

CRI (Carter's) posted a large EPS beat — tariff refunds were a major contributor, though management noted sales came in ahead of plan with early benefits from productivity initiatives and improved marketing.

NWL (Newell) beat big on margins and EPS with a guidance bump; tariff refunds helped, but organic revenue of +2.3% versus the Street's +1.75% and a raised organic revenue guide (+1-2% versus prior 0-2%) suggest genuine underlying improvement from turnaround initiatives.

CVX (Chevron) reported Q2 adjusted EPS of $6.06 versus the Street's $5.65, with the beat concentrated in Downstream. Production rose 20% — ahead of plan — driven by Hess contribution plus Permian and Gulf growth. Cash flow was robust.

XOM (Exxon) posted a slim Q2 EPS miss at $3.52 versus the Street's $3.54, with weakness in Energy Products, though free cash flow outperformed at $17.2B and production beat expectations.

ARES (Ares Management) delivered after-tax realized income of $1.29 per share versus $1.27 expected, with FRE margins expanding 100 basis points year-over-year and coming in roughly 40 basis points above plan. AUM hit $671.3B versus Street estimates of $666.5B on $34.4B in net inflows.

CBOE (Cboe Global Markets) beat on Q2 adjusted EPS at $3.56 versus $3.47, and raised its organic revenue growth target for the year from 11-15% to 15-19%.

MRNA (Moderna) reported Q2 EPS of -$1.97 versus the Street's -$2.01 — not as bad as feared. Full-year expense guidance was lowered and year-end cash guidance raised, with revenue guidance reiterated (up to +10%). The key negative: mRNA-1403, its norovirus vaccine candidate, failed to meet statistical criteria for early success at a Phase 3 interim analysis.

NVO (Novo Nordisk) announced negative data results for heart drug ziltivekimab.

ETN (Eaton) beat on Q2 EPS at $3.15 versus $3.08, with organic growth of 14% crushing the Street's 9.7% and margins of 23.1% topping the 22.8% estimate. Full-year organic sales guidance raised to +11-13% (from +9-11%) and EPS to $13.40-$13.60 (from $13.05-$13.50), driven by data center demand and broad end-market strength.

LIN (Linde) posted EPS of $4.50, essentially inline with the Street's $4.49. Operating margins fell 60 basis points as cost inflation offset pricing and productivity gains. Full-year EPS guidance nudged to $17.70-$17.90 from $17.60-$17.90. Management noted robust customer proposal activity in electronics.

LYB (LyondellBasell) crushed on Q2 EBITDA at $2.12B versus the Street's $1.78B, benefiting from strong execution and geopolitical supply chain tailwinds related to the Iran conflict.

NVT (nVent) delivered a blowout quarter — organic sales +47% versus Street +26%, EPS of $1.45 versus $1.15, and full-year organic growth guidance raised to +32-34% from +21-23%. The caveat: orders grew far less than revenue and backlog slipped to $2.5B from $2.6B in Q1 — worth watching.

Westinghouse Electric Company has confidentially filed an S-1 with the SEC for an IPO. The nuclear technology and services supplier's move is notable given the broader resurgence of interest in nuclear power infrastructure.
