MARKET OUTLOOK

Stocks are holding in reasonably well given the geopolitical noise — the bid in tech, healthcare, and energy is doing real work here. The Iran situation looks messy on the surface but Tehran's demands are essentially a request to return to the prior MOU framework, and Trump's notably relaxed tone to Axios over the weekend signals the White House isn't itching to escalate. The real risk event this week is Wednesday's CPI — a hot print reopens the hawkish Fed narrative and pressures duration; a soft one reinforces the soft-landing trade. Bias remains constructive on equities into year-end, but Hormuz is a live tail risk that keeps energy vol elevated.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran's Supreme National Security Council issued a list of extreme demands over the weekend before it would reopen Hormuz traffic, suggesting a deal is less imminent than it appeared last week (NYT, WSJ). Trump told Axios on Sunday the US will maintain economic pressure on Tehran but will not resume airstrikes — a notably conciliatory posture.

- General Dan Caine, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, has been pressing White House officials for an off-ramp from the Iran conflict, arguing escalatory options are limited and airstrike effectiveness has peaked (CNN).

- American weapons stockpiles have fallen to "troubling" levels, a dynamic that could embolden Beijing and Moscow and is giving Tehran leverage in negotiations (NYT).

- UAE's Adnoc, unconstrained by OPEC quotas, announced $8B+ in production capacity expansion and is studying an LNG export terminal that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz entirely (WSJ, Bloomberg).

- Yemen is sliding back into civil war as the fragile truce between the government and the Houthis collapses (NYT).

- China's July inflation missed on both PPI (+3.5% vs. +3.9% expected; down from +4.1%) and CPI (+0.5% vs. +0.8%; down from +1.0%), flagging continued demand softness (Reuters).

- BOJ's July meeting summary revealed growing concern about Japan's inflation path, raising odds of a September rate hike (Reuters).

COMPANY NEWS

AAON crushed Q2 estimates with EPS of $0.69 vs. $0.51 expected and revenue of $627MM vs. $508MM, driven by data center demand. Full-year sales guidance lifted to +55–60% from +40–55%, though backlog dipped to $2B from $2.1B and gross margin guidance was trimmed to 25–26% from 27–28%.

AAPL is testing memory chips from China's CXMT across iPhone and Mac product lines and is seeking White House approval to use them in China-sold devices (WSJ). Jefferies downgraded the stock today, contributing to its underperformance.

ACHR (Archer Aviation) is acquiring Boeing's Wisk Aero, SkyGrid, and Insitu subsidiaries; Boeing will receive shares equal to 19.75% of Class A outstanding plus two warrants.

B (Barrick) posted Q2 adjusted EPS of $0.82, inline with consensus, as strong gold production from Loulo-Gounkoto, Pueblo Viejo, and Cortez was offset by higher fuel costs. Separately, Barrick and NEM (Newmont) agreed to expand the Nevada Gold Mines JV, and Newmont consented to Barrick's proposed North American gold assets IPO.

BRK/B posted solid Q2 operating income upside and deployed a meaningful portion of its cash hoard, sending shares higher.

BWMN (Bowman) will be acquired by Bernhard Capital Partners for $43/share cash (~$1B deal).

DOLE reported Q2 adjusted EPS of $0.46, inline, but trimmed full-year EBITDA guidance to "approximately $400MM" from "at least $400MM."

EBAY — GameStop's Ryan Cohen is reportedly weighing withdrawal of his $5.6B bid (Bloomberg).

FERG (Ferguson) beat Q2 EPS by a few cents ($3.39 vs. $3.32), with sales up 4.6% to $8.75B and gross margins of 31% vs. 30.6% expected. Full-year sales guidance raised to +5% from +1–5%.

HZO (MarineMax) is nearing a $53/share cash acquisition by Blackstone's Safe Harbor Marinas (~$1.5B deal) (Reuters).

INTC announced a $15B common stock offering to fund growth and preserve its investment-grade rating, pressuring shares.

JAZZ (Jazz Pharma) is acquiring Actio Biosciences for $820MM upfront plus up to $500MM in contingent payments.

MNDY (Monday.com) beat Q2 EPS and revenue but left full-year sales guidance unchanged and guided Q3 revenue to $368–370MM vs. the Street's $373MM.

MSFT plans to ramp production of its Maia AI chips significantly, targeting 300K+ units at TSMC in a bid to reduce Nvidia dependence (The Information).

PSKY/WBD — Paramount has made written commitments to major theater chains to release 30 films per year if the Warner Bros. Discovery merger closes, potentially a template for settling state AG opposition (Bloomberg).
