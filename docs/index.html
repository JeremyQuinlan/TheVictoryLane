▼ ORCL Oracle Massive Capex

Oracle was the subject of a negative FT report over the weekend focused on poor price action in debt tied to a massive Oracle-linked data center in New Mexico known as 'Project Jupiter.' Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have 'hit a wall,' raising questions about Oracle's off-balance-sheet data center financing strategy. This adds to a broader concern about Big Tech AI off-balance-sheet liability exposure, which has risen roughly $300 billion in just the last 12 months.

Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points (65 basis points), Nasdaq +329 points (110 basis points), and Russell 2000 +47 basis points. Asian markets closed broadly higher led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while major Eurozone indices gained 85-120 basis points with strength in industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The primary driver of risk-on sentiment appears to be diplomatic optimism surrounding the UN General Assembly in New York (September 22-28), where Trump signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, overshadowing a weekend full of negative geopolitical headlines including escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and Ukraine's largest-ever drone strike on Russia.

Treasuries are catching a bid with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The Fed remains in a hawkish posture — markets are pricing roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes across the final two FOMC meetings of the year, with roughly 50/50 odds of an increase at the October 28 meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of hikes priced for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that inflation is running too hot and is broad-based beyond just energy. Treasury is also planning to issue over one trillion dollars in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt and above the Treasury's own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is the primary bullish catalyst, with Trump signaling willingness to meet Iran's president and reports that China and Pakistan are urging Iran to rein in the Houthis. Even a small improvement in Middle East tensions could trigger an outsized decline in oil from current levels near $102, which would lower yields, ease inflation, improve corporate margins, and boost consumer confidence. Treasuries are rallying alongside equities, suggesting the move has some macro credibility. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday also offers a potential upside surprise on tariff reductions.

BEAR CASE

Nearly every piece of weekend news flow was net negative — the Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest-ever drone strike on Russia, Tehran warned of a fierce response to any resumed US military action, and the State Department issued a warning about rapid escalation risk in the Middle East. On the AI and tech side, a flood of negative headlines emerged after Friday's close including Anthropic's delayed IPO, underwhelming 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. The US-China trade truce extension was not reached over the weekend, and Washington is expected to raise aggregate tariff rates on Chinese imports back toward pre-SCOTUS IEEPA levels in coming weeks, meaning any Xi summit goodwill may be short-lived.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday's session, driven entirely by UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism around Iran and the Middle East — the thesis being that even a modest reduction in geopolitical tension could trigger an outsized drop in oil prices and unleash a cascade of favorable macro effects. The key catalyst to watch is any signal of diplomatic progress between the US and Iran at the UN General Assembly, as well as developments around the Trump/Xi summit scheduled for Thursday.

Sector Watch

AI and data center infrastructure stocks face meaningful headwinds from a cluster of negative weekend headlines: Anthropic's delayed IPO, disappointing 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn of roughly $280 billion in negative free cash flow projected through 2030, and Oracle data center debt problems. However, data center pick-and-shovel stocks are actually outperforming in Europe Monday morning, suggesting some selective resilience. Energy is a key macro sector to watch — Brent crude is sliding 170 basis points to roughly $102.10 as UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism takes hold. Chinese memory supplier CXMT announced mass production of its next-gen platform, which could pressure pricing for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Acer's CEO confirmed Chinese memory supply is helping ease component shortages and cap prices, a mixed read for Western memory names. European financials are outperforming, led by SocGen after the company provided bullish capital markets day targets including ROTE of 13-14% in 2029 and above 15% in 2030 and beyond.
