RATES & FED

The market had been pricing roughly 50 basis points of Fed hikes for the year as of late July, but that figure has since been cut nearly in half to 23 basis points (as of Thursday's close), driven by the soft July jobs report and this week's cooler CPI and PPI prints. Within the PPI, energy and food did the heavy disinflationary lifting while core ran a touch firmer than expected — and a very hot financial services reading signals upward pressure on the PCE later this month. The Atlanta Fed wage tracker also re-accelerated in July to +3.8% (job switchers: +4.4%), a reminder that the all-clear on inflation isn't here yet.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Bulls clearly won the week — AI momentum is intact, inflation is easing, and the earnings tape was broadly constructive even where stocks sold off on the news. The Iran/Hormuz situation is real but increasingly a sideshow for US equities; the author's view is that the market's bear narrative on the Middle East has overshot and that both Tehran and Washington have strong incentives to de-escalate. The bigger structural risk isn't geopolitical — it's whether the AI capex machine can sustain itself as op. cash flow lags capex by wide margins and the financing patchwork (debt, vendor financing, prepayments) is not guaranteed to last forever. A potential compute glut from the $500B+ Nvidia financing blueprint is a legitimate medium-term overhang worth watching. For now, though, the money tsunami is still flooding in, and the path of least resistance remains higher.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran-US talks remain deadlocked — Tehran shifted the goalposts again this week with a new slate of unrealistic demands, and the Iran/Oman deal that was supposed to pave the way for Hormuz normalization still hasn't been formally unveiled. Brent spiked Monday and Tuesday on the deterioration (Vital Knowledge).

- Energy Secretary Wright posted data Wednesday claiming far more oil is traversing the Strait of Hormuz than markets realize, though opacity in export data makes verification difficult (Bloomberg).

- Iran faces mounting economic pressure from sanctions and risks accelerating the world's buildout of alternative infrastructure the longer it holds Hormuz hostage — a powerful incentive to eventually deal. The Red Sea (Houthis) and Black Sea (Russia-Ukraine) add additional shipping disruption risk to the inflationary backdrop.

COMPANY NEWS

CSCO (Cisco) reported solid results and described a "networking supercycle," citing ramping demand well beyond AI hyperscalers. The stock saw a sell-the-news reaction despite the constructive print.

AMAT (Applied Materials) also delivered robust numbers Thursday night alongside bullish commentary: "Cloud service providers continue to increase their investments in AI infrastructure. Importantly, many of these companies are already generating positive returns on their investments." Shares similarly sold off post-release.

CRWV and NBIS both reported this week and spent considerable time addressing AI ROI/payback dynamics on capex — the key bear concern — while their results reinforced the ravenous cash consumption of the AI buildout, with capex running multiples of revenue.

NVDA (Nvidia) outlined a $500B+ financing blueprint Monday framed as entirely third-party capital, though the company may backstop up to 25% of projects originated by the new financial consortium — a distinction that matters (Bloomberg).

WDAY (Workday) spiked Thursday after Reuters reported Silver Lake is considering a buyout in what would be one of the largest software deals in history — a signal that value investors are warming to legacy SaaS names despite AI disruption fears.

BRK (Berkshire Hathaway) posted upside on operating income/EPS, with economically sensitive businesses performing well and management beginning to deploy its massive cash hoard. Insurance underwriting was soft, particularly at GEICO; the headline beat was somewhat smaller than it appeared.

SMCI, COHR, LITE, NBIS, CBRS, SNDK — all reported or held analyst meetings with broadly bullish AI-related commentary, reinforcing the infrastructure buildout narrative even where individual stocks didn't rally.

FERG (Ferguson) delivered a beat-and-raise quarter. Maersk reported strong Q2 results on volume and pricing strength and raised full-year guidance — a positive macro read-through.

INTC (Intel) launched a $20B equity raise, underscoring the scale of capital required to compete in AI infrastructure.

ONON, TPR, YETI — consumer names hit hard after disappointing earnings results this week.
