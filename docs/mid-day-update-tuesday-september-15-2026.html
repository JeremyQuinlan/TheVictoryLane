MARKET OUTLOOK

The bull case here is getting harder to construct intraday — oil is the primary villain, with Brent extending gains on a Libya outage layered on top of Yemen/Houthis, the Saudi East-West Pipeline disruption, and Iran/Hormuz tensions. The equal-weight S&P underperforming tells you this isn't just mega-cap drag; the pain is broad. Treasury Sec. Bessent's "everything is fine" posture before the House isn't calming anyone. That said, the risk/reward on Brent at these levels is asymmetric in the market's favor — conditions in the Middle East are already priced for maximum bleak, and any marginal improvement could trigger a sharp reversal in oil and a relief rally in equities.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Saudi Arabia is described as being in a state of "shock," confronting its worst-case scenario as US protection from Houthi attacks evaporates and Iran grows more emboldened (NYT).

- A Libya production outage has joined the pile of Middle East supply disruptions, adding to pressure from Yemen/Houthis, the Saudi East-West Pipeline, and Iran's Hormuz threat.

- Pentagon inspector general warns the Iran war has produced a munitions shortage and supply chain bottlenecks (NBC News).

- Ukraine offered to halt energy attacks if Russia reciprocated, though Kyiv is openly skeptical Moscow will agree (CNBC). Putin warned that any European peacekeeping troop deployment to Ukraine would mean war (Washington Post).

- Japan is considering raising its deficit spending target to 3.5% of GDP under US pressure — Bloomberg flags this could "send a shockwave through markets" already nervous about Tokyo's fiscal trajectory. Separately, Japan's cabinet approved a temporary cut in the food sales tax from 8% to 1% (Bloomberg).

- China's August data disappointed on retail sales (+0.4% vs. +0.8% expected) and fixed asset investment, with only industrial production modestly beating at +5.2% vs. +4.8% expected (WSJ, FT). Beijing also tightened international travel restrictions for certain citizens to guard state secrets and retain skilled workers (FT).

- Hedge funds are becoming a dominant force in the Treasury market as pensions exit, increasing volatility — the NY Fed has begun surveying investors on the dynamic (WSJ).

- Warsh's arrival had temporarily cooled Trump's Fed criticism, but a Wednesday rate hike could shatter that truce (WSJ).
