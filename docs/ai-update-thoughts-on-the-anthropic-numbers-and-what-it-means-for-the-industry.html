AI update: thoughts on the Anthropic numbers and what it means for the industry

Oct 14, 2026 · 09:22 PM ET

Here's the key read heading into Wednesday's session. The big story overnight is Anthropic, which held investor meetings and revealed third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion — up 45% quarter-over-quarter. On an absolute basis that's strong, but the rate of change is cooling sharply. For context, they grew 147% quarter-over-quarter in the second quarter, so this is a meaningful step-down. The company is also claiming positive free cash flow, adjusted operating margins of 11%, and adjusted gross margins of 85%. But the opacity around those numbers is a real problem — what's being excluded, how are they defining gross versus net, training versus inference? There is no audited clarity here, and that lack of transparency is a structural issue for the entire private AI universe, including OpenAI. Beyond the numbers, two key demand sources for Anthropic are expected to fade — Meta is building its own tools, and Anthropic is cracking down on distillation. A powerful new frontier model could reignite growth, but frontier leadership is extremely fluid right now with no single player holding the crown for more than a few months. On the broader market, the central problem in AI right now comes down to capital access. Credit default swap spreads for CoreWeave and Oracle are widening, and that is the market sending a message that borrowing is becoming prohibitively expensive for large parts of the AI infrastructure ecosystem. If capital dries up, capex slows, and that pain flows down the entire supply chain — including Nvidia, which has been trying to act as a lender of last resort for partners but cannot carry the whole industry alone. One important macro signal: Treasuries did not sell off despite a hot consumer price index print. Adam reads that as seller exhaustion, and expects it to act as a green light for further declines in rates — especially if AI anxiety stays elevated. That rate dynamic should benefit deeply out-of-favor, under-owned sectors like regional banks, non-data center industrials, and consumer discretionary. The key event to watch is Meta's earnings on October 21. Meta has been the most disruptive AI player lately with its Muse agent and models, and there is speculation it could announce a large equity offering around that report — which would further pressure the AI funding and IPO landscape. The overall directional read is cautious on AI infrastructure and neocloud names. It is too early to buy that dip. The better trade is rotation into unloved sectors that benefit from lower yields. Cash-flow-rich hyperscalers — Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Microsoft — are relatively advantaged in this environment as smaller competitors struggle to access capital. Stay selective, stay defensive within tech, and watch rates.
