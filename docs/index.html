Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +329 points/110 basis points, and R2K +13.5 points/47 basis points, despite a weekend full of negative headlines. Asian and European markets are broadly higher, with Hang Seng +1.18% and major Eurozone indices up 85-120 basis points. The primary driver appears to be diplomatic optimism ahead of the UN General Assembly in New York, with hopes that Middle East tensions — and by extension oil prices — could ease; Brent is sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10.

Treasury yields are down 2-3 basis points across the curve, providing a modest tailwind for risk assets. The Fed remains in hawkish territory — markets are pricing in roughly 33 basis points of additional rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and another roughly 35 basis points of increases priced in for 2027. Fed's Schmid and Kashkari both made hawkish remarks recently, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices. The Treasury is set to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt and above the Treasury's own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is providing a genuine lift — even a small improvement in Middle East tensions could trigger an outsized drop in oil, unleashing a cascade of bullish macro effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing global pressure on Tehran. Trump called off planned air strikes against the Houthis at the last minute and signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, which markets are reading as a de-escalation signal. The upcoming Trump-Xi summit on Thursday also offers a potential catalyst for modest tariff reductions.

BEAR CASE

Nearly all of the weekend's actual news flow was negative — delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, Oracle data center debt problems, and a dramatic escalation in Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia. The geopolitical backdrop remains grim, with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis, while Russia-Ukraine drone attacks continue to knock out global refining capacity. The rally's reliance on UN General Assembly optimism feels fragile — a diplomatic breakthrough would be a genuine surprise, and any disappointment could reverse gains quickly. On trade, Washington is still expected to hike the aggregate tariff rate on Chinese imports back to pre-SCOTUS IEEPA levels in the coming weeks, regardless of any summit goodwill.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday's session, with the key catalyst being diplomatic developments at the UN General Assembly, particularly any progress toward a Trump-Iran meeting that could take pressure off oil prices. However, he is skeptical that a breakthrough will materialize and warns that the weekend's negative AI and geopolitical headlines remain an unresolved overhang.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe Monday morning. AI-linked names broadly face headwinds from negative weekend headlines including the Anthropic IPO delay, OpenAI's projected negative free cash flow of roughly $280 billion from 2026-2030, and Oracle data center debt stress. Big Tech AI off-balance-sheet liability exposure has risen roughly $300 billion in the last 12 months, flagged as a growing risk. Chinese memory giant CXMT says its next-generation platform is in mass production, which along with comments from Acer's CEO suggests Chinese supply is easing component shortages and capping memory prices — a headwind for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Energy and oil tanker stocks face crosscurrents: Brent is sliding but geopolitical risk remains extreme. Industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks outperform in Europe; utilities, energy, healthcare, real estate, retail, and luxury underperform.
