▼ NVO Novo Nordisk Other

Novo Nordisk shares are slumping in European trading after the company held an analyst meeting Monday and failed to provide more granular financial targets, disappointing investors who were expecting concrete guidance. The lack of specificity at a capital markets event is being read as a negative signal for near-term visibility.

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times report Friday, focusing on poor price action in debt tied to a massive Oracle-linked data center in New Mexico known as Project Jupiter. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have reportedly hit a wall, raising concerns about Oracle's off-balance-sheet AI infrastructure exposure.

Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P up 49 points or 65 basis points, Nasdaq up 110 basis points, and European indices up 85-120 basis points. Asian markets closed broadly higher with Hang Seng up 1.18% and Korea KOSPI up 1.65%. The strength is surprising given net negative weekend news flow including escalating Middle East geopolitics and troubling AI headlines, with the primary driver appearing to be diplomatic optimism ahead of the UN General Assembly in New York.

Treasuries have a bid with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The Fed remains hawkish — markets are pricing in approximately 33 basis points of rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year with roughly 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of increases priced into 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that inflation is running hot beyond just energy costs, while Treasury plans to issue over one trillion dollars in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

The UN General Assembly runs September 22-28 in New York, bringing together leaders from Iran and across the Middle East, creating a rare diplomatic window for de-escalation. Even a minor improvement in Middle East tensions would likely trigger an outsized drop in oil prices, with powerful knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing global alarm and potential for coordinated diplomatic pressure. The Trump-Xi summit on Thursday could also yield modest tariff reductions and LNG deal progress, adding another bullish catalyst to the week.

BEAR CASE

Nearly all of the weekend's news flow was net negative — the Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Trump nearly ordered airstrikes on Yemen, Iran warned of fierce retaliation to any US military action, and Ukraine launched its largest ever drone strike on Russia including Moscow. AI headlines were also deeply troubling: Anthropic's IPO has been delayed, its 2027 ARR projections disappointed, OpenAI faces negative free cash flow of approximately 280 billion dollars through 2030, and Oracle's New Mexico data center debt is hitting a wall. Oil tanker costs are surging and motor oil is being rationed at US retailers, meaning the energy price shock is already working through the real economy and will pressure margins and consumer spending in coming quarters.

Market Outlook

Adam leans cautiously bullish for Monday's session driven entirely by geopolitical optionality — specifically the hope that UN General Assembly meetings this week could produce even a small de-escalation in the Iran/Middle East conflict, which would cascade into lower oil prices, lower yields, and broad macro relief. The key catalyst to watch is any diplomatic signal out of the UN General Assembly, particularly around a potential Trump-Iran presidential meeting and progress at the Trump-Xi summit Thursday.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe Monday. AI infrastructure broadly is under pressure from a wave of negative weekend headlines including Anthropic's delayed IPO, disappointing ARR projections, OpenAI's massive projected cash burn of approximately 280 billion dollars through 2030, and Oracle data center debt stress — all pointing to growing skepticism about the pace of AI monetization relative to spend. Chinese memory producer CXMT entering mass production on next-gen chips is a structural headwind for Western semiconductor memory pricing. Energy and oil-linked sectors face a binary catalyst in Middle East diplomacy this week — any de-escalation at the UN General Assembly could trigger a sharp selloff in energy stocks even as the underlying supply picture remains structurally tight.
