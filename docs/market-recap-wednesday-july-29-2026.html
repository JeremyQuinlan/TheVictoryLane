RATES & FED

The curve steepened sharply, with 2-year yields falling roughly 5 basis points while 10-year and 30-year yields surged 6 and 11 basis points, respectively. The Fed held rates unchanged, with Chair Warsh noting that the bond market had effectively "hiked for us" — a line that briefly lifted equities before the rally faded hard. Markets had priced roughly 30% odds of a hike going in, and the hawkish hold resolves nothing; it just biases long-end yields higher from here.

MARKET OUTLOOK

AI remains the dominant market force — full stop — and the data center capex boom is still the engine driving the broader tape, but the sustainability question is becoming impossible to ignore. Capex is overwhelming operating cash flow, and markets are showing real indigestion absorbing the weekly flood of debt and equity issuance required to fund the buildout. On Iran, expect this to behave like tariffs or Ukraine — a persistent background risk with periodic flare-ups, painful but not fatal to equities. The Fed should have simply hiked and moved on; the hawkish hold only invites more volatility in long-duration rates. Tech earnings are strong in absolute terms, but the bar is punishingly high, and "merely good" keeps getting sold.
