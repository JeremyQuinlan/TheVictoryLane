MARKET OUTLOOK

All eyes on the August jobs report at 8:30am, but don't over-trade it — Waller has already told you it's the CPI that matters. The real risk premium in this market is geopolitical, not macro: Iran is stalemated, rare earth restrictions are quietly tightening, and diesel prices just hit fresh all-time highs (NYT). Consumer earnings are deteriorating fast — LULU and OXM cut guidance badly — while tech beat-and-raise cycles are being met with selling (see PANW). The AI boom is real but competition is crushing industry economics, and with the market priced for perfection in tech, that's a dangerous combination heading into a week packed with CPI, ECB, and Oracle earnings.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The White House has told Middle East intermediaries it won't return to the MOU with Iran and now wants a comprehensive deal covering the nuclear issue (FT). Iran's economy is buckling under sanctions and the blockade — the financial pain is "growing increasingly difficult to withstand" (Reuters) — but Tehran remains hardline. Republicans have quietly accepted the war will run past the midterms (Politico).

- Europe has formally joined Washington's "Operation Economic Outcast" against Iran, deepening financial pressure on Tehran (CNBC). South Korea is weighing deployment of naval assets to Hormuz at Washington's request (SCMP).

- Ukraine is launching a campaign of drone swarms designed to shut down Russia's commercial airspace entirely (WSJ).

- China's rare earth firms are halting some US shipments over geopolitical concerns — a topic expected to dominate the upcoming Trump-Xi summit (Reuters). Trade chief Greer expressed optimism about stabilizing US-China trade ties ahead of the summit (SCMP).

- Norway's $2T+ sovereign wealth fund proposes cutting its sovereign debt holdings, which would meaningfully reduce its Treasury ownership (FT). Meanwhile, Chinese banks are actually increasing Treasury purchases, attracted by yields relative to domestic bonds (Reuters).

- Global food prices hit their highest level since late 2022 due to geopolitical disruption and extreme weather (Bloomberg). Diesel prices exceeded their 2022 post-Ukraine war peak (NYT).

COMPANY NEWS

LULU posted a brutal FQ2 miss — sales of $2.415B vs. Street $2.46B, comps -10% FXN vs. Street -4.3% — and slashed full-year guidance to $10.35-10.5B in sales and EPS of $9.48-9.73 (vs. prior $11-11.15B / $10.95-11.15). Q3 EPS guide of 93-98c vs. Street $2.41 is catastrophic.

OXM reported FQ2 sales of $394MM inline, EPS a touch better, but slashed full-year guidance to sales of $1.43-1.47B (vs. prior $1.475-1.505B) and EPS of $1.60-2.00 (vs. prior $2.30-2.70), citing Lilly Pulitzer softness and macro consumer pressure. Q3 EPS outlook: -$1.30 vs. Street -60c.

DOCU beat on FQ2 EPS ($1.16 vs. Street $1.09) and sales ($875.7MM vs. Street $867.1MM), with AI and the IAM platform driving momentum and full-year guidance moving higher.

IOT beat FQ2 across the board: EPS 20c vs. Street 16c, ARR $2.125B vs. Street $2.095B, sales $508.4MM vs. Street $483.4MM. Full-year guide raised.

ZS beat FQ4 with better F27 guidance and highlighted AI as a major tailwind — but elevated expectations remain a headwind for the group.

GWRE posted modest FQ4 upside on EPS, sales, and ARR; F27 guide slightly ahead (ARR $1.455B vs. Street $1.45B, sales $1.717B vs. Street $1.7B), but FQ1 outlook disappoints.

PATH reported FQ2 op. income of $89MM (vs. Street $75MM) and sales of $410MM (+13%, vs. Street $398MM). Full-year guide raised on both metrics.

TSLA held a low-key Cybercab event Thursday — Musk didn't appear — unveiling the vehicle and announcing limited Austin Robotaxi deployment. The company also disclosed 1M unsupervised miles driven, up sharply from Q2 (Reuters, WSJ, Electrek).

AAPL is expected to raise iPhone prices 10-20% at next week's event due to surging memory costs (SCMP). Separately, foldable iPhone production is running at just a few hundred units per day due to quality control, though Apple targets 8-10M units this year (Nikkei).

ADBE named Anil Chakravarthy as next CEO, effective December 1, 2026. David Wadhwani, the other finalist, is departing.

FLEX is acquiring EPC Power for $4.4B (18.3x projected 2027 EBITDA), expanding into data center power conversion.

BBCP beat FQ3 on sales ($116.8MM vs. Street $110MM) and EBITDA ($30.4MM vs. Street $28.5MM), raised full-year EBITDA guidance to $103-108MM, and initiated a ~5.6% dividend yield.

MU is targeting ~100K HBM wafers/month by end of 2026, roughly doubling current capacity (Digitimes).

NX beat FQ3 with EPS of 79c vs. Street 67c and sales of $501.8MM vs. Street $497.3MM.
