MARKET OUTLOOK

The real market problem isn't geopolitics — it's the AI/tech complex, which is cracking under its own weight as capex swamps operating cash flow and capital markets are pushing back hard on new debt and equity issuance. The tumble in SPCX shares is a particularly ugly red flag for AI-linked IPOs; without that capital formation, datacenter spending slows, and the entire bull thesis for semis and hyperscalers gets stress-tested. Layer on QCOM hiking chip prices by double digits (Bloomberg), CTOs globally rotating toward cheap Chinese open-source models (WSJ), and flash PMIs showing input cost inflation at a 14-month high — this is a stagflationary setup that the market hasn't fully priced. Geopolitical de-escalation on Iran is a genuine positive, but it's not the swing factor it once was.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Trump held off on Iran airstrikes Friday and Saturday — the first pause in nearly two weeks — after the NYT reported he decided against major escalation due to critical shortages of Patriot antimissile interceptors and other munitions (NYT). Iran indicated it will halt its own attacks so long as Washington observes the ceasefire (Reuters). Our view remains: no troops, no exit, so a negotiated deal is the only real off-ramp — though Iran's new supreme leader is reportedly far more interested in acquiring a nuclear weapon than his predecessor (NYT), making talks genuinely hard.

- A second front is opening: Saudi Arabia conducted additional strikes on Yemen and intercepted Houthi missiles aimed at its refineries on Saturday. Several leading maritime insurance firms announced they will no longer cover Saudi ships in the Red Sea (FT) — a material escalation in commercial risk.

- Iran remains militarily formidable; its Kheibar Shekan missile is getting more accurate and more lethal, per a detailed WSJ breakdown.

COMPANY NEWS

AAPL (Apple) is lobbying the White House hard for permission to use Chinese memory chips in devices sold outside the US. MU (Micron) is fiercely opposed, warning of the same decimation dynamic seen in steel and manufacturing (WSJ).

QCOM (Qualcomm) is pushing through double-digit price increases on chips, the clearest single data point yet on inflationary pressure spreading through the tech supply chain (Bloomberg).

NVDA (Nvidia) published an open letter urging Washington against sweeping restrictions on open-weight AI models (Barron's, Bloomberg), even as OpenAI and Anthropic lobby in the opposite direction — quietly pushing for curbs on Chinese open-weight models while publicly saying otherwise (NYT). OpenAI's Sam Altman heads to Washington this week to preview a powerful new model (Axios).

SPCX (SpaceX) successfully launched its 13th Starship test flight Friday (Barron's). Separately, telecom carriers AT&T, T-Mobile, and Verizon pushed back on Starlink displacement fears, noting satellite usage remains "infinitesimal" versus terrestrial networks (MarketWatch). UBER faces a new wrinkle: Google's Waymo is reportedly considering exiting its Uber partnership as the two become direct competitors in autonomous vehicles (FT).

HOOD (Robinhood) is in talks to bring Crypto.com prediction market contracts onto its platform, diversifying away from its current reliance on Kalshi (WSJ).

PSKY (Paramount) and WBD (Warner) merger is on hold until at least June 2027 while a judge considers a multistate legal challenge (NYT).

Samsung announced a $200B, five-year chip supply deal with AVGO (Broadcom) spanning memory, foundry, and advanced packaging (Reuters). NKE (Nike) performance categories including running are showing early recovery signs — WSJ flags this as a stock catalyst. HZO (MarineMax) is in final-round acquisition talks with Blackstone, Donerail, and Centerbridge (Reuters). Anduril is in talks to raise at a $100B valuation, up from $61B just two months ago (Reuters). Boring Company eyes ~$4B raise at a $20B valuation vs. $5.7B in 2022 (WSJ). Kuwait struck a $16B deal selling a 49% stake in its national oil pipeline to Blackstone, KKR, and Brookfield (FT). Unicredit signaled progress on a Commerzbank takeover (Bloomberg).
