UPDATE

Flash PMIs are too strong for stocks

Sep 23, 2026 · 09:53 AM ET
Macro

Flash PMIs for September came in very strong, with the composite index at 58.4, up from 56 in August and well above the consensus of 55.3. S&P notes the U.S. economy "continues to boom," with output growing at the fastest pace in 5 years, implying annualized growth of roughly 5% for the month and ~4% for Q3 overall. However, the blowout growth numbers are driving yields higher, creating a net headwind for stocks, while supply chain bottlenecks are among the worst in 20 years excluding COVID, and input cost inflation has hit its highest level since October 2022.

Market Outlook

The macro read is mixed-to-bearish for equities in the near term. While economic growth is booming, the too-hot PMI data is pushing yields higher, which is a headwind for risk sentiment — stocks right now would actually prefer slightly cooler growth if it meant lower borrowing costs. Corporate margins are also at risk as input cost inflation is surging faster than selling price inflation, compressing profitability. The key catalyst to watch is how the bond market digests the PMI data and where yields settle, as that will set the tone for equity risk appetite for the rest of the session.

Sector Watch

No specific sector commentary provided. Broad macro pressure is most acute for rate-sensitive sectors, as surging yields driven by the blowout PMI data create a general headwind across equities. Supply chain bottlenecks near 20-year highs (ex-COVID) are a particular concern for goods-producing and manufacturing sectors. Margin risk from rising input costs outpacing selling price inflation is a watch item for corporate earnings broadly.
