RATES & FED

Treasuries caught a modest bid Tuesday as Brent pulled back from its morning highs, with yields down 1–3 basis points across the curve. The relief in oil is tied to tentative signals out of the Middle East that tensions around the Strait of Hormuz could ease. No Fed speakers of note today.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The equal-weight S&P outperforming cap-weight is a constructive signal — this isn't just mega-cap momentum carrying the tape. The Iran situation is the key macro wildcard, and our read is that both sides have incentive to step back; a return to the June MOU framework would likely be acceptable to both Tehran and Washington, which is the bull case for energy and risk broadly. Eyes firmly on the critical tech earnings queue this week — CRWV, SMCI, CSCO, COHR, and AMAT will collectively say a lot about whether the data center buildout thesis remains intact. Wednesday's US CPI print for July is the other must-watch event.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Gulf state governments are increasingly accepting that Iran will retain control over the Strait of Hormuz, with many preferring that status quo to renewed conflict (WSJ). Iran's new leadership simultaneously signals a harder line toward Washington (WSJ, FT) — a tension the market is navigating in real time.

- The BOJ remains caught between two forces: elevated inflation and a weak yen argue for rate hikes, while fiscal imbalances and rising JGB yields suggest the bank may need balance sheet tools to contain the bond market (Reuters).

- Australia's RBA held rates steady as expected but flagged persistent inflation concerns and left the door open to another hike this year (Reuters, WSJ). South Korea's monetary officials are similarly signaling further hikes as resilient growth creates upside inflation risk (Bloomberg).

- On crypto regulation, after the Clarity Act failed in Congress, the SEC and CFTC are reportedly preparing a set of pro-crypto rules (Politico).

COMPANY NEWS

ARMK (Aramark) delivered solid FQ3 results — EPS of 52 cents beat the Street's 49 cents (+29% Y/Y), with 9% organic revenue growth topping the consensus +7.3%. The company landed an initial AI data center deal in Texas and raised its FY organic sales outlook to +9–10% from +8–9%.

CAH (Cardinal Health) posted a large FQ4 EPS beat at $2.91 vs. the Street's $2.42, partly boosted by IEEPA tariff refunds — though even stripping those out, EPS of ~$2.60 would have been ~18 cents ahead. FY27 EPS guidance midpoint of $12.50 tops consensus of $12.06.

ONON (On Running) delivered a rare sales miss and trimmed its full-year revenue outlook — a potential read-through benefiting NKE, DECK, and LULU on the competitive landscape.

SE (Sea Limited) reported Q2 revenue of $7.78B (+48.1% Y/Y) well above the Street's $7.1B, but EBITDA margins disappointed at 11.77% — missing by roughly 120 basis points — as Sales & Marketing expense surged 64.5% and credit loss provisions jumped 71.5%.

NVDA (Nvidia) confirmed its $500B+ AI infrastructure financing deal — viewed near-term as a net positive since it accelerates the data center boom without burdening Nvidia's balance sheet, though critics see it as circular financing masking thin free cash flow. Separately, Nvidia is pushing to launch a world-leading open-weight AI model to drive chip demand (The Information).

INTC (Intel) priced 210.5M shares at $95, raising $20B — well above its initial $15B target (WSJ).

HIMS (Hims & Hers) beat Q2 on both EBITDA ($60.3MM vs. $47MM Street) and sales ($753.2MM vs. $699MM Street), but paradoxically cut full-year EBITDA guidance to $275–325MM from $275–350MM.

ACM (AECOM) badly missed FQ3 — EBITDA came in at -$8MM vs. the Street's +$263MM, with sales down 14% to $3.5B vs. $4.3B expected. FY EPS guide slashed to $3.95–4.15 from $5.90–6.10 due to a $327MM pre-tax loss on a delayed construction management project.

RIOT (Riot Platforms) struck a $9.1B compute deal with Anthropic (Bloomberg). Anthropic itself is targeting a September/October IPO and has been addressing investor concerns around Chinese open-weight models and White House tensions in pre-deal roadshows (WSJ).

SPG (Simon Property) posted real estate FFO of $3.29 vs. $3.22 Street and raised full-year FFO guidance to $13.20–13.30 from $13.10–13.25, citing broad leasing demand and accelerating traffic.

UPWK (Upwork) beat Q2 estimates but cut full-year revenue guidance to $730–750MM (from $760–790MM) and EBITDA to $225–235MM (from $250–260MM) as management acknowledged "lower-complexity work continues to shift toward automation."

PSX / KMI / DINO are partnering on a ~$5B Western Gateway Pipeline to move fuel from Texas to California and Arizona (WSJ).

AAPL (Apple) — a Monday analyst downgrade citing no high-end all-glass iPhone was rebutted by a Bloomberg report confirming the product remains on track for 2027.

BMY (Bristol-Myers) and AstraZeneca were reportedly close to a deal that fell apart after Astra shares sold off on the news and the UK government signaled opposition (FT).
