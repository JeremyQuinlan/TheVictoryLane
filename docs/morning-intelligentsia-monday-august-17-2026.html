COMPANY NEWS

NVDA (Nvidia) has scaled back its financial backstop for OpenAI's Ohio data center from $250B to $120B (~5GW of power vs. the original 10GW plan), though it may still provide ~$350B in vendor chip financing to OpenAI — an aggregate commitment potentially reaching ~$470B. Separately, Nvidia is in talks to invest up to $3B in SB Energy, the SoftBank-backed developer building the Ohio facility (WSJ, The Information).

PYPL (PayPal) is in active talks to sell itself to a consortium including Stripe and Advent, with negotiations now above the initial $60.50/share proposal (WSJ). Stripe is simultaneously acquiring AI model-switching startup OpenRouter for over $7B, months after it raised at a ~$1.3B valuation (Bloomberg).

AAPL (Apple) was warned by Commerce Secretary Lutnick to avoid Chinese memory chips, but the company may proceed with purchases for non-US products amid acute supply shortages (WSJ).

BABA (Alibaba) is selling its Lingxi Games subsidiary to Trustar Capital for over $2B (Reuters), while its Qwen open-weight AI models have surpassed 3B downloads in six months, making it the world's most-downloaded model family — ahead of Meta and Google (Bloomberg).

MSGS (Madison Square Garden Sports) filed SEC paperwork to split into two entities separating the Knicks and Rangers (WSJ).

DSX/GNK: Diana Shipping withdrew its acquisition offer for Genco, citing the Genco board's "unrealistic" position, though DSX will remain GNK's largest shareholder.

Argenx rallied in European trading after reporting additional positive trial data for autoimmune drug Vyvgart (Bloomberg). AstraZeneca halted a late-stage trial for cancer drug volrustomig after determining it was unlikely to meet its primary endpoints (Reuters).

Anthropic reported Q2 revenue exceeding $11.5B — up ~14-fold year-over-year — with positive adjusted operating income. The S1 could drop within weeks ahead of an October IPO targeting a valuation anchored to $190–200B in 2028 revenue at a ~40–50x multiple (Bloomberg, Reuters).
