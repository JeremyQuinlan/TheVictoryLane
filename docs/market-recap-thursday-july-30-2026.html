MARKET OUTLOOK

Thursday's monster rally was narrower than the headline numbers suggest — the equal-weight S&P finished in the red, which tells you the breadth story isn't as clean as the index gains imply. The dominant narrative ("it was all just Situational Awareness forced selling, not fundamentals") is seductive but dangerous; AI is not in the clear. PCE is still running well above target, oil is an inflationary wildcard given the Middle East, tariffs are increasingly permanent (WSJ), and a Fed chair whose credibility is eroding won't be able to hold the long end down. Q2 earnings are solid but the beat-and-raise bar was simply higher than in Q1, which explains the muted aggregate reaction. Stay selective — chip infrastructure and capital goods have earned their moves; software/services and insurance have not bottomed.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The US conducted roughly two hours of airstrikes against Iranian IRGC targets Wednesday night in retaliation for a missile attack on American forces in Jordan (Reuters). CENTCOM head Adm. Brad Cooper has a 10-14 day intensive strike plan on the table, but Trump may balk given munition shortage concerns (WSJ).

- Pakistan says US-Iran negotiations are ongoing despite the violence (Bloomberg). The most likely endgame: Trump loses interest, Iran tacitly controls Hormuz, and individual countries cut side deals with Tehran — deeply negative for US strategic interests but a scenario markets can ultimately look past.

- Poland is blaming Russia for firing a missile into its territory early Thursday (CNN).

- China hinted at supportive economic measures but signaled minimal appetite for a major stimulus package (Bloomberg, WSJ).

- The White House is reportedly considering a $100K fee for foreign students seeking to work in the US after graduation — another self-defeating policy in a long line of them (WSJ).

- Companies are increasingly pricing tariffs as permanent, anticipating Trump's trade agenda outlasting his presidency (WSJ).

COMPANY NEWS

EME (EMCOR) demolished Q2 estimates — EPS of $9.06 vs. Street $7.20, revenue $5.15B vs. $4.71B — and raised full-year EPS guidance to $32-33.25 from $28.25-29.75.

PWR (Quanta) posted Q2 EPS of $4.24 vs. $3.24 and revenue of $9.55B vs. $8.5B, with backlog jumping to $53.4B. Full-year EBITDA guide lifted to $4.09-4.21B from $3.49-3.65B.

BMY (Bristol-Myers Squibb) delivered Q2 EPS of $2.04 vs. Street $1.60 and sales of $12.97B vs. $11.73B, with the growth portfolio up 15% and Eliquis surging 22%. Full-year EPS raised to $6.75-7.00 from $6.05-6.35.

MSFT (Microsoft) posted strong results and a robust guide, helping lead the AI infrastructure rally. No specific EPS figures provided pre-close.

WCC (Wesco) reported Q2 EPS of $4.57 vs. $3.97, with 13% organic growth, data center sales up 45%, backlog up 60%, and raised full-year guidance.

MA (Mastercard) delivered Q2 EPS of $5.04 vs. $4.77, sales up 14% to $9.27B, operating margins expanded 120 basis points to 61.1%. Full-year sales and expense guide raised.

HII (Huntington Ingalls) reported Q2 EPS of $5.27 vs. $3.81, revenue $3.41B vs. $3.15B, with shipbuilding margins rising 100 basis points. Guidance raised.

META tumbled after earnings despite broadly decent results — market read the report as a disappointment.

FICO was among the weakest SPX names after its earnings print.

KKR reported adjusted net income of $1.63/share vs. $1.42, with AUM growing 16% to $796B and $34B in fresh capital raised in Q2.

MKTX (MarketAxess) agreed to be acquired by ICE (Intercontinental Exchange) for $167/share cash in a deal valued at approximately $6B.

GOOGL (Google) will provide billions in financing guarantees and chip purchase credits for an Anthropic data center being built in Texas (WSJ).

FISV (Fiserv) is facing intensified pressure from activist Jana Partners, which is pushing for a formal strategic review of the entire company, not just select assets (Reuters).

Schneider Electric reported Q2 organic revenue growth of +17% vs. Street +10.5%, citing triple-digit data center demand growth. Guidance raised.

Rolls-Royce boosted full-year operating profit guidance to GBP 4.7-4.9B from GBP 4.0-4.2B on strong Civil, Defense, and Power Systems demand.

Shell reported Q2 adjusted net income of $9.83B vs. Street $8.6B and announced a new $3B buyback.

AGCO missed on EPS/revenue and cut full-year EPS guidance to $5.50-5.75 from $6.00, citing farmer caution on equipment purchases amid input cost uncertainty.

META and CHRW (C.H. Robinson) were notable earnings-linked underperformers on the session.
