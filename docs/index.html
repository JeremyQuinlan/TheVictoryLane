VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTHROPIC Anthropic Other

A cluster of post-Friday-close headlines hit Anthropic hard: the IPO timeline slipped to November from October, year-end ARR guidance came in above $100-110 billion versus bull hopes of $120 billion or higher, and the company is planning to raise $100 billion-plus at a valuation above $2 trillion. Separately, Anthropic is considering launching a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture momentum lost to OpenAI's Astra launch, and the company has told investors it targets 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

A negative Financial Times article Friday highlighted that efforts to offload loans tied to Oracle's massive 'Project Jupiter' data center in New Mexico have 'hit a wall,' with poor price action in the associated debt raising fresh concerns about the company's data center financing exposure. This adds to the broader Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liability risk theme.

◆ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit against its Warner Bros. Discovery merger deal. A resolution could require Paramount to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central, potentially clearing a significant regulatory overhang on the deal.

◆ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

The Paramount-Warner merger faces an advanced settlement negotiation with California's AG that could require divestitures including Comedy Central and editorial independence commitments for CNN. Resolution of the regulatory dispute would represent a meaningful step toward closing the deal.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure Friday after Meritage Hospitality Group, one of its largest franchisees, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Franchisee financial distress signals stress in the quick-service restaurant operating environment and could weigh on Wendy's royalty streams and unit count.

◆ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. This reflects ongoing retailer-supplier tension around pricing power and could signal broader private label or supplier negotiation dynamics across the consumer staples space.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk Activist Entry

Shareholders are pressuring Novo Nordisk to diversify away from its concentrated GLP-1 exposure through M&A. The pressure reflects investor concern about single-category risk even as GLP-1 demand remains robust, and could accelerate deal activity from the Danish pharma giant.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner capsule to be used on ten or more new missions into low-Earth orbit, representing a meaningful commercial and reputational rehabilitation opportunity for the troubled spacecraft program after its high-profile difficulties.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment, announced Friday night alongside the Anthropic IPO news. While a modest contract, it positions Accenture at the center of the emerging AI governance and safety ecosystem.

▲ NSCL Nscale Other

London-based neocloud firm Nscale filed its S1 publicly Friday afternoon and aims to list on the NYSE under the ticker NSCL. The IPO adds to the wave of AI infrastructure companies seeking public market access and will be watched as a read-through on investor appetite for neocloud valuations.

Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically ratcheted up missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia, with Iran warning the US against resuming military actions and the State Department cautioning Americans in the region about potential for unforeseen escalation. Ukraine also launched a massive drone strike on Russia including Moscow, adding to global risk-off pressure heading into the week.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction, with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. The BOJ's recent rate hike was slightly more dovish than expected, but paradoxically triggered macro concern by pressuring the yen lower and rekindling fears Japan could sell Treasuries to defend its currency. Markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over the coming weeks, with Kansas City Fed President Schmid warning Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all current macro headwinds trace back to oil, meaning even a modest geopolitical improvement at the UN General Assembly could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump/Xi summit Thursday could produce select tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion worth of goods, including potentially eliminating Chinese tariffs on US LNG. Saudi Arabia is actively increasing ship-to-ship oil transfers to compensate for supply shortages, and Pentagon escorts have pushed Hormuz oil shipments to a six-month high.

BEAR CASE

Iran continues to tighten its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis in Yemen, while the weekend saw a dramatic escalation in attacks on Saudi Arabia. The economy and corporate earnings have held up over the summer, but the latest spike in energy prices will begin working through the system quickly, risking demand destruction. On top of geopolitics, the AI industry faces a wave of negative sentiment including OpenAI's staggering projected negative free cash flow of $280 billion from 2026 to 2030, rising Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liabilities up roughly $300 billion in the last 12 months alone, and a softening Anthropic IPO narrative.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bearish into the week given net negative weekend news flow across AI and geopolitics. The key catalysts to watch are the Trump/Xi summit Thursday and the UN General Assembly, where any Middle East diplomatic progress would be the most powerful bullish wildcard — but Adam views that as wishful thinking more than a base case.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a wave of negative sentiment heading into the week — rising off-balance sheet liabilities up $300 billion in 12 months, OpenAI's massive projected cash burn, and a softening Anthropic IPO narrative are all headwinds. Energy is the macro swing factor: geopolitical escalation in the Middle East is driving diesel and motor oil price spikes, rationing at US retailers, and supply concerns that could begin hitting corporate margins and consumer confidence if sustained. Memory/semiconductors have a mild positive from Acer's CEO noting Chinese memory supply is easing component shortages and capping prices, though CXMT's announcement of next-gen mass production adds competitive pressure on Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Auto sector is under pressure following Volkswagen's large downside preannouncement — full-year operating margins now guided to 'up to 1%' versus prior guidance of 4 to 5.5% and Street estimates of 4.1%, driven by a Porsche goodwill impairment, weak China demand, and restructuring charges.
