RATES & FED

Treasuries caught a solid bid Tuesday with yields sliding 3–4 basis points across the curve, helped by a JOLTs print that came in softer than feared and Iran-linked oil weakness. The combination of cooling energy prices and a less-hot labor print is giving the Fed room to breathe, and markets are reading the macro tea leaves constructively heading into Friday's jobs report.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The SPX is at fresh all-time highs after an aggressive four-session ramp driven heavily by MSFT, AMZN, CAT, and PLTR — and the near-term conversation is getting panicked, with plenty of players clearly underpositioned for this move. The next major catalysts don't land until Nvidia earnings (Wed 8/26 AMC) and Warsh's Jackson Hole address (Fri 8/28 morning), but July economic data — Friday's jobs report and next week's CPI — will matter in the interim. Optical names are spiking on the Chinese transceiver ban draft (Reuters), adding another tailwind. The risk is that the rally becomes self-reinforcing before those big macro checkpoints arrive, leaving latecomers chasing.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Treasury Secretary Bessent told CNBC a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz may arrive within 24–48 hours, which sent oil sharply lower and helped pull yields down alongside it (CNBC).

- Saudi Aramco's Q2 adjusted net income came in at $33.4B vs. the Street's $31.1B, though free cash flow disappointed on a large working capital build; the company cited its East-West Pipeline and strategic storage as key to maintaining business continuity despite Hormuz disruptions (Saudi Aramco).

- The White House is drafting a ban on Chinese optical transceiver imports for data centers, sparking a sharp spike in optical stocks including CIEN, COHR, and ANET (Reuters).

- Leading PC OEMs — HP, Asus, and Acer — have quietly begun sourcing memory from China's CXMT amid a global supply shortage, though only in limited quantities and for non-US markets (Nikkei). CXMT is also reportedly preparing high-end smartphone memory chips on par with SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron by year-end (Bloomberg).

- AI now thoroughly dominates US economic activity — spending, hiring, and capital markets — and has transformed the economy beyond recognition (WSJ).

COMPANY NEWS

CAT (Caterpillar) delivered the blowout of the day: Q2 EPS of $8.17 demolished the Street's $6.17, with MP&E sales jumping 25% to $19.58B, adjusted operating margins spiking 430 basis points Y/Y to 21.9% (aided by $392MM in IEEPA tariff refunds), a soaring backlog, and increased full-year sales guidance.

PLTR (Palantir) posted Q2 EPS of 41c vs. the Street's 35c, on revenue of $1.935B vs. $1.808B expected, with a 62% operating margin vs. 60% consensus. Full-year sales guidance raised to $8.15–8.158B from $7.65–7.662B.

SNAP reported very strong Q2 results: revenue $1.598B vs. $1.539B, EBITDA $249.6MM vs. $192MM, DAUs 493MM vs. 488MM, and free cash flow of $121MM vs. a paltry $10.4MM consensus. Q3 EBITDA guide of $325MM is roughly inline.

SPOT (Spotify) Q2 operating income of €655MM beat the €636MM consensus on better gross and operating margins, but revenue of €4.77B missed slightly vs. €4.79B, and Q3 MAU guidance of 788MM trails the 793MM Street estimate.

ADM (Archer-Daniels) posted Q2 EPS of $1.84 vs. $1.49 expected and raised full-year EPS guidance to $5.15–5.60 from $4.15–4.70, with all three business units contributing.

PFE (Pfizer) beat Q2 EPS/revenue at 77c/$15B vs. 68c/$14.4B; Eliquis revenue of $2.42B crushed the $2.09B estimate. Full-year EPS and sales guidance both raised.

APTV (Aptiv) trimmed full-year EPS guidance to $5.60–5.80 from $5.70–6.10, citing a "dynamic macroeconomic landscape" and adverse customer mix.

AMZN — Jeff Bezos filed to sell 15 million Amazon shares, worth approximately $4B (CNBC).

GOOGL/AVGO — Google and Broadcom reportedly structured a $200B financing scheme for Anthropic to build data centers and purchase TPUs (FT).

W (Wayfair) beat Q2 EPS/revenue at 95c/$3.51B vs. 92c/$3.47B with free cash flow of $301MM vs. $267MM; Q3 sales and margin guidance set above expectations.

ZBRA (Zebra Technologies) posted Q2 EPS of $6.35 vs. the Street's $4.37, aided by tariff refunds, with higher full-year guidance.

WHR (Whirlpool) missed Q2 EPS at -21c vs. -5c expected; full-year EPS guidance cut to $2.50–3.00 from $3.00–3.50 on higher interest expense.
