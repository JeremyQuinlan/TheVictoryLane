MARKET OUTLOOK

The rotation story is becoming impossible to ignore — tech and semis are getting sold hard while equal-weight S&P is ripping, and this dynamic is accelerating, not fading. The core anxiety is structural: AI-linked debt issuance has already hit ~$270B this year, nearly double all of 2025 (WSJ), and if capital markets buckle under the supply tsunami, capex assumptions for the whole ecosystem get dialed back fast. GLW's underwhelming Q3 guide today was a perfect example of how the bar for pick-and-shovel names has become almost impossibly high. The broadening is real and constructive — healthcare, staples, insurance, payments, and housing are all working — but tech needs to find a floor before the overall market can sustainably push higher.

COMPANY NEWS

AAPL is making a fresh push into the smart home market with new products including an Apple TV 4K box, HomePod mini, and a Siri AI hub (Bloomberg). Separately, Apple launched "Apple Upgrade," a leasing program in partnership with KLAR (Klarna) covering iPhone, Apple Watch, Mac, and iPad.

AMT posted AFFO/share of $2.71 vs. the Street's $2.73, but EBITDA and revenue beat, tone was upbeat ("record leasing activity at CoreSite"), and full-year AFFO guidance was raised to $11.00–$11.17 from $10.90–$11.07.

BA missed on EPS (-$0.76 vs. Street -$0.28) but revenue rose 8% to $24.56B (vs. $24.26B est.), free cash flow came in at positive ~$600MM vs. Street's negative $333MM forecast, and the backlog swelled to $715B from $695B.

CARR beat on EPS (86c vs. 82c) with 3% organic sales growth vs. Street's -2% estimate. Data center orders surged more than 300%, commercial HVAC up ~65%, total orders up ~40%. Full-year EPS guide raised to $2.90 from $2.80.

CLS (Celestica) crushed Q2 EPS at $2.54 vs. Street $2.30, with sales up 62% to $4.7B. Full-year guide lifted to $11.30/$20.5B from $10.15/$19B, and the preliminary 2027 sales outlook implies north of $34B vs. Street at $27.6B.

GLW (Corning) Q3 guidance landed soft — sales of $4.9–$5.0B and EPS of 85–89c vs. Street's $5.0B and 86c — not a disaster fundamentally, but not what an anxious market needed from a key AI infrastructure name.

KO beat Q2 EPS at 97c vs. 93c, with 6% organic growth (Street +3.6%) and 90 basis points of operating margin expansion. Full-year guide nudged higher.

META has told bankers it will need to raise hundreds of billions to fund its AI buildout, is in talks with BlackRock and others, and announced a $14B El Paso data center with BlackRock this morning (WSJ). Separately, Meta faces massive litigation exposure on teen/child safety (WSJ).

NVDA is leasing a $50B Texas data center — planning to sublease to neoclouds to sustain chip demand (FT). Chinese AI firms including Moonshot (planning Kimi K4) remain dependent on Nvidia Blackwell chips for model training (The Information).

PYPL beat EPS at $1.38 vs. Street $1.27, with TPV up 9% FXN to $486.4B. Low-end of full-year EPS guide raised, though some will be disappointed the raise wasn't more aggressive.

RCL posted Q2 EPS of $4.21 vs. Street $3.98. Full-year guide raised to $17.73–$17.87 from $17.10–$17.50, though management acknowledged a modest bookings headwind.

SHW beat Q2 EPS at $3.70 vs. Street $3.51, with sales up 7.5% to $6.78B. Full-year EPS guide raised to $11.80–$12.20 from $11.50–$11.90.

UPS beat Q2 EPS at $1.76 vs. Street $1.67, with revenue of $22.8B (~$1B above consensus) driven by aggressive pricing — US revenue per piece up 9.3%, international up 18.9%. Full-year sales guide raised to $91.2B.

V (Visa) plans to cut approximately 7% of its workforce (~2,600 employees) in an efficiency drive (Bloomberg).

WBD/PSKY merger is being priced at roughly 50/50 odds by Wall Street (Barron's), with some Warner executives privately hoping a multi-state antitrust lawsuit derails the deal entirely (Variety).
