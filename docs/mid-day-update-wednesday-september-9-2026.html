RATES & FED

Treasuries are selling off on the back of surging oil prices and a disappointing Treasury buyback — the "up to $6B" figure came in above initial guidance of "at least $4B" but well short of the $10B+ the market wanted. Higher energy costs are keeping upward pressure on yields, giving central banks little room to pivot, with the ECB up Thursday and the FOMC and BOJ both meeting next week.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Oil above $100 is the dominant force right now, and as long as the Middle East situation stays stalemated — Iran escalating rather than negotiating — there's no credible path to yield relief. Central banks will keep tightening, which caps the upside for equities, particularly in rate-sensitive and consumer-facing names. Tech is holding up better than the tape suggests, with AI enthusiasm (Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Astra demand surge) providing a genuine offset. The Treasury buyback disappointment stings at the margin but isn't the story — energy and geopolitics are. Until Brent cools, risk assets stay defensive.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The US struck five Iranian tankers Tuesday, sinking one, in retaliation for a missile attack on an American navy warship; Tehran responded with a barrage against a Pentagon base in Jordan — 18 of 20 missiles intercepted, no casualties (CNN, WSJ).

- Iran is responding to intensifying US economic pressure with escalation rather than diplomacy, and is increasingly turning to cryptocurrency to circumvent tightening American sanctions (NYT, FT).

- Saudi Arabia vowed to defend itself against Houthi attacks while leaving the door open to a diplomatic solution; Pakistan pledged to honor its defense pact with the Kingdom if attacked (Al Arabiya, Bloomberg).

- Iraq is seeking a significant increase in its OPEC+ production quota as the cartel conducts a quota review (Bloomberg).

- The White House escalated its trade war with Canada, warning of potential bans on certain Canadian alcohol, motorcycles, and dairy products effective September 29 (Washington Post).

COMPANY NEWS

META formally launched Muse Tuesday, a personal AI agent "that understands your goals and works 24/7 to get things done for you" — described as essentially OpenAI's operator-style product aimed at mainstream consumers with a heavy security focus (TechCrunch). The launch is powering a positive pivot in the AI narrative across tech broadly.

ASO (Academy Sports) beat on Q2 EPS thanks to tariff refunds, with sales roughly inline and comps slightly light; full-year EPS and free cash flow guidance raised, comp/sales forecast held. Management's tone was notably more constructive than recent updates from DKS and ONON.

CHWY (Chewy) posted Q2 EBITDA of $226.7MM vs. the Street's $212MM, with revenue +7.3% to $3.33B vs. $3.31B expected; full-year guide raised but management flagged margin pressure in Q3.

SIG (Signet) delivered Q2 EPS of $2.19 vs. $1.73 expected (tariff refunds helped), comps +2.2% vs. +1.7% expected; full-year EPS guide lifted to $10.45–$12.15 from $9.20–$11, and the YTD buyback represents ~12% of market cap.

SUNB (Sunbelt Rentals) beat Q2 EBITDA at $1.315B vs. $1.245B, revenue +11.2% to $3.115B; full-year revenue guide raised to +6–9% from +4.5–7.5%, EBITDA now $4.92–$5.12B vs. $4.85–$5.05B prior.

CASY (Casey's) beat FQ1 EPS at $7.37 vs. $6.77, though EBITDA of $485MM barely cleared $480MM consensus; softness in Grocery & General Merchandise comps and gas gallons; full-year guidance reiterated.

SFD (Smithfield Foods) preannounced a sharp FQ3 miss — operating income of $115–175MM vs. consensus ~$296MM — citing hog market spread compression and lower hog prices.

KMB (Kimberly-Clark) warned at Barclays that Q3 will fall short on three North America headwinds: a product launch delay, distributor disruption in Professional, and higher freight costs; 2027–2028 plan unchanged.

SYY (Sysco) reaffirmed F27 guidance, unveiled a $500MM AI-powered efficiency program targeting F29, and raised long-term targets to sales +4–7% and EPS +9–11%.

ADI (Analog Devices) is acquiring Alif Semiconductor for $1.35B in cash.

CSR/IRT — Independence Realty (IRT) is acquiring Centerspace (CSR) at 3.8 IRT shares per CSR share, creating an ~$8.1B enterprise value REIT with 44K+ apartment units; deal is expected to be ~5% accretive to IRT's 2027 Core FFO.

ROKU — The DOJ is launching a broader review of Fox's proposed $22B acquisition (Semafor).

GLW (Corning) said at Citi TMT that Q3 is tracking at the high end or slightly above July guidance, adding "we expect Q4 to be bigger than Q3."

V (Visa) told Goldman Sachs that consumer spending remains "strong and stable" both domestically and internationally.
