Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying to start the week with the S&P up 49 points or 65 basis points, Nasdaq up 110 basis points, and Russell 2000 up 47 basis points. European indices are up 85-120 basis points led by industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks, while Asian markets also closed higher across the board. The primary driver appears to be diplomatic optimism ahead of the UN General Assembly in New York, with Trump signaling openness to meeting Iran's president, despite a weekend filled with negative news including Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine's largest drone strike on Russia, and a deluge of troubling AI headlines.

Treasury yields are down 2-3 basis points across the curve, providing a modest tailwind. Fed expectations are holding fairly steady with the market pricing in roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of increases priced in for 2027. Fed Presidents Schmid and Kashkari both made hawkish remarks recently, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices, and the Treasury plans to issue over one trillion dollars in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above its 24 percent target share of marketable debt.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is the primary bull catalyst — even a small de-escalation in the Iran conflict could trigger an outsized drop in oil prices, which would lower yields, reduce inflation, ease margin pressure, and boost consumer confidence in a chain reaction of positive effects. China and Pakistan are both reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing international pressure toward a resolution. Saudi Arabia is also working to increase ship-to-ship oil transfers to offset supply shortages, and the Trump-Xi summit later this week could yield modest tariff reductions on roughly 30 billion dollars worth of goods.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was overwhelmingly negative — Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia dramatically escalated, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike ever on Russia including Moscow, and a wave of troubling AI headlines hit after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. The geopolitical landscape remains deeply concerning with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies through Hormuz, aligned militias, and the Houthis, while oil tanker costs are surging and motor oil is being rationed at US retailers. US-China trade truce extension talks stalled and Washington is expected to hike the aggregate tariff rate on Chinese imports back to pre-Supreme Court IEEPA levels in coming weeks, adding another macro headwind.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish on a session basis purely driven by geopolitical optionality — if any diplomatic progress emerges at the UN General Assembly around Iran and the Middle East, oil could drop sharply and unleash a cascade of positive macro effects. The key catalyst to watch today is any headline out of the UN General Assembly regarding US-Iran diplomacy or a potential Trump-Iran presidential meeting.

Sector Watch

AI infrastructure and data center picks-and-shovels stocks are outperforming in Europe this morning despite a wave of negative weekend headlines for the sector including delayed Anthropic IPO, poor OpenAI free cash flow projections of negative 280 billion dollars through 2030, and Oracle data center debt issues. Big Tech AI off-balance-sheet liability exposure has risen roughly 300 billion dollars in just the last 12 months, creating a growing risk overhang. Chinese memory giant CXMT announced its next-gen platform has entered mass production, which alongside Acer CEO comments about Chinese memory supply easing component shortages, is a modest headwind for pricing power at Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Energy and oil tanker stocks face a complex setup — Brent is sliding 170 basis points to around 102 dollars and ten cents but underlying supply constraints remain severe.
