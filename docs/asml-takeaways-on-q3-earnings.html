ASML: takeaways on Q3 earnings

Oct 14, 2026 · 03:56 AM ET

Here's your earnings flash on ASML. The Dutch semiconductor equipment giant just posted a very strong third quarter, and the headline is a massive earnings beat. ASML earned EU12.93 per share against the Street's estimate of EU10.71 — that's a significant outperformance driven almost entirely by margin strength. Gross margins came in roughly 140 basis points better than expected, and operating margins blew the doors off at 44.4% versus the consensus call of 41.8%. Revenue was essentially in line, coming in just a touch light at EU11.66 billion against the EU11.7 billion estimate — so essentially a non-issue. Looking into the fourth quarter, the guide is ahead of plan across the board. The sales midpoint is EU14.7 billion versus the Street at EU14 billion, and gross margin guidance of 57% tops consensus at 56.4%. The qualitative commentary from management is where things get really interesting. Customers are actively placing orders with visibility out to 2029 and even 2030, driven by their own conviction around the size and duration of AI-related capital investment cycles. The order book is growing sequentially every quarter this year. Now here's the one potential wrinkle worth watching: ASML is now investigating ways to expand production capacity for 2028 beyond the 30% increase they had previously communicated. Management said their current planned supply for both 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet customer demand. On one hand, that's a ringing endorsement of the demand environment. On the other hand, chip investors have a long memory when it comes to big capacity buildouts — it's the kind of language that historically triggers concern about future oversupply. So the setup is net bullish — the beat was real, the guide is solid, and the AI demand narrative is being validated by actual customer order behavior at the furthest time horizons we've seen. But keep one eye on how the broader semiconductor equipment names trade today, because capacity expansion language can cut both ways depending on market mood.
