RATES & FED

Treasuries have a modest bid this morning with yields down 1–2 basis points across the curve. The market is still pricing roughly 22 basis points worth of cuts across the Fed's final three meetings of the year. All eyes now turn to Warsh's Jackson Hole speech Friday, August 28 at 10am ET — the question is whether he softens his anti-QE rhetoric enough to put any downward pressure on the long end.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The near-term setup is cautiously constructive — yields ticking down and oil pulling back gives equities some breathing room into the weekend. But the structural problem hasn't changed: a tidal wave of AI-linked corporate issuance (see: Broadcom's ~$100B debt deal) and extreme fiscal imbalances are the dominant force pushing long rates higher, and Bessent's buyback expansion is, as the FT puts it, a "band-aid on a bullet hole." Warsh at Jackson Hole is the single most important catalyst of the next two weeks — if he signals any flexibility on the balance sheet, that's a genuine relief valve for rates. If he doesn't, the pressure on equities resumes. Watch PCE on Wednesday August 26 and NVDA/CRM earnings that same evening as the next major sentiment tests.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Trump is signaling an aggressive pivot away from large-scale military action against Iran, but that pivot limits his leverage and raises the risk Tehran decides to escalate kinetically instead (NYT). Bessent will hold a press conference Monday August 24 to unveil intensified financial pressure, though its effectiveness is questionable absent meaningful penalties on China, Iran's primary financial lifeline.

- America's allies are growing increasingly skeptical of the US security umbrella as the Pentagon struggles to shield Middle East shipping lanes from Iranian retaliation (WSJ).

- Piracy is surging in parallel — two hijackings in four days off Somalia as bad actors exploit the regional chaos (NYT).

- Ukraine is seeking Elon Musk's help to use Starlink for targeting Russian ballistic missile launchers (FT).

- Grain prices are rising following an escalation in Russia-Ukraine port strikes, adding another inflationary layer beyond oil.

COMPANY NEWS

ROST (Ross Stores) delivered the best retailer print of the July-end cycle: FQ2 EPS of $2.66 vs. the Street's $1.96. Even stripping out tariff refunds, EPS would have been $2.06 — still a beat — driven by comparable sales up 10%, roughly 240 basis points ahead of consensus. Q3 comp guidance of +6.5% (Street: +3.2%) and Q4 comps of +4.5% (Street: +2.5%). New store opening plan is being expanded.

AVGO (Broadcom) is in talks with lenders to raise ~$100B in AI chip-linked financing, with Broadcom potentially guaranteeing a senior tranche worth $60–70B, benefiting Anthropic and others (Bloomberg). This is arguably the single biggest structural driver of higher long-term yields right now.

FLO (Flowers Foods) missed on FQ2 EPS ($0.21 vs. $0.22 Street) and EBITDA ($111.3MM vs. $118.4MM), with sales down 4% to $1.193B vs. $1.228B expected. Full-year EPS guidance cut to $0.75–$0.85 from $0.80–$0.90.

AAPL (Apple) may pivot under new CEO John Ternus toward significantly higher R&D and capex spending as it races to stay competitive in AI (Barron's).

MU (Micron) CEO told CNBC that AI has permanently reduced the cyclicality of the memory industry, with DRAM far more central to the AI buildout than prior tech cycles.

NVDA (Nvidia) is in talks with South Korean AI chip designer Rebellions about a potential partnership or outright acquisition (Bloomberg).

ASTS (AST SpaceMobile) and SpaceX are pursuing a ~$6B spectrum acquisition from Grain Management to support space-based wireless phone services (Bloomberg).

ABT (Abbott) will pay $670MM to settle litigation related to specialty formulas for premature infants (WSJ).

MS (Morgan Stanley) has selected Dallas for a major expansion, anchoring a new $1.3B skyscraper (NY Post).

GM (General Motors) risks losing its title as the top-selling US auto OEM to Toyota, though the market may not care much as GM focuses on margins over volume (WSJ).

VIRT (Virtu) is exploring a sale of its agency brokerage and algo business, potentially worth $3.5–4B (Bloomberg).

PSKY/WBD — California AG Bonta said states suing to block the Paramount-Warner Bros. Discovery deal would consider settlement, but only with "robust structural remedies" (CNBC).

Samsung Electronics outlined $65–80B in shareholder returns for 2026 (Bloomberg, Reuters).
