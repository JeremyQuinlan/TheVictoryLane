Mid Morning Look 2026-08-28

Aug 28, 2026 · 10:31 AM ET

MACRO / FED

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh spoke at Jackson Hole signaling the inflation fight may not be over, stating financing conditions don't look restrictive and recent better price readings haven't convinced him of a sustained trend. He stopped short of committing to a September hike but made clear the 2% PCE target is "firm and fixed" — with three officials having already voted to hike last month and others open to joining. Markets repriced accordingly: 2-year Treasury yields jumped to 4.286% (highest since July 31), the dollar index hit session highs near 99.45, and gold sold off $65 to $4,599.

Chicago PMI was a major miss — 47.1 vs. 57.9 consensus, the weakest reading of 2026. UMich sentiment came in at 51.7 final for August (consensus 51.0), but the 1-year inflation outlook eased to 4.0% from the 4.3% preliminary read. The BLS preliminary benchmark revision shows nonfarm payrolls will be revised down 79,000 (0.1%) at the March 2026 level.

SECTOR

Dry Bulk Shipping (SBLK, GOGL, GNK, SHIP, NMM, SB): The Baltic Dry Index surged to its highest level since June 2, up 2.5% on the day to 3,186 — a 12.1% weekly gain. Capesize jumped 3.9% to 5,336, also up 17.2% on the week.

Application/Enterprise Software: Mixed picture across the group. ADSK beat Q2 and raised FY27 billings guidance to $8.575B–$8.65B and revenue to $8.295B–$8.345B, absorbing MaintainX dilution. WDAY posted in-line Q2 but guidance disappointed — FQ3 cRPO growth guided to ~11.5% and initial FY28 subscription growth of 11%, both slight downticks, though 2pts of margin expansion implied for FY28.

Data/Security Software: ESTC is the standout, surging 21% on strong Q1 acceleration and a raised FY27 revenue outlook of $1.998B–$2.010B. RBRK beat ARR estimates by $20M with a $25M raise to FY ARR guidance. S is the laggard, guiding FY26 adj. EPS $0.30–$0.32 vs. $0.34 consensus with Q3 revenue and EPS also below expectations.

Chemicals: SOLS jumped 16% after mutually terminating its $14.5B merger with ESI, with both shareholder bases preferring independence.

STOCKS

PYPL -11%: Advent and Stripe dropped their $60.50/share (~$53B) takeover bid after PayPal's board had previously deemed it inadequate. Removal of the deal premium is driving the sharp selloff.

MRVL -6%: Beat-and-raise quarter — Q2 adj. EPS $0.94 vs. $0.92 est., revenue +37% y/y to $2.74B, data center revenue +46% to $2.17B — wasn't enough after shares had already surged 184% YTD heading into the print. Weakness is dragging AI-adjacent names COHR and LITE lower in sympathy.

ESTC +21%: Q1 showed slight top-line acceleration with consistent 20% cRPO growth; management raised FY27 revenue guidance to $1.998B–$2.010B and adj. EPS to $3.29–$3.37, both above consensus.

AFRM +8%: Q4 revenue rose 33% y/y to $1.17B with GMV up 36% to $14.1B; net income hit $1.62B boosted by a deferred tax asset valuation allowance release, and operating income more than doubled y/y to $147M.

GAP +17%: Q2 EPS of $0.52 (ex-tariff refunds) beat the $0.48 estimate despite Old Navy top-line softness; stronger gross margins and SG&A discipline drove the upside surprise.

S -5%: FY26 adj. EPS guided to $0.30–$0.32 vs. $0.34 consensus; Q3 revenue and EPS guidance also missed, overshadowing a modest Q2 beat.

BNTX -7%: Terminated its Phase 2 trial of autogene cevumeran in colorectal cancer after the DSMB flagged a numerical overall survival imbalance in a specific patient population.

BTAI -73%: Entered an asset sale agreement with TEVA and commenced voluntary Chapter 11 proceedings across its subsidiaries.

IREN -8%: Q4 net loss of -$684M vs. net income of $176.9M a year ago as costs from the bitcoin-to-AI cloud pivot weighed heavily; 2026 compute capacity is largely sold out with late-stage 2027 talks underway.

QNRX +50%: Interim Phase 2/3 data showed QRX003 significantly improved disease severity in 4 of 6 Netherton syndrome patients at 12 weeks with no serious safety concerns; company also raised up to $50M from institutional investors.

SOLS +16%: Mutual termination of the $14.5B merger with ESI was cheered by the market, with shareholders on both sides preferring standalone strategies.

KLAR +4%: CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski purchased 692,506 shares at $14.37/share (~$9.9M), signaling insider conviction.
