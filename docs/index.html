Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +329 points/110 basis points, and Russell 2000 +47 basis points despite a weekend full of net negative news flow. Asian markets closed broadly higher led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while major Eurozone indices are up 85-120 basis points with strength in industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The primary driver of Monday's strength appears to be diplomatic optimism surrounding the UN General Assembly in New York this week, with hopes that Iran-related tensions could cool following Trump's openness to meeting Iran's president.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. Fed expectations remain hawkish — the market is pricing approximately 33 basis points of rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with roughly 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of increases priced into 2027. Fed Presidents Schmid and Kashkari both made hawkish remarks recently, warning that elevated inflation is broad-based and not solely driven by energy prices. Treasury also plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt and above the Treasury's own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is real — Trump has signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, and even incremental progress could cause an outsized decline in oil prices. A sustained drop in Brent from current levels near $102 would be a powerful macro tailwind, reducing inflation, easing monetary policy pressure, compressing yields, and improving corporate margins. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday also creates potential for modest tariff relief and an extension of the trade truce, adding another pillar of optimism. Stocks in Asia and Europe are already pricing in some of this hope with broad-based gains.

BEAR CASE

Nearly every piece of weekend news was net negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest-ever drone strike on Russia, Tehran warned of fierce retaliation if the US resumes military actions, and State Department issued a Middle East escalation warning. The AI complex faces fresh headwinds with a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming revenue projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt issues surfacing simultaneously. Monetary policy globally remains pointed hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month, and core inflation is running hot beyond just energy. UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism may prove to be wishful thinking given the depth of the current conflict.

Market Outlook

The directional lean is cautiously bullish on diplomatic optionality but fundamentally fragile given the macro backdrop. The key catalyst to watch today and this week is the UN General Assembly, specifically any signals of diplomatic progress on the Iran conflict — even a modest de-escalation could trigger a sharp drop in oil prices and unleash a cascade of bullish macro effects including lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, and improved consumer sentiment.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in European trading Monday. The AI complex broadly faces headwinds following a deluge of negative weekend headlines including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI's projected negative free cash flow of approximately $280 billion over the 2026-2030 period, and Oracle data center debt concerns. Big Tech AI off-balance-sheet liability exposure has surged approximately $300 billion in the last 12 months, creating a fresh risk for the sector. Energy stocks are underperforming in Europe despite Brent sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10. Industrials and basic resources are among the European outperformers on diplomatic optimism.
