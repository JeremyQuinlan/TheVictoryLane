RATES & FED

Treasuries are flat to up 1 basis point this morning with Fed expectations holding steady — markets are pricing roughly 32 basis points of hikes across the final three meetings of the year, with ~55% odds of a move on September 16. Fed's Daly (Reuters) said she was "completely supportive" of the recent decision to hold, while this week brought relief on several rate pressure points: cooler ADP data, inline Treasury refunding, progress on Hormuz, and US assistance on JPY intervention reducing Japan's incentive to sell Treasuries.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The dominant story today is a tech hangover — APP, HUBS, SNDK, and WDC all disappointed relative to elevated expectations, and the pain is spreading across both legacy software and AI-linked components. That's a real problem for a market that's been leaning heavily on tech to carry the load. The WSJ's report that Trump has been calling Fed Chair Warsh repeatedly is a genuine threat to monetary credibility and shouldn't be dismissed; Bessent's push for an expanded FIMA repo facility to support JPY intervention adds another layer of political encroachment on the Fed's turf. On the bullish side, Hormuz progress and Treasury refunding clarity are constructive, and the consumer earnings slate — BROS, ELF, DASH, MELI — was broadly strong. Friday's jobs report is the next big test.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran/Hormuz: Negotiations are between Tehran and Oman, not the US and Iran directly. Inbound ships may be forced to hug the Iranian coast, and while an initial ~60-day toll-free period is possible, Iran appears intent on eventual charges. Key open questions: US blockade, MOU reinstatement, Houthi activity in Yemen (they struck a Saudi tanker Wednesday), and IDF strikes in Southern Lebanon following Hezbollah ceasefire violations. The author's read: Trump lacks credible military options and Iran is hardline — don't expect a durable resolution.

COMPANY NEWS

APP (AppLovin) posted Q2 sales of $1.924B (+53% Y/Y) vs. the Street's $1.94B, with the Q3 guide midpoint of $2.07B also light vs. consensus $2.072B. Not outright bad, but the bar was sky-high and the stock is paying for it.

AXON beat Q2 EBITDA at $242MM vs. $220MM expected, revenue +35% to $904MM vs. $875MM, future contracted bookings +41% Y/Y to $15.1B, and raised full-year sales growth guidance to +32–34%.

HUBS (HubSpot) trimmed full-year FXN sales growth to +16% from +17%, with Q3 revenue guidance of $924–925MM well below the Street's $943MM.

SNDK (Sandisk) beat FQ4 EPS at $39.25 vs. $34.37 and added $14B to buyback authorization, but FQ1 sales guide of $10.3–10.8B fell short of the Street's $11.1B; EPS midpoint ~$45 vs. $45.57 consensus.

WDC (Western Digital) beat FQ4 EPS at $3.56 vs. $3.31, with FQ1 revenue guidance +42–49% Y/Y vs. Street +43% and EPS ~$4 vs. $3.83.

DASH (DoorDash) delivered EBITDA of $914MM vs. $843MM, revenue of $4.454B vs. $4.34B, and EBITDA margins up 40 basis points Q/Q.

MELI (Mercado Libre) reported revenue spiking 50% — fastest in four years — with GMV and TPV beating; operating margins dipped 550 basis points to 6.7% due to investment, but absolute operating income beat on revenue strength.

ELF posted blow-out Q2 sales of $479.4MM vs. $431MM; full-year EPS guide raised by ~20 cents, less than the ~$1 Q1 beat.

ALL (Allstate) reported Q2 EPS of $8.99 vs. $5.93, with combined ratio outperforming by ~600 basis points headline.

ALB (Albemarle) beat Q2 EBITDA at $858MM vs. $779MM and raised Specialties guidance.

MCK (McKesson) beat FQ1 EPS at $9.93 vs. $9.53 and raised full-year EPS guide to $44.20–$45.00 from $43.80–$44.60.
