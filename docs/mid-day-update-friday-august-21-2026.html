RATES & FED

Treasuries are under pressure with yields up 2–5 basis points across the curve, even after an attempted rally earlier in the session. The market is now pricing in 25 basis points of hikes this year, with odds of a September 16th increase sitting at roughly 40% — a mild hawkish drift on the day. All eyes are on Warsh's Jackson Hole address Friday the 28th, where any dovish balance sheet commentary could move rates meaningfully lower, though it would also stoke monetary independence concerns.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The core tension here is straightforward: the economy is growing robustly (flash PMIs confirmed it), but that strength is precisely what's keeping upward pressure on yields — and the White House has almost no credible tools to fight it. Bessent's buyback expansion this week read as panic, not policy, and the bond market saw through it instantly (FT). A real fiscal consolidation initiative remains politically impossible, which means the Fed is the only entity with genuine firepower. Until Warsh tips his hand at Jackson Hole, expect rates to remain the dominant headwind for risk assets. Gold, silver, and crypto outperforming today suggests the market is quietly hedging the "no good options" scenario.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Trump is pivoting hard away from large-scale military action against Iran, but that shift dramatically reduces his leverage to end the conflict and leaves open the question of how Washington responds if Tehran decides to escalate kinetically (NYT). Iran's president called for the war to end, though hardline factions — not the president — are actually driving policy in Tehran right now.

- America's allies are growing openly skeptical of the US security umbrella after the Pentagon demonstrated an inability to shield the Middle East from Iranian retaliation (WSJ).

- China's vice finance minister pledged additional fiscal support measures to bolster growth (Reuters), while Beijing separately signaled it won't alter its economic relationship with Iran — making Trump's "Economic D-Day" proclamation look like yet another empty threat.

- Trump signed a directive mandating the US conduct at least 1,000 commercial space launches annually by 2030, up from just 178 last year (Reuters).

COMPANY NEWS

ROST (Ross Stores) delivered a blowout FQ2 EPS of $2.66 vs. the Street's $1.96. Tariff refunds were a meaningful contributor, but even stripping those out EPS would have been approximately $2.06 — still a clean beat on robust comps. H2 guidance is moving higher.

BJ (BJ's Wholesale) beat expectations and is rallying on earnings, contributing to the broader consumer discretionary outperformance today.

FLO (Flowers Foods) missed on FQ2 EPS ($0.21 vs. Street $0.22) and EBITDA ($111.3MM vs. $118.4MM), with sales down 4% to $1.193B vs. the Street's $1.228B. Full-year EPS guidance cut to $0.75–$0.85 from $0.80–$0.90.

AAPL (Apple) — under new CEO John Ternus, the company is reportedly considering a meaningful step-up in R&D and capex spending to stay competitive in AI (Barron's).

AVGO (Broadcom) is in talks with lenders to arrange roughly $100B in AI chip financing, with Broadcom potentially guaranteeing a senior tranche worth $60–70B, structured to benefit Anthropic and others (Bloomberg).

MU (Micron) CEO told CNBC that AI has "fundamentally and irreversibly" changed the memory industry, making it structurally less cyclical, with DRAM playing a far more central role in the AI buildout than in prior tech cycles.

SPCX (SpaceX) and ASTS (AST SpaceMobile) are jointly pursuing spectrum assets owned by Grain Management valued at approximately $6B, critical for space-based wireless phone services (Bloomberg).

Samsung Electronics announced plans for $65–80B in shareholder returns in 2026 (Reuters, Bloomberg).

Monte dei Paschi launched bids for both Banca Generali and Banco BPM in a defensive move against a hostile approach from Intesa Sanpaolo (WSJ).

Anthropic's S-1 could drop as soon as end of August, with the company targeting an IPO size that matches or exceeds SpaceX's record raise (Bloomberg).
