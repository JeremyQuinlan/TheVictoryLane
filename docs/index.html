VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTHROPIC Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO was pushed to November from October, and year-end ARR projections of greater than $100-110 billion came in below some bull expectations of $120 billion or higher. The company is considering launching a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture momentum lost to OpenAI's Astra, while also announcing plans to have 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end and 10 gigawatts by end of 2027.

◆ OPENAI OpenAI Massive Capex

OpenAI expects negative free cash flow of approximately $280 billion during the 2026-2030 period as spending dramatically outpaces revenue, according to the Financial Times. However, OpenAI is also seeing renewed growth momentum following the launch of its Astra product, with reports of a sharp inflection higher in OpenAI growth versus Anthropic over recent weeks.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focusing on poor price action in debt tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center facility in New Mexico. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have reportedly 'hit a wall,' raising concerns about Oracle's data center financing strategy.

◆ PSKY Paramount Global M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit against its Warner Bros. Discovery merger deal. Under a potential settlement, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central.

◆ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Warner Bros. Discovery's pending merger with Paramount faces a potential resolution as Paramount enters advanced settlement talks with California regulators. The deal's path forward may require editorial independence commitments for CNN and divestitures of cable assets.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. This raises concerns about franchisee financial health and potential near-term headwinds to Wendy's unit economics and system growth.

▼ VW Volkswagen Guidance Cut

Volkswagen issued a large downside preannouncement Friday, cutting its 2026 operating margin guidance to up to 1% from prior guidance of 4 to 5.5% and well below the Street's 4.1% estimate. The miss was driven by a goodwill impairment at Porsche, a challenging auto market especially in China, and additional restructuring expenses, though adjusted for special items margins would be approximately 4%.

◆ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. This reflects broader retailer pressure on consumer staples suppliers and could signal further shelf space risks for premium-priced consumer brands.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner vehicle to be used on 10 or more new flights into low-Earth orbit. This represents a meaningful commercial rehabilitation opportunity for the troubled Starliner program following its high-profile difficulties.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk Activist Entry

Novo Nordisk is facing shareholder pressure to diversify away from its heavy GLP-1 exposure through mergers and acquisitions. The pressure reflects investor concern about concentration risk in the GLP-1 drug category as competition intensifies.

Weekend newsflow was net negative, driven by a major escalation in the Middle East as Houthis dramatically ramped up missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabian cities, raising fears of broader energy infrastructure disruption. On the AI front, a cluster of negative headlines hit after Friday's close, including an Anthropic IPO delay, disappointing ARR projections, and a staggering OpenAI free cash flow burn figure of negative $280 billion through 2030. The BOJ's latest move was slightly more dovish than expected, but it pressured the yen lower and rekindled concerns about Japan potentially selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid delivered hawkish remarks Friday, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small improvement in Middle East geopolitics could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with meaningful knock-on effects — lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump/Xi summit Thursday could produce select tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion in goods, with the US potentially bringing aggregate China tariff rates back toward 20% over time. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, a signal that global pressure is building. Saudi Arabia is also increasing ship-to-ship oil transfers to help compensate for supply shortages, providing some near-term buffer.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape is deteriorating rapidly — Houthis are escalating attacks on Saudi Arabia, Iran is threatening fierce retaliation if the US resumes military action, and multiple conflict fronts are straining Pentagon resources. The latest spike in energy prices hasn't fully worked its way through the economy yet, and demand destruction risk is rising. On the AI side, a wave of negative headlines including a massive OpenAI cash burn, rising Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liability exposure up $300 billion in 12 months, and an Anthropic IPO delay all create incremental headwinds for high-multiple tech names. Hawkish central bank signals globally, with the market pricing over 50% odds of more hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE in coming weeks, add further pressure on risk assets.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautious to bearish heading into the week. The weekend's news flow was net negative across both macro (Middle East escalation, energy supply risk) and micro (AI industry headwinds). The key catalysts to watch are the Trump/Xi summit Thursday, the UN General Assembly for any Middle East diplomatic progress, and Wednesday's US flash PMIs — any de-escalation in the Middle East or tariff relief from the Xi meeting could be the key upside surprise.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a cluster of headwinds this week — rising off-balance sheet liability exposure up $300 billion in 12 months, massive OpenAI cash burn, Anthropic IPO delays, and growing regulatory noise around AI safety including California Governor Newsom's executive order mandating exploration of kill switches for advanced models. Energy is a key sector to watch given Middle East escalation — oil and diesel supply disruptions tied to the Iran conflict are intensifying, with motor oil already being rationed at US retailers. Autos face pressure from Volkswagen's major guidance cut and a challenging China market backdrop. Chinese memory supply from CXMT entering mass production on its next-gen platform could provide a lid on memory prices, with read-throughs to PC and server supply chains. Defense and aerospace have mixed signals — Boeing gets a potential Starliner contract win but Anduril flags business impact from the delayed Taiwan arms sale.
