Mid-Day Update · Tuesday September 29, 2026
Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Wednesday morning economic data was mixed but the headline story is a notably cool August PCE print, with core coming in at +0.2% month-over-month versus the Street's +0.3% estimate and +3.0% year-over-year versus +3.3% expected. Q2 GDP was revised meaningfully higher to +2.2% annualized from +1.5%, driven by upward revisions to investment, consumer spending, and government spending. ADP's September jobs report came in slightly ahead at +90K private sector jobs versus a 75K forecast, rebounding from a weak +36K in August.

The cooler core PCE print is considered dovish and bullish for risk assets, particularly coming on the heels of hawkish-leaning Williams remarks from Tuesday. However, Adam flags that methodological changes to PCE calculation starting with the August reading had a downward impact on price momentum, meaning markets may discount the softer number if it reflects calculation shifts rather than genuine disinflation. The Friday BLS jobs report is the next key catalyst for yield relief, with markets preferring a soft but not catastrophic print in the 50–75K range.

Market Outlook

Adam leans cautiously bullish on the back of the cool PCE data, but tempers enthusiasm given methodological changes that may have artificially depressed the reading. The key near-term catalysts are Friday's BLS September jobs report and the October 14th CPI print, which will carry more weight than today's August PCE as investors have largely moved on from August data.

Sector Watch

Construction, manufacturing, education/health services, and leisure/hospitality showed job gains in September per ADP. Financial activities and professional/business services were areas of weakness, shedding jobs in September.
