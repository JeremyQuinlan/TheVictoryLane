VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTH Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO has been pushed to November rather than October, slightly later than many expected. Year-end ARR projections came in above 100 billion dollars per the NYT and above 110 billion dollars per the WSJ, modestly below some bull expectations of 120 billion dollars or higher. The company is considering launching a powerful new model before the IPO to recapture momentum from OpenAI's Astra, and plans to have 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focusing on poor price action in debt tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center in New Mexico. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have reportedly hit a wall, raising concerns about the company's data center financing strategy.

◆ PSKY Paramount Global M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit against its Warner Bros. Discovery merger deal. Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central, potentially clearing a key regulatory hurdle for the deal.

◆ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Warner Bros. Discovery's proposed merger with Paramount is moving closer to resolution as Paramount enters advanced settlement talks with California regulators. Key concessions under discussion include preserving CNN's editorial independence and divesting cable assets such as Comedy Central.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. This raises concerns about franchisee financial health and potential store closures or disruptions within the Wendy's system.

◆ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery chain pushes back against supplier price hikes. This signals continued pressure on consumer packaged goods pricing power as major retailers flex their leverage against suppliers.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk Activist Entry

Novo Nordisk is facing shareholder pressure to diversify away from its heavy GLP-1 exposure through mergers and acquisitions. Investors are increasingly concerned about concentration risk as the GLP-1 competitive landscape intensifies.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on 10 or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, a potentially significant contract win that could help rehabilitate the program's reputation following prior setbacks.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment. While a modest revenue item, it positions Accenture as a key partner in the growing AI governance and safety infrastructure market.

◆ NSCL Nscale Other

London-based neocloud firm Nscale filed its S1 publicly on Friday afternoon and aims to list on the NYSE under the ticker NSCL. The company is entering the market as investor appetite for AI infrastructure names remains elevated but volatile.

Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by worsening Middle East conditions and a cluster of negative AI headlines. The Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Iran warned of fierce retaliation if the US resumes military action, and the State Department flagged risk of unforeseen escalation. Ukraine also launched a massive drone attack on Russia over the weekend, adding further geopolitical pressure.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction — the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiked this month. The BOJ's latest hike was slightly more dovish than expected but triggered concern by pressuring the yen and rekindling fears of Japan selling Treasuries. Markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over the coming weeks. Kansas City Fed President Schmid made hawkish remarks Friday warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small improvement in Middle East geopolitics would likely trigger a sharp drop in energy prices with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump-Xi summit could produce select tariff reductions on roughly 30 billion dollars worth of goods, offering a modest positive for risk sentiment. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, signaling broader global alarm that could nudge diplomacy forward. Saudi Arabia is also boosting ship-to-ship oil transfers to partially offset supply disruptions, showing some ability to adapt.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical backdrop is deteriorating fast — Iran is tightening its grip on global energy via Hormuz, aligned Iraqi militias, and the Houthis, who just dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia. Energy price spikes will increasingly work their way through the system and threaten demand destruction. On the AI side, a dump of negative headlines hit after Friday's close — OpenAI's projected negative free cash flow of roughly 280 billion dollars through 2030, concerns about Oracle-linked data center debt, rising Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liabilities up approximately 300 billion dollars in the last 12 months, and a slight delay and modest miss on Anthropic's IPO timeline and ARR projections all weigh on sentiment. Global monetary policy remains hawkish with multiple central banks likely to hike in coming weeks.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautious to bearish for the week ahead given the net negative weekend news flow. The two key catalysts to watch are the Trump-Xi summit on Thursday September 24th — which could deliver modest tariff relief — and any diplomatic developments at the UN General Assembly running September 22nd through 28th in New York, where Middle East de-escalation remains a long shot but is the most powerful potential upside surprise.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a cluster of negative overhangs including rising off-balance sheet liabilities up approximately 300 billion dollars in the last year, OpenAI's massive projected negative free cash flow, and incremental regulatory pressure from both California and the White House. Energy is the central macro swing factor — Middle East escalation continues to drive diesel and motor oil shortages in the US, but any geopolitical progress could trigger a sharp reversal lower in oil prices. Autos face dual pressure from a challenging global demand environment, particularly in China, as highlighted by Volkswagen's major downside preannouncement on 2026 operating margins now seen at up to 1 percent versus prior guidance of 4 to 5.5 percent. Memory and PC components are seeing some relief as Chinese memory supply helps ease shortages according to Acer's CEO. Financials, healthcare, energy, and consumer sectors have major sell-side conferences this week.
