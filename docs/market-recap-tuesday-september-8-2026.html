RATES & FED

Treasuries sold off Tuesday with yields rising 1–3 basis points across the curve, driven largely by the surge in oil amid Middle East tensions. Fed expectations held steady with roughly 35 basis points of hikes priced for the year and about a 60% probability of a move at the September 16 meeting.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The market's internal rotation is the real story here — AI pick-and-shovel names ripped while software, payments, and financial data providers got dumped, a sign that the AI trade is evolving fast, not dying. Geopolitical risk remains elevated and September seasonality is brutal, but bulls have real catalysts: the Iran-Oman Hormuz arrangement could materially reduce oil risk, the Treasury buyback program starts Wednesday and should put downward pressure on yields, and the OpenAI Astra hype cycle is far from over. Bears aren't wrong about the headwinds — three major central banks hiking, fiscal deficits widening, and a Middle East tinder box — but the AI infrastructure buildout is proving sticky. Watch Thursday's PPI and Friday's CPI closely; if inflation surprises to the upside, the rate narrative gets a lot uglier fast.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Houthis struck energy facilities and cities in Saudi Arabia, wounding 70+, escalating Iran's proxy war campaign across the region (Reuters, CNN). The US struck additional Iranian tankers Tuesday in retaliation for a second attack on American ships — headlines crossed just before the close.

- Iran's economy is collapsing under US sanctions but Tehran is choosing kinetic escalation over diplomacy, though back-channel talk of returning to the June MOU continues (CNN, NYT). The US is broadly winning the Hormuz war, successfully ferrying non-Iranian oil through the strait (WSJ).

- An Iran-Oman agreement creating a "safe route" through Hormuz could be announced imminently — US acceptance would be a significant de-escalation catalyst (Bloomberg).

- Witkoff and Kushner's weekend meetings in Moscow and Kyiv produced no breakthrough; Putin plans to press on (FT, Bloomberg).

- Canada imposed tariffs on ~$20B of US goods as the US-Canada trade war shows no signs of resolution (NYT). Transportation Secretary Duffy called Ford's Chinese tech partnerships "unacceptable" (WSJ).

- Panama Canal's administrator warned daily transits may need to be cut further due to low water levels (FT).

- AfD surged to first place in Germany's Saxony-Anhalt election but fell short of an absolute majority (FT, CNN).

- Democrats hold a 10-point party identification advantage over Republicans, the largest since 2008 (NYT). Trump's approval hit a record low of 33% in a new FT poll.

- Chinese companies are routing advanced US AI chips through Southeast Asian subsidiaries to circumvent export controls (NYT).

COMPANY NEWS

ABM reported Q2 EPS of $1.04 vs. Street $1.01, with free cash flow of $128.4MM crushing the $43MM estimate; full-year EPS guidance raised to a $3.95–$4.10 range (new midpoint ~$4.03).

AMGN was among the market's weakest names after a Novartis/Ionis cholesterol drug failed its trial — Amgen is developing a similar therapy, raising comp concerns (WSJ).

BA delivered just 51 planes in August, the lowest in four months (Bloomberg), and faces a looming Pentagon decision on the Navy's next-gen stealth fighter contract against NOC (Reuters).

BSX warned it will miss prior Q3 and full-year guidance due to a cybersecurity incident, sending shares lower despite progress restoring affected systems.

GE announced an $11.75B acquisition of Consolidated Precision Products from Warburg Pincus and Berkshire Partners (26x headline multiple, 18x with synergies); deal is expected to be accretive to adjusted EPS and free cash flow in year one. HWM tumbled on fears of competitive impact.

GME confirmed Q2 results in line with its August 31 preannouncement and raised full-year EBITDA guidance to >$650MM from >$600MM.

QCOM announced a multi-generation AI silicon collaboration with Amazon for large-scale data centers, issuing Amazon warrants for up to 25M shares at $161.26, fully vesting upon $60B in chip purchases.

META launched Muse, a personal AI agent designed to work autonomously toward user goals (TechCrunch).

GLW signed a multi-billion dollar deal to supply 80+ million miles of optical fiber to Verizon from 2027–2032.

STZ underperformed after management flagged a "pretty lackluster August" in off-premises sales at the Barclays conference.

SYK fell after cautioning at the Wells Fargo conference on persistent supply headwinds in peripheral vascular and softer-than-expected hip replacement seasonality.

CRCL agreed to acquire Singapore-based B2B payments firm Tazapay for $400MM in stock.

WAFD announced a $3.9B acquisition of EverBank; the combined entity will trade as "EVBK," with ~29% EPS accretion expected in 2027.

PM raised full-year EPS guidance modestly, citing a larger FX tailwind.

NVS shares fell in European trading after a second consecutive clinical failure — a neuromuscular drug acquired via the $12B Avidity Biosciences deal missed its trial endpoint (WSJ, FT).

JEF disclosed that its Point Bonita fund within Leucadia Asset Management holds nearly $500M of exposure to troubled Radiant World, higher than previously reported (FT).

TSLA received FSD approval in Slovenia, its 6th European market (Reuters).

OpenAI solved the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem using a next-gen model more capable than Astra, deploying 10K agents generating 130B output tokens (WSJ). The CFO separately described OpenAI's planned ad product as "if Google and Meta had a baby" (Business Insider). Cognition raised $2B at a $48B valuation, nearly double its $26B mark from three months ago (Bloomberg). Ramp is in talks to raise at a $60B valuation, up ~50% from $44B just three months ago (Bloomberg). Anthropic walked away from its planned ~$6B acquisition of Decart AI (Bloomberg); its S1 is now expected in late September with a roadshow kicking off mid-October (Reuters).
