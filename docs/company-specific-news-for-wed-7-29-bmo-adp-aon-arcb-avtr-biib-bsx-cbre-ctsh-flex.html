MARKET OUTLOOK

The earnings tape for Wednesday tells a nuanced story: beats are happening, but quality matters enormously. Tariff refunds are flattering bottom lines at SWK, MAS, and GNRC — strip those out and the results look far more pedestrian. Meanwhile, top-line misses at VRT and WING signal that demand isn't as robust as the bulls would like, and with VRT trading north of 40x this year's earnings, the market won't be forgiving. Defense (GD) and data center infrastructure remain the cleanest longs; consumer-facing names are still grinding through a "challenging geopolitical and economic environment" — PG's words, not ours.

COMPANY NEWS

PG (Procter & Gamble) posted modest EPS upside ($1.43 vs. Street $1.41), but organic sales were flat against a Street forecast of +1.85%, with all five major divisions missing consensus. Management cited a "very challenging geopolitical and economic environment." F27 guidance midpoints landed near inline on both EPS ($7.00 vs. Street $7.02) and organic sales (+2% vs. Street +2.44%).

VFC (VF Corp.) delivered FQ1 results better than feared — underlying sales flat on an FX-neutral basis vs. guidance of down 1–3%, with an adjusted operating loss of $95MM vs. guidance of $100MM. Full-year revenue guidance was nudged higher to 2%+ FX-neutral vs. prior +1–2%. Vans Americas DTC grew but was offset by wholesale declines globally.

WING (Wingstop) posted modest EPS/EBITDA beats, but same-store comps were a clear disappointment at -7.5% vs. the Street's -5.5%, and full-year comp guidance was cut to -4–6% from the prior -1–3%.

AON reported barely-there upside — EPS of $3.81 vs. Street $3.80, organic revenue +5% vs. Street +4.8% — with Commercial Risk weakness offset by other divisions. Full-year guidance was reiterated.

CBRE delivered solid Q2 EPS of $1.56 vs. Street $1.47, with all four segments growing operating profit more than 25%. Full-year EPS guidance was raised to $7.80–7.90 from $7.60–7.80.

BIIB (Biogen) was a standout beat — Q2 EPS of $3.60 vs. Street $2.89, revenue of $2.73B vs. Street $2.46B — with underlying full-year EPS guidance lifted by 60 cents.

BSX (Boston Scientific) posted EPS of 86 cents vs. Street 83 cents on 7% sales growth vs. Street +5.6%, but cut full-year organic sales guidance to +5–6% from +6.5–8%, with EPS trimmed to $3.28–3.32 from $3.34–3.41.

HUM (Humana) beat Q2 EPS at $7.61 vs. Street $7.14, but merely reiterated full-year guidance rather than raising it — underwhelming given the first-half performance.

GD (General Dynamics) was one of the cleanest beats of the day: EPS $4.24 vs. Street $3.97, sales $14.1B vs. Street $13.5B, and a book-to-bill of 1.4x. Aerospace was a standout, with operating margins up 130 basis points and a 1.5x book-to-bill; backlog rose 20%.

JCI (Johnson Controls) delivered very strong FQ3 results — EPS $1.42 vs. Street $1.30, organic sales +10% vs. Street +6.2%, orders up 27%. Q4 guidance calls for organic sales +9–10% vs. Street +6.6%.

VRT (Vertiv) beat on Q2 EPS ($1.52 vs. Street $1.43) with margins expanding 410 basis points to 22.6%, but organic sales growth of 18% badly missed the Street's 24.7% model. Management blamed "temporary supply chain congestion." Full-year organic revenue guidance was raised to +30–32% from +20–31%, with EPS of $6.65–6.75 vs. prior $6.30–6.60. Bottom line: at 40x+ this year's earnings, the top-line miss is hard to forgive.

GNRC (Generac) posted a massive EPS beat ($2.91 vs. Street $2.02), but strip out tariff refunds and the story gets ordinary fast — EBITDA margins of 24.8% vs. Street 18% were heavily refund-driven, total sales were inline at $1.17B, and the full-year guide is essentially reiterated ex-refunds.

GRMN (Garmin) was a genuine beat — EPS $2.81 vs. Street $2.28 — driven by Fitness revenue strength and margin expansion, with full-year guidance raised.

FLEX reported strong FQ1 EPS of $1.00 vs. Street $0.91 on sales of $7.9B vs. Street $7.5B. Full-year guidance was bumped to +23% sales growth and +39% EPS growth vs. prior +18% and +32%, respectively.

CTSH (Cognizant) was essentially inline on Q2 EPS and revenue, but bookings fell 6% and full-year FX-neutral revenue guidance was trimmed to +4–5.5% from +4–6.5%. Net negative.

ADP posted a routine FQ4 beat with F27 guidance of 5–6% sales growth and 9–11% EPS growth, roughly in line with the Street.

MAS (Masco) posted a big EPS beat ($1.64 vs. Street $1.32) driven largely by tariff refunds — operating margins of 24.2% vs. Street 19.3% — though sales were light at -3% to $1.99B vs. Street $2.08B. Full-year EPS guidance raised to $4.50 from $4.20, with the caveat that the lift is mostly refund-related.

SWK (Stanley Black & Decker) beat on EPS ($1.57 vs. Street $1.22), but ex-tariff refunds EPS would have been ~$1.40. Full-year range nudged to $5.20–5.80 from $4.90–5.70. Decent, not exciting.

ODFL (Old Dominion) delivered Q2 EPS of $1.68 vs. Street $1.53, with an operating ratio improvement of approximately 450 basis points year-over-year on improving demand trends.
