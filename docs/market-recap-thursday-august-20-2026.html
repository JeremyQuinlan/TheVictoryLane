RATES & FED

Treasuries sold off Thursday with yields rising 2–5 basis points across the curve, reversing the modest post-buyback bid from Wednesday. The market is pricing roughly 23 basis points of hikes across the Fed's final three meetings of the year. Bessent's CNBC appearance — meant to reassure — landed poorly, with investors concluding that Treasury levers like the expanded buyback are trivially small against the structural forces driving rates higher.

MARKET OUTLOOK

"Issuance" has rapidly become the single dominant macro conversation, eclipsing even Iran, as sovereign supply and a tidal wave of AI-linked corporate paper collide with already strained bond markets. The catch-22 is real: the AI capex boom requires issuance since capital spending is swamping operating cash flow, yet that same issuance is the thing breaking the bond market. Anthropic's S1 could drop as soon as month-end, targeting a raise that matches or beats SpaceX's record (Bloomberg) — that's a significant additional supply event to price. Technically, volatile price action is inflicting performance pain on certain funds and amplifying selling pressure (FT). Until there's clarity on Iran and the issuance pipeline, risk assets remain on defense.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Trump declared an "Economic D-Day" against Iran; Bessent confirmed a press conference Monday 8/24 will detail additional financial sanctions. The move risks straining US-China relations since Beijing is a key financial lifeline for Tehran.

- More oil is clearing the Strait of Hormuz than markets realize, thanks to active US Navy escort operations — a modest bullish offset (NYT, Axios).

- The EPA announced plans to accelerate sales of winter-blend gasoline to help curb consumer fuel costs (Bloomberg).

- North Korea dismissed Trump's diplomatic overtures, contradicting White House claims of contact (FT).

- US and Canada are moving toward a trade deal that would cut tariffs on steel, aluminum, and auto imports (Reuters).

- Grain prices are surging following an escalation in port strikes between Russia and Ukraine, adding to the inflationary picture beyond the Middle East.

- US gross national debt crossed $40 trillion for the first time.

- China is rapidly closing the gap in the AI race and, by some metrics, has already taken the lead (Bloomberg).

COMPANY NEWS

WMT beat on EPS and aggregate revenue and raised full-year guidance, but US comparable store sales missed by a wide margin — in-store comps were down low single digits, dragged by drug pricing headwinds and aggressive discounting as the company reinvested tariff refunds. FQ3 EPS guidance came in below consensus. The macro read is disinflationary: lower drug prices and broader price cuts in general merchandise.

AAP posted a large FQ2 EPS beat ($1.03 vs. Street $0.81), but strip out tariff refunds and the underlying EPS was $0.72 — below consensus. Comps missed, and full-year operating margin guidance was left unchanged despite the refund tailwind, implying an implicit H2 reduction.

DE delivered a clean FQ3 beat — EPS of $5.10 vs. Street $4.70 — on slightly better revenue ($10.99B vs. $10.78B), margin outperformance, and stronger financial services income. Full-year net income guidance lifted to $4.75–5.0B from $4.5–5.0B. Management is increasingly confident 2026 marks the agricultural cycle trough.

BABA reported FQ1 revenue roughly inline (+9% to RMB268.95B vs. Street RMB268.5B), but EPS collapsed 42% Y/Y to RMB8.52 vs. Street RMB11.28 as EBITDA margins contracted 300 basis points due to elevated tech infrastructure investment.

COTY beat on FQ4 sales (LFL -1% vs. Street -4.6%), but EPS missed slightly and FQ1 guidance disappointed, with management calling F27 a "transition year."

NDSN topped FQ3 estimates — EPS $3.25 vs. Street $3.09, sales +10% to $818MM vs. Street $780MM — with backlog up 35% Y/Y and full-year guidance raised.

BILL reported strong FQ3 EPS of 84c vs. Street 71c, driven by operating margins of 23.3% vs. consensus 19.75%. F27 guide was mixed.

WOLF missed FQ4 EBITDA (-$62MM vs. Street -$52MM) on gross margin deterioration (-20% vs. Street -14.6%), and management guided for continued negative GMs in Q1.

NVDA — reports suggest Nvidia is developing an LPU chip for the Chinese market by year-end, though Beijing approval is uncertain; Nvidia told Bloomberg the report was incorrect.

AAPL — Barron's reports the company may pivot under new CEO John Ternus toward significantly higher R&D and capex spending to remain competitive in AI.

GOOGL is building a custom chip to improve Waymo robotaxi performance (Bloomberg). Separately, YouTube is offering top creators multimillion-dollar exclusivity payments to avoid working with Netflix (Bloomberg).

SMCI completed its export compliance investigation and found no evidence that senior management had knowledge of any alleged diversion of restricted products.

VIRT is exploring a sale of its agency brokerage and technology business in a deal potentially worth $3.5–4B (Bloomberg).

MSFT — Meta has quietly become one of Microsoft's largest AI customers (Bloomberg).
