Mid-Day Update · Monday September 21, 2026

Here's your midday update for Monday, September 21st.

The headline story today is a Nasdaq-led rally driven by two very distinct themes — agentic AI optimism and falling oil prices. The Nasdaq is up 153 basis points, the S&P 500 is up 94 basis points, but the equal-weight S&P is only up 30 basis points. That tells you everything — this is a concentrated, mega-cap tech and AI-driven move, not a broad market rally. Treasuries have a modest bid with yields down 1 to 3 basis points across the curve. Brent crude is down roughly 400 basis points, falling below $100 a barrel.

Let's talk about the two big themes.

First, Meta Muse. The company's new agentic AI app is showing strong early demand, and traders are front-running Zuckerberg's keynote at the Connect Conference Wednesday night. The Meta catalyst has rippled into the entire CPU ecosystem — AMD, ARM, and Intel are all surging — because agentic AI is significantly more CPU-intensive than prior AI iterations. This is a genuine re-rating moment for the CPU names if agentic AI goes mainstream. OpenAI is reportedly scrambling to develop a competing product, which validates the significance of what Meta is doing here.

Second, oil. Trump called off airstrikes against the Houthis at the last minute Sunday night, and markets are now hoping the UN General Assembly this week can produce some diplomatic progress on Middle East tensions. Trump has even signaled openness to meeting with Iran's president. That diplomatic optimism is driving oil lower and lifting travel, leisure, and consumer discretionary names.

On the negative side — HP Inc. guided for roughly a 5 percent decline in PC unit volumes in calendar 2027, and the stock is sliding. Potash names — CF Industries, Mosaic, and Nutrien — all dropped sharply after Trump said the US is close to a massive supply deal with Belarus at prices substantially below current Canadian potash rates. That's a direct pricing threat to the group. And Oracle faces a negative read after an FT piece highlighted that loans tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center in New Mexico are struggling to find buyers.

On the merger front, Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery are both jumping after Paramount struck a deal with state attorneys general to clear the legal obstacle blocking their merger. Reports indicate Paramount will preserve CNN's editorial independence and may divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central.

For the rest of the week, Wednesday night's Zuckerberg keynote is the single biggest event risk for the AI trade. The US-China trade truce expiration on November 10th is also moving into focus after weekend talks failed to produce an extension. And the Middle East remains a live risk — the Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia over the weekend, and the situation can reverse quickly if diplomatic talks at the UN fail.

Bottom line — the bull case is real today but narrow. Stay focused on the AI and CPU complex into Wednesday, watch oil for any reversal, and keep an eye on the equal-weight tape for signs of broadening or deterioration.
