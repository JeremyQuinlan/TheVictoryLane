◆ ANTHROPIC Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO has been pushed to November from October, with year-end ARR projections coming in above $100-110 billion — slightly below the $120 billion-plus some bulls had hoped for. The company is considering launching a powerful new model before its IPO to recapture momentum lost to OpenAI's Astra, and plans to have 5 gigawatts of compute available by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027. The IPO target remains a raise of $100 billion-plus at a valuation above $2 trillion.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focused on poor price action in debt tied to its massive 'Project Jupiter' data center facility in New Mexico. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have 'hit a wall,' raising concerns about Oracle's data center financing risk. This adds to the broader theme of rising off-balance sheet AI infrastructure liabilities for Big Tech.

▲ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. Under the potential settlement, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central. A resolution would remove a key regulatory overhang on the deal closing.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's came under pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Franchisee financial stress is a direct negative signal for the brand's unit economics and system health, particularly as the consumer environment tightens under elevated energy prices.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk M&A

Shareholders are pressuring Novo Nordisk to diversify away from its heavy GLP-1 concentration by pursuing M&A. While the pressure reflects concern about single-product dependency, any meaningful acquisition could be a catalyst for repositioning the stock's narrative and valuation multiple.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in active talks with NASA for its Starliner capsule to be used on ten or more new missions into low-Earth orbit, a meaningful commercial recovery opportunity for the troubled program. If the contract materializes, it would represent a significant rehabilitation of Starliner's reputation following its high-profile difficulties.

▼ MU Micron Technology Sector Reprice

Acer's CEO noted that Chinese memory supply from CXMT and others is helping ease component shortages and placing a ceiling on prices, which is a competitive headwind for Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix. Separately, CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, narrowing the gap with leading memory players.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment, part of a new oversight arrangement announced Friday night. The deal reinforces Accenture's positioning as a key enterprise partner in the AI governance space ahead of anticipated regulatory growth.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores nationwide as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. The move signals Kroger is willing to use shelf-space leverage aggressively to protect margins, which is a negative for Red Bull and a broader cautionary sign for consumer packaged goods companies seeking to hold elevated price points.

Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Weekend newsflow was net negative driven by two major themes: escalating Middle East tensions and a wave of disappointing AI industry headlines. Houthis dramatically ramped up missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabian cities, raising fears of further energy infrastructure disruption, while Iran warned the US against resuming military actions. Ukraine also launched a massive drone strike on Moscow over the weekend, adding to geopolitical risk.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction — the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiked this month, and markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over the coming weeks. The BOJ's latest hike was slightly more dovish than expected but paradoxically sparked concern by pressuring the yen lower and rekindling fears of Japan selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small improvement in Middle East geopolitics could trigger an outsized drop in oil prices with favorable knock-on effects — lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The UN General Assembly brings Iran and key regional players to New York, creating at least a window for diplomatic progress. The Trump/Xi summit could yield select tariff cuts on roughly $30 billion worth of goods, providing a modest risk-on catalyst. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing international pressure for de-escalation.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape is deteriorating rapidly, with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supply through Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis now escalating attacks on Saudi Arabia itself. Energy price spikes are beginning to work through the system and could trigger demand destruction as we move into Q4. On the AI side, a cluster of negative headlines — OpenAI's projected negative $280 billion in free cash flow through 2030, Anthropic's IPO delay, disappointing ARR projections, and rising off-balance sheet Big Tech AI liabilities — could weigh on the sector that has been the primary market driver. Global central banks remain uniformly hawkish, leaving no policy backstop in sight.

Market Outlook

Adam's tone is cautious to bearish heading into the week. The combination of worsening Middle East geopolitics, a hawkish global central bank backdrop, and negative AI-sector headlines creates meaningful headwinds. The key catalyst to watch is the Trump/Xi summit on Thursday September 24th, which could deliver modest tariff relief, alongside any diplomatic signals from the UN General Assembly on Iran.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a cluster of negative headwinds heading into the week — rising off-balance sheet liabilities up roughly $300 billion in the past year, OpenAI's projected negative $280 billion in free cash flow through 2030, and Anthropic's IPO delay all weigh on sentiment. Memory/semiconductors face incremental competitive pressure from Chinese producers like CXMT entering mass production on next-gen platforms. Energy is the central macro sector to watch given Houthi escalation against Saudi Arabia and ongoing Hormuz tension. Consumer staples face a margin squeeze risk as franchisee bankruptcies rise and retailers push back on supplier price hikes.
