ACN, BAC, and FAST: company-specific news

Oct 14, 2026 · 07:10 AM ET

Good morning. This is a focused earnings note covering three names — Accenture, Bank of America, and Fastenal — all out this morning on October 14th. No broad macro or Fed commentary is included in today's release, though Bank of America's management did characterize the overall macro environment as fairly healthy.

Let's start with Accenture. The company is holding an analyst meeting today and dropped forward guidance ahead of it. Fiscal 2028 is roughly in line — about five percent foreign exchange neutral sales growth and twenty basis points of operating margin expansion. But fiscal 2029 is the real story — they guided to five to nine percent foreign exchange neutral sales growth, well above the Street's roughly five percent estimate. That outer-year upside is a meaningful positive and should support the stock.

Over to Bank of America. This one is a miss across the board. Headline EPS came in at 91 cents versus the Street's one dollar and nine cents, with a large chunk of the gap explained by a one-point-two billion dollar AML charge announced Tuesday night. Strip that out and adjusted EPS was one dollar and eight cents — still a penny light. Revenue rose three percent to thirty-point-one billion but missed the thirty-point-six billion consensus. FICC trading was particularly ugly, falling seventeen percent to two-point-five-five billion versus expectations of two-point-eight-six billion. Investment banking fees also fell eleven percent. Expenses ran hot — the efficiency ratio ex the charge was sixty-two percent, about one hundred forty basis points worse than anticipated. The only upside was provisions coming in roughly two hundred million below consensus, but that's a low-quality beat. The franchise is solid and macro is fine, but this release is hard to spin positively.

Finally, Fastenal. A modest beat — thirty-five cents versus the Street's thirty-four cents — driven by fifteen-point-five percent daily sales growth, ninety basis points of operating margin expansion to twenty-one-point-five percent, and gross margins that contracted only fifty basis points to forty-four-point-eight percent, better than feared. The concern is September: daily sales growth decelerated more than two hundred basis points sequentially, slowing to fourteen-point-four percent from sixteen-point-six percent in August. That deceleration introduces some caution on the industrial demand trajectory heading into the fourth quarter.

Bottom line for today: Accenture is the cleanest positive with better-than-expected long-range guidance. Bank of America is the headache — revenue miss, expense miss, weak trading, and a large legal charge. Fastenal is mixed — solid execution but softening monthly trends. Keep an eye on how the broader financial sector trades around the BAC print, and watch Accenture's analyst meeting for additional color on the IT spending environment.
