Carnival reports strong FQ3 results

Sep 29, 2026 · 09:25 AM ET

Here is your earnings update for September 29th. Carnival posted a solid beat on its fiscal third quarter results this morning. EPS came in at one dollar and forty-three cents versus the Street's estimate of one dollar and thirty-five cents, and EBITDA landed at two point nine nine billion against expectations of two point eight nine billion. The upside was driven by two things: robust revenue and tight cost controls. On the revenue side, net yields on a currency-neutral basis came in roughly one hundred basis points above what management guided back in June, fueled by healthy demand and elevated pricing. On the cost side, adjusted expenses excluding fuel per available lower berth day in currency-neutral terms also beat the June guide by about one hundred basis points. Those positives more than offset higher fuel costs and unfavorable foreign exchange headwinds. The full-year outlook is moving higher. Adjusted net income guidance was raised by one hundred fifty million dollars compared to the June guide, and that is happening despite an ongoing fuel cost headwind, which tells you the revenue and efficiency story is doing real work. Looking further out, the qualitative commentary is encouraging. For 2027, both booked occupancy and pricing are at record levels. For 2028, they are off to a strong start at higher occupancy and prices than the prior year. Booking trends continued to strengthen throughout the quarter, with volumes meaningfully ahead of last year and well outpacing capacity growth. The one soft spot is the fiscal fourth quarter. Carnival guided adjusted EPS to twenty cents for the quarter versus the Street's twenty-six cent estimate, and that shortfall could create some near-term noise in the stock. The bottom line here is that travel demand remains healthy even as the cruise industry pushes prices higher to absorb elevated fuel expenses. Cost discipline and fuel efficiency measures are supporting margins, and the forward booking picture is as strong as it has been. This print is broadly constructive for the cruise sector and reinforces that the leisure consumer is still spending. Watch how the market reacts to that fiscal Q4 guidance miss — that will be the key debate in the stock today.
