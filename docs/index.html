VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTH Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO has been pushed from October to November, and its anticipated year-end ARR of greater than 100 to 110 billion dollars disappointed bulls who were hoping for 120 billion dollars or higher. OpenAI's renewed momentum following the Astra launch is a competitive concern, and questions remain about whether Anthropic's ARR is temporarily inflated by Meta and China distillation. On a more positive note, Anthropic may launch a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture frontier leadership, and its compute plans — 5 gigawatts by year-end and 10 gigawatts by end of 2027 — remain aggressive.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focusing on poor price action in debt tied to a massive Oracle-linked data center in New Mexico known as Project Jupiter, with efforts to offload the loans having hit a wall. This raises concerns about Oracle's off-balance sheet data center financing risk and the broader health of hyperscaler infrastructure deals.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

This directly competes with SpaceX for NASA low-Earth orbit missions.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment, positioning the firm as a key enterprise partner in the rapidly growing AI governance and safety space. This is a modest but strategically notable engagement ahead of Anthropic's IPO.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores nationwide as the grocery chain pushes back against supplier price hikes, signaling increased retailer leverage and pressure on consumer staples brands with premium pricing. This is a notable escalation in the grocery-supplier price dispute dynamic.

◆ NSCL Nscale Other

London-based neocloud firm Nscale filed its S1 publicly on Friday afternoon and plans to list on the NYSE under the ticker NSCL. The company is entering an increasingly crowded AI infrastructure market at a time when investor scrutiny of neocloud unit economics and customer concentration is rising.

Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by a deteriorating Middle East situation and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. Houthis dramatically escalated missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities over the weekend, worsening the Iran conflict that continues to pressure global energy supplies through Hormuz, Iraqi militias, and Yemen. Ukraine also launched a massive drone attack on Russia including Moscow, adding further geopolitical risk heading into the week.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction, with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. The BOJ's latest rate hike was slightly more dovish than expected, which paradoxically sparked macro concern by pressuring the yen lower and rekindling fears of Japan selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over the coming weeks, and Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems trace back to energy prices, and even a small geopolitical improvement at the UN General Assembly could trigger an outsized drop in oil, with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump/Xi summit could produce select tariff reductions on roughly 30 billion dollars worth of goods, a modest but real positive for risk sentiment. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing global pressure for de-escalation, and the Pentagon noted oil shipments through Hormuz have hit a six-month high.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape remains deeply grim, with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis now escalating attacks on Saudi Arabian cities, while Russia-Ukraine continues to take offline significant global refining capacity. AI sentiment took multiple hits over the weekend — Anthropic's IPO was pushed to November, its ARR projection underwhelmed the most bullish expectations, OpenAI disclosed roughly 280 billion dollars in negative free cash flow projected through 2030, and Big Tech's off-balance sheet AI liability exposure surged roughly 300 billion dollars in just the last twelve months. The energy price spike has yet to fully work its way through the economic system, and it is difficult to imagine it won't eventually trigger meaningful demand destruction and margin pressure.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautious-to-bearish heading into the week, with the weekend's negative AI and geopolitical headlines creating headwinds. The key catalysts to watch are the Trump/Xi summit on Thursday September 24th — which could yield modest tariff relief — and the UN General Assembly running September 22nd through 28th, where any Middle East diplomatic progress would be a significant positive surprise for markets.

Sector Watch

AI and tech broadly face a negative setup heading into the week after a wave of weekend headlines including Anthropic's IPO delay, OpenAI's massive projected cash burn of negative 280 billion dollars through 2030, and rising Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liability exposure of roughly 300 billion dollars over the last twelve months. Energy is in focus given Houthi escalation against Saudi Arabia and Iran's continued grip on Hormuz, with motor oil prices surging and retailers rationing supplies in the US. Autos face a double headwind from Volkswagen's large downside preannouncement — now guiding to operating margins of up to one percent versus prior guidance of four to five and a half percent — and ongoing China market weakness. Memory/semiconductors see a mixed signal as Chinese supplier CXMT announced mass production of its next-generation platform, which Acer's CEO says is helping ease component shortages and cap prices, a potential headwind for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron pricing power.
