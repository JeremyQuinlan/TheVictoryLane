RATES & FED

Long-end yields were the dominant story — rising despite a wave of dovish July data (soft jobs, retail sales, CPI, PPI, and import/export prices all cooled) — driven by sovereign fiscal imbalances and AI-linked bond issuance. The Wednesday FOMC minutes were more hawkish than expected in places, with "several participants" favoring a 25 basis points hike at the last meeting and "many" saying further tightening would be needed if inflation doesn't fall — though the offsetting dovish lines, including that "most participants anticipated inflation would step down" and that tariff pass-through was "largely complete," kept the net read mixed. All eyes pivot to Fed Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole speech Friday August 28th at 10am ET, where any softening on QE rhetoric could be the most potent near-term lever on long-end rates.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Bears took the week on a convergence of elevated yields, Iran escalation, and a consumer earnings tape that was more miss than hit — and the "issuance" problem isn't going away. The real risk isn't any single data point; it's the structural collision between a fiscal deficit that keeps supply overwhelming demand and an AI capex build that's calling on capital markets to plug a widening cash flow gap. Bulls have legitimate ammunition — dovish July inflation prints, strong regional Fed surveys (Philly at 47.4, Empire at 20.6), solid Eurozone PMIs, and a consumer that's battered but not broken — but the market needs either a credible fiscal consolidation signal or a Warsh pivot on the balance sheet to stabilize rates. Until one of those materializes, the path of least resistance stays choppy.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The White House is preparing to launch "Economic D-Day" against Tehran, with Treasury Secretary Bessent holding a press conference Monday — a move that risks inflaming Iran further and complicating the US-China relationship, since Beijing remains a key financial lifeline for Tehran (Axios, Bloomberg).

- Behind the scenes, Iran is reportedly growing increasingly alarmed about its economic deterioration, raising the possibility it seeks a deal; a return to an MOU framework plus tacit US recognition of the Hormuz traffic arrangement being brokered by Oman could offer a path forward.

- More oil is clearing Hormuz than markets appreciate, aided by US military escorts — but surging cracking spreads mean end-user fuel prices, especially diesel, are rising faster than crude, a significant inflationary transmission risk for commerce.

- Grain prices are surging following an escalation in port strikes between Russia and Ukraine, spreading geopolitical inflation beyond the Middle East.

- The US and Canada are moving toward a trade deal that would cut certain tariffs on steel, aluminum, and auto imports from Canada.

COMPANY NEWS

WMT — Walmart's US comp miss was larger than the headline suggested, but context matters: in-store comps were dragged down by drug price deflation and deliberate general merchandise price cuts as management reinvested tariff refunds — not fundamental demand weakness. FQ3 EPS guidance came in below consensus, though full-year guidance was raised. The macro read is actually disinflationary.

HD — Home Depot only reiterated full-year guidance despite a large incremental tariff refund benefit, underwhelming a market that expected upside leverage from that tailwind.

LOW — Lowe's missed on comps and trimmed full-year guidance despite also receiving a significant tariff refund benefit — a more concerning signal for the home improvement category than Home Depot's hold.

TGT / ROST / BJ / AS / EL — A mixed-but-mostly-resilient group. TJX posted a comp miss in its key Marmaxx division. ROST was a bright spot. Together they reinforce the "consumer alive but selective" thesis.

DE — Deere beat in FQ3 and nudged full-year guidance higher; management is increasingly confident 2026 marks the trough of the agricultural cycle.

NVDA — Confirmed a financial commitment to the PORTS-Pike data center in Ohio capped at $105B (vs. the $250B initially floated by WSJ), with vendor financing to OpenAI for chip purchases likely per media reports. The relationship remains circular, but the scale is more digestible than feared.

BABA — Alibaba's EPS and free cash flow were weighed down by elevated tech infrastructure spending — a recurring theme across AI-adjacent names.

JD (JD Sports) — Cut full-year guidance, citing "highly promotional pricing" and "incremental cost-of-living pressures" on the consumer.

COTY — Guidance disappointed; management called FY27 a "transition year."

LZB — La-Z-Boy missed on both EPS and sales, with soft guidance tied to wholesale weakness.
