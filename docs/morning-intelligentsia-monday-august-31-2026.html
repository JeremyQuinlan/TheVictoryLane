RATES & FED

Post-Jackson Hole, 2-year yields are down 2.5 basis points Monday while 10- and 30-year yields sit flat — a modest reversal after Friday's spike. Markets are now pricing ~36 basis points of hikes this year (up from ~25 basis points pre-Warsh), with odds of a September 16 move at ~60% versus ~35-40% before the speech. The long end remains structurally capped by AI-linked issuance, fiscal imbalances, and sticky inflation, though several near-term factors — JPY intervention support, Treasury buybacks, and the prospect of a Fed hike bolstering credibility — make a tactical rally plausible.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Warsh was explicit: not worried about labor, concerned about inflation, and a September hike looks probable barring a dramatic data miss. Counterintuitively, we think a "one-and-done" hike is net positive — it shores up Fed credibility and should pull long-end yields lower more than it damages growth. Corporate profits are booming on AI, investment gains, tariff refunds, and resilient demand (WSJ), which argues for continued equity upside. That said, September is historically the worst month for stocks, momentum trades are suddenly unraveling badly (WSJ), and the FOMC meeting on 9/16 is a live catalyst for volatility — don't get complacent. Earnings from AVGO and DELL this week will be a key read on AI spend durability.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The US and Iran exchanged fire Sunday night for the first time in a month — Iranian missiles and drones targeting US assets in Jordan were all intercepted — but full-scale resumption of bombing looks unlikely given US munition shortages and extreme Iranian economic strain (NYT, CNN). Qatar and Pakistan's diplomatic push has fizzled, and neither side is near a deal (Reuters).

- Iran is experiencing gas lines as the naval blockade and sanctions squeeze fuel supplies; the government fears lifting energy subsidies would ignite domestic unrest (NYT). Treasury's action against UAE branches of Egypt's Banque Misr was widely dismissed as underwhelming — the world largely shrugged off Bessent's "Economic D-Day" (Bloomberg).

- Pentagon planners warn the Iran war has drained US munition stockpiles to a degree that complicates deterrence against China and Russia (WSJ), and senior military leaders have warned Hegseth that large-scale Iran operations are unsustainable (Washington Post).

- Russia is preparing "massive strikes" on Ukraine's energy infrastructure (CNBC) and has launched a wave of "gray zone" attacks across Europe as its Ukraine campaign stalls (WSJ). CIA Director Ratcliffe proposed a Trump-Putin-Zelensky trilateral summit during his Moscow trip (Axios). Russia extended its diesel export ban through at least end of September (Bloomberg).

- Trump's Venezuela deal — a 35% passive US stake in a new entity controlling ~65 billion barrels of reserves — is politically charged but a non-event for near-term energy prices; the fields require massive capital investment over years before production materializes, and the heavy crude sells at a steep discount to Brent (Barron's, NYT, WSJ, FT). CVX (Chevron) is separately on the cusp of expanding its Venezuelan footprint (NYT).

- Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit opens Monday in Kyrgyzstan, bringing together Xi, Putin, Modi, and Iran's Pezeshkian (Nikkei).
