Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

News flow this week favored the bears as yields surged and Brent crude rose above $100, though the S&P 500 and Nasdaq pushed higher on AI enthusiasm surrounding Meta's personal AI agent Muse. The single biggest macro event was the September flash PMIs, which came in scorching hot — pointing to healthy growth but triggering a 15 basis point surge in 10-year yields on Wednesday, along with higher inflation, margin headwinds, and supply chain conditions among the most stressed in decades outside of peak COVID. The Trump-Xi meeting was largely a nonevent, extending the existing US-China trade truce by two months with nothing additional to show for it.

Ten-year yields surged 15 basis points on Wednesday alone following the hot September flash PMI release, with secular upward pressure on yields driven by resilient growth, elevated inflation, AI-related debt issuance, and extreme fiscal imbalances. Central banks globally continue to tighten, with Norway hiking 25 basis points Thursday and markets pricing greater than 50% odds of rate hikes from the Bank of Canada on October 28th, the FOMC on October 28th, the ECB on October 29th, and the Bank of England on November 5th. Fed officials have been issuing daily warnings about upside inflation risks and the need for further policy tightening.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bearish — stocks are holding in largely due to TACO dynamics and Muse-driven AI enthusiasm, but the underlying macro backdrop is deteriorating with yields spiking, oil above $100, unresolved geopolitical conflicts, and mounting headwinds to the data center boom that underpins much of the current earnings cycle. The key catalyst to watch is whether the US-Iran diplomatic process produces a tangible breakthrough that brings oil prices lower, which would be the single most powerful relief valve for the market.

Sector Watch

AI and data center infrastructure stocks were lifted by Meta Muse enthusiasm, but Adam warns the sector faces mounting headwinds including delayed IPOs, massive cash burn, fierce frontier model competition, data center moratoriums and construction delays, and Oracle's force majeure — all at a time when the US economy has never been more levered to a single industry. Housing remains under pressure as evidenced by KB Home's weak guidance. Casual dining showed cracks with Darden's comp misses across multiple brands. Global central bank tightening continues to tighten financial conditions broadly, with rate hike odds rising for the Fed, ECB, Bank of Canada, and Bank of England over the next five weeks.

VK · Key Names
◆ META Meta Platforms Product Launch

Meta's personal AI agent Muse dominated the week's AI narrative, breaking personal AI agents into the mainstream consumer market and creating a new source of compute demand that propelled AI-linked stocks higher. However, Adam flags that Muse enthusiasm may not be sustainable as a flood of competing personal AI agents is likely coming in the weeks ahead, potentially as soon as OpenAI's Dev Day on September 29th. The optimism is also masking broader negative industry developments including delayed IPOs, reports of huge cash burn, and fierce frontier competition.

▼ MCD McDonald's Guidance Cut

McDonald's shares were hit hard following this week's analyst meeting after management warned that comparable sales are pacing slightly below expectations in Q3. The company also announced plans to significantly increase capital spending, which will meaningfully reduce free cash flow conversion — a double negative for the stock.

▼ AZO AutoZone Earnings Miss

AutoZone reported comparable sales that fell short of expectations, representing an underwhelming earnings result that weighed on the stock. The miss added to a pattern of consumer-facing companies struggling to meet elevated Street estimates.

▼ DRI Darden Restaurants Earnings Miss

Darden reported comparable sales misses at both its Olive Garden and LongHorn Steakhouse concepts, disappointing investors who had expected more resilient consumer spending at casual dining. The shortfall across multiple brands signals broader softness in the casual dining segment.

▼ KBH KB Home Guidance Cut

KB Home issued weak fiscal Q4 guidance amid continued headwinds in the housing market, raising concerns about the near-term trajectory of homebuilder earnings. The guidance cut reflects the combined pressure of elevated mortgage rates and affordability constraints weighing on new home demand.

▼ PAYX Paychex Earnings Miss

Paychex disappointed with its Management Solutions business posting revenue below Wall Street expectations, an underwhelming result for what is typically viewed as a steady, predictable compounder. The miss raises questions about whether small business hiring and payroll activity is beginning to soften.

▼ SNX TD SYNNEX Earnings Miss

TD SYNNEX shares were hit following its earnings release, with weak gross margins the primary culprit for investor disappointment. The margin miss highlights the input cost and pricing pressure dynamic flagged broadly in the September flash PMIs.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle declared force majeure on data center commitments, cited as one of several negative recent developments in the AI infrastructure build-out that Muse enthusiasm has overshadowed. This is a meaningful signal of stress in the data center supply chain that could have read-across implications for hyperscalers and their suppliers.
