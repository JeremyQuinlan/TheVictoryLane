Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +110 basis points, and R2K +47 basis points, despite a weekend filled with net negative news including escalating Middle East geopolitics and troubling AI headlines. Asian markets closed higher across the board — Hang Seng +1.18%, Korea KOSPI +1.65%, Taiwan TAIEX +1.14% — while major Eurozone indices are up 85-120 basis points. The primary driver of Monday strength appears to be optimism around the UN General Assembly in New York this week, with hopes for diplomatic progress on the Iran conflict, particularly after Trump signaled openness to meeting Iran's president.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The Fed is priced for roughly 33 basis points of additional rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28 meeting, and another 35 basis points of increases priced in for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that inflation is running hot beyond just oil, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari echoed those hawkish sentiments Sunday, reinforcing that the global monetary policy backdrop remains firmly tilted toward tightening across the BOJ, ECB, FOMC, and several other major central banks.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all the market's macro problems trace back to elevated oil prices, and even a modest geopolitical de-escalation at the UN General Assembly could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with powerful knock-on benefits: lower yields, easing inflation, margin relief, and improved consumer confidence. Trump called off Houthi air strikes at the last minute and signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, suggesting a diplomatic off-ramp may be emerging. China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, indicating growing global consensus around de-escalation. Saudi Arabia is also increasing ship-to-ship oil transfers to help compensate for supply shortages, showing some supply-side flexibility.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was overwhelmingly negative — Houthis launched a brazen missile and drone attack on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine initiated its largest drone strike of the war against Russia, and a slew of troubling AI headlines hit after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. The geopolitical landscape remains grim with Iran tightening its grip on global energy via Hormuz, aligned militias, and the Houthis, while Russia's refining capacity continues to be degraded. The UNGA optimism may simply be wishful thinking, and the US is still expected to hike the aggregate tariff rate on Chinese imports back to pre-SCOTUS IEEPA levels in the coming weeks.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for today's session driven entirely by geopolitical optimism around the UN General Assembly, but he flags this as fragile and potentially wishful thinking. The key catalyst to watch is any signal of diplomatic progress at the UNGA — specifically whether Trump meets Iran's president — which could trigger a meaningful oil selloff and a cascade of bullish macro effects including lower yields and reduced inflation pressure.

Sector Watch

AI and data center infrastructure names face notable headwinds after a weekend of negative news: delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI projecting roughly $280 billion in negative free cash flow from 2026-2030, and Oracle data center debt stress. Big Tech AI off-balance sheet liability exposure has risen roughly $300 billion in just the last 12 months, creating a fresh and growing source of risk. European data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming this morning despite the negative headlines. Energy sector faces continued volatility with Brent sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10 as geopolitical risk is slightly offset by UNGA diplomatic optimism. Chinese memory supply from CXMT entering mass production on its next-gen platform could pressure pricing for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron over time.
