MARKET OUTLOOK

The earnings tape for August 5 is broadly constructive — beats are outpacing misses by a wide margin, and full-year guidance is being raised more often than cut. The standouts are LLY, CVS, SHOP, and SN, all of which posted results that materially reset expectations higher. The few weak spots (LPX, MDLN, GPN) are idiosyncratic rather than macro-driven, which is an encouraging signal for the broader market environment.

---

GEOPOLITICAL

- Middle East conflict is creating inflationary cost pressure in medical supplies, with MDLN citing it as a key driver behind cutting its full-year EBITDA guide (Vital Knowledge).

- GPN trimmed its full-year EPS guidance to $13.70 from $13.90, specifically attributing part of the shortfall to Middle East impact on its travel-linked businesses (Vital Knowledge).

COMPANY NEWS

LLY (Eli Lilly) delivered a blowout Q2 — EPS of $8.38 vs. the Street's $6.00, revenue of $23B vs. $20.6B expected. Mounjaro came in roughly $1B better than anticipated, with Zepbound, Trulicity, and Jardiance also outperforming. Full-year guidance raised.

CVS posted a very strong Q2 with EPS of $2.85 vs. $1.85 expected on revenue of $106B vs. $100B. The medical cost ratio of 87.4% came in roughly 200 basis points better than anticipated. Full-year EPS raised to ~$8 from ~$7.40; pharmacy comps beat at +2.6% vs. +2.2% expected.

SHOP (Shopify) had a monster Q2 — GMV of $115.5B vs. $112.1B, free cash flow of $654MM vs. $550MM. Q3 revenue guide of +31-33% blows past the Street's +26.7%, with free cash flow margins of 18-22% vs. 18% expected.

SN (SharkNinja) beat on EBITDA at $265MM vs. $250MM with net sales accelerating to +22.2%. Full-year sales now seen +16-17% vs. prior +11.5-12.5%; EPS midpoint raised 45 cents, including 15 cents from tariff refunds.

DIS (Disney) reported FQ3 EPS of $2.06 vs. $1.86, with operating income of $5.55B vs. $5.23B. Revenue rose 7% to $25.24B, a touch light vs. $25.4B expected. Prior F26/F27 guidance reiterated. Management flagged Moana box office underperformance and softer domestic SVOD advertising as Q4 headwinds, while Experiences held up and forward bookings at Walt Disney World remain robust.

BLMN (Bloomin' Brands) beat Q2 EPS at 39 cents vs. 29 cents expected and raised full-year EPS guidance to $0.90-$1.00 from $0.75-$0.90.

CPRI (Capri Holdings) beat FQ1 EPS at 67 cents vs. 40 cents on sales of $769MM vs. $752MM, with operating margins of 3.6% vs. 1.4% expected. However, full-year sales guidance was trimmed to $3.4B due to Michael Kors headwinds — lower inventory, EMEA softness, and FX. EPS outlook held at $2.15 via cost cuts; FQ2 guidance missed badly on both sales and EPS.

KHC (Kraft Heinz) posted Q2 organic sales of -1.3% vs. -3.5% feared and EPS of 56 cents vs. 53 cents. Full-year sales and gross margin guidance raised; EPS range only tightened rather than raised due to increased spending plans.

CG (Carlyle) reported distributable EPS of $1.07 vs. $0.91, with inflows of $16.8B vs. $14B expected.

BWA (BorgWarner) beat Q2 EPS at $1.42 vs. $1.28 and added $1B to its buyback authorization. Full-year EPS raised solely to reflect higher buybacks; all other guidance unchanged.

OC (Owens Corning) crushed Q2 with EPS of $3.93 vs. $3.09 and EBITDA of $660MM vs. $565MM. Q3 guidance roughly in line.

ZBH (Zimmer Biomet) delivered 4% FX-neutral organic growth — about double consensus — with EPS of $2.07 vs. $2.01. Full-year EPS raised to $8.47-$8.59 from $8.40-$8.55.

CDW reported Q2 EPS of $2.91 vs. $2.80 on sales of $6.57B (+10%) vs. $6.2B expected, though gross margins fell 70 basis points year-over-year to 20.1%.

UBER came in essentially inline on EBITDA and revenue. Bookings modestly beat driven by Delivery and Freight; free cash flow missed at $2.8B vs. $3.2B. Q3 bookings guide of $59.25B is a hair below $59.32B consensus; EBITDA guide of $2.91B edges past $2.88B.

LPX (Louisiana-Pacific) missed Q2 badly — EPS 40 cents vs. 58 cents, EBITDA $79MM vs. $98MM — on raw material inflation. Q3 EBITDA guided at $55MM vs. $90MM; full-year EBITDA slashed to $255-$270MM from $345-$360MM.

MDLN (Medline) cut its full-year EBITDA guide to $3.3-$3.4B from $3.5-$3.6B, citing Middle East-driven inflation, operational investments, quality remediation, and retail softness.
