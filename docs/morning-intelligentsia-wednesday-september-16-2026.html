RATES & FED

Treasuries are flat across the curve ahead of today's FOMC. The market is pricing roughly a 25 basis point hike today, another 25 basis points on December 9th, and 1–1.5 additional 25 basis point hikes in 2027. Blackstone's CFO put it well at the Morgan Stanley conference: expect a "dovish hike" that keeps the long end in check without choking the real economy.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

All roads lead to oil — fix that, and nearly every macro overhang on equities dissolves. The Fed hike today is actually manageable (one-and-done or two-and-done messaging), and so long as the updated dots don't pencil in more than 75 basis points of cumulative tightening through end-2027, stocks should hold fine. The asymmetry on Brent is interesting right now: conditions are already so dire in the Middle East that even a marginal improvement — a rescheduled GCC-Iran meeting, a pipeline coming back online — could spark an outsized price drop. VP Vance told the NY Post the Iran conflict enters a "much different phase in a couple of months," and Tehran is under real financial strain. Watch the Trump/Xi summit next week too — tariff relief there would be a meaningful secondary catalyst for equities.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The US is burning through interceptor stockpiles at a rapid rate as Iran's missile attacks grow more sophisticated, stretching Pentagon resources thin across theaters (WSJ).

- CBS News photos reveal widespread damage to US military infrastructure across the Middle East from Iranian drone and missile strikes over recent months (CBS).

- AMZN's AWS says it cannot restore cloud facilities in Bahrain and the UAE more than six months after Iranian drone strikes (CNBC).

- Americans have spent ~$107B more on fuel as a direct result of the Iran war (WSJ); the CBO estimates the conflict added 230 basis points to Q2 headline PCE, which printed at 5.3% (CBO).

- Saudi Arabia suspended Red Sea loadings at Yanbu and is rerouting more oil through Hormuz; Energy Secretary Wright says the East-West Pipeline could be back within days (Bloomberg, CNBC).

- Half of Russia's top diesel-producing refineries have cut output after Ukrainian drone strikes; China may reimpose refined fuel export restrictions amid plunging domestic inventories (Reuters, Bloomberg).

- House voted for the third time to require Congressional authorization for the Iran war — 7 Republicans crossed over, up from 4 previously (CNN). White House approved a $2.8B heavy bomb sale to Israel (NYT).

- US/China tariff reductions on select goods could be announced at the Trump/Xi summit (Sept. 24); Bessent meets his Chinese counterpart this weekend as preparation (Bloomberg, Reuters).

- US-Mexico trade talks resume next week covering steel, aluminum, and auto tariffs; Washington is pressing Mexico to reduce Chinese content in AI-related tech products (WSJ).

- Europe is considering making Canada its first-ever "associate member" (Reuters).

COMPANY NEWS

AMZN (Amazon) is planning an "unprecedented expansion" of its same-day delivery network — dubbed Project Mercury — which could grow 10x by 2031 to roughly 1,000 fulfillment centers, directly challenging Walmart (Business Insider).

NSC (Norfolk Southern) CFO described current diesel prices as "science fiction," warning of an unexpected ~250 basis point headwind to the Q3 operating ratio. Fuel is modestly positive for volumes as freight shifts from truck to rail, but the downstream demand question — how much can consumers absorb before pulling back? — is real and bears watching.

DHI (DR Horton) raised its F2026 buyback target from $2.5B to $3.25B and added $5B to its overall repurchase authorization.

JPM (JPMorgan) at the Barclays conference guided Q3 trading and IB fees up 15–19% Y/Y, well ahead of consensus estimates of +12.5% and +10.75% respectively (Barclays conference).

INTC/SKHY — SK Hynix is in talks to potentially build memory chips at an Intel facility in Ohio, or form a broader partnership with Intel and hyperscalers seeking expanded memory access (Reuters).

BX (Blackstone) CFO at the Morgan Stanley conference described the macro backdrop as constructive and flagged today's hike as likely "dovish," adding it should keep the long end anchored.

OpenAI is in talks to raise at a $1.2–1.5T valuation ahead of a planned 2027 IPO — investors approached at $1.2T, OpenAI is pushing for $1.5T (NYT). Separately, OpenAI is rushing to build an ad business to support its free ChatGPT tier, though early results have been mixed (WSJ). Softbank CDS are widening on funding concerns (Bloomberg).

ByteDance reported H1 revenue up 30% to $120B, though profit fell by a high single-digit percentage to $20B as AI spending ramped sharply (The Information).

WFC (Wells Fargo) CFO noted Q3 NIM is tracking slightly better than prior guidance; consumer remains resilient (Barclays conference). JPM guided well above consensus on trading and IB fees (see above).
