Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +110 basis points, and R2K +47 basis points, despite a weekend full of negative headlines including geopolitical escalation and troubling AI sector news. Asian and European markets are broadly higher, with data center pick-and-shovel stocks outperforming in Europe. Treasuries have a bid with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve, while Brent crude slides 170 basis points to around $102.10 and bitcoin surges 350 basis points to roughly $83,890.

Fed expectations are holding fairly steady with the market pricing in roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of increases priced in for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari echoed those hawkish sentiments Sunday morning. The BOJ's recent rate hike was slightly more dovish than expected, but globally monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. Treasury plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing bills above 24% of marketable debt and above the Treasury's own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems can be traced back to oil, and even a minor geopolitical improvement could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices, with knock-on benefits including lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. The UN General Assembly running September 22-28 in New York brings together officials from multiple Middle Eastern countries including Iran, creating a genuine window for diplomatic progress. Trump reportedly called off planned air strikes against the Houthis at the last minute and expressed openness to meeting Iran's president, suggesting a negotiated off-ramp may be possible. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, a sign global pressure is building.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape remains genuinely grim — Iran is tightening its grip on global energy supplies via the Strait of Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis in Yemen who dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia over the weekend. The economy and corporate earnings have held up well over the summer, but the latest spike in energy prices will begin working through the system soon and could trigger meaningful demand destruction. The AI sector faces fresh headwinds including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI's staggering projected negative free cash flow of $280 billion through 2030, and Oracle data center debt stress. UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism may simply turn out to be wishful thinking.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday, driven almost entirely by diplomatic optimism surrounding the UN General Assembly and the possibility of Iran-related de-escalation, which could crack oil prices lower and unleash a cascade of favorable macro effects. The key catalyst to watch is any headline out of the UN General Assembly suggesting diplomatic progress on the Middle East or Iran, as well as the Trump/Xi summit Thursday.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe Monday morning. AI infrastructure broadly faces negative sentiment after a wave of troubling weekend headlines including Anthropic IPO delays, underwhelming ARR projections, OpenAI's massive projected cash burn of negative $280 billion through 2030, and Oracle data center debt stress. Big Tech AI off-balance sheet liability exposure has risen roughly $300 billion in the last 12 months, representing a growing risk. Chinese memory giant CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, which combined with Acer's CEO comments that Chinese memory supply is easing component shortages, puts mild downward pressure on memory pricing — a headwind for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Energy and oil-related names face a complicated setup with Brent sliding but geopolitical risk remaining extremely elevated.
