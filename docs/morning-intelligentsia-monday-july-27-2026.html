MARKET OUTLOOK

The geopolitical relief rally is real but don't mistake a ceasefire pause for a resolution — Iran remains divided, stubborn, and militarily formidable, and the Houthis are opening a second front that keeps energy risk elevated. More importantly for the S&P 500, AI is the dominant variable, and the Nvidia-Ohio data center story (WSJ) is not reassuring — when a chip company has to potentially guarantee $600B in financing to keep data center spend alive, that tells you capex is swamping operating cash flow and capital markets are doing the heavy lifting. The week's macro calendar is packed: Fed Wednesday, PCE and GDP Thursday, and a wall of mega-cap earnings. Position accordingly — the relief trade has legs intraday, but the structural AI monetization story remains unresolved.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The US observed its first strike-free night against Iran in nearly two weeks on Friday, holding off Saturday and Sunday as well; Iran says it will maintain its own ceasefire so long as Washington does the same (Reuters). A NYT report reveals Trump held back due to a critical shortage of Patriot interceptors and other munitions — not a change of heart.

- Iran and Oman are holding talks on managing shipping through the Strait of Hormuz (FT). Iran's new supreme leader is reportedly far more interested in acquiring a nuclear weapon than his predecessor (NYT), complicating any negotiated path.

- Saudi Arabia conducted additional strikes on Yemen Friday and intercepted missiles aimed at its refineries Saturday; several major maritime insurers said they will no longer cover Saudi vessels in the Red Sea (FT).

- Ukraine and the US are discussing a potential air ceasefire proposal to submit to Russia as a basis for renewed peace talks; Trump and Zelenskyy are set to meet in Washington (Reuters).

COMPANY NEWS

NVDA (Nvidia) may provide ~$600B in financing guarantees on a massive Ohio data center, per the WSJ. The author's read: this is a sign that capex in AI infrastructure has so outpaced operating cash flow that Nvidia itself must backstop the funding — hard to feel constructive on AI-linked equities after digesting that.

BKR (Baker Hughes) posted blowout Q2 results: EPS of 64 cents vs. the Street's 50 cents, revenue of $6.7B vs. $6.5B expected, and book-to-bill of 1.57x. IET orders were $7.1B vs. the Street's $3.6B — the company is raising full-year IET order guidance while keeping overall EBITDA guidance intact.

AstraZeneca beat Q2 EPS estimates by delivering $2.63 on an FX-neutral basis (+18% Y/Y) vs. the Street's $2.48, with strength in Oncology and Rare Disease; full-year guidance reiterated. Shares outperformed in London.

FBRX (Forte Biosciences) is being acquired by ARGX (Argenx) for $77/share cash, valuing the deal at ~$2.2B (WSJ).

AAPL / MU: Apple is lobbying the White House to allow Chinese memory chips in devices sold outside the US; Micron is fiercely opposing the move, warning of the same hollowing-out dynamic seen in steel (WSJ).

HOOD (Robinhood) is in talks with Crypto.com to bring prediction market contracts onto its platform, moving beyond its existing Kalshi relationship (WSJ).

UAL (United Airlines) approached DAL (Delta) last year about a merger; talks never advanced. United also approached American Airlines earlier this year and was rebuffed (WSJ).

BF/B (Brown-Forman): Sazerac is going directly to shareholders — including the Brown family — to push its $32/share bid and hinted the price could rise if the board engaged; so far being rejected (WSJ, Bloomberg).

NKE (Nike): Performance categories including running are showing renewed momentum, which the WSJ argues is a positive setup for the stock.

CXMT, the Chinese memory IPO in Shanghai, surged ~466% on debut, briefly giving it a ~$550B market cap and eclipsing Tencent as China's most valuable company (FT).
