RATES & FED

Treasuries have a solid bid Thursday with yields falling 5–7 basis points across the curve, pulled lower by declining oil prices and a run of dovish central bank signals globally. The BOE added fuel by announcing it will permanently hold GBP 120 billion of the longest-dated gilts on its balance sheet — a meaningful dovish tweak to its QT policy. Markets are currently pricing roughly 31 basis points of Fed hikes across the final two meetings of the year.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The AI boom remains the single most important macro force in the market, and Thursday's tape is proof — pick-and-shovel AI names are leading the charge while oil pulls back and yields cooperate. CoreWeave's willingness to launch a ~$6 billion capital raise signals management confidence that the market can absorb the paper, which bodes well for the broader AI IPO pipeline. The Saudi pipeline recovery story (partial East-West restart within days, per Bloomberg) and China leaning on Iran to rein in the Houthis (Reuters) are incrementally constructive for oil supply, though Brent holding above $100 keeps the inflation risk alive. The Fed's Wednesday hike actually bolstered monetary credibility rather than rattling equities, and that's a healthy dynamic. Stay long AI infrastructure; be cautious on defensives and anything exposed to a softening consumer or ad cycle.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Saudi Arabia aims to bring roughly half of the East-West Pipeline back online within days and is increasing ship-to-ship oil transfers off Oman's Sohar port to ease global supply strains (Bloomberg, Reuters).

- China privately pressed Iran to help rein in the Houthis following a Saudi appeal to Beijing — a notable diplomatic development that could reduce Red Sea disruption risk (Reuters).

- Trump plans to meet Gulf leaders on the sidelines of next week's UN General Assembly in New York (Sept. 22–28) to discuss the Iran war, though no deal appears imminent (Axios).

- Gas and diesel prices are expected to surge further as geopolitical fallout intensifies (Washington Post); Chinese domestic oil prices have already hit a record high as Beijing is forced to buy on the open market after drawing down inventories aggressively (FT).

- Iran's economy is under severe strain — the US is blocking exports through Hormuz and land routes are increasingly compromised (WSJ). Ukraine struck a major Russian oil refinery (Bloomberg).

- Trump threatened new EU tariffs if the bloc deepens its relationship with Canada, calling it a "hostile act" (Bloomberg). The House passed a Russia sanctions bill giving Trump sweeping new tariff powers (Politico, The Atlantic).

- China cut its US Treasury holdings to an 18-year low in July (SCMP).

COMPANY NEWS

CRWV (CoreWeave) announced a ~$6B capital raise comprising a $3B convertible note and a 35-million-share ATM offering. Management's comfort launching at this scale signals confidence in market appetite and is broadly positive for the AI IPO pipeline.

GNRC (Generac) surged after announcing a backup power supply agreement with AMZN (Amazon), underscoring that hyperscaler data center buildout remains firmly on track.

EAT (Brinker International) laid out long-term guidance through F2029 at its analyst day: 4–6% annual revenue growth, double-digit EPS growth, and 3–5% annual share buybacks.

YETI outlined F2030 targets at its analyst day: 5–9% revenue CAGR and 10–19% EPS CAGR. At the ~15% midpoint and assuming ~$3 in 2026 EPS, that implies ~$5.25 in 2030 EPS — ahead of current consensus.

LEN (Lennar) missed FQ3 EPS with weakness across deliveries, margins, and orders; FQ4 guidance was also soft. The stock is shrugging it off Thursday as falling rates lift the broader housing complex.

CRM (Salesforce) reaffirmed its F2030 revenue target of $63B at its analyst day and expects organic growth to reaccelerate in H2:F27.

ARM — CEO said the company is more confident now than at the July earnings call about hitting its $2B data center chip revenue goal (CNBC).

AS (Amer Sports) pre-announced upside for FQ3: sales now seen +20–22% vs. prior guidance of +18–20%, with operating margins slightly above the high end of the 13.5–14% range.

ETN (Eaton) struck a bullish tone at the Morgan Stanley conference: "We feel really good about Q3…very strong July, very strong August…aiming at the high end of guidance."

WDAY (Workday) — CNBC's David Faber reported Thursday that LBO financing efforts are continuing.

GFS (GlobalFoundries) is in talks to operate a large new chip fab in the US that Japan may fund as part of a bilateral tariff agreement (Nikkei).

Huawei unveiled its Atlas 960 SuperPoD AI computing cluster and 11 new AI-related chips in a direct challenge to NVDA, INTC, and AMD (SCMP, Nikkei, Reuters).

Wall Street trading desks warned at sell-side conferences this week that the blockbuster Q2 trading revenues will not repeat in Q3 (FT). Payment giants MA, V, and AXP are all moving to facilitate agentic AI shopping as the industry scrambles to stay relevant (WSJ).

Holtec, the nuclear IPO, postponed its offering citing investor caution around data centers and the broader economy (FT).
