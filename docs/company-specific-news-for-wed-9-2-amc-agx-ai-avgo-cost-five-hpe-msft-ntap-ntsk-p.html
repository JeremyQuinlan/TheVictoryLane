COMPANY NEWS

COST (Costco) reported August US core comps ex-gas and FX of +5.6%, missing the Street's +6.0% estimate and decelerating from +6.9% in July — a modest but notable softening in the consumer spending trend.

FIVE (Five Below) delivered a blowout FQ2 with EPS of $1.68 vs. the Street's $1.39 and comps of +14.1% vs. the Street's +10.6%. Full-year guidance was raised to comps of +10–12% (from +6–8%) and EPS of $9.83–$10.31 (from $8.65–$9.05). Particularly impressive given store count grew 8.8% Y/Y to 2,022 locations.

PVH (PVH Corp.) posted FQ2 EPS of $3.70 vs. the Street's $3.08, though $1.80 of that beat came from tariff refunds. FX-neutral sales fell 3%, slightly better than the –4–5% guide, with Tommy outperforming and Calvin lagging. Full-year guidance was merely reaffirmed — a muted signal given the quality of the earnings beat.

AGX (Argan) crushed FQ2 EBITDA at $70MM vs. the Street's $49MM, with revenue of $384MM vs. $300MM expected and EBITDA margins expanding 200 basis points Y/Y. The one blemish: backlog slipped to $2.51B from $2.93B a year ago and $2.77B last quarter.

AI (C3.ai) posted EPS of –$0.20 vs. the Street's –$0.26 with inline sales. The full-year operating loss outlook improved slightly to –$123–$155MM from –$128–$160MM. The real headline: bookings spiked 73% Q/Q, suggesting the sales reorganization is gaining traction. Cash rose $76MM Q/Q to ~$650MM, roughly 39% of market cap.

AVGO (Broadcom) reported FQ3 EPS of $3.32 vs. $3.23 expected, with total revenue up 86% to $29.6B (vs. $29.5B). AI accelerators surged 221% to $16.7B, beating the $15.9B estimate, while software came in at $8.75B vs. $8.8B. The FQ4 guide of $34.8B in sales missed the Street's $35.05B. Bottom line: the AI accelerator trajectory is strong in absolute terms, but upside is thinning and software softness echoes some of the IBM-linked disappointment from July.

HPE (HP Enterprise) delivered a strong FQ3 beat — EPS of $1.11 vs. $0.93, revenue of $12.2B (+34% Y/Y) vs. $11.9B, with gross margins surging 1,050 basis points to 40.4% and operating margins up 770 basis points. FQ4 guidance calls for EPS of $1.25 vs. the Street's $1.07 and sales of $14.35B vs. $12.88B. Management guided F27 EPS to ~$4.50 vs. the Street's $4.11. Separately, HPE announced Oracle will adopt Juniper networking equipment in its data centers, with HPE issuing Oracle warrants as part of the deal. Networking did fall slightly short in the quarter — the Dell comparison still doesn't flatter HPE.

MSFT (Microsoft) announced a restructuring of its financial reporting starting in F27, collapsing the old three-segment structure into two: Agents & Infra and Devices & Consumer. In a new disclosure, Azure was revealed as the company's largest business at $101.9B in F26 revenue (~30% of total), with M365 Cloud close behind at $100.3B — together representing ~60% of the top line. The WSJ had recently criticized Microsoft's lack of Azure transparency, and this directly addresses that. Prior FQ1 guidance was reiterated. Investors should view this as unambiguously positive.

NTAP (NetApp) posted a strong FQ1 with EPS of $2.58 vs. $2.12 and revenue of $2.025B vs. $1.832B. Full-year guidance moved sharply higher to $7.975–$8.225B in sales (from $7.325–$7.575B) and EPS of $9.73–$10.03 (from $8.70–$9.00).

NTSK (Netskope) beat on FQ EPS at –$0.03 vs. –$0.07, with revenue up 29% to $221MM vs. $214MM expected. ARR rose 27% to $899MM, edging past the $893MM estimate. Full-year sales guide raised to $888–$892MM from $879–$883MM.

SNOW (Snowflake) delivered a standout beat — revenue up 35% to $1.55B vs. $1.485B, net revenue retention of 126%, and operating margins of 15.3% vs. the Street's 12.6%. Full-year product revenue growth guide raised to 36% from 31%, with operating margins moving to 14.5% from 13.5%. Management cited AI as a compounding flywheel. The one miss: RPOs came in at $9B vs. the Street's $9.3B. Valuation remains rich, but SNOW continues to prove it's a core AI infrastructure beneficiary.
