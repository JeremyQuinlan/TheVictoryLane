Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P up 49 points or 65 basis points, Nasdaq up 329 points or 110 basis points, and Russell 2000 up 47 basis points. Asian markets closed broadly higher led by Korea KOSPI up 1.65% and Hong Kong Hang Seng up 1.18%, while major Eurozone indices gained 85 to 120 basis points with strength in industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The primary driver behind Monday's strength appears to be optimism surrounding the UN General Assembly in New York this week, despite a weekend full of negative headlines including escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and Ukraine's largest drone strike on Russia.

Treasuries have a bid with yields down 2 to 3 basis points across the curve. The market is pricing in approximately 33 basis points of rate hikes over the Fed's final two meetings of the year, with roughly 50/50 odds of an increase at the October 28th meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of hikes priced in for 2027. Fed officials remain hawkish — Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari echoed those sentiments Sunday, saying inflation is reaching all corners of the US economy.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

The UN General Assembly running September 22 through 28 brings together leaders from Iran and key Middle East nations, creating a genuine window for diplomatic progress that could ease the Iran war premium baked into oil prices. Trump called off air strikes against the Houthis at the last minute Sunday and said he would be open to meeting Iran's president, suggesting a de-escalation path exists. Even a modest improvement in Middle East tensions would likely trigger an outsized drop in Brent crude, which at roughly $102 per barrel is already down on the week, potentially unleashing lower yields, better margins, and improved consumer confidence simultaneously. The Trump-Xi summit on Thursday September 24th also offers a near-term positive catalyst with modest tariff reductions possible on roughly $30 billion in goods.

BEAR CASE

Nearly all the weekend news flow was net negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia including targeting Riyadh's airport fuel depot, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia hitting oil refinery infrastructure, and Iran warned of a fierce response to any resumed US military action. The AI complex faces fresh headwinds after the Friday close brought a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI's expected negative free cash flow of approximately $280 billion from 2026 to 2030, and Oracle data center debt issues in New Mexico. Oil tanker costs are surging, motor oil is being rationed by US retailers, and the global refining capacity shock from the Russia-Ukraine conflict has not been priced in by corporate earnings yet — demand destruction looks increasingly inevitable as energy price spikes work through the system.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously mixed with a conditional bull bias — the key catalyst to watch today and this week is diplomatic progress at the UN General Assembly, specifically any signal of a Trump-Iran meeting or ceasefire movement that could trigger a meaningful drop in oil prices. If oil cracks lower, it unlocks a cascade of bullish macro effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, and improved consumer confidence, but the geopolitical backdrop remains fragile enough that a diplomatic disappointment could reverse today's gains quickly.
