Equal-weight S&P is the real story today, up 160 basis points and crushing the cap-weighted index — that spread tells you everything about what the Treasury buyback is doing to market internals.

---

RATES & FED

The Treasury's expanded buyback announcement is dragging long-end yields down sharply — 10-year off 6 basis points, 30-year down 10 basis points — while the 2-year ticks up 1 basis point, steepening the curve. The Fed pricing is essentially unchanged, with markets still discounting roughly 24 basis points of hikes by year-end. The rate move is the single biggest driver of today's rotation out of AI and into rate-sensitive equities.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Today's session is a textbook rotation trade: lower long-end yields are lifting housing, healthcare, autos, and non-AI tech while the crowded AI/data center complex continues its slide that started Tuesday on the negative OpenAI coverage (WSJ). The equal-weight S&P outperforming by a wide margin is constructive for breadth bulls, but don't mistake one day's rotation for a structural shift — the AI underperformance is being compounded by real concerns about revenue growth and competitive pricing pressure. The Canada tariff pause (WSJ) removes a near-term tail risk, though the Iran situation keeps energy and geopolitical risk premiums alive. Gold surging 3.45% alongside a weaker dollar suggests the macro unease hasn't gone away.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran's leadership is growing increasingly anxious about the economic toll of prolonged U.S. pressure, fearing domestic unrest if conditions worsen (Reuters).

- Iran continues striking shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, but Washington is not responding kinetically, raising regional alarm about U.S. strategy (WSJ).

- The UAE has suspended trade and commercial ties with Iran following Tehran's firing of two ballistic missiles toward the Gulf state (FT).

- Iran has threatened to target U.S. military installations in Europe if Trump escalates (FT).

- Fuel prices remain severely elevated not because crude is spiking but because global refining capacity has been gutted by the Iran and Ukraine wars, sending crack spreads surging — "The world isn't running out of oil. It is running out of places to turn that oil into fuel" (NYT).

- Trump paused 50% tariffs on select Canadian goods for three days while the two sides finalize a trade deal (WSJ).

- Pennsylvania Governor Shapiro is imposing stringent new data center development requirements, the latest in a wave of political pushback against the AI buildout (Washington Post).

COMPANY NEWS

ADI (Analog Devices) posted strong FQ3 results — EPS of $3.45 vs. Street $3.35, revenue up 40% to $4.02B vs. Street $3.92B, with beats across all segments and gross/operating margin expansion of 330 and 780 basis points Y/Y. FQ4 guidance of $3.86 EPS/$4.3B revenue also topped consensus of $3.55/$4.08B, yet the stock is underperforming — a sign expectations are simply too high for the sector.

EL (Estée Lauder) beat FQ4 EPS at 39c vs. Street 32c, with gross margins up 150 basis points and operating margins up 320 basis points. Tariff refunds helped, but management indicated outperformance would have occurred regardless. F27 operating margin guidance raised to 12.7–13.5%; sales growth midpoint in line at +4%.

LOW (Lowe's) posted a tariff refund-driven FQ2 EPS beat ($4.40 vs. Street $4.22, with 11c from refunds) but comps of +0.2% missed Street's +0.74% and trailed HD's +1.7%. Pro and online were bright spots; DIY remains soft. Full-year guidance was cut despite the refund benefit.

TGT (Target) delivered a beat-and-raise even stripping out tariff refunds — a genuinely solid print, though the stock's reaction reflects elevated expectations going in.

TJX beat on EPS, margins, and overall comps and raised EPS/margin guidance — tariff refunds or not, this would have been a beat-and-raise. The blemish: Marmaxx comps fell short of consensus.

MRNA/MRK — Moderna and Merck reported positive trial data from a joint mRNA-based neoantigen therapy combined with Keytruda in melanoma. MRNA is the top stock in the SPX by a wide margin; meaningful short interest is amplifying the move. Healthcare is one of the day's top sectors.

MRVL (Marvell) announced a custom semiconductor partnership with Google — one of the few AI-linked names in the green today.

VIK (Viking) posted Q2 sales of $2.19B (+16.5%, vs. Street $2.14B) and EBITDA of $748.4MM (+18.2%, vs. Street $719MM). Booking momentum is strong: 96% of 2026 capacity sold (vs. 92% in May) and 53% of 2027 (vs. 38% in May).

KEYS (Keysight) beat hard — FQ3 EPS $3.07 vs. Street $2.48, sales $1.85B vs. Street $1.745B — with bullish FQ4 guidance ($3.37/$1.94B midpoints vs. $2.68/$1.83B). Stock is still underperforming, which says a lot about where the bar sits for tech right now.

LZB (La-Z-Boy) missed FQ1 EPS (43c vs. 49c) and sales ($475MM vs. $501MM), with wholesale weakness overwhelming retail strength. FQ2 guide is soft.

NVDA — China slightly eased H200 restrictions; ByteDance and Tencent each received ~10K chips recently, with more firms potentially in line. Beijing is still firmly prioritizing domestic chip development (FT).

GOOGL was forced to pay nearly 7% on an Australian bond sale, underscoring how AI data center financing costs are surging (Bloomberg).

PGR (Progressive) reported July net premiums written up 5% Y/Y, but combined ratio deteriorated 150 basis points Y/Y, pushing EPS down 11% Y/Y.

VLTO (Veralto) announced a deal to acquire Cleanwater1 for a net purchase price of $452MM after tax benefits.

PPC (Pilgrim's Pride) — JBS offered to acquire the remaining PPC stake it doesn't own at 2.086 JBS shares per PPC share, roughly equivalent to ~$28.50/share, in line with Tuesday's close.

OpenAI's Q2 financials disappointed shareholders — revenue grew only 18% Q/Q while losses deepened, numbers that put the company behind Anthropic on growth metrics (WSJ). The AI model price war is intensifying, with OpenAI slashing prices on certain models via OpenRouter and gaining share as a result (The Information).
