Legacy software: Schneider Electric is nearing a deal to buy PTC for $20B+

Oct 04, 2026 · 06:17 PM ET

Here's the key development out of Sunday afternoon. Schneider Electric, the European industrial conglomerate, is reportedly nearing a deal to acquire PTC — the industrial software company — for more than twenty billion dollars. This comes from the Financial Times and represents a major potential transaction in the legacy software space. A twenty billion dollar equity value would put the acquisition price around one hundred eighty-five dollars per share for PTC. For context, PTC's all-time high was roughly two hundred fifteen dollars, hit back in August of last year, so the deal would come at a discount to peak but still represents a meaningful premium to where the stock has been trading. This is not happening in a vacuum. Over the past couple of months, a series of data points have been pushing legacy software and IT services stocks higher. Silver Lake's deal chatter around Workday first surfaced in mid-August. Salesforce reported a strong second quarter at the end of August. And just last week, Accenture delivered a solid fiscal fourth quarter. Taken together, these events have driven a real narrative pivot for the legacy software group. The fear was that artificial intelligence would rapidly disrupt and displace these incumbents. What's actually happening is more nuanced — AI hasn't been nearly as disruptive as feared in the near term, and in certain cases it's functioning as a genuine growth tailwind for these companies, making them more valuable, not less. The bull case here is straightforward. M&A activity validates the intrinsic value sitting inside these legacy platforms. Strategic buyers like Schneider are willing to pay twenty billion-plus to get their hands on industrial software capabilities, and that sends a signal to the entire sector. If PTC is worth that, the market needs to ask what Workday, Salesforce, and others are worth in a world where AI enhances rather than destroys their moats. The bear case is that deal chatter without a signed agreement can fade fast, and PTC's implied price is still well below its prior highs. AI risk hasn't disappeared — it's just been delayed. Any stumble in deal execution or a shift in macro sentiment could reverse these gains quickly. For traders, the focus today is on PTC directly and on read-through names across legacy software and services. Watch for any official confirmation or denial from either Schneider or PTC, and monitor how the broader group trades off this headline.
