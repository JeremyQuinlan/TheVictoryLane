RATES & FED

Treasuries are under pressure, with yields up 1-3 basis points across the curve, driven largely by the oil spike following Trump's "Economic D-Day" threat against Iran. The Fed funds market has 22 basis points of hikes priced across the final three meetings of the year, essentially unchanged. The FOMC minutes were modestly dovish — "most participants anticipated that inflation would step down over the rest of the year" and "several participants assessed that the pass-through of past tariff increases into prices was now largely complete" — though secular forces (massive issuance, war-driven inflation, AI capex debt) continue to dominate the rate picture.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The bull/bear tension right now is really an oil/inflation story wearing a geopolitical costume. Trump's "Economic D-Day" rhetoric keeps Brent elevated and crack spreads — which matter more for actual consumer fuel costs than headline crude — are hitting records, with diesel powering roughly 70% of freight, rail, ag, and construction (Barron's). The US gross national debt crossing $40T this week (doubling since January 2017, per the fastest non-COVID pace ever) is an ominous backdrop for anyone hoping yields roll over quietly. Treasury's buyback expansion and yen intervention support are well-intentioned but modest relative to the issuance tidal wave hitting from both sovereigns and AI-hungry corporates. The dovish lean in the FOMC minutes gives bulls something to hold onto, and a US-Canada trade deal cutting steel/aluminum tariffs to 25% and auto tariffs to 15% is genuinely constructive at the margin (Bloomberg) — but until the Iran situation shows a clear off-ramp, upside feels capped.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Trump announced "Economic D-Day" against Iran Wednesday evening, vowing severe economic consequences for any country providing Tehran financial assistance. Details remain vague, but Bessent had telegraphed an intensified pressure campaign last week (Vital-adjacent reporting, WSJ).

- The UAE suspended financial and economic transactions with Iran on Tuesday at Washington's urging — a significant blow given Dubai serves as Iran's primary lifeline to the global economy (WSJ).

- A US-created shipping corridor in the Strait of Hormuz is allowing approximately 10 million barrels per day to transit, more than most realize (NYT, Axios). Iran, meanwhile, is more privately worried about its deteriorating domestic economy than its public posture suggests (Reuters).

- North Korea dismissed Trump's overtures, denying any contact with Washington and downplaying reduced US-South Korean military drills. Seoul estimates Pyongyang may possess up to 120 nuclear warheads (FT, Bloomberg).

- China sentenced Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan to life in prison (WSJ). Separately, Beijing is slowing exports of key aerospace and optical materials to Taiwan (Nikkei), and China is closing the AI gap with the US — leading on several critical metrics (Bloomberg).

- Taiwan proposed its largest-ever defense budget at over $35B for 2027 (WSJ).

- Grain prices are spiking due to Russia-Ukraine war fallout — described as "worse for the wheat market than Hormuz was for crude" (FT).

- US-Canada trade deal appears close, with steel/aluminum tariffs potentially cut to 25% and auto tariffs to 15% from 25% (Bloomberg, Reuters).
