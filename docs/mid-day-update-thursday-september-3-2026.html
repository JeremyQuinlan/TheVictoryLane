RATES & FED

Treasuries are well bid, with yields down 2–4 basis points across the curve. The market is now pricing 33 basis points of hikes for the year (down from 40 basis points post-Jackson Hole), with roughly 50/50 odds on a September 16 rate increase. Fed Governor Waller drove the dovish shift, arguing underlying inflation is improving even more than the headlines suggest, citing distortions in nonmarket services.

MARKET OUTLOOK

Today's setup is about as good as it gets for risk — a dovish Fed pivot narrative, a surging yen removing upward pressure on global borrowing costs, and solid tech earnings underneath the surface noise. The Waller speech feels like a meaningful signal, not just noise, and the Williams comments Wednesday reinforce the direction of travel. The August jobs report Friday is the next real test; a soft number firms up the September pause case and likely extends the rally. Longer term, BOJ tightening pace bears watching — if Japan's capital repatriation accelerates, it reshapes the global rates picture in ways that aren't fully priced.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered to roughly 17–18 million barrels per day, not far from pre-conflict levels of 20 million, as the Pentagon continues degrading Iran's ability to control the waterway (CNN). Syria is emerging as a corridor helping Iraqi oil bypass Hormuz entirely (Washington Post).

- Trump has discussed with aides declaring the Iran war over, though the White House may hold off on escalating kinetic activity until after the midterms (WSJ, Reuters). Separately, the Pentagon is extending Middle East troop deployments into 2027, keeping military options open (WSJ).

- The BOJ is said to favor a 25 basis point hike later this month and may accelerate tightening thereafter — Japan's elevated JGB yields are already triggering a reversal of the long-running capital outflow from the country (Bloomberg, Reuters).

- China's Caixin services PMI for August beat at 51.4 versus the 50.6 consensus (Bloomberg). Switzerland's August CPI surprised to the upside at +0.8% year-over-year versus +0.5% expected (Bloomberg).

COMPANY NEWS

NVDA formally announced a $13 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, deepening its open-weight AI footprint in a deal that had been circulating in the media for weeks.

AVGO reported mostly inline FQ3 results; the FQ4 and F27 guide was underwhelming, though the F28 outlook carried a bullish tone. Stock is among the session's biggest laggards.

CIEN posted strong FQ3 EPS of $2.11 versus the $1.74 consensus on sales of $1.67 billion (+37% year-over-year), with gross margins up 450 basis points and operating margins surging 1,180 basis points year-over-year. FQ4 revenue guidance of $1.75 billion tops the $1.70 billion estimate — yet the stock is selling off hard, a frustrating post-earnings reaction given the fundamental quality of the print.

HPE delivered solid FQ3 EPS/sales upside with bullish FQ4 and F27 guidance, but like CIEN, the stock is getting hit — a pattern worth noting across hardware names today.

NTAP reported blowout FQ1 EPS of $2.58 versus $2.12 expected and revenue of $2.025 billion versus $1.832 billion, with full-year guidance raised. Clear outperformer.

SNOW posted FQ results with sales up 35% to $1.55 billion (versus $1.485 billion expected), net revenue retention of 126%, and operating margins of 15.3% versus 12.6% expected. Full-year product revenue growth guide raised to 36% from 31%.

BRC beat FQ4 EPS ($1.48 versus $1.35) and sales ($437 million versus $428 million), with F27 EPS guidance midpoint of $6.50 above the $6.39 consensus.

COST reported August US core comps (ex-gas/FX) of +5.6%, missing the +6.0% estimate and decelerating from +6.9% in July.

AMZN projects 90% of US packages will be delivered through its own logistics network by 2029 (Business Insider).

F is targeting 100,000+ unit sales in year one for its new ~$30,000 Ford Fathom EV pickup — a bold goal (WSJ).

AGX blew out FQ2 EBITDA at $70 million versus $49 million expected, on revenue of $384 million versus $300 million, though backlog declined both sequentially and year-over-year.
