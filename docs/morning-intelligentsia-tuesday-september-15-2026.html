RATES & FED

Treasuries are under pressure, with yields rising 1–4 basis points across the curve; 10-year yields in Europe and Japan are up 4–6 basis points as sovereign debt sells off globally. Markets are pricing 53 basis points of Fed hikes across the final three meetings of the year, with better than 90% odds of a hike on Wednesday. The Warsh-Trump truce is looking fragile — WSJ notes Trump has tempered his Fed criticism for months, but a rate hike could reignite that war.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The oil-yield doom loop is the defining macro dynamic right now, and neither pressure point is close to resolving. With Brent above $107 and yields creeping higher globally, equity sentiment has very little room to breathe — particularly ahead of Wednesday's FOMC where the dots and Warsh's presser carry real risk of surprising hawkishly. The AI frontier pacing debate has quieted somewhat overnight, and while headline risk stays elevated, capex doesn't appear to be slowing and draconian regulation isn't coming — but Monday's weakness in pick-and-shovel names is not a buying opportunity. If oil gets addressed (diplomatically or otherwise), a lot of the macro overhang lifts quickly; until then, the path of least resistance is lower.

GEOPOLITICAL

- The Middle East energy situation is at near-crisis levels: Hormuz remains under assault, Houthis now control Yemen's entire west coast (commanding Red Sea shipping leverage), and Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline is offline. Saudi Arabia is in a state of "shock" at how swiftly the Houthis expanded and at the lack of U.S. support (NYT).

- Trump said Iran "wants to make a deal, quickly and badly" and the U.S. is open to reengaging Tehran; VP Vance said the U.S. is speaking directly to the Houthis and feels "on top of the situation" — though the Pentagon's inspector general warns the Iran war has produced a munitions shortage and supply chain bottlenecks (NBC News).

- Ukraine hit Russia's Syzran oil refinery Tuesday morning per Zelensky, even as Trump claimed Monday that both sides agreed to halt energy infrastructure strikes. Kyiv says it would stop if Russia does — but is skeptical Moscow will comply (CNBC).

- Putin warned Europe that deploying even peacekeeping troops to Ukraine would mean war (Washington Post). Treasury separately sanctioned Russia's VTB Bank for allegedly financing Iran (NYT).

- Japan considering raising defense spending to 3.5% of GDP — Bloomberg calls it a potential "shockwave" for markets already anxious about fiscal trajectory. Cabinet also approved cutting the food sales tax to 1% from 8%. Both moves underscore that G7 governments remain completely unserious about fiscal discipline at precisely the moment bond markets are revolting (Bloomberg).
