Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with the S&P up 49 points or 65 basis points, Nasdaq up 329 points or 110 basis points, and the Russell 2000 up 47 basis points. European indices are up 85 to 120 basis points and Asian markets posted broad gains overnight led by Korea's KOSPI up 165 basis points and Hong Kong's Hang Seng up 118 basis points. The rally is somewhat puzzling given a weekend full of negative headlines — escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, a massive Ukrainian drone strike on Russia, and a slew of troubling AI-sector developments — with the primary driver appearing to be optimism around diplomatic progress at the UN General Assembly this week.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2 to 3 basis points across the curve. The Fed remains in hawkish mode — markets are pricing roughly 33 basis points of additional rate hikes across the final two 2026 meetings, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of increases priced into 2027. Fed Presidents Schmid and Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that elevated inflation is broad-based and not solely driven by energy prices. Separately, Treasury plans to issue over one trillion dollars in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24 percent of marketable debt and above the Treasury's own 20 percent target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly every macro headwind the market faces right now traces back to elevated oil prices, and even a modest de-escalation at the UN General Assembly — where Iran's president is expected to attend alongside Trump — could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices. Lower oil would cascade positively into yields, inflation, margins, and consumer confidence simultaneously. Trump called off Houthi airstrikes at the last minute and signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, suggesting a potential opening for diplomacy. China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, adding international weight behind a potential de-escalation.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape remains deeply troubled — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia over the weekend, Ukraine launched its largest ever drone strike on Russia taking out refinery capacity, and Tehran is warning of a fierce response to any resumed US military action. Weekend AI headlines were uniformly negative, including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI's staggering projected negative free cash flow of 280 billion dollars through 2030, and Oracle data center debt concerns. On trade, the US-China truce extension was not reached Sunday and Washington is expected to raise the aggregate China tariff rate back to pre-SCOTUS IEEPA levels in coming weeks. The current equity rally looks like it is pricing in a diplomatic outcome that is far from certain.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously mixed to slightly bullish for Monday's session, with the key catalyst being diplomatic developments at the UN General Assembly — specifically any signal of progress on Iran or Middle East tensions that could pull oil prices meaningfully lower. The rally feels fragile given the overwhelmingly negative weekend news backdrop, and the real upside would come from an oil price shock to the downside driven by geopolitical de-escalation rather than any fundamental improvement in earnings or macro data.

Sector Watch

AI and data center names face negative sentiment after a weekend wave of troubling headlines including a delayed Anthropic IPO, weak Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI's massive projected cash burn of 280 billion dollars through 2030, and Oracle data center debt concerns — yet data center pick-and-shovel stocks are actually outperforming in European trading Monday, suggesting the market may be shrugging off some of the noise. Energy is sliding with Brent crude down 170 basis points to around 102.10, extending losses from last week, as the market prices in some diplomatic optimism ahead of the UN General Assembly. Chinese memory supply from CXMT entering mass production on its next-generation platform could pressure pricing for DRAM players including Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Financials in Europe are seeing a boost from SocGen's bullish capital markets day targets including return on tangible equity of 13 to 14 percent in 2029 and above 15 percent thereafter.
