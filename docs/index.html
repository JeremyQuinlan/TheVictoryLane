Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points, Nasdaq +329 points, and Russell 2000 +13.5 points, despite a weekend dominated by negative headlines including geopolitical escalation and troubling AI-sector news. Asian markets posted broad gains led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while European indices are up 85-120 basis points. The primary driver of morning strength appears to be diplomatic optimism ahead of the UN General Assembly in New York, where Trump signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, raising hopes for some de-escalation in the Middle East conflict.

Treasury yields are down 2-3 basis points across the curve, providing a modest bid to risk assets. Fed expectations remain hawkish — the market is pricing roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes across the final two 2026 meetings, with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, plus an additional 35 basis points of increases penciled in for 2027. Fed's Schmid and Kashkari both made hawkish comments over the weekend, warning that inflation is elevated beyond just oil and is spreading across the broader economy. Treasury plans to issue over one trillion dollars in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt, above its own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is the primary near-term catalyst, with Trump signaling willingness to meet Iran's president and reports that China and Pakistan are pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis. Even a small de-escalation in the Middle East could trigger an outsized drop in oil from current levels around $102 per barrel, which would cascade into lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. The upcoming Trump-Xi summit on September 24th could also produce modest tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion worth of goods, adding another incremental positive. Treasuries are catching a bid and yields are slightly lower, supporting equity valuations at the margin.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was almost uniformly negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia including hitting a fuel depot at Riyadh's airport, Ukraine launched its largest ever drone strike on Russia targeting oil infrastructure, and a cascade of bearish AI headlines hit after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI burning through roughly $280 billion in free cash flow over 2026-2030, and Oracle data center debt hitting a wall. Monetary policy globally remains firmly hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month and multiple additional hikes expected across major central banks in the weeks ahead. The rally feels like it is front-running diplomatic progress that may never materialize, and even if a deal is struck, the Iran war has too many fronts for a quick resolution.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday's session, driven almost entirely by geopolitical optionality — specifically the hope that the UN General Assembly produces some form of Middle East diplomatic progress that could trigger a sharp drop in oil prices. However, he frames this as fragile optimism given that the underlying news flow is net negative, AI capex concerns are mounting, and the Iran war continues to escalate. The key catalyst to watch is any signal out of the UN General Assembly regarding Iran talks or a potential Trump-Iran meeting.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in European trading Monday morning. AI sector faces mounting pressure from multiple weekend headlines including delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt stress — the combined off-balance sheet AI liability exposure for Big Tech has risen roughly $300 billion in just the last 12 months. Energy is under pressure with Brent crude sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10 despite Houthi escalation, as diplomatic optimism offsets geopolitical risk premium. Industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks are outperforming in Europe while utilities, energy, healthcare, real estate, retail, and luxury lag.
