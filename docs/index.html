Macro · Rates

US equity futures are in the red Thursday morning with the S&P down 44 points, Nasdaq off 105 basis points, and European indices sliding 50-60 basis points led by weakness in industrials, autos, tech, and banks. Asian markets were mostly lower overnight with China's Shanghai Composite falling 1.22% and Shenzhen dropping nearly 2%. The key macro driver is Brent crude spiking 275 basis points back to roughly $106 as US-Iran diplomatic talks show little real progress, compounding the yield pressure already set in motion by Wednesday's blowout flash PMIs.

Two-year Treasury yields are flat while 10 and 30-year yields climb 2-3 basis points, extending gains from Wednesday's hot PMI data. The market is now pricing in 38 basis points of incremental Fed hikes this year with odds of an October 28th increase above 70%, plus an additional roughly 55 basis points in 2027. Fed Governor Barr on Wednesday became the latest official to signal further tightening is needed, and the Japanese yen continues to weaken, creeping back toward 160 versus the dollar.

Market Outlook

Adam is bearish-leaning heading into Thursday's session. The dual headwinds of rising oil prices and climbing long-end yields — both driven by geopolitical stalemate on Iran — are the primary pressure points, and the Trump-Xi summit is expected to disappoint with only a modest two-month extension of the Busan trade agreement. The key catalyst to watch is the Trump-Xi meeting at the White House starting around 10am Eastern, alongside any developments on the Iran diplomatic front.

Sector Watch

Energy is the key sector to watch — Brent back at $106 is a tailwind for energy stocks but a headwind for the broad market via yields and inflation. Tech and AI-linked names face headwind from net negative industry news flow over the past week, AI discounting trends reported by The Information, and a Meta keynote that failed to exceed expectations. Industrials are underperforming in Europe. Consumer discretionary faces pressure from McDonald's guidance cut. Healthcare gets a modest positive from Grail's FDA advisory committee recommendation. Nicotine and tobacco names could see upside if the FDA moves to ease restrictions on vape and pouch product authorizations.

VK · Key Names
◆ META Meta Platforms Product Launch

Zuckerberg's Meta Connect keynote Wednesday night was viewed as at best inline with expectations. While AI narrative around Muse's momentum remains positive, the broader AI industry news flow over the past week has been net negative in aggregate, limiting the upside from the event.

▼ BA Boeing Other

Boeing will likely not receive any incremental plane orders from China during the Trump-Xi summit, with the company instead focused on finalizing the 200-unit order announced back in May. This removes a key positive catalyst that markets had been anticipating from the high-profile diplomatic meeting.

▲ GOOGL Alphabet Product Launch

Google is nearing the release of its next-generation flagship frontier model, Gemini 4, according to the head of its DeepMind unit. This positions Google competitively in the accelerating frontier model race, though the broader AI news flow backdrop remains net negative for the sector.

◆ GRAL Grail FDA Approval

An FDA advisory committee recommended approval for Grail's multicancer early detection blood test, though support was described as somewhat tepid regarding effectiveness and overall benefits. The outcome was largely expected following the earlier publication of briefing documents, limiting incremental upside surprise.

▲ KNF Knife River Activist Entry

Starboard Value is pushing Knife River to explore a sale, adding activist pressure to the construction materials company. This is a classic activist-driven strategic review catalyst that typically provides a floor under the stock price.

▼ MCD McDonald's Guidance Cut

McDonald's shares slumped after the company warned at its analyst meeting that Q3 comparable sales are tracking slightly below consensus and that free cash flow conversion will come in below prior guidance due to higher capital spending. The company did offer a 2030 operating margin target ahead of expectations, but near-term disappointment dominated the reaction.

◆ MGM MGM Resorts M&A

Barry Diller on Wednesday withdrew People Inc's proposed takeout bid for MGM Resorts but stated he remains open to and interested in a strategic transaction with the casino giant. The withdrawal removes a hard bid catalyst, though Diller's continued interest keeps some takeover premium in the stock.

◆ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is looking to raise equity as the Warner deal moves closer to completion and is reportedly considering approaching Elon Musk for an investment to bolster its balance sheet. Equity issuance is typically dilutive, though the strategic rationale around the Warner deal could provide longer-term upside.

▲ FUL HB Fuller Earnings Beat

HB Fuller reported modest upside on EPS of $1.52 versus the Street's $1.46, driven by margin expansion with gross margins up 120 basis points and EBITDA margins up 80 basis points as pricing increases offset raw material inflation. Sales were inline at $938 million and full-year guidance was left roughly unchanged with the EPS midpoint moving slightly to $4.77 from $4.75.

▲ LLY Eli Lilly Partnership

Eli Lilly struck a research collaboration and licensing agreement with China's InnoCare Pharma worth up to $3.35 billion. The deal expands Lilly's pipeline exposure and reflects continued aggressive investment in broadening its therapeutic reach beyond its blockbuster GLP-1 franchise.
