RATES & FED

Treasuries catching a modest bid Thursday morning with yields down 3-4.5 basis points across the curve. Markets are pricing roughly 33 basis points of additional hikes across the final two 2026 meetings (Oct 28 and Dec 9), plus another ~38 basis points into 2027 — notably more aggressive than the Fed's own dots, which called for just one more 25 basis point move this year and nothing in 2027. Trump says he still backs Warsh despite the hike, blaming the board rather than the chair (WSJ).

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The Fed delivered a mixed verdict Wednesday — relatively dovish on the supplemental but hawkish in the press conference — and the net read is constructive: the Fed rebuilt credibility without materially burdening the economy, which should push long-end yields lower over time. That said, oil is the real variable here, and with Brent still above $100 and geopolitical risks skewed to the upside, the ceiling on any rates rally is real. Stocks are right to bounce this morning on the crude dip and yield pullback, but don't confuse a relief rally with a trend change. The next major signposts are Aug PCE (Sep 30), the Sep jobs report (Oct 2), and Sep CPI (Oct 14) — until those land, this market stays in data-watching mode.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Saudi Arabia hopes to bring roughly half the East-West Pipeline back online within days and is offering ship-to-ship oil transfers off Oman's Sohar port to ease supply strains (Bloomberg, Reuters) — modest downward pressure on Brent, but structurally the situation remains precarious.

- Trump plans to meet Gulf leaders on the UN GA sidelines in New York next week; a deal does not appear imminent (Axios). His "war will end soon" rhetoric Wednesday night moved markets briefly but deserves serious skepticism at this point.

- Iran's economy is under severe strain — the US is blocking Hormuz exports and land routes are also failing Tehran (WSJ). The GCC-Iran meeting still hasn't been rescheduled.

- The US military is burning through interceptor stocks defending Hormuz while Houthi forces now control western Yemen, tightening their grip on Red Sea shipping (WSJ, CBS News). The Pentagon simply cannot cover Hormuz, the Red Sea, and the East-West Pipeline simultaneously.

- Ukraine struck a major Russian oil refinery Thursday morning; Russia hit Kyiv in response (Bloomberg) — adding further diesel supply pressure globally.

- Chinese oil prices hit a record high as the country buys on the open market after aggressively drawing down inventories (FT). Gas and diesel prices are set to surge further in coming weeks (Washington Post).

- Continental Resources signed an MOU to develop a Venezuelan oilfield (FT); Exxon is nearing a preliminary deal on several Venezuelan fields (WSJ) — both moves signal Washington is quietly opening the door to Venezuelan supply.

- House passed a Russia sanctions bill granting Trump sweeping new tariff powers (Politico, The Atlantic). Trump also threatened fresh EU tariffs if the bloc deepens ties with Canada (Bloomberg).

- China cut its US Treasury holdings to an 18-year low in July (SCMP).

COMPANY NEWS
