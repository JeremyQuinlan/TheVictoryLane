RATES & FED

Treasuries sold off meaningfully Monday, with yields climbing 4–6 basis points across the curve — largely a direct response to the spike in oil prices. The Fed pricing barely budged; markets are now pricing 31 basis points of hikes across the final three meetings of the year, with odds of a September 16 hike sitting at roughly 50/50.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Stocks held in reasonably well given the trifecta of headwinds — surging oil, rising yields, and a weaker yen — but the path of least resistance remains challenged. The yield problem isn't going away: upward pressure is coming from elevated inflation, geopolitical risk, AI capital demand, sovereign bond supply, and Japan's FX situation simultaneously. CPI this week is only expected to cool 10 basis points in July — still well above target — so don't expect the Fed to ride to the rescue. The AI boom is real (TSMC's July revenue +45% confirms it), but the capital model is becoming a risk in itself: when Nvidia has to arrange $500B in vendor financing to keep data center orders flowing, that's not a sign of strength, it's a sign of fragility at the margins.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran's Supreme National Security Council laid out a series of extreme, largely impractical demands before agreeing to reopen Hormuz — walking back momentum from last week's apparent progress. Trump's response Monday was equally maximalist, demanding Iran pay damages for 50 years of regime-attributed deaths (NYT, WSJ). Bulls note Tehran has strong economic incentives to deal, with Iranian oil terminals looking increasingly idle under the US naval blockade (Bloomberg).

- General Dan Caine, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, has been privately urging the White House to find an off-ramp from the Iran conflict, warning that escalatory options are exhausted and airstrike effectiveness has peaked (CNN). The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve has fallen below 300 million barrels — the lowest since 1983 — further constraining Washington's options (CNBC).

- The Houthis in Yemen are growing more aggressive, attacking the formal Yemeni government and Saudi Arabia as the fragile ceasefire breaks down (NYT). American weapons stockpiles have dropped to "troubling" levels, a dynamic that emboldens Beijing and Moscow and gives Tehran more negotiating leverage (NYT).

- UAE's ADNOC, freed from OPEC quotas, announced $8B+ in production expansion and is exploring an LNG export facility that bypasses Hormuz entirely — the world is already moving to route around Iran's leverage (WSJ, Bloomberg).

- BOJ's July meeting summary showed growing official concern about inflation, raising the odds of a September rate hike. A stronger yen remains a risk for Treasury markets if Japan is forced to sell US paper to fund currency intervention (Reuters).

- China's July inflation undershot on both PPI (+3.5% vs. +3.9% expected) and CPI (+0.5% vs. +0.8% expected), signaling demand remains soft despite the AI-driven export surge (Reuters).

- France's budget minister warned the country cannot defer fiscal consolidation until after the 2027 presidential election, urging politicians to make hard choices now (FT). Private credit is showing "increasing signs of stress" as defaults continue to climb (WSJ).

COMPANY NEWS

NVDA — Wall Street giants including Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman, and KKR are in talks with Nvidia on a ~$500B financing package for future AI data center development — extending Nvidia's balance sheet in a major way to support key customers (FT). Separately, the stock faced selling pressure on the day as the headline raised balance sheet sustainability questions.

INTC — Intel announced a $15B common stock offering to fund growth opportunities while maintaining investment-grade status. The raise exemplifies the AI industry's voracious capital appetite — companies are spending well beyond operating cash flow and leaning on equity and debt markets to bridge the gap.

MSFT — Microsoft plans to dramatically scale production of its proprietary Maia AI chips, targeting capacity at TSMC for more than 300,000 units — a major jump from current volumes — as it seeks to reduce dependence on Nvidia silicon (The Information).

META — Zuckerberg published a 6,500-word essay outlining Meta's AI vision and plans to release a new open-weight model called Muse Glimmer, plus an open-weight version of its most advanced model, Muse Spark 1.2, in the coming weeks (WSJ, Meta).

AAON — Q2 EPS of 69c and revenue of $627MM demolished Street estimates of 51c/$508MM. Full-year sales guidance raised to +55–60% from +40–55%, though backlog dipped slightly to $2B from $2.1B and gross margin guidance was trimmed to 25–26% from 27–28%. Stock was an earnings-linked underperformer, suggesting the GM cut and backlog dip spooked investors.

FERG — Ferguson posted a clean beat-and-raise: Q2 EPS of $3.39 vs. $3.32 expected, revenue +4.6% to $8.75B vs. $8.58B expected, driven by gross margins of 31% vs. 30.6% expected. Residential returned to growth despite a tough housing backdrop. Full-year sales guidance raised to +5% from +1–5%, operating margins guided to 9.5–9.8% vs. prior 9.4–9.8%.

BRK/B — Berkshire reported solid Q2 operating income beat, and management deployed a meaningful portion of its massive cash hoard. The headline beat was real but not as dramatic as initial coverage implied; economically sensitive businesses performed well.

TSM — July revenue rose ~45% year-over-year, confirming AI demand remains robust and on-trend (Bloomberg).

ACHR — Archer Aviation will acquire Boeing's Wisk Aero, SkyGrid, and Insitu subsidiaries in exchange for shares equal to 19.75% of Class A shares outstanding plus two warrants.

EBAY — GameStop's Ryan Cohen is reportedly weighing withdrawal of his $5.6B bid for eBay (Bloomberg).

HZO — MarineMax is nearing a deal to be acquired by Blackstone's Safe Harbor Marinas for $53/share in cash (Reuters).

BWMN — Bowman Consulting will be acquired by Bernhard Capital Partners for $43/share cash, valuing the deal at ~$1B.

VREX — Varex Imaging will be acquired by TDY (Teledyne) for $18.90/share — a massive premium to Friday's close of $12.41 — in a deal worth $1.1B.

JAZZ — Jazz Pharma agreed to acquire Actio Biosciences for $820M upfront plus up to $500M in contingent payments.

MNDY — Monday.com reported solid Q2 EPS and revenue upside, and raised full-year operating margin guidance, but left revenue guidance unchanged and Q3 revenue guidance of $368–370M came in light vs. the Street's $373M.
