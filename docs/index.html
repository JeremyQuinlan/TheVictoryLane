Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

The September jobs report came in weak on the Establishment Survey at +29K, well below the consensus of +90K and down from +133K in August, with negative revisions to July and August totaling 60K. However, the Household Survey was notably strong with a 406K jump in employed people and the participation rate jumping 20 basis points to 61.8%. The knee-jerk market reaction is dovish, which should provide a tailwind for equities given how much rising yields have pressured sentiment recently.

Average hourly wage growth was soft at +0.1% month-over-month versus the expected +0.3%, and +3.0% year-over-year versus +3.1% expected, supporting a dovish rates narrative. Fed Funds Rate expectations have shifted in a dovish direction over the last few days, reinforced by cooler August PCE data and relatively dovish remarks from Williams and Jefferson. The rough consensus still calls for a single 25 basis point hike this year and two 25 basis point increases in 2027, and this report alone is unlikely to shift that materially absent sustained disinflation evidence.

Market Outlook

Leaning cautiously bullish on the session given the dovish read from the weak Establishment Survey, soft wages, and the resulting relief on yields. The key catalyst to watch is whether the bond market sustains a rally on this data or fades it given the Fed's clear inflation-first mandate — October CPI on October 14th is the next major inflection point.
