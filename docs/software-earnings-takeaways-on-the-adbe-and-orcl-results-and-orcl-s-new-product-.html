MARKET OUTLOOK

The software sector remains caught between genuine fundamental strength and unresolved AI disruption risk — that tension is exactly what ADBE's "OK but not good enough" quarter illustrates. ORCL is executing at an exceptional level, but the market will demand the F27 guide get revised more aggressively given the magnitude of FQ1 outperformance. The PLTR competitive threat from Oracle's new AI Data Platform is real and shouldn't be dismissed as noise — when a hyperscaler-adjacent giant explicitly names you and says they've automated your core value proposition, that's a stock-level risk worth pricing in.

COMPANY NEWS

ADBE (Adobe) posted FQ3 EPS of $6.13 vs. the Street at $6.08, with total sales up 13% to $6.76B against consensus of $6.7B. ARR came in at $27.5B (Street $27.43B), AI-first ARR surged more than 150% year-over-year (though still modest at above $650MM), and creative freemium MAUs crossed 100M, growing more than 70% year-over-year. The buyback remained robust at 9.6M shares repurchased in the quarter. On the negative side, RPOs were light at $22.16B (up only 8% on an FX-neutral basis), and the FQ4 revenue guide of $6.80–6.85B barely meets the Street's $6.85B midpoint expectation. Bottom line: a passable quarter that does nothing to lift the existential AI overhang on the stock — expect knee-jerk selling, particularly after the recent run-up.

ORCL (Oracle) delivered a standout FQ1, with EPS of $1.92 on an FX-neutral basis (up 30%) crushing the Street's $1.75 estimate. Total sales growth of 30% FX-neutral came in approximately 180 basis points ahead of expectations, with operating margins of 42.1% topping consensus at 40.8%. IaaS surged roughly 500 basis points above plan. RPOs exploded to $664B, up approximately $210B year-over-year and above the $640B consensus — notably, management says this backlog expansion carries "no incremental impact on its plans to raise capital." Free cash flow significantly beat at negative $5B vs. the Street's negative $10B, powered by operating cash flow up 184% to $23B; capex, however, ran hot at $28.5B vs. the $19.7B consensus. Oracle sold $20B of equity via its ATM program during the quarter. The FQ2 guide calls for total sales up 32% (roughly 100 basis points ahead of plan) and Cloud revenue up 67% (about 180 basis points above consensus), with EPS of approximately $1.89 vs. the Street's $1.90. F27 guidance was nudged to "at least $90B" in sales with EPS of $8.10 vs. the prior $8.05 — a modest revision that may underwhelm given the scale of FQ1 upside.

PLTR (Palantir) faces a direct competitive challenge after Oracle announced its new AI Data Platform, which Oracle explicitly describes as automating the creation of Enterprise Ontologies — the same core capability Palantir built its enterprise AI franchise around. Oracle's framing was pointed: "Palantir pioneered the use of AI on private enterprise data... The new Oracle AI Data Platform fully automates the creation of Enterprise Ontologies — making it inexpensive, easy and fast." This is a material competitive development and warrants scrutiny on PLTR's moat narrative.
