Macro · Rates

The September Dallas Fed Manufacturing Index echoed the themes from last week's flash PMIs, painting a picture of scorching hot growth paired with elevated inflation and margin pressure. This combination is net negative for equity sentiment. The production index spiked 13 points to 29.5, signaling robust output expansion, while inflation readings are running well above historical averages.

The flash PMIs released last Wednesday caused yields to surge, and the Dallas Fed data reinforces that inflationary pressure narrative. Raw material prices jumped 8 points to 52.2, well above the series average of 28, which complicates the Fed's path. No direct Fed commentary in this note, but the stagflationary undertone keeps rate cut expectations under pressure.

Market Outlook

Bearish lean for today's session. The confluence of hot growth, elevated inflation, and margin compression signals a difficult environment for equities. The key catalyst to watch is whether bond markets react further to this data, pushing yields higher and adding additional pressure to risk assets.

Sector Watch

Manufacturing sector faces a difficult backdrop — strong output growth is being offset by surging raw material costs that cannot be fully passed through to finished goods prices, creating meaningful margin headwinds across industrial and manufacturing names.
