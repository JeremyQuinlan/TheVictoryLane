RATES & FED

The 2-year yield jumped 6 basis points on a hawkish read of Fed Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole speech, while 10-year yields held flat and 30-year yields dipped 3 basis points. Markets are now pricing roughly 35 basis points of hikes this year — up from 27 basis points pre-Jackson Hole — with September odds around 55%. The equal-weight S&P going flat while the R2K slides tells you this is a liquidity-driven, mega-cap story today, not a broad risk-on move.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Don't let the word "hawkish" spook you here — a one-and-done or two-and-done mini tightening cycle is actually constructive for equities if it rebuilds monetary credibility and anchors long-end yields lower without gutting the underlying economy. Warsh was explicit that inflation is the Fed's focus ("on the employment side…our country is doing well…on the price-stability side, the numbers are more concerning"), and that clarity matters. The soft Chicago PMI for August is a concern, but the tumbling 1-year Michigan inflation expectation is an encouraging offset. Tech earnings were decent-to-great this cycle but several names (MRVL, ADSK, S) failed to clear elevated bars — a reminder that valuation discipline still matters even in a bull market.

GEOPOLITICAL

- US-Iran diplomacy has effectively stalled — Qatar and Pakistan are attempting mediation but there are no substantive Washington-Tehran communications occurring (Reuters). Separately, Admiral Brad Cooper confirmed the Strait of Hormuz is now mine-free (Bloomberg), though more oil than commonly assumed is already moving out of the region.

- Putin reportedly views the US as strategically weakened by the Iran war and is eyeing opportunities to capitalize on Washington's perceived setback — the Russia-Ukraine situation is described as "dangerously deteriorating" (Washington Post).

- Venezuela is "closely examining" an OPEC exit as it deepens ties with the US, potentially delivering another blow to the cartel's cohesion (Bloomberg).

- Canada signaled trade negotiations with the US could resume after Washington backed away from challenging Canadian French-language and cultural protection laws (NYT).

- The White House is drafting new AI chip restriction rules targeting Chinese firms that access advanced American silicon remotely via data centers in third countries like Thailand and Singapore (The Information).

- European inflation is heating up: France's EU-harmonized CPI hit +2.7% Y/Y in August vs. +2.6% expected (Bloomberg); Spain came in at +4.5%, above July's +3.9% (Bloomberg). Tokyo CPI was inline at +1.9% headline and +2.0% core (Bloomberg). Rising yields globally are being driven by stronger growth expectations, not fiscal or credibility fears (FT).

- Nippon Life, Japan's largest life insurer, signaled it could become a net buyer of JGBs next fiscal year at current yield levels (Bloomberg).

COMPANY NEWS

WDAY (Workday) beat FQ2 EPS at $2.75 vs. the Street's $2.61, with operating margins of 31.1% — 110 basis points ahead of consensus. Full-year subscription revenue guidance nudged to $9.94–9.95B with operating margins lifted to 31% from 30.5%. The Silver Lake speculation came up on the call; management said "no comment."

AFRM (Affirm) delivered strong FQ4 results: operating margins of 30% were ~140 basis points ahead of plan, RLTC grew +39% to $589MM vs. $548MM expected, and GMV rose +36% to $14.1B vs. $13.4B expected. F27 guidance was solid on margins and roughly inline on GMV.

ULTA (Ulta Beauty) posted Q2 EPS of $6.55 (+13.3% vs. Street $6.19) with comps of +3.8% vs. +2.4% expected, and raised full-year guidance. The only blemish was a small gross margin miss.

GAP reported earnings upside driven by margin strength; sales came in slightly light with Old Navy and Athleta dragging on Gap brand strength. Full-year EPS guidance was raised and Old Navy's management team is being overhauled.

ESTC (Elastic) beat FQ1 EPS at $0.70 vs. $0.59 expected, with sales +15% to $478MM (vs. $470MM). Full-year revenue growth guidance raised to +15.3% FXN from +14.5%, with operating margins guided to 19.4% from 19.0%.

ADSK (Autodesk) beat FQ2 EPS on margin strength but the full-year revenue and billings guide was only modestly tweaked higher; operating margin forecast left unchanged — the bar was too high.

MRVL (Marvell) barely cleared FQ2 estimates — EPS of $0.94 vs. $0.93, revenue +37% to $2.739B vs. $2.711B, GMs of 58.9% vs. 58.8% expected. FQ3 guide roughly matched print estimates; stock is down on underwhelming upside.

RBRK (Rubrik) beat on all major FQ2 metrics — sales, ARR, and especially operating margins — and raised full-year guidance: ARR of $1.88–1.885B (prior $1.854–1.862B), EPS of $0.50 (prior $0.30). Still trading lower, likely victim of high expectations.

S (SentinelOne) reported modest FQ2 upside on EPS, revenue, and ARR, but the full-year EPS guide was cut to $0.30–0.32 from $0.32–0.38 due to share count dilution — that's the detail that stings.

PYPL (PayPal) is tumbling after Bloomberg reported Stripe and Advent have abandoned their pursuit of the company, pulling the bid-premium out of the stock.

NVDA (Nvidia) is pausing its revenue-sharing program with neoclouds amid antitrust concerns (WSJ). Separately, Barron's argues NVDA's compressed multiple mirrors Apple in the early 2010s — before a prolonged re-rating higher.

AMD gets a bullish shoutout from Hot Chips conference analysis — described as fielding the best CPU on display — along with HBM vendors Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung, and Google (TPU). SNDK is flagged negatively on HBF skepticism (Tae Kim).

Anthropic plans to file its S-1 after Labor Day with an investor day targeted for mid-September (The Information). A federal judge also ruled the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic illegal (NYT).
