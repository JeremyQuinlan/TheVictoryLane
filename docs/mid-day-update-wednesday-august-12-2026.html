The headline tape looks better than it actually is — the equal-weight S&P is negative on the day, with software, hyperscalers, housing, and healthcare all dragging. GEOPOLITICAL

- The White House is banking on economic pressure to drag Tehran to the table, but Iran is actively restructuring into a "survival economy" to blunt the impact — a playbook with historical precedent (WSJ). The regime's posture mirrors the Carter-era stalling and goalpost-moving, and Trump's options remain constrained by depleted munitions, no appetite for ground troops, and tight global oil inventories. The Strait of Hormuz remains materially impaired as a commercial shipping lane.

- The IEA's latest report flags a worsening oil supply outlook: global demand is seen falling 1.6 million barrels per day in 2026 as high prices crimp consumption, but supply is expected to drop 4.3 million barrels per day before a sharp 8.3 million barrel per day rebound in 2027 (IEA).

- Trump is weighing a push for capital gains tax cuts and exemptions on certain home sales, looking to build Republican political momentum ahead of the midterms (Bloomberg).

- Wisconsin Democrats pushed back against the party's far-left wing Tuesday night, as progressive candidate Francesca Hong suffered a notable loss in the gubernatorial primary (Politico).

COMPANY NEWS

AMCR (Amcor) beat on Q2 sales (+26% to $6.4B vs. Street $5.99B) and EPS (+23% to $1.23 vs. Street $1.20), driven by the Berry acquisition, raw material pass-throughs, and FX. However, guidance disappointed — the EPS mid-point for the Sept/Dec quarters implies ~$1.85 vs. consensus $1.96.

EAT (Brinker) posted a fractional EPS miss ($3.07 vs. Street $3.09), but Chili's comps came in at +5.6% (Street +5.55%) and management says momentum accelerated in July. The F27 EPS guide mid-point of $13 is comfortably ahead of the Street's $12.50.

CAVA reported Q2 comps of +9% — roughly 150 basis points above consensus — with guest traffic up 5.3%. Restaurant margins were modestly soft and EBITDA of $54.7MM barely cleared the Street's $53MM. Full-year guidance was reaffirmed.

CRWV (CoreWeave) disclosed a sharp jump in revenue backlog in the opening weeks of Q3 and raised full-year guidance for revenue, operating income, and capex.

SMCI (Supermicro) had already pre-announced strong FQ4 bookings and margins; the formal report confirmed bullish guidance as the key deliverable.

LITE (Lumentum) posted very strong FQ1 guidance, though expectations were already elevated heading into the print.

KTB (Kontoor Brands) beat Q2 EPS ($1.06 vs. Street $1.04) on strong gross margins (+710 basis points adjusted) and solid Helly Hansen revenue. Full-year EPS guide raised to $5.25–5.35 from $5.15–5.25, and management announced a $400MM accelerated share repurchase.

PFGC (Performance Food) missed FQ4 EPS ($1.59 vs. Street $1.61) on softer sales, higher opex, and a higher tax rate. F27 guidance mid-points for both sales ($72.75B vs. Street $72.79B) and EBITDA ($2.175B vs. Street $2.19B) are slightly light.

TRMB (Trimble) beat Q2 handily — EPS 86c vs. Street 80c, revenue $972MM vs. Street $950MM — and raised full-year EPS guidance to $3.60–3.70 from $3.47–3.64, plus announced a new $1B buyback. ARR was roughly inline and Q3 EPS guidance mid-point is a touch light.

NVDA (Nvidia) — the $500B+ financing consortium announced Monday was framed as entirely third-party capital, but reporting suggests Nvidia itself may backstop up to 25% of originated projects (The Information). The structure exists largely because many of Nvidia's customers simply can't afford its chips (WSJ).

ORCL (Oracle) is planning another round of layoffs this month as the company aggressively cuts costs to fund its AI infrastructure buildout (Business Insider).

WEN (Wendy's) — a Trian-led consortium is preparing a takeout bid for the company (FT).

T (AT&T) says open-weight AI models now account for 25% of internal token usage and expects that figure to reach 70–80% (WSJ).

PSKY/WBD — California gubernatorial frontrunner Xavier Becerra said the Paramount/Warner Bros. Discovery merger should reach a settlement with state AGs seeking to block it (Bloomberg).

HD (Home Depot) — Chair, President, and CEO Ted Decker is taking a temporary medical leave of absence; the company expects his return within a few months.
