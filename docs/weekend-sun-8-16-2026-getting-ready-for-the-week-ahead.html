RATES & FED

July retail sales came in at -0.6% M/M versus the Street's +0.1% estimate, marking the fourth consecutive dovish data point (after jobs, CPI, and PPI) and further reducing the odds of a September rate hike. Chicago Fed's Goolsbee said Friday he's encouraged by July inflation readings but wants more evidence before declaring victory on the path back to 2% (Bloomberg). FOMC minutes from the July 29th meeting drop Wednesday at 2pmET and are likely to read hawkish — though the data since then has moved decisively the other way.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Four straight dovish macro prints out of July is a real shift, and the Fed is going to have a hard time sounding tough in September with this data backdrop. That said, Michigan consumer confidence for August was genuinely concerning — the Expectations index fell to 50.6 from 55.4, well below the 55.2 consensus — so this isn't a clean soft-landing picture. The next major read on the consumer comes from the retail earnings gauntlet this week (HD, WMT, LOW, TJX, ROST, BJ), and those results will matter more than the FOMC minutes. Nvidia's FQ2 report on August 26th is the single biggest catalyst on the calendar, but don't sleep on Anthropic's S1, which could land before an October IPO and reset AI valuation expectations across the board. Iran remains a wildcard — kinetic escalation seems unlikely, but the financial pressure campaign Bessent telegraphed is real, and Hormuz traffic is already crawling.

COMPANY NEWS

NVDA (Nvidia) is scaling back its construction loan guarantee for OpenAI's Ohio data center to $120B (from $250B), covering roughly 5GW of the originally planned 10GW, but may still extend ~$350B in vendor chip financing — an aggregate exposure approaching $470B (WSJ). Separately, Nvidia is in talks to invest up to $3B into SB Energy, the SoftBank-backed developer building the facility (The Information), and disclosed a 122.8M share stake in SpaceX worth ~$17.2B (CNBC). Jensen Huang went public with the broader $500B+ data center funding initiative after slow progress cobbling it together privately (Bloomberg).

PYPL (PayPal) is in active talks to sell itself to a consortium including Stripe and Advent International, with negotiations now above the initial $60.50/share proposal (WSJ).

AAPL (Apple) was urged by Commerce Secretary Lutnick to avoid Chinese memory chips, but the company is reportedly weighing purchases anyway for non-US products amid acute supply shortages (WSJ).

BABA (Alibaba)'s Qwen open-weight model family has surpassed 3 billion downloads over the last six months, making it the world's most popular — ahead of Meta and Google (Bloomberg).

MSGS (Madison Square Garden Sports) filed with the SEC to split into two separate entities, one holding the Knicks and one holding the Rangers (WSJ).

SPCX (SpaceX) completed its $60B acquisition of Cursor, effective August 14th (Bloomberg).

ORCL (Oracle) faces a ~6-month delay on the natural gas pipeline serving its "Project Jupiter" data center in New Mexico, pushing delivery into 2027 (Bloomberg).

PSKY/WBD (Paramount/Warner Bros. Discovery) merger odds are improving as the companies explore settlement talks with state AGs challenging the deal (Bloomberg).

CAT/CMI (Caterpillar/Cummins) are seeing data center demand become an increasingly meaningful business driver (WSJ).

TSLA (Tesla) is nearing an unveiling of a refreshed Roadster, including an exotic "flying" version reportedly priced above $1M that cannot legally be driven on roads (The Information).

Berkshire Hathaway boosted its Google stake by ~50M shares in Q2 to ~106M shares (~$36B), making it the third-largest holding behind Apple (~$66B) and Amex. BAC was trimmed by ~30M shares. Delta was added (+17.5M shares to 57.3M) while Kroger was cut. Berkshire also completed the $8.5B purchase of Taylor Morrison, added to Lennar, and initiated a small position in DR Horton.
