Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a cluster of negative AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically ratcheted up missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia, worsening the Iran conflict and raising energy supply fears. Ukraine also launched a massive drone strike on Russia including Moscow, adding further geopolitical pressure heading into the week.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction, with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. The BOJ's latest hike was slightly more dovish than expected but triggered yen weakness and renewed worries about Japan selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid made hawkish remarks Friday warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil, and markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of further hikes from multiple central banks over the coming weeks.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small geopolitical improvement in the Middle East would likely trigger an outsized drop in energy prices, with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump-Xi summit on Thursday could produce select tariff reductions on roughly 30 billion dollars worth of goods, a modest but real positive for risk assets. The UN General Assembly brings together key Middle East players including Iran, creating a forum for incremental diplomatic progress. Saudi Arabia is also actively increasing ship-to-ship oil transfers to help compensate for supply shortages, providing some near-term relief.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape remains grim with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis now dramatically escalating attacks on Saudi Arabia. The weekend AI news dump was broadly negative — Anthropic's IPO slipped to November, its ARR projections disappointed the most optimistic bulls, OpenAI faces roughly 280 billion dollars in negative free cash flow through 2030, and Big Tech's AI off-balance sheet liability exposure has surged roughly 300 billion dollars in just the last 12 months. Energy price spikes are beginning to work their way through the system and could trigger demand destruction and margin compression in coming weeks. Global monetary policy is still tightening with multiple central banks expected to hike further.

Market Outlook

Adam leans cautiously bearish into the week given the net negative weekend news flow — escalating Middle East conflict, disappointing Anthropic metrics, and rising AI off-balance sheet liability concerns. The key catalyst to watch is the Trump-Xi summit on Thursday September 24th, which could deliver modest tariff relief and a positive risk-on jolt, while the UN General Assembly running September 22nd through 28th offers a slim but real chance for Middle East diplomatic progress.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a fresh headwind from rapidly rising off-balance sheet liability exposure, up roughly 300 billion dollars in the last 12 months, alongside concerns about massive cash burn at frontier AI labs. Energy sector is being driven by Middle East escalation — Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and Iranian pressure on Hormuz are the primary drivers of diesel and motor oil price spikes, not the Ukraine conflict. The auto sector faces pressure from the Volkswagen profit warning, which now sees 2026 operating margins of up to 1% versus prior guidance of 4 to 5.5%, reflecting a challenging global environment especially in China. Memory/semiconductor components are seeing some price relief as Chinese DRAM supply from CXMT ramps. Defense and aerospace could see increased focus given Pentagon bandwidth concerns and Boeing-NASA Starliner talks.
