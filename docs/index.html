VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTHROPIC Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO was pushed to November from October, with year-end ARR projected at over 100 billion dollars per the NYT and over 110 billion dollars per the WSJ — below some bull expectations of 120 billion dollars or more. The company is considering releasing a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture momentum lost to OpenAI's Astra launch, and plans to have 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027. The S1 remains pending public release.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focused on poor price action in debt tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center in New Mexico. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have reportedly 'hit a wall,' raising concerns about the company's exposure to large off-balance-sheet AI infrastructure commitments.

▼ OPENAI OpenAI Other

OpenAI expects negative free cash flow of approximately 280 billion dollars during the 2026 through 2030 period as spending dramatically outpaces revenue. The company has gained competitive momentum following the launch of its Astra platform, which has taken share from Anthropic over recent weeks.

▲ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. Under a potential deal, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central, clearing a key regulatory hurdle for the transaction.

▲ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Warner Bros. Discovery's deal with Paramount is moving closer to resolution as Paramount enters advanced settlement talks with California's AG. Potential divestitures including Comedy Central could be the price of regulatory approval, representing a meaningful step forward for the merger.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The franchisee failure raises concerns about the health of Wendy's franchise network and broader quick-service restaurant demand.

◆ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores nationwide as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. The move signals intensifying pressure on branded consumer goods suppliers as retailers use shelf space as leverage in pricing negotiations.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk Activist Entry

Novo Nordisk is facing shareholder pressure to diversify away from GLP-1 concentration by conducting M&A. The activist push reflects growing investor concern about single-franchise dependency as competition in the obesity and diabetes drug market intensifies.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on 10 or more new flights into low-Earth orbit. This would represent a meaningful commercial rehabilitation for the Starliner program following its high-profile crew mission difficulties.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment. The arrangement positions Accenture at the center of the growing AI governance space and deepens its relationship with one of the most high-profile pre-IPO AI companies.

Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East conflict and a cluster of negative AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia over the weekend, raising fears of broader energy infrastructure disruption, while Iran warned the US against resuming military actions. Ukraine also launched a massive drone strike on Russia including Moscow, adding further geopolitical risk heading into the week.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction, with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. The BOJ's latest hike was slightly more dovish than expected but sparked concern about yen weakness and the potential for Japan to sell Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid made hawkish remarks Friday, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems trace back to oil, meaning even a modest geopolitical improvement could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The UN General Assembly brings together Middle East leaders including Iran, creating a real forum for diplomatic progress. The Trump/Xi summit could produce select tariff reductions on roughly 30 billion dollars of goods, and US-China discussions on eliminating LNG tariffs are a concrete positive. China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, signaling global alarm that could accelerate de-escalation.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape is genuinely grim — Iran is tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned Iraqi militias, and an increasingly aggressive Houthi campaign against Saudi Arabia, while Russia-Ukraine conflict continues to take offline significant global refining capacity. Energy price spikes will begin working through the economic system quickly, threatening demand destruction and margin compression across corporate America. The AI industry faces a fresh set of headwinds including a massive OpenAI cash burn projection of negative 280 billion dollars in free cash flow through 2030, rising off-balance sheet liabilities for Big Tech, and a slight delay and lower-than-hoped ARR figures for Anthropic's IPO. Hope for Middle East diplomatic progress at the UN General Assembly may simply be wishful thinking given the pace of current escalation.

Market Outlook

Adam's tone is cautious to bearish heading into the week. The combination of escalating Middle East conflict, negative AI industry news flow, and a hawkish global rate backdrop creates meaningful headwinds. The key catalysts to watch are the Trump/Xi summit Thursday and the UN General Assembly, which could offer diplomatic relief on energy — but a breakthrough is far from guaranteed.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a confluence of headwinds including rising off-balance sheet liability exposure (up roughly 300 billion dollars in the past 12 months), massive cash burn at frontier model companies, and intensifying regulatory noise from both California and the White House. Energy remains the macro sector to watch — escalating Middle East conflict is the primary driver of diesel and motor oil price spikes, and any de-escalation would have outsized positive knock-on effects across the broader market. Autos are under pressure globally with Volkswagen issuing a large profit warning, now seeing operating margins of up to 1 percent versus prior guidance of 4 to 5.5 percent, driven by a Porsche goodwill impairment, China market weakness, and restructuring costs. Memory and PC components are seeing modest relief as Chinese supply from CXMT helps ease shortages and cap prices. Media and streaming see incremental M&A progress with the Paramount-Warner settlement moving forward.
