Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +110 basis points, and R2K +47 basis points despite a weekend full of negative headlines including escalating Middle East geopolitics and troubling AI sector news. Asian markets posted broad gains led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while major Eurozone indices are up 85-120 basis points. The primary driver of equity strength appears to be optimism around this week's UN General Assembly in New York, where diplomatic progress on Iran and the Middle East could be achieved, with a potential Trump-Iran presidential meeting on the table.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The Fed remains in a hawkish posture — markets are pricing in roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes across the final two 2026 meetings with about 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28th meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of increases penciled in for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both delivered hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that elevated inflation is broad-based and not solely driven by oil. Treasury is also set to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt and above its own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems can be traced back to oil, and even a small geopolitical improvement at the UN General Assembly could trigger a sharp drop in energy prices, unleashing lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. Trump reportedly called off Houthi air strikes at the last minute and indicated openness to meeting Iran's president, raising the possibility of a diplomatic breakthrough that markets have not priced in. Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe, suggesting AI infrastructure demand remains intact despite negative Anthropic and OpenAI headlines. The Trump-Xi summit on Thursday also offers a potential positive surprise via select tariff reductions.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was overwhelmingly negative — Houthis launched a major missile and drone attack on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine executed its largest drone strike on Russia including Moscow, Anthropic delayed its IPO and posted underwhelming 2027 ARR projections, and OpenAI disclosed expected negative free cash flow of roughly $280 billion through 2030. Geopolitical conditions continue to worsen with new fronts opening weekly and the Pentagon stretched thin, while the Iran war is tightening the grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis. Monetary policy globally remains firmly hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month and more increases expected, and AI off-balance-sheet liability exposure for big tech has surged roughly $300 billion in just the last 12 months.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday on diplomatic optionality alone — the setup is that bad news is already known and any Middle East de-escalation at the UN General Assembly could trigger an outsized oil price decline with cascading macro benefits. The key catalyst to watch is any signal from the UN General Assembly of diplomatic progress between the US and Iran, as well as developments around Thursday's Trump-Xi summit which could produce modest tariff relief.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe, suggesting AI infrastructure demand remains resilient despite negative weekend headlines around Anthropic and OpenAI. AI off-balance-sheet liabilities for big tech firms have risen roughly $300 billion in the past 12 months, creating a growing source of sector-level risk. Chinese memory giant CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, which Acer's CEO noted is helping ease component shortages and cap PC component prices — a modest negative read for memory pricing at Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. The energy sector faces compounding headwinds from surging oil tanker costs, motor oil shortages and retail rationing in the US, and continued geopolitical supply disruption tied to the Iran war. European industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks are outperforming today while utilities, energy, healthcare, real estate, retail, and luxury lag.
