RATES & FED

The Warsh Jackson Hole speech drove yields sharply higher, particularly at the Fed-sensitive 2-year end, with markets repricing from one 25 basis points hike to roughly 1.5 hikes. A September 16th rate increase now looks highly probable barring a dramatic collapse in jobs or inflation. The strategic intent behind Warsh's hawkishness appears to be bolstering Fed credibility to suppress long-end yields — but absent QE, that effort faces powerful headwinds from fiscal imbalances and AI-linked bond issuance.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

A "one-and-done" or "two-and-done" Fed hiking cycle is net positive for equities — it shores up monetary credibility far more than it damages growth, and a more credible Fed should help pull long-end yields lower over time. That said, the structural case for sustainably lower long yields remains weak given the $40T+ US debt pile, elevated inflation, and a tidal wave of AI-linked issuance. Near-term, there's a reasonable tactical case for a Treasury rally driven by sluggish July data, JPY intervention dynamics, enlarged buybacks, and improving Middle East oil flows. Equities face a busy week of macro catalysts — ISMs, jobs Friday, and a heavy tech earnings slate — so volatility is the base case. Watch the August jobs number closely; the Street is penciling in +60K after July's ugly -23K print.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran: Tehran is increasingly acknowledging economic pain from the war and US sanctions — gas lines are forming as the naval blockade squeezes fuel supplies — yet Iran shows no sign of abandoning maximalist demands (Reuters, NYT). The Treasury's action against UAE branches of Egypt's Banque Misr was underwhelming, underscoring how limited Washington's escalation options really are; Bessent's "Economic D-Day" framing was met with a global shrug (Bloomberg). Pentagon planners warn the Iran conflict has drained US munition stockpiles to levels that complicate deterrence of China and Russia (WSJ), while senior military leaders are cautioning Hegseth that large-scale operations aren't sustainable (Washington Post). On the positive side, Pakistan and Qatar are working to reinstate the June MOU, and Middle East oil exports appear higher than widely assumed.

- Venezuela: Trump announced the US secured majority control over ~65 billion barrels of Venezuelan oil reserves — a 35% passive stake in a new entity and rights to purchase 20% of production at cost (CNN, WSJ, NYT, FT). CVX (Chevron) is separately near a deal to expand its Venezuelan footprint (NYT). Near-term energy price impact: essentially zero — development will take years and funding questions remain wide open (Barron's). The deal is widely read as Trump seeking a political win amid an unpopular, stalemated Iran war (WSJ).

- Russia/Ukraine: CIA Director Ratcliffe proposed a Trump-Putin-Zelensky trilateral summit during his Moscow visit (Axios). Ukraine warns Russia may be planning a fresh ground offensive toward Kyiv (Bloomberg), while Putin escalates with a new wave of "gray zone" attacks across Europe as the Ukraine war stalls (WSJ). Russia extended its diesel export ban through at least end of September (Bloomberg).

- China/US: The US-China trade agreement from Busan is widely expected to be extended when Xi visits Washington on September 24th (SCMP). Beijing also unveiled fresh housing market support measures (Bloomberg). The Shanghai Cooperation Summit kicks off Monday in Kyrgyzstan, bringing Xi, Putin, Modi, and Iran's Pezeshkian together (Nikkei).
