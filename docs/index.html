Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points (65 basis points), Nasdaq +329 points (110 basis points), and Russell 2000 +47 basis points. Asian and European markets are broadly higher, with Eurozone indices up 85-120 basis points led by industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The primary driver appears to be optimism around this week's UN General Assembly in New York, where diplomatic progress on the Middle East/Iran conflict is hoped for, despite a weekend filled with negative news including escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and Ukraine's largest drone strike on Russia.

Treasury yields are down 2-3 basis points across the curve, providing a modest tailwind. The Fed and global central banks remain in hawkish mode — the market is pricing approximately 33 basis points of additional rate hikes over the Fed's final two meetings of the year, with roughly 50/50 odds of a hike at the October 28 meeting, plus about 35 basis points of further increases expected in 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks recently, warning that elevated inflation is broad-based and not solely driven by energy prices. Treasury also plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of total marketable debt, above its own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

The single biggest macro tailwind would be any diplomatic progress at the UN General Assembly (September 22-28) that cools Middle East tensions — Trump signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, and China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis. Nearly all of the market's current macro headwinds trace back to oil, so even a modest de-escalation could unleash a cascade of favorable effects: lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday could also deliver modest tariff reductions and a trade truce extension, adding another incremental positive. Treasuries are catching a bid this morning, with yields down 2-3 basis points, supporting equity valuations.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was almost entirely negative — Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalated dramatically, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia hitting oil infrastructure, and a deluge of troubling AI headlines emerged including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic revenue projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. Monetary policy globally remains aggressively hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and Fed all hiking this month and more increases expected. Energy prices remain a structural headwind — motor oil is being rationed in the US, tanker costs are surging, and Qatar's energy minister flatly rejected Bessent's optimism about Hormuz becoming irrelevant, suggesting relief on the oil front may be wishful thinking.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday's session, driven entirely by diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly and hopes for de-escalation in the Middle East that could push oil prices lower. The key catalyst to watch is any signal from Trump-Iran or US-China meetings at the UN General Assembly — even a small positive development on the Iran/Houthi front could trigger an outsized oil sell-off with broad bullish knock-on effects across rates, margins, and consumer confidence.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in European trading. AI infrastructure broadly faces headwinds after a weekend of negative headlines: delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI expected to burn approximately $280 billion in free cash flow from 2026 to 2030, and Oracle data center debt issues. Chinese memory maker CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, which Acer's CEO noted is helping ease component shortages and cap prices — a modest negative read-through for memory pricing for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Energy sector faces continued pressure from surging tanker costs and motor oil shortages in the US driven by the Iran war. Industrials and banks outperform in Europe while utilities, energy, healthcare, real estate, retail, and luxury underperform.
