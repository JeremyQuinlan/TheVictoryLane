RATES & FED

Treasuries rallied Tuesday with yields falling 4–5 basis points across the curve, helped by easing Iran tensions, a cooler-than-expected JOLTs report, and US assistance to Japan on yen intervention. The structural bear case on rates remains intact — elevated borrowing costs are being driven by firm inflation, resilient growth, tightening labor markets, the AI capex boom, and extreme sovereign debt issuance.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Tuesday's surge was propelled by three catalysts: a monster Caterpillar print, a massive Palantir beat-and-raise, and Bessent's encouraging Hormuz commentary — and the market is now in full chase mode as the SPX sprints toward fresh all-time highs. The near-term sentiment is getting panicky, with investors terrified of being left behind, which is fueling aggressive and potentially reckless positioning. The next truly major catalysts don't arrive for weeks — Nvidia reports August 26 after the close, and Fed Chair Warsh speaks at Jackson Hole August 28. The bears are right to flag that the AI debate is far from settled, the Iran conflict has no clean resolution in sight, and a string of consumer-facing earnings (MCD, KMB, BRBR, WHR) were underwhelming. Don't confuse momentum for fundamentals here.

---

GEOPOLITICAL

- Treasury Secretary Bessent said on CNBC Tuesday morning that a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz could arrive within days, sparking the equity rally; a Bloomberg report followed suggesting Iran may allow European nations to clear mines from the waterway (Bloomberg).

- The bear case: the US has depleted virtually all of its long-range precision munitions in the Iran conflict, leaving Trump without a credible military option — the best achievable outcome may be an MOU heavily skewed toward Tehran, potentially including Iranian-imposed transit fees on passing ships (Reuters, NYT).

- The Iran conflict is unlikely to fully resolve while Trump is in office; investors should expect a sustained, episodic conflict with ebbs and flows in tension — right now, tensions are ebbing.

- The White House is drafting a ban on Chinese optical transceivers for data centers, sparking a sharp rally in US optical names Tuesday (Reuters).

COMPANY NEWS

CAT (Caterpillar) delivered a blowout Q2 with EPS of $8.17 vs. the Street's $6.17, MP&E sales jumping 25% to $19.58B, adjusted operating margins of 21.9% (+430 basis points Y/Y), and $392MM in IEEPA tariff refunds boosting results. The backlog soared and full-year sales guidance was raised.

PLTR (Palantir) posted Q2 EPS of 41c vs. 35c expected on revenue of $1.935B vs. $1.808B, with a 62% operating margin. Full-year sales guidance raised to $8.15–8.158B from $7.65–7.662B.

ADM (Archer-Daniels-Midland) beat Q2 EPS at $1.84 vs. $1.49 and raised full-year EPS guidance to $5.15–5.60 from $4.15–4.70 across all three business units.

SNAP posted very strong Q2 results: revenue $1.598B vs. $1.539B, EBITDA $249.6MM vs. $192MM, DAUs 493MM vs. 488MM, and free cash flow of $121MM vs. the Street's $10.4MM.

MRK (Merck) reported Q2 sales of $16.6B vs. $16.3B with strong Keytruda upside; EPS was weighed by acquisition charges but the full-year sales guide was nudged higher.

PFE (Pfizer) beat Q2 with EPS of 77c vs. 68c and revenue of $15B vs. $14.4B; Eliquis revenue of $2.42B significantly topped the $2.09B estimate. Full-year guidance raised.

PG (Procter & Gamble) agreed to acquire supplements firm Thorne for $3.8B from PE firm L Chatterton (WSJ).

CMG (Chipotle) shares fell on reports linking the chain to a salmonella outbreak in Minnesota tied to jalapeño peppers (WSJ).

AMZN (Amazon) — Jeff Bezos filed to sell 15 million shares worth approximately $4B (CNBC).

NVO (Novo Nordisk) sold off despite beating on sales/EPS and raising guidance, as investors were disappointed by the modest size of the outlook hike and inline Wegovy pill revenue.

F (Ford) reported July sales down 10.2% to 169.95K vehicles; EVs tumbled 75%, hybrids fell 25.1%, and ICE declined 5.2% (CNBC).
