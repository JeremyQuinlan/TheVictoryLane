Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

AI stocks have been trading poorly as capital access tightens across the data center and neocloud space. CDS spreads for CoreWeave, Oracle, and similar companies are signaling that borrowing is becoming prohibitively expensive for much of the industry, with negative knock-on effects down the supply chain. Treasuries did not sell off despite a hot CPI print, suggesting seller exhaustion and a potential green light for further rate declines.

The hot CPI print failed to push Treasuries lower, which Adam interprets as seller exhaustion rather than renewed inflation fear. This dynamic should act as a catalyst for further declines in rates, particularly if AI-sector anxiety remains elevated. Lower yields would benefit out-of-favor, under-owned sectors over the near term.

Market Outlook

Bearish lean on AI and related infrastructure names in the near term, with excess still being squeezed out of the space. Adam sees it as too early to buy the dip in AI. Rotation into deeply out-of-favor, under-owned sectors — regional banks, non-data center industrials, consumer discretionary — is the preferred tactical trade. Key catalyst to watch: Meta's earnings on October 21 and any potential large equity offering from Meta that could further pressure the AI IPO landscape.

Sector Watch

Regional banks, non-data center industrials, and consumer discretionary are highlighted as extremely out of favor and under-owned sectors that should outperform in the current environment as AI anxiety pressures yields lower. AI infrastructure and neocloud names face continued headwinds from tightening capital access and widening CDS spreads. Mega-cap cash-flow hyperscalers are relatively advantaged within the tech space.

VK · Key Names
▼ ANTH Anthropic Other

Anthropic reported Q3 revenue of $16.8 billion, up 45% quarter-over-quarter, a sharp deceleration from 147% growth in Q2. The company claims positive free cash flow with adjusted operating margins of 11% and adjusted gross margins of 85%, though the opacity of these figures — what is excluded, training vs. inference, gross vs. net — raises serious credibility concerns. Two key demand sources are set to fade as Meta begins using its own tools and Anthropic cracks down on distillation, while frontier model leadership remains highly fluid.

◆ META Meta Platforms Other

Meta has been the most disruptive AI force in recent months with its Muse personal AI agent and Muse Wonder models, and reports its earnings on October 21. The Street is modeling approximately $200 billion of capex in 2027, roughly 65% of projected revenue, and there is speculation Meta could announce a large equity sale around earnings to bolster its balance sheet — a move that would pressure the broader AI IPO and funding landscape. As a cash-flow-rich hyperscaler, Meta is also quietly advantaged as neocloud funding tightens.

▼ NVDA Nvidia Other

Nvidia recognized the capital access problem in the AI ecosystem months ago and has been aggressively arranging alternative financing for partners, using its own balance sheet and coordinating with allies. However, it is unclear whether Nvidia alone can serve as the central bank for the entire AI industry as CDS spreads widen and borrowing costs rise across the neocloud space. This is a key overhang for the stock and the broader AI supply chain.

▼ CRWV CoreWeave Other

CoreWeave's CDS spreads are widening, signaling that the market views its borrowing costs as increasingly prohibitive. This is emblematic of broader tightening capital access across neocloud and data center operators, which could force a pullback in capex across the AI infrastructure build-out. Negative knock-on effects down the supply chain are expected if these companies hit a financing roadblock.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle's CDS spreads are also widening alongside CoreWeave's, reflecting growing market concern about the cost and availability of capital for data center-heavy businesses. Adam flags this as a statement by the market that leveraged AI infrastructure spending faces a reckoning. If capital constraints materialize, Oracle's aggressive cloud and AI capex plans could be at risk.

▲ AMZN Amazon Sector Reprice

Amazon is identified as one of the mega-cap hyperscalers — alongside Alphabet, Meta, and Microsoft — that is quietly advantaged as neocloud and AI startup funding conditions tighten. Robust operating cash flow insulates these companies from the capital access problems plaguing smaller AI infrastructure players. This dynamic could accelerate share gains in enterprise AI and cloud.

▲ GOOGL Alphabet Sector Reprice

Alphabet is cited alongside Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft as a cash-flow-rich beneficiary of the tightening funding landscape for AI startups and neoclouds. As competitors struggle to access debt and equity markets, Alphabet's financial strength positions it to capture incremental AI and cloud workloads. No specific near-term catalyst mentioned beyond the sector repricing dynamic.

▲ MSFT Microsoft Sector Reprice

Microsoft is grouped with Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta as a hyperscaler that benefits from the current AI funding squeeze. Its robust balance sheet and operating cash flow allow it to continue investing in AI infrastructure while smaller competitors face capital constraints. The competitive moat is widening as the funding environment turns hostile for neoclouds.
