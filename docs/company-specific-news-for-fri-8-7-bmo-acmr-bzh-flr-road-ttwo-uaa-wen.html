COMPANY NEWS

ACMR (ACM Research) delivered a blowout Q2, with EPS of 61c crushing the Street's 34c estimate. The beat was driven by meaningfully higher revenue and roughly 200 basis points of gross margin outperformance. Full-year sales guidance was raised to $1.125–1.175B from the prior $1.08–1.175B — a clean raise on both top and bottom.

BZH (Beazer Homes) is being acquired by DFH (Dream Finders Homes) in a $2.2B deal at $33.50/share in cash. Separately, BZH's Q2 EPS loss of -16c was considerably better than the Street's -34c fear, with average selling prices up 5.9% to $547.8K and underlying gross margins of 12.8% coming in roughly 13 basis points ahead of consensus. Dream Finders reaffirmed its full-year 2026 outlook of approximately 9,250 home closings.

FLR (Fluor) posted decent Q2 upside with EBITDA of $149MM versus the Street's $135MM and revenue of $4.3B versus $3.9B expected, alongside a solid book-to-bill of 1.4x. The full-year EBITDA guide was trimmed to $500–525MM from $525–560MM, reflecting the removal of the previously anticipated second-half contribution from its JV in Mexico — a known headwind, but a guide cut is a guide cut.

ROAD (Construction Partners) beat on Q2 revenue ($999.4MM vs. $950MM expected) and EBITDA ($163MM vs. $158MM), with backlog continuing to build. Full-year EBITDA guidance was nudged higher to $559–569MM from $552–564MM — modest, but directionally right.

TTWO (Take-Two Interactive) posted FQ1 bookings of $1.39B, just ahead of the Street's $1.36B, but the FY bookings forecast was merely reiterated at $8–8.2B (the Street was modeling $8.6B+), and FY non-GAAP EBITDA was trimmed to $993MM–$1.053B from $1.013–$1.07B. The market wanted a raise ahead of GTA VI; it didn't get one.

UAA (Under Armour) beat on FQ1 EPS (5c vs. 2c expected) thanks to tariff refunds and cost discipline, but the revenue picture is deteriorating — sales fell 3% to $1.09B versus the Street's $1.11B, with North America down 9%. Full-year sales guidance was cut to down approximately 5% versus the prior "down slightly," and the maintained EPS range of 8–12c now bakes in the IEEPA tariff refund benefit, which is not a recurring tailwind.

WEN (Wendy's) wasn't as catastrophic as feared on margins — restaurant margins shrank 240 basis points year-over-year, though investors had braced for worse. But comps were ugly at -6.3% overall versus the Street's -4.6% (U.S. down ~7%), the dividend was cut again (to 7c quarterly from 14c, following a prior cut from 25c to 14c in May 2025), and full-year guidance was withdrawn entirely as new leadership conducts a comprehensive business review. This one is deep turnaround territory.
