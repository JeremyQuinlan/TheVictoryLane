RATES & FED

The August jobs report pushed yields higher on the headline beat, though the details were benign — no further drop in unemployment and no acceleration in wages. The view here is that a September "one and done" hike would be net positive for equities by bolstering Fed credibility without materially burdening the economy; staying on hold simply extends the overhang to the October 28 and December 9 meetings. August CPI on Friday 9/11 is expected to hold steady at +3.4% headline with core ticking down just 10 basis points to +2.4%.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The jobs report was, on net, the right kind of strong — enough to validate Fed credibility but not enough to panic the bond market, and equities should take comfort in that distinction. The bigger near-term risk isn't rates; it's geopolitics. An escalatory spiral in the Strait of Hormuz — where total oil volumes remain below pre-war levels and global inventories are severely depleted — could reprice energy and risk assets simultaneously. On the AI side, the capex boom is alive and well (Foxconn +52% Y/Y in August), but intensifying model competition raises real questions about compute ROI, and that tension will increasingly weigh on multiples for infrastructure names. The Anthropic S1, when it finally drops in late September, will be the single most important data point the market gets on frontier AI economics — and it's likely to move the whole sector.

GEOPOLITICAL

- US-Iran (Hormuz): Iran fired ballistic missiles at two US Navy warships Saturday; the ships evaded "multiple unprovoked attacks" with no casualties. The Pentagon responded by "permanently disabling" two Iranian oil tankers and "completely destroying" a third, calling them part of a "multibillion-dollar shadow network" funding the IRGC. The US is winning the economic war — Iran's financial strangulation is "growing increasingly difficult to withstand" (Reuters) — but the NYT reports Iran has grown "more confident" in confronting Washington, plans to extend the conflict for months, and could pursue "significant escalation." The FT adds that Washington has abandoned the June MOU and now wants a broader deal encompassing Iran's nuclear program, which Tehran is flatly rejecting. American banks remain a "weak link" in the sanctions architecture (WSJ).

- Houthis/Red Sea: The Houthis are advancing on key Yemeni territory that, if taken, would give them greater control over the Bab al-Mandab Strait (NYT).

- Russia-Ukraine: The Wikoff/Kushner weekend trips to Moscow and Kyiv appear unlikely to move the needle on a peace deal. Both sides are escalating — Ukraine's Security Service HQ was struck Friday, and Kyiv is targeting Russian commercial airports via drone swarms — while Moscow steps up gray-zone provocations against NATO members (NYT). Putin is facing unusual pointed criticism from allies urging him to end the war (WSJ).

- China: Beijing announced a ~$54B capital injection into state-owned banks and insurers Sunday (Reuters). Chinese firms are circumventing export controls by routing advanced US AI chips through Southeast Asian subsidiaries (NYT).

- OPEC+: Held output unchanged at Sunday's meeting, as widely expected (WSJ).

- Japan: BOJ hawks are calling for an accelerated tightening pace before their terms end in July (Nikkei). Tokyo says progress has been made on a $550B investment pact with the US (Bloomberg).
