RATES & FED

The 2-year is steady while 10- and 30-year yields tick up another ~2 basis points, extending the post-Fed steepening trend from Wednesday. The Fed's hawkish hold — paired with another meandering press conference from Warsh — failed to deliver the credibility markets needed, and the view here is that a rate hike with clearer forward guidance would have been the better outcome. Odds of a September 16 hike sit at only ~70%, and long-end yields will likely remain biased higher until the Fed acts.

MARKET OUTLOOK

AI remains the single most important market driver — it dwarfs Iran, the Fed, and everything else. The problem isn't appetite for capex; hyperscalers still have religious fervor for AI spending. The problem is financing — capex is overwhelming operating cash flow, and markets are showing real indigestion absorbing the weekly flood of debt and equity issuance. Nvidia is effectively acting as the AI industry's central bank, extending liquidity to keep projects alive, but it can't do it alone. Microsoft's strong pre-market move helps stabilize tech sentiment at the margin, but Meta's miss and ongoing post-earnings selling in ARM and QCOM are reminders that the bar is high. Until the capex funding question resolves, expect continued volatility in mega-cap tech.

COMPANY NEWS

MSFT reported solid FQ4 EPS and revenue upside; the FQ1 guide was bullish with underlying capex for the calendar year unchanged. Shares rallying solidly in pre-market, providing a stabilizing effect on tech sentiment.

META missed on EPS due to one-time charges; adjusting those out puts operating income roughly in line. The Q3 revenue guide came in modestly below expectations, and Zuckerberg pushed back on questions about selling compute capacity to third parties.

ARM beat FQ1 EPS (45c vs. Street 40c) on sales of $1.29B (+22% Y/Y). FQ2 guide is decent at 47c/$1.38B vs. Street 45c/$1.35B, but management flagged AGI CPU guidance upside is supply-constrained. Shares seeing post-earnings selling.

QCOM reported FQ3 EPS inline at $2.21 with revenue upside ($9.94B vs. Street $9.62B; auto +61% Y/Y), but margins disappointed and the FQ4 EPS midpoint of $2.15 falls well short of Street's $2.35.

SBUX crushed FQ3: EPS 85c vs. Street 65c, operating margins +430 basis points, aggregate comps +7.9% vs. Street +5.7%. Full-year EPS guidance raised to $2.55–2.65 from $2.25–2.45.

FTNT delivered strong Q2 EPS of 90c vs. Street 75c; billings surged 33% to $2.37B. Full-year billings guidance raised to $8.8–9.1B with EPS of $3.41–3.47.

HOOD beat with EPS of 62c vs. Street 44c; transaction-based revenue of $776MM topped estimates, with options contract revenue surging more than 10x. Expense guidance trimmed lower.

LRCX posted solid FQ4 EPS of $1.82 vs. Street $1.70 on revenue of $6.72B, and the FQ1 guide of $2.15/$8.1B blew past Street estimates of $1.84/$7.13B.

BOOT beat FQ1 EPS handily ($2.29 vs. Street $1.71) on tariff refunds and comps of +4.7%, but management flagged slowing July comps and guided FQ2 below Street on both sales and EPS.

CMG posted modest EPS/comp upside (33c/+2.2% vs. Street 32c/+1.3%) and nudged the full-year comp guide to +1–3% from flat.

ORLY beat modestly on EPS (87c vs. 86c) and comps (+6% vs. +5.4%); full-year EPS guide raised to $3.20–3.30.

LHX beat Q2 EPS at $3.13 vs. Street $2.80 with segment margins up 10 basis points to 16% and a healthy 1.2x book-to-bill; full-year EPS raised to $11.80–12.00.

CVNA delivered Q2 EBITDA roughly inline as stronger sales were offset by margin compression (EBITDA margins -200 basis points Y/Y); full-year EBITDA midpoint of $2.85B fell short of Street's $2.98B.

Rolls-Royce surged in London after H1 results smashed expectations; full-year operating profit guidance raised to GBP4.7–4.9B from GBP4.0–4.2B on strong Civil, Defense, and Power Systems demand.

Schneider Electric reported Q2 organic revenue growth of +17% vs. Street +10.5%, with data center demand described as "up triple-digit" in Q2. Full-year guidance raised.

Shell reported Q2 adjusted net income of $9.83B vs. Street $8.6B, reduced net debt, and announced a new $3B buyback.
