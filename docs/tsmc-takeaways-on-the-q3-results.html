TSMC: takeaways on the Q3 results

Oct 15, 2026 · 04:01 AM ET

Here's your TSMC earnings breakdown. This is a mixed print — strong on revenue, softer on margins, with the AI story very much intact but international fab costs creating a real headwind that management says isn't going away anytime soon. Let's walk through it. On the quarter, gross margins came in at 66.4%, just a hair below the Street's 66.5% estimate. Operating margins were 58.7%, which was actually about 60 basis points ahead of plan — so a slight split there. Revenue was already known since TSMC reports monthly sales figures, so the real focus was always going to be margins and guidance. On guidance, the top line is impressive. Q4 revenue is guided to 53.3 billion dollars at the mid-point — that's well above the Street's 50.4 billion estimate. That's the headline that bulls will grab onto. But margins tell a more complicated story. Q4 gross margins are guided to 66% at the mid-point, roughly in line with consensus, and operating margins are guided to 57.5%, which is below the Street's 58% expectation. Gross margins fell sequentially in Q3 and are set to fall again in Q4. The culprit is the ramp of the 2-nanometer process and the costs associated with bringing international fabs online — and critically, management was direct on the call that this margin dilution is not a one-quarter story. As they bring more US and international fabs online, those facilities are structurally more expensive than their domestic Taiwan operations, and that pressure persists. Capex for 2026 was reiterated at 60 to 64 billion dollars, though actual Q3 capex came in about 2 billion under the Street at 13.8 billion. Elevated spending is expected to continue beyond this year. Now, the AI commentary was unambiguously bullish. Management said the AI market continues to be very strong, they're seeing increasing demand from agentic AI adoption and strong uptake of open-source models, and they saw zero signs of a slowdown. That's a meaningful data point for the entire AI supply chain. The offset is what's happening outside AI — consumer and price-sensitive end markets are being challenged by rising component prices and macro uncertainty. So the setup is this: the AI demand cycle is real and accelerating, the revenue beat on Q4 guidance is genuine, but margin compression from fab expansion is a structural overhang that isn't going away. Net net, this is a mixed print. Bulls get the revenue upside and the AI conviction. Bears get the margin miss and the explicit warning that dilution continues. Watch how the stock reacts to the margin trajectory versus the revenue beat — that'll tell you where sentiment really sits.
