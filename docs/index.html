Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points/65 basis points, Nasdaq +329 points/110 basis points, and Russell 2000 +47 basis points despite a weekend full of negative headlines. Asian markets were broadly higher led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while European indices are up 85-120 basis points. The primary driver of strength is diplomatic optimism around this week's UN General Assembly in New York, where Trump indicated openness to meeting Iran's president, raising hopes for Middle East de-escalation. Brent crude is sliding 170 basis points to around $102.10, extending its recent losses.

Treasury yields are down 2-3 basis points across the curve Monday morning, giving bonds a mild bid. The market is pricing in roughly 33 basis points of rate hikes across the Fed's final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds on a hike at the October 28th meeting and approximately 35 basis points of additional increases priced for 2027. Fed hawks were out over the weekend — Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned inflation is running hot beyond just oil, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari echoed those concerns Sunday, keeping the policy backdrop firmly hawkish. Treasury is also set to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt, above the Treasury's own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly every macro headwind the market faces — elevated yields, margin pressure, weak consumer confidence — traces back to elevated oil prices, and a credible step toward Middle East de-escalation at the UN General Assembly could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with powerful positive knock-on effects. Trump pulled back from authorizing Houthi air strikes at the last minute, suggesting the administration prefers a diplomatic path, and China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday could deliver modest tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion in goods and possible elimination of Chinese tariffs on US LNG, adding a second geopolitical tailwind.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was almost uniformly negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia including hitting a fuel depot at Riyadh's airport, Ukraine launched its largest drone strike on Russia targeting oil infrastructure, and Iran warned of a fierce response to any resumed US military action. On the AI front, there was a flood of troubling headlines including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI projecting negative free cash flow of roughly $280 billion through 2030, and Oracle data center debt for its Project Jupiter facility hitting a wall in credit markets. Energy supply disruptions are increasingly feeding through to motor oil shortages and tanker cost surges, and monetary policy globally remains hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all having hiked this month.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday's session, driven entirely by geopolitical optionality — the UN General Assembly creates a low-cost option on Middle East diplomatic progress, and even a small improvement in Iran/Hormuz tensions would cascade bullishly through oil, yields, margins, and consumer confidence. The key catalyst to watch is any headline from the UN General Assembly regarding Iran diplomacy or a potential Trump-Iran president meeting, as well as any update on the Trump/Xi summit expected Thursday.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in European trading Monday. AI infrastructure broadly faces a negative sentiment overhang from the weekend news cycle — delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI's massive projected cash burn through 2030, and Oracle data center debt issues. Chinese memory giant CXMT announced its next-gen platform has entered mass production, which Acer's CEO says is helping ease component shortages and cap prices — a mild negative for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron on pricing power. Energy stocks face pressure as Brent slides, while tanker and logistics names could see volatility given surging oil tanker costs and supply chain strain from geopolitical disruption.
