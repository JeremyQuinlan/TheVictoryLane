RATES & FED

Treasuries rallied solidly Thursday, with yields falling 4–7 basis points across the curve. Markets are now pricing just 23 basis points of hikes across the Fed's final three meetings of the year, with only 35% odds of a move on September 16. Cleveland Fed's Hammack pushed back, reiterating rates should rise now despite the modest inflation cooling, while Richmond's Barkin argued the opposite — that disinflation can happen without further tightening.

MARKET OUTLOOK

The bull case is holding up better than the bears want to admit — AI spending data keeps coming in strong, inflation is directionally improving, and buyout activity in software signals that smart money sees value. That said, don't get complacent: core PPI ran firmer than expected, the Atlanta Fed wage tracker accelerated to +3.8% in July, and yields have deeply structural upward forces — fiscal imbalances, AI-driven debt issuance, tariffs, and a shrinking workforce among them. The real test comes the week of August 25: PCE Wednesday morning, Nvidia earnings Wednesday night, and Fed Chair Warsh at Jackson Hole Friday. Until then, AI momentum and cooling headline inflation give the bulls enough to work with.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran remains intransigent, effectively holding the Strait of Hormuz hostage while its economy digs in against U.S. sanctions and regional proxies grow more aggressive. Energy Secretary Wright claimed more oil is flowing through Hormuz than markets realize (Bloomberg), which helped push Brent lower Thursday — but the structural risk hasn't gone away.

- Russia is escalating attacks on Ukrainian ports and Black Sea shipping lanes, threatening global food supplies as Ukrainian farmers can't move their harvests (Reuters, NYT).

- The Houthis continue assaulting Red Sea traffic, compounding shipping disruptions that feed inflationary pressure globally.

- Mexico is pushing Washington to lower tariffs on North American autos as part of ongoing bilateral trade negotiations, a move that could reduce vehicle retail prices (WSJ).
