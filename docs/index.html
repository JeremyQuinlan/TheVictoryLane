◆ ANTH Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO has been pushed to November from October, and its anticipated year-end ARR of over $110 billion per the WSJ fell short of some bulls hoping for $120 billion or higher. The company is considering launching a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture momentum lost to OpenAI's Astra, and has disclosed plans for 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027. Accenture will embed a team of evaluators to monitor safety and alignment at the firm.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative FT article focused on poor price action in debt tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center facility in New Mexico, with efforts to offload loans tied to the facility having 'hit a wall.' This adds to the broader concerns around Big Tech's rapidly rising off-balance sheet AI liability exposure, which is up roughly $300 billion in just the last twelve months.

Resolution of this regulatory overhang would be a meaningful positive for closing the deal.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's came under pressure Friday after Meritage Hospitality Group, one of its largest franchisees, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Franchisee distress signals strain in the broader restaurant consumer environment and could weigh on Wendy's unit economics and development pipeline.

◆ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. This reflects growing retailer leverage over consumer brands and could signal broader pricing pressure dynamics across the consumer staples space.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk Activist Entry

Shareholders are pressuring Novo Nordisk to diversify away from GLP-1 exposure through M&A activity. The pressure reflects concern about concentration risk in the company's current product portfolio despite GLP-1's strong demand.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on ten or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, representing a meaningful commercial and reputational recovery opportunity for the troubled program. This comes after Starliner's prior difficulties and would be a positive development for Boeing's space division.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment, representing a new type of enterprise engagement in the AI governance space. The arrangement signals a growing commercial opportunity for Accenture in AI oversight services.

▼ VOW Volkswagen Guidance Cut

Volkswagen issued a large downside preannouncement for 2026 operating margins, now guiding to 'up to 1%' versus the Street's roughly 4.1% estimate and prior guidance of 4 to 5.5%, driven by a goodwill impairment charge at Porsche, a challenging auto market especially in China, and additional restructuring costs. Adjusted for special items, operating margins would be approximately 4%, roughly in line with consensus, but the headline miss is significant.

Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically ratcheted up missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities, worsening the Iran war's impact on global energy supplies and refining capacity. Ukraine also launched a massive drone attack on Russia including Moscow, adding further pressure to already strained global energy markets.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction, with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month. The BOJ's rate hike was slightly more dovish than expected, but paradoxically triggered concern by pressuring the yen lower and rekindling fears about Japan selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid made hawkish remarks Friday, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small geopolitical improvement in the Middle East would likely trigger an outsized drop in energy prices, with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump/Xi summit could produce select tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion worth of goods, and discussions are underway to eliminate Chinese tariffs on American LNG. Saudi Arabia is actively increasing ship-to-ship oil transfers to compensate for supply shortages, and Hormuz shipments have hit a six-month high according to the Pentagon.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape remains deeply troubled — Iran is tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, Iraqi militias, and an increasingly aggressive Houthi campaign against Saudi Arabia, while Russia-Ukraine conflict continues to take offline significant global refining capacity. The latest AI headlines were broadly negative, including a massive OpenAI free cash flow burn of negative $280 billion projected through 2030, a concerning FT report on Oracle-linked data center debt, and Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liability exposure rising roughly $300 billion in just the last twelve months. Global monetary policy remains hawkish with markets pricing over 50% odds of additional hikes from multiple major central banks in coming weeks, and the energy price spike has yet to fully work its way through corporate earnings and consumer spending.

Market Outlook

Adam leans bearish into the week given net negative weekend newsflow — escalating Middle East conflict, AI industry headwinds, and persistently hawkish central banks. The key catalyst to watch is the Trump/Xi summit on Thursday September 24th, which could produce modest tariff relief, and the UN General Assembly running September 22nd through 28th, which carries a slim but real chance of Middle East diplomatic progress.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a cluster of negative overhangs including rising off-balance sheet liability exposure up roughly $300 billion in twelve months, massive OpenAI cash burn projections, and regulatory noise around safety and alignment. Energy remains the central macro pressure point — the Iran war is the primary driver of diesel and motor oil shortages, not Ukraine, and Houthi escalation against Saudi Arabia adds fresh upside risk to oil prices. The auto sector is under pressure globally with Volkswagen's dramatic margin cut highlighting China weakness and restructuring costs. Memory/PC components may see some relief as Chinese supply from CXMT ramps and Acer's CEO notes easing shortages.
