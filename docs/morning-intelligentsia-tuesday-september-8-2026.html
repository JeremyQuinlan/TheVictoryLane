MARKET OUTLOOK

The mood is unambiguously sour — September's historically ugly seasonality is colliding with a genuinely dangerous geopolitical backdrop, rising yields, monetary tightening, and a more uncertain AI narrative. Oil spiking toward $100 is a tax on every consumer and business, and the $100B in extra energy costs already absorbed by American households (Axios) isn't going away soon. The Anthropic IPO supply overhang is real, and OpenAI's Astra — while impressive — doesn't resolve the industry's deeply questionable economics. Until the Iran situation finds an off-ramp, expect risk assets to remain defensive.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Houthis staged dramatic strikes on Saudi energy facilities and cities, wounding 70+, as Iran leverages proxies to escalate rather than negotiate (Reuters, CNN). Iran is choosing financial pain over compromise, with intelligence reports indicating Tehran has grown "more confident" in confronting Washington and plans to extend the conflict for months with potential for "significant escalation" (NYT, CNN).

- US/Iran had another kinetic exchange Saturday — Iran fired ballistic missiles at two US Navy warships (both evaded); the Pentagon responded by permanently disabling two Iranian oil tankers and destroying a third, describing them as part of a multibillion-dollar IRGC shadow network.

- Iran and Oman are reportedly days away from formalizing a Hormuz "safe route" agreement (Bloomberg). Separately, the WSJ notes the US is "winning" the war of Hormuz economically, though total volumes remain below pre-war levels and global inventories are severely depleted.

- Witkoff and Kushner's weekend trips to Moscow and Kyiv produced no breakthrough; Putin plans to press on with the war (FT, Bloomberg). The conflict is entering "uncharted and extremely risky territory" with intensifying missile/drone exchanges and Russian provocations against NATO states (NYT).

- Canada implemented tariffs on ~$20B of American imports as US-Canada trade talks remain deadlocked (NYT).

- AfD surged to first place in Germany's Saxony-Anhalt election but fell short of an absolute majority (FT, CNN).

- Panama Canal administrator warns daily transits may be cut further due to water shortages (FT).

- Pentagon is reviewing a sharp reduction in US military footprint in the Middle East post-conflict (CNN).

COMPANY NEWS

NVS (Novartis) is tumbling in European trading after a third negative drug trial readout in recent days — this one for myotonic dystrophy type 1, a neuromuscular disease, involving a drug acquired via the $12B Avidity Biosciences deal. A separate NVS/IONS (Ionis) cholesterol drug study also failed to meet its primary endpoint (WSJ, FT).

GOOGL (Alphabet) received effusive praise for its TPU v7/Ironwood chip, which SemiAnalysis reports delivers 50% better performance per dollar versus Nvidia's B200/B300 — a genuinely noteworthy competitive data point ahead of GOOGL's conference presentation at 1:10pmET Tuesday (SemiAnalysis).

TSLA (Tesla) got another regulatory win as Slovenia became the 6th European country to approve Full Self-Driving, ahead of a broader EU vote (Reuters).

WAFD (WaFd) is acquiring EverBank in a $3.9B deal; the combined entity will trade as "EVBK" under the EverBank name, with expected 2027 EPS accretion of ~29% for WaFd shareholders and a tangible book value earn-back of under two years.

JEF (Jefferies): its Point Bonita fund within Leucadia Asset Management has nearly $500M in exposure to troubled Radiant World, higher than previously reported (FT).

IREN executive stated that global computing supply may never catch up to AI demand (FT). Anthropic abandoned its ~$6B bid for AI startup Decart AI (Bloomberg), while its S1 is now expected late September with a roadshow kicking off mid-October (Reuters).
