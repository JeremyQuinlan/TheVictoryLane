VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTH Anthropic Other

A flood of post-Friday-close headlines hit Anthropic with mixed-to-negative sentiment. The IPO was pushed to November from October, year-end ARR projections of greater than 100-110 billion dollars came in below the 120 billion dollar bull case, the company is targeting a 100 billion dollar raise at a greater than 2 trillion dollar valuation, and OpenAI's Astra launch has sparked concerns about frontier leadership shifting. On the positive side, Anthropic may launch a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture momentum, and it is planning 5 gigawatts of compute by year-end doubling to 10 gigawatts by end of 2027.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article Friday focused on the poor price action in debt tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center facility in New Mexico. Efforts to offload loans tied to the facility have reportedly 'hit a wall,' raising concerns about Oracle's data center financing and exposure to AI infrastructure stress.

▲ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. Under a potential deal, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central, which would clear a key regulatory hurdle for the transaction.

▲ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Warner Bros. Discovery's pending merger with Paramount moved closer to resolution as Paramount entered advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general. A deal requiring CNN editorial independence protections and divestiture of select cable channels could remove a significant regulatory obstacle to closing.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The franchisee failure raises concerns about the health of Wendy's franchise network and the broader quick-service restaurant consumer environment.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk Activist Entry

Novo Nordisk is facing shareholder pressure to diversify away from GLP-1 concentration through mergers and acquisitions. The pressure reflects growing investor concern about over-reliance on a single drug class even as that class remains a dominant growth driver.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on ten or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, a potential meaningful commercial and reputational win for the program after its troubled history. This would represent a significant contract opportunity competing directly with SpaceX.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture announced it will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor AI safety and alignment. The arrangement highlights Accenture's deepening role in enterprise AI governance and positions the firm as a key partner to frontier AI labs ahead of Anthropic's IPO.

◆ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. The move signals intensifying pressure on branded consumer goods companies from large retailers seeking to protect margins amid an elevated cost environment.

▼ VOW Volkswagen Guidance Cut

Volkswagen issued a large downside preannouncement for 2026 operating margins on Friday, now guiding to up to 1% versus the Street's 4.1% estimate and prior guidance of 4 to 5.5%. The miss was driven by a goodwill impairment charge at Porsche, a challenging auto market especially in China, and additional restructuring expenses — though adjusted for special items margins would be roughly 4%, essentially in line with consensus.

Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East conflict and a cluster of concerning AI industry headlines. Houthis dramatically ratcheted up missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities over the weekend, worsening an already fragile geopolitical landscape as Iran activates proxy partners across multiple fronts. Ukraine also launched a massive drone attack on Russia including Moscow, while the State Department warned American citizens in the Middle East of potential for unforeseen escalation.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction — the world's three biggest central banks (BOJ, ECB, and FOMC) all hiked this month. Markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over the coming weeks. The BOJ's latest hike was slightly more dovish than anticipated, but paradoxically sparked macro concern by pressuring the yen lower and rekindling fears about Japan selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid made hawkish remarks Friday warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small improvement in Middle Eastern geopolitics could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The UN General Assembly running September 22-28 in New York brings together officials from multiple Middle East countries including Iran, creating a window for diplomatic progress. The Trump/Xi summit on Thursday could produce modest tariff cuts on roughly 30 billion dollars worth of goods, a modest positive for risk sentiment. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting international pressure is building for de-escalation.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape deteriorated meaningfully over the weekend with Houthis escalating attacks on Saudi Arabian cities and Iran warning of fierce retaliation if the US resumes military actions against it. The AI industry faced a barrage of negative headlines — Anthropic's IPO pushed to November, ARR projections came in below the highest bull expectations, OpenAI is projecting negative 280 billion dollars in free cash flow from 2026 to 2030, and Big Tech's AI off-balance sheet liabilities surged roughly 300 billion dollars in just the last twelve months. Energy price spikes from the Iran war are beginning to work their way through the system and could trigger demand destruction and margin compression. UN General Assembly diplomacy may prove to be wishful thinking given the pace of escalation on multiple fronts.

Market Outlook

Adam's tone is cautious to bearish heading into the week. The net negative weekend news flow — concentrated in AI sentiment and Middle East escalation — sets up a risk-off open Monday. The key catalyst to watch this week is the Trump/Xi summit on Thursday, which could produce modest tariff relief and a positive risk catalyst, but the geopolitical overhang from Iran and the Houthis will remain the dominant macro variable.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a fresh headwind from rapidly rising off-balance sheet liability exposure, up roughly 300 billion dollars in just the last twelve months per the Financial Times, creating a new risk dimension for the sector. The auto sector remains under pressure from weak China demand as evidenced by Volkswagen's severe margin cut. Energy markets are being distorted by the Iran war, with diesel prices spiking and motor oil supplies being rationed at US retailers — a negative for consumer staples and transportation but a tailwind for energy producers. The defense and aerospace sector is caught between opportunity from multiplying global conflicts and Pentagon bandwidth constraints. Semiconductors and memory may get a modest lift from Acer's CEO noting Chinese memory supply is easing component shortages and capping prices, though Chinese competitor CXMT announcing its next-gen platform entering mass production is a longer-term threat to Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron.
