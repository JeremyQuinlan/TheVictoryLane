Closing Recap 2026-08-28

Aug 28, 2026 · 04:04 PM ET

MACRO / FED

Fed Chair Warsh delivered his Jackson Hole remarks, signaling the Fed "will have work to do" if inflation confidence doesn't materialize — his clearest hint yet that rate hikes remain on the table. Fed funds futures repriced sharply, with odds of a September hike jumping to 57% from 35% prior, while the 2-year yield surged 12bps to 4.35% and the 10-year rose 5.6bps to 4.728%. The dollar (DXY) climbed 0.5% to 99.65, reclaiming both its 100dma and 200dma.

Chicago PMI was a major miss at 47.1 vs. 57.9 consensus — the weakest reading of 2026. UMich Consumer Sentiment came in at 51.7 final vs. 51.0 estimate, with the 1-year inflation outlook cooling to 4.0% from a 4.3% preliminary. The BLS also flagged a preliminary NFP benchmark revision of -79,000 for March 2026.

---

SECTOR

Precious metals got crushed on the Warsh hawkish pivot — December gold fell $134.10 (-2.88%) to $4,529.90 and December silver dropped $2.45 (-3.49%) to $67.79. Rate-sensitive sectors — utilities, REITs, telecom, and small caps — bore the brunt of the equity selloff, with the Russell 2000 dropping 1.39%.

Beef/protein names (TSN, PPC, JBS) came under pressure after President Trump accused the four major U.S. meat processors of running a "nasty monopoly" and threatened to allow farmers and ranchers to self-process food.

California utilities (PCG, EIX) sold off sharply after California lawmakers blocked Gov. Newsom's push to shield utilities from insurance company lawsuits stemming from wildfires, keeping wildfire liability reform unresolved ahead of the Aug. 31 session end.

Online sports betting (DKNG, FLUT) popped after a U.S. appeals court rejected Kalshi's bid for an injunction blocking Nevada Gaming regulators' oversight of sports event contracts — a win for regulated sportsbook operators.

Dry bulk shipping (SBLK, GOGL, GNK, NMM, SB) caught a bid as the Baltic Dry Index rose 2.5% to 3,186, its highest since June 2, with a 12.1% weekly gain. The Capesize index surged 17.2% on the week.

STOCKS

ULTA — beat and raise. Q2 EPS $6.55 vs. $6.20 estimate; comps +3.8% vs. +2.3% expected; two-year comp trend improved to +10.5% from +8.2%. Raised full-year guidance.

GAP — shares rose after Q2 EPS of $0.52 (ex-tariff refunds) beat the $0.48 estimate on stronger gross margins and lower SG&A, despite Old Navy top-line weakness. Management was constructive on Old Navy's trajectory heading into 2H.

AFRM — rallied on strong Q4 results: revenue +33% y/y to $1.17B, GMV +36% to $14.1B, net income $1.62B (boosted by deferred tax asset release). Q1 guidance affirmed at GMV $13.7B-$14B and revenue $1.19B-$1.22B.

PYPL — dropped after Bloomberg reported the Advent/Stripe buyout consortium abandoned its pursuit. The group had offered $60.50/share (~$53B valuation), which PYPL's board deemed inadequate.

SOLS — jumped after mutually agreeing with ESI to scrap their $14.5B merger, with both companies citing shareholder preference for independence. RBC flagged SOLS as the most compelling near-term risk/reward in chemicals.

ADSK — solid Q2 beat and guidance raise; raised FY27 billings guidance to $8.575B-$8.65B and revenue to $8.295B-$8.345B, absorbing MaintainX acquisition dilution into prior margin outlook.

WDAY — in-line Q2, mixed guide. FQ3 cRPO growth guided at ~11.5% (a sequential step-down) and initial FY28 subscription growth of 11% disappointed, partially offset by 2pt margin expansion implied in FY28 33% operating margin outlook.

MRVL — beat and raise; Q2 adj. EPS $0.94 on revenue of $2.74B (+37% y/y), topping estimates. Data center revenue +46% y/y to $2.17B; raised FY27/FY28 revenue growth outlook to 45%/50% from 40%/45%. The Google custom-chip deal (up to $120B through FY2033) dominated Q&A but near-term revenue contribution remains unclear.

RBRK — strong quarter; $20M FQ2 ARR beat, $25M raise to FY ARR guide, ARR growth accelerated 1pt to 33%, and NNARR accelerated to 35% from 16% last quarter.

ESTC — shares soared on Q1 results showing slight top-line acceleration, consistent 20% cRPO growth, and a raised FY2027 outlook (revenue $1.998B-$2.010B vs. $1.99B consensus; adj. EPS $3.29-$3.37 vs. $3.24 estimate).

S (SentinelOne) — slid after guiding FY26 adj. EPS $0.30-$0.32, missing the $0.34 estimate, with Q3 revenue and EPS guidance also below consensus. Q2 was a modest beat ($0.08/$292M vs. $0.07/$290.2M).
