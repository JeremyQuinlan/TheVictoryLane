VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTH Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO was pushed to November from October, a delay attributed to volatile AI stock trading, safety/alignment debate, questions about Meta-boosted ARR, and OpenAI's renewed momentum post-Astra launch. Year-end ARR projections came in at over $100-110 billion versus some bulls hoping for $120 billion or higher, and the company is targeting a raise of over $100 billion at a valuation above $2 trillion. Anthropic may launch a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture frontier momentum from OpenAI.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focused on poor price action in debt tied to its massive 'Project Jupiter' data center facility in New Mexico, with efforts to offload loans reportedly hitting a wall. This adds to the broader concern about Big Tech's off-balance sheet AI liability exposure, which has risen roughly $300 billion in just the last 12 months. The report is a meaningful negative for Oracle's data center growth narrative.

▲ PSKY Paramount Global M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. Under a potential settlement, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central. Progress toward settlement removes a key regulatory overhang on the Warner deal closing.

▲ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Warner Bros. Discovery's pending merger with Paramount moved closer to resolution as Paramount entered advanced settlement talks with California's AG. Potential terms include preserving CNN's editorial independence and divesting cable channels like Comedy Central. Clearing this regulatory hurdle is a net positive for deal certainty.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Franchisee financial stress is a direct negative signal for same-store sales momentum and near-term royalty revenue. This adds to broader concerns about consumer spending weakness at quick-service restaurants.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes. The move signals Kroger is willing to use its shelf space as leverage in pricing disputes, which could pressure other premium-priced consumer brands. For Red Bull and similar suppliers, this is a meaningful distribution loss.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk M&A

Novo Nordisk is being pressured by shareholders to diversify away from GLP-1 exposure through mergers and acquisitions. The shareholder pressure reflects concern about overconcentration risk in the weight-loss drug franchise. Any M&A action could be meaningful given Novo's large balance sheet.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on ten or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, a potential significant contract win following Starliner's troubled recent history. This would be a meaningful positive for Boeing's space and defense segment and help rehabilitate the Starliner program's reputation. The talks represent a direct competitive dynamic with SpaceX.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators at Anthropic to monitor safety and alignment, a partnership announced Friday night. The deal positions Accenture as a key enterprise AI governance partner and could open further commercial relationships with frontier AI labs. It is modest in size but strategically meaningful for Accenture's AI services positioning.

◆ NSCL Nscale Other

London-based neocloud firm Nscale filed its S-1 publicly on Friday afternoon and aims to list on the NYSE under the ticker NSCL. The filing adds to the growing pipeline of AI infrastructure IPOs and will be closely watched as a read on investor appetite for neocloud compute businesses. Details on financials and valuation targets were not specified in this update.

Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. Houthis dramatically ramped up missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities over the weekend, with the Iran war continuing to mutate and open new fronts. Ukraine also launched a massive drone attack on Russia including Moscow, adding to the global geopolitical risk premium.

Global monetary policy remains pointed in a hawkish direction, with the world's three biggest central banks — BOJ, ECB, and FOMC — all hiking this month. The BOJ's rate hike was more dovish than anticipated, but paradoxically triggered macro concern by pressuring the yen lower and rekindling fears about Japan selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid made hawkish remarks Friday, warning that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil prices.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems trace back to oil, and even a tiny geopolitical improvement could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with favorable knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better margins, and improved consumer confidence. The UN General Assembly brings together officials from key Middle East countries including Iran, creating a real opportunity for diplomatic progress. The Trump/Xi summit could deliver modest but meaningful tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion worth of goods, and China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis — a sign the world is alarmed enough to act.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape is quite grim, with Iran tightening its grip on global energy supplies via Hormuz, aligned militias in Iraq, and the Houthis who just dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia. Energy price spikes will begin working through the system very quickly, and it's hard to imagine this won't trigger demand destruction. On top of geopolitics, the AI industry saw a cluster of negative headlines after Friday's close — OpenAI's projected negative free cash flow of $280 billion through 2030, concerns about Oracle-linked data center debt, and Anthropic's IPO being pushed to November with ARR projections coming in below the most bullish expectations.

Market Outlook

Adam leans bearish heading into the week, with the weekend news flow net negative on AI and geopolitics. The key catalysts to watch are the Trump/Xi summit Thursday September 24th and the UN General Assembly running all week, which could provide either a diplomatic surprise to the upside or further escalation to the downside on Middle East/Iran.

Sector Watch

AI and technology broadly face a cluster of negative overhangs heading into the week, including rising off-balance sheet liability exposure up roughly $300 billion in 12 months, OpenAI's massive projected cash burn, and Anthropic's IPO delay. Energy and geopolitical risk remain the dominant macro sector theme, with oil infrastructure and refining capacity under pressure from the Iran war. Auto sector is challenged, with Volkswagen issuing a large profit warning citing weak China demand and restructuring costs. Consumer discretionary faces franchisee stress signals from Wendy's and retailer-supplier tensions at Kroger.
