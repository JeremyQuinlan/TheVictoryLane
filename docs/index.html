Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with the S&P up 49 points or 65 basis points, Nasdaq up 329 points or 110 basis points, and Russell 2000 up 47 basis points. Asian markets closed higher across the board with the Hang Seng up 1.18% and Korea KOSPI up 1.65%, while European indices are up 85-120 basis points. The strength is surprising given a weekend full of negative headlines including escalating Middle East geopolitics, troubling AI industry news, and no US-China trade truce extension — the primary driver appears to be optimism around the UN General Assembly and potential Iran diplomatic progress.

Treasuries have a bid with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The Fed is still in a hawkish posture — markets are pricing in roughly 33 basis points of additional rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds on a hike at the October 28 meeting and another 35 basis points of increases priced in for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks, warning that inflation is broad-based and not just an energy story. Treasury plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt and above its own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Nearly all of the market's macro problems trace back to elevated oil prices, and even a modest geopolitical improvement could trigger an outsized decline in energy, unleashing lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. The UN General Assembly running September 22-28 in New York brings together officials from multiple Middle East nations including Iran, creating a genuine diplomatic window. Trump backed off air strikes against the Houthis at the last minute Sunday and signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, a meaningfully dovish shift. China and Pakistan are both reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting broader international alignment toward de-escalation.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape remains deeply troubled — the Houthis dramatically escalated their attacks on Saudi Arabia over the weekend, Iran is warning of fierce retaliation if the US resumes military action, and Ukraine launched its largest drone strike of the war against Russia. Weekend AI headlines were uniformly negative: Anthropic's IPO is delayed, its 2027 ARR projections disappointed, OpenAI is burning cash at a staggering rate projecting negative 280 billion dollars in free cash flow through 2030, and Oracle's data center debt tied to Project Jupiter has hit a wall with lenders. Oil tanker costs are surging, motor oil is being rationed at US retailers, and higher energy costs will increasingly filter through to corporate margins and consumer spending — the demand destruction has not yet fully arrived in the data.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday's session, driven entirely by diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly and hopes for Iran de-escalation. The key catalyst to watch is any signal of progress on Iran-related talks at the GA, particularly whether Trump meets with Iran's president — even a small step toward de-escalation could trigger a sharp drop in oil prices and a cascade of bullish macro effects including lower yields, better margins, and improved consumer confidence.

Sector Watch

Data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in European trading Monday morning. AI sector faces meaningful negative sentiment after a weekend of damaging headlines: Anthropic's IPO delayed, its 2027 ARR projections underwhelmed, OpenAI projecting roughly 280 billion dollars in negative free cash flow through 2030, and Oracle data center debt issues surfacing. Big Tech AI off-balance sheet liability exposure has risen roughly 300 billion dollars in the last 12 months, creating a new risk overhang for the sector. Chinese memory giant CXMT announced its next-generation platform is in mass production, putting additional competitive pressure on Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Energy sector faces complex crosscurrents with Brent crude sliding 170 basis points to around 102 dollars and 10 cents even as geopolitical risks remain extreme — oil tanker costs surging and motor oil being rationed at US retailers.
