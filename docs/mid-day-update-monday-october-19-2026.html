Mid-Day Update · Monday October 19, 2026

Oct 19, 2026 · 10:58 AM ET

Here's your midday market update for Monday October 19th. Stocks are mostly higher but the rally is narrow and tech-driven. The S&P 500 is up about 48 points or 63 basis points, the Nasdaq is up 144 basis points, but the equal-weight S&P is actually in the red, down 38 basis points, and the Dow is slightly negative. That divergence tells you everything — this is a large-cap tech relief bounce, not a broad market advance. Treasuries are selling off with yields up 4 to 6 basis points across the curve, while Fed rate expectations are holding steady. Brent crude is down about 140 basis points as markets digest news that Russia may be willing to make some concessions around Ukraine, though nobody should get too excited — Putin's maximalist demands haven't changed and the Middle East situation remains serious, with Saudi Arabia canceling in-person events at its Future Investment Initiative conference. On the macro data front, China delivered a beat — Q3 GDP came in at plus 4.5% versus expectations of plus 4.4%, and September retail sales crushed it at plus 1.1% versus the half-percent expected. On the US side, the October NAHB housing index was very weak, adding to concerns about domestic rate sensitivity. Now to company news. Cleveland-Cliffs is the big earnings winner today — they beat Q3 EPS and EBITDA estimates and guided 2027 EBITDA to greater than 4 billion dollars versus the Street's 2.5 billion dollar estimate. The stock is spiking. Fifth Third Bancorp also beat, posting adjusted EPS of $1.13 versus the $1.08 expected, with outperformance driven by tight cost controls and lower-than-expected loan loss provisions. Apple is getting positive attention after saying demand for its new Duo foldable phone has been — in their words — nothing short of extraordinary, with delivery times pushed into January. Werner Enterprises is the ugly story — they warned Q3 EPS will come in at 15 to 16 cents versus Street expectations of 36 cents, a massive miss driven by fuel costs, driver retention spending, and insurance reserve charges. Blue Owl Capital is reportedly in play after bankers began approaching them about a full or partial sale. And the big AI datacenter debate continues — SemiAnalysis published a bullish piece this morning arguing that large compute sites are getting built on time and that 2027 will be a year of acceleration, pushing back on the recent wave of datacenter skepticism. Sentiment overall remains cautious. Most market participants are treating today's advance warily. The real test comes Tuesday night when SPCX reports, followed by META on Wednesday night. Those two prints will set the tone for the entire AI spending narrative for the rest of the year. Stay focused, stay disciplined, and don't chase the bounce before those reports hit.
