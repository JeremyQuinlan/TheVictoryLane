RATES & FED

Treasuries caught a modest bid Monday as yields fell 3–5 basis points across the curve, helped by the drop in oil and Washington's FX support for Japan. The move was notably tepid given that two major yield drivers were supposedly addressed over the weekend — the market isn't buying the all-clear. The Fed's Quarterly Refunding Statement revealed borrowing in the September quarter will come in significantly above May estimates, keeping supply pressure firmly in play.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

Monday's rally was real but don't get too comfortable — the hyperscaler blowouts from Microsoft and Amazon have dramatically improved the AI capex narrative, yet the underlying FCF picture is deteriorating (Google, Amazon went FCF negative; Meta is at breakeven), meaning capital markets will be called upon to plug a widening financing hole. That's a structural overhang that one good earnings week doesn't resolve. The Treasury tepidness is the tell: if yields can't fall meaningfully when oil drops AND Japan gets FX help simultaneously, the bond market is telling you something about the durability of this equity move. Iran is fading to background noise for equities — Trump will be forced toward a heavily Tehran-favorable MOU — but the broader yield, inflation, and AI financing story remains the dominant risk. Trade the rally; don't marry it.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Iran/US: Trump called off a threatened airstrike against Iran, citing an imminent "deal," but Tehran publicly denied holding talks with Washington. The most optimistic outcome is a sustained lull during which Iran expands control over the Strait of Hormuz; the worst case is a brief deescalation followed by renewed kinetic activity. Depleted global oil inventories have removed a key buffer, and multiple reports indicate US military forces in the region are stretched dangerously thin.

- Japan/FX: Treasury Secretary Bessent confirmed the US stands ready to intervene further to support the yen, and is calling on the Fed to expand its repurchase facility for foreign central banks — potentially allowing Japan to fund FX intervention without selling Treasuries. Analysts caution the Treasury's current firepower is limited absent extraordinary measures (Bloomberg).

- OPEC+: Members agreed to raise output by ~188,000 BPD — a move that fully unwinds the 1.65 million BPD cut from 2023 but is largely symbolic given ongoing Middle East disruptions (Bloomberg, WSJ).

- Fed credibility: St. Louis Fed President Musalem said the spike in bond yields signals markets are questioning the Fed's inflation-fighting credibility, and suggested he would have voted for a rate hike at the last meeting (FT). Reports that incoming Fed Chair Warsh may reduce annual FOMC meetings from eight to four are amplifying monetary transparency concerns (NYT, WSJ).
