VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ AZO AutoZone Earnings Beat

AutoZone beat FQ4 EPS with $56.05 vs. the Street's $54.03, but the beat was low quality — driven by a 145 basis point tariff refund tailwind and a 105 basis point non-cash LIFO benefit to gross margins. Domestic comps missed at +1.6% vs. the Street's +3.1%, and the operating expense ratio worsened 100 basis points year-over-year to 33.4% of sales. Management noted improving sales linearity in the back half of the quarter and expressed confidence heading into fiscal 2027.

▲ CGNX Cognex M&A

Cognex announced the acquisition of RealSense, the market leader in depth-sensing cameras and vision technology for robotic perception and Physical AI, for approximately $500 million upfront plus an additional ~$106.5 million. RealSense is expected to generate $80–90 million in revenue, representing more than 50% year-over-year growth. The deal positions Cognex to expand meaningfully into the fast-growing Physical AI and robotics perception space.

◆ MLKN MillerKnoll Earnings Beat

MillerKnoll reported solid FQ1 EPS of 53 cents vs. the Street's 36 cents, and notably would have still beaten even excluding an 11-cent tariff refund tailwind — adjusted EPS of 42 cents still cleared consensus. Sales missed, declining 3.3% organically to $923.4 million vs. the Street's $943.2 million, though orders rose 3.5% year-over-year. Fiscal 2027 sales guidance was trimmed to $3.88–4.03 billion from $3.93–4.13 billion, while the EPS range of $1.85–2.15 was left unchanged.

◆ PNR Pentair Other

Pentair named Bob Hau as its new CFO effective November 1, with current interim CFO Bob Fishman staying on through that date to ensure a smooth transition. Hau brings prior CFO experience at Fiserv, TE Connectivity, and Lennox International. This is a leadership change with no immediate financial impact disclosed.

▼ THO THOR Industries Earnings Miss

THOR Industries missed FQ4 EPS at 78 cents vs. the Street's 87 cents, as gross margins fell 230 basis points year-over-year to 12.4% against the Street's 13.85% estimate, despite revenue coming in slightly above at $2.311 billion vs. the Street's $2.17 billion. Management cited stubborn interest rates, elevated fuel costs, and inflationary pressure squeezing household budgets and keeping retail soft. Some bright spots included healthy dealer inventory, structural cost reduction actions underway, and a ~75% year-over-year surge in North American towable RV order backlog, though the company declined to provide formal fiscal 2027 guidance until later in the fall.

▲ VKTX Viking Therapeutics FDA Approval

Viking Therapeutics shares spiked in pre-market trading after reporting positive clinical trial results for its GLP-1 drug VK2735, showing 22% weight loss in trial participants. The results are a significant positive catalyst for the company as it competes in the high-growth GLP-1 obesity drug market. This positions VKTX as a meaningful contender in the space dominated by larger pharma players.
