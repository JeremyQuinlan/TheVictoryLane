MARKET OUTLOOK

The Thursday BMO earnings slate is a genuinely mixed bag — you've got industrials and aerospace printing clean beats while consumer names show cracks, and fintech (FISV) is a flat-out mess. The tariff refund tailwind is real but finite, and management teams are already telegraphing that it won't repeat. If the consumer-facing misses (CELH, KVUE, ZTS, SGI) start clustering, that's a trend worth respecting heading into the back half.

COMPANY NEWS

ARHS (Arhaus) delivered a strong Q2 beat — sales came in at $385MM vs. the Street's $365MM — aided by IEEPA tariff refunds, though management says they would have beaten even without them. Full-year EBITDA guidance was raised by less than the refund amount as the company reinvests a portion back into operations, and the sales outlook was held flat to reflect "uncertainty related to the consumer environment, geopolitical conditions, and the timing of written sales conversion to delivered sales."

CELH (Celsius) missed across the board in Q2, posting EPS of 36c on revenue of $818MM versus the Street at 41c/$872MM. Sales fell 11.7% Y/Y, pressured by elevated trade and promotional spending, inventory rebalancing-driven shipment timing, club channel softness, a deliberate pullback in innovation activity, and SKU rationalization — with higher aluminum costs adding margin pressure on top.

FUN (Six Flags) posted headline revenue and EBITDA shortfalls in Q2, but management pushed back, arguing that on an apples-to-apples basis (stripping out sold/closed parks) the company grew revenue, EBITDA, and attendance Y/Y — calling the results evidence that its turnaround is working.

GOLF (Acushnet) beat on Q2 sales and EBITDA, driven by the earlier-than-typical launch of Titleist GTS drivers and fairways, plus IEEPA tariff refunds. Full-year guidance was raised, and management stressed the beat was organic even before the refund benefit.

KDP (Keurig Dr Pepper) posted modest Q2 upside — EPS of 57c on revenue of $7.31B vs. 53c/$7.23B — with US Refreshment and International leading while US Coffee disappointed. Full-year guidance was reaffirmed.

KVUE (Kenvue) missed on both EPS (31c vs. 32c expected) and organic sales growth (+1.6% vs. +2.3% expected), with Self Care and Essential Health both falling short. The KMB deal is expected to close in Q4.

PLNT (Planet Fitness) beat on Q2 EPS (88c vs. 84c) and comps (+1.7% vs. +1.0%), nudging full-year EPS growth guidance to 6% from 4%, with buybacks driving the delta. A solid recovery from the weak Q1 print.

QSR (Restaurant Brands) beat on Q2 EPS ($1.07 vs. $1.03) and system comps (+3.8% vs. +3.1%), with Burger King a standout at +8.6%, while Tim Hortons, Popeyes, and Firehouse all missed.

SGI (Somnigroup) missed on Q2 sales ($1.823B vs. $1.88B, -3% Y/Y) and trimmed full-year EPS guidance, with the new midpoint at $3.00 vs. the prior $3.20.

TAP (Molson Coors) eked out a few cents of EPS upside ($1.58 vs. $1.52) and volume declines of -4.8% were less severe than the -6.5% the Street feared. Full-year guidance was reiterated despite what management called "heightened global macroeconomic headwinds."

USFD (US Foods) delivered solid Q2 results — sales of $10.53B vs. $10.46B, EPS of $1.44 vs. $1.36 — though chain channel case volumes were light. Independent volumes held up well, and full-year guidance was affirmed. Management's read on the macro: "challenging but stable."

FISV (Fiserv) was the clearest disappointment of the morning — Q2 revenue missed (-5% organic vs. -3% expected), EPS came in at $1.84 vs. $1.91, and full-year EPS guidance was slashed to $7.30 from $8.15. Management's reiteration of medium-term growth targets is the only silver lining, and consensus cuts are coming.

BDX (Becton Dickinson) beat in FQ3 on both sales (+4.4% FXN vs. +2.6% expected) and EPS ($3.23 vs. $3.14), with strength in Connected Care, BioPharma Systems, and Interventional. Full-year EPS midpoint nudged to $12.67 from $12.62.

VTRS (Viatris) posted strong Q2 upside — EPS of 69c on $3.8B revenue vs. 60c/$3.674B — with China a key driver, and raised full-year EPS guidance midpoint to $2.52 from $2.40.

ZTS (Zoetis) beat on EPS by a couple cents but missed on sales and cut full-year guidance again, with the new EPS midpoint at $6.20 vs. the prior ~$6.93. Management cited "lower clinic visits and pet owner price sensitivity" alongside heightened competition in key categories.

DAN (Dana) posted modest Q2 EBITDA and revenue upside, raised full-year guidance, and announced it is restarting its share buyback program.

FAST (Fastenal) reported July daily sales growth of 15.5%, a tick above June's +15%.

HWM (Howmet) delivered strong Q2 results — EPS of $1.33 and revenue of $2.54B vs. $1.25/$2.42B expected — and raised full-year EPS guidance midpoint by 33 cents to $5.27.

PH (Parker Hannifin) printed a blowout FQ4 — EPS of $9.27 vs. $8.21, organic sales +8% vs. +4.5% — and issued bullish F27 guidance calling for 7% organic growth and EPS of $34.75, both well above consensus.

DDOG (Datadog) beat on Q2 sales and earnings and raised full-year EPS guidance to $2.50–$2.54 from $2.36–$2.44. Solid, but the magnitude of upside was notably smaller than last quarter's blowout.

EPAM (EPAM Systems) posted decent Q2 results but trimmed full-year FXN organic revenue growth guidance to +2–3
