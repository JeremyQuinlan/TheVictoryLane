RATES & FED

Treasuries are extending Monday's losses with yields up another 1–3 basis points across the curve Tuesday morning, pressured by elevated inflation expectations, geopolitical risk, and AI-driven bond issuance. July CPI (Wednesday) is expected to cool only 10 basis points versus June, keeping both headline and core well above the Fed's target — not exactly the relief the market needs.

---

MARKET OUTLOOK

The resilience here is genuinely impressive — equities are holding up with Brent kissing $90 and yields grinding higher, which tells you the market isn't ready to roll over just yet. That said, the bull case is increasingly dependent on a Hormuz de-escalation that frankly hasn't materialized, and the rate backdrop is unambiguously hostile. Wednesday's CPI print is the week's pivotal event — a hot number could finally break the equity market's complacency. The AI infrastructure buildout (Nvidia's $500B+ deal, FRMI's first lease, RIOT's Anthropic compute agreement) remains a powerful underlying bid. Stay alert but don't chase.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Gulf state governments are increasingly accepting Iranian control over Hormuz as a "new normal," preferring accommodation over renewed conflict (WSJ). Iran's new leadership, meanwhile, is signaling a hardline approach toward Washington (WSJ, FT) — though the author's view is the narrative has swung too far bearish, with both sides holding real incentives to de-escalate toward a return to MOU terms.

- The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve has dropped below 300 million barrels, the lowest since 1983, constraining Washington's options (CNBC). Houthis in Yemen are growing more aggressive, attacking both the formal Yemeni government and Saudi Arabia.

- Ukraine is considering rerouting grain exports via rail through Moldova amid intensified Russian strikes on Black Sea shipping (Reuters).

- Australia's RBA held rates steady but warned another hike is "quite possible" given elevated inflation (Reuters, WSJ). South Korea's central bank deputy governor signaled further hikes ahead as growth stays resilient (Bloomberg). Japan's BOJ faces a two-front dilemma: inflation and yen weakness argue for hikes, while fiscal imbalances may require balance sheet expansion to cap JGB yields (Reuters).

- Singapore revised its 2026 GDP forecast higher, citing AI-linked tailwinds (CNBC).

- The SEC and CFTC are drafting pro-crypto rules following the Clarity Act's failure in Congress (Politico).

COMPANY NEWS

NVDA formally confirmed its $500B+ AI infrastructure financing partnership — the biggest single AI capital commitment on record.

RIOT (Riot Platforms) struck a $9.1B compute deal with Anthropic (Bloomberg). Separately, Anthropic is targeting a Sept/Oct IPO and has been actively pushing back on investor concerns around Chinese open-weight models, White House tensions, and data center backlash (WSJ).

INTC priced 210.5M shares at $95, raising $20B — well above the original $15B target (WSJ).

FRMI (Fermi) executed its first binding lease at Project Matador in Texas with TensorWave covering 222MW, expected to generate ~$6.5B in contracted revenue over 15 years.

AAPL was hit Monday on a downgrade citing no all-glass iPhone, but Bloomberg reported after the close that the product remains on track for 2027.

ALC (Alcon) beat Q2 EPS handily (84c vs. 76c Street) on 7% FXN sales growth and 150 basis points of margin expansion; full-year EPS guide raised to +12–15% (IHG results release).

HIMS posted strong Q2 beats — EBITDA $60.3MM vs. $47MM, sales $753.2MM vs. $699MM — but cut full-year EBITDA guidance to $275–325MM from $275–350MM as margins compress.

ACM (AECOM) badly missed FQ3 on a $327MM pre-tax construction project loss; FY EPS guide slashed to $3.95–4.15 from $5.90–6.10. Underlying business (B2B 1.6x) is intact.

ONON (On) reported a rare Q2 sales miss driven by wholesale softness and FX; trimmed full-year FXN revenue growth to low-20% from "at least 23%" — raises questions about Nike recapturing running share.

SPG (Simon) beat on real estate FFO ($3.29 vs. $3.22) and raised full-year guidance to $13.20–13.30 from $13.10–13.25.

SFD (Smithfield) beat Q2 EPS by 2 cents (62c vs. 60c) on $3.7B sales, but cut full-year op. income guidance to $1.225–1.375B from $1.325–1.475B citing cautious consumers and input costs.

BMY / AstraZeneca deal collapsed after Astra shares fell on the news and the UK government signaled discomfort (FT).

EBAY — GameStop's Ryan Cohen is reportedly weighing withdrawal of his $56B bid (Bloomberg).

GM is exiting the SynergyCells battery JV as Samsung SDI buys its stake (WSJ).

LULU Chief Strategy Officer departed one month before incoming CEO Heidi O'Neill takes over (Bloomberg).

CELH (Celsius) announced management changes including the COO's departure following weak earnings and activist pressure from Rockstar Energy founder Russ Savage (~4.7% stake).

Shein IPO could launch as soon as next Wednesday in Hong Kong at a sub-$30B valuation — roughly 70% below its peak (FT, Reuters).
