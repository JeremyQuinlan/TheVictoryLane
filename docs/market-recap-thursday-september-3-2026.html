Market Recap · Thursday September 3, 2026

Sep 03, 2026 · 04:04 PM ET

MARKETS

SPX +1.06%, Nasdaq +1.40%, Dow +623 points, R2K +51 basis points. DXY -60 basis points, JPY +190 basis points vs. USD. Brent roughly flat. Gold +220 basis points, Silver +270 basis points, BTC +565 basis points.

RATES & FED

Treasuries posted modest gains Thursday with yields dipping 1–3 basis points following dovish remarks from Fed Governor Waller. Markets now price 33 basis points of hikes remaining this year, with roughly 50/50 odds on a move at the September 16 meeting. Waller was explicit: the August CPI on September 11 — not Friday's jobs report — is the deciding factor for the September 16 decision.

MARKET OUTLOOK

Thursday's rally was clean and broad-based, driven by a credible dovish pivot from Waller and a blowout SNOW print that lit up the software complex. The real risk here isn't jobs — both Warsh and Waller have effectively dismissed employment as a near-term policy lever — it's the August CPI. A hot print on September 11 reopens the hike debate aggressively. AI competition is intensifying in ways that are starting to hurt industry economics: token pricing is sliding, custom accelerators are eating into GPU share, and the frontier model race is compressing margins across the stack. Don't confuse capex strength with uniform equity upside — the bifurcation between infrastructure beneficiaries and commoditizing model providers is only going to widen.

GEOPOLITICAL

- Approximately 17–18 million barrels of oil transited Hormuz Monday and Tuesday, not far from pre-war levels of 20 million, as the Pentagon gradually degrades Iran's ability to control the waterway (CNN). Syria is playing a growing role helping Iraqi oil bypass Hormuz via pipeline (Washington Post).

- Trump has discussed with aides declaring the Iran war over, though the White House may escalate kinetically after the midterms (WSJ, Reuters). The Pentagon is extending Middle East troop deployments into 2027, preserving military options (WSJ).

- The Iran conflict remains dangerously stalemated — Tehran continues firing on ships in Hormuz and US bases throughout the region, Houthis are targeting Red Sea shipping, and refined product shortages (particularly diesel) persist.

- Japan's elevated JGB yields are reversing a long-running outflow of capital from the country, with money returning home in response to higher domestic rates — a seismic shift for global capital flows (Reuters). The BOJ is said to favor a 25 basis point hike later this month with potential to accelerate thereafter (Bloomberg).

- China's Caixin services PMI for August came in at 51.4, well ahead of the 50.6 consensus and up from 50.4 in July (Bloomberg). Switzerland's August CPI overshot at +0.8% Y/Y vs. +0.5% expected (Bloomberg).

COMPANY NEWS

SNOW reported blowout FQ results: sales +35% to $1.55B (vs. Street $1.485B), net revenue retention of 126%, and operating margins of 15.3% vs. the Street's 12.6%. Full-year product revenue growth guide lifted to 36% from 31%, with operating margins guided to 14.5% vs. prior 13.5%. The print fueled a broader software rally.

NVDA formally announced plans to acquire Hugging Face for $13 billion, a deal that had been speculated about for weeks.

AVGO reported FQ3 results mostly inline; FQ4 and F27 guidance was underwhelming, though F28 commentary was bullish. The stock slumped despite solid numbers — expectations bar is simply too high. Notably, Broadcom said Anthropic and OpenAI are set to become its #1 and #2 XPU customers.

CIEN reported strong FQ3 EPS of $2.11 (vs. Street $1.74) on sales of $1.67B (+37%), with gross margins up 450 basis points Y/Y and operating margins up 1,180 basis points. FQ4 guide of $1.75B topped the Street's $1.7B — yet the stock sold off, same story as AVGO.

NTAP reported FQ1 EPS of $2.58 and revenue of $2.025B, blowing past Street estimates of $2.12 and $1.832B respectively. Full-year guidance raised.

CPB reported FQ4 EPS inline at 39 cents but missed on organic sales (-1% vs. Street -0.2%), gross margins compressed 190 basis points (tariffs and supply chain costs), and the quarterly dividend was slashed from 39 cents to 25 cents per share. F27 guidance was ugly.

TSN issued a negative preannouncement: F26 sales now seen +1.5–2% vs. prior guidance of +2.5–3.5%, with operating income guided to $1.85–2.05B vs. prior $2.1–2.3B. Food staples broadly are getting crushed by MAHA/GLP-1 headwinds, private labels, tariffs, and weak consumer spending.

AGX posted blowout FQ2 EBITDA of $70MM vs. Street $49MM, with revenue of $384MM vs. Street $300MM. Margins expanded 200 basis points Y/Y, though backlog declined both sequentially and year-over-year.

MSFT announced it will increase financial disclosure starting in F27, breaking out Azure and M365 Cloud more explicitly — viewed as an important positive for the stock.

META rolled out Muse Spark 1.3, with Zuckerberg calling it "frontier performance almost too cheap to meter." The next potential disruption: Meta pivoting its own internal token consumption away from third-party frontier models entirely.

COST reported August US core comps (ex-gas and FX) of +5.6%, a modest miss vs. the Street's +6% and down from +6.9% in July.

BRC beat FQ4 on EPS ($1.48 vs. Street $1.35) and sales ($437MM vs. Street $428MM); F27 EPS guide midpoint of $6.50 above the Street's $6.39.

VSXY posted FQ2 EPS of 95 cents ex-tariff refunds (vs. Street 78 cents, guidance 65–75 cents), driven by margin expansion. Sales up 10%, near the top of guidance. Q3 operating income outlook came in light.

ODFL reported August revenue per day +12.4%, driven by fuel surcharges and pricing; tons per day fell 0.9%.

F aims to sell 100,000+ units of its new ~$30K EV pickup, the Ford Fathom, in year one — described as a very ambitious target (WSJ).

FIVE beat FQ2 estimates handily: EPS $1.68 vs. Street $1.39, comps +14.1% vs. Street +10.6%. Full-year guide raised.
