Market Recap · Wednesday September 30, 2026
Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Markets are experiencing a yield slump at the start of a new month and new quarter, driven primarily by technical factors — Treasuries had become extremely oversold after a brutal multi-month selloff. The manufacturing ISM print was quite hot with a scorching inflation figure, while Brent crude extended its gains. The key question is whether the yield pullback is a temporary technical bounce or the beginning of a larger trend reversal.

Fed Vice Chair Jefferson echoed Williams in signaling the Fed will likely take no action at the upcoming October meeting, though this was already consensus and has limited bearing on long-end yields. A dovish Fed stance could actually fuel higher 10- and 30-year yields rather than suppress them. Friday's September jobs report is expected to show a sharp slowdown in hiring — consensus around +88K versus +162K in August — which, combined with recent PCE data, could blunt the aggressive-tightening narrative.

Market Outlook

Neutral to cautiously bearish lean. The yield relief is viewed as technical and temporary rather than fundamentally justified. The real catalyst to watch is Friday's September payrolls print — a weak number near consensus could extend the yield rally, but the underlying inflation and energy backdrop remains unfavorable for sustained bond strength.

Sector Watch

Energy remains firm with Brent extending gains, supported by stalled US-Iran diplomacy and a worsening global shortage of refined products. Memory semiconductors are seeing a structural repricing higher driven by AI demand, with both Micron and Samsung commentary confirming the trend. Fixed income and rate-sensitive sectors face a murky near-term outlook — yield relief may be technical and temporary.

Calendar
Oct 01 — Manufacturing ISM (already released, hot print with strong inflation component)
Oct 03 — September Nonfarm Payrolls (consensus ~+88K vs. +162K prior)
