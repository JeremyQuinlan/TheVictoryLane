Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points (65 basis points), Nasdaq +329 points (110 basis points), and Russell 2000 +47 basis points. Asian markets posted broad gains led by Korea KOSPI +1.65% and Hang Seng +1.18%, while European indices are up 85-120 basis points with strength in industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The primary driver appears to be diplomatic optimism ahead of the UN General Assembly in New York (September 22-28), where leaders hope meetings can cool Middle East tensions — this despite a weekend full of net negative news including escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine's largest-ever drone strike on Russia, and troubling AI industry headlines.

Treasuries have a bid this morning with yields down 2-3 basis points across the curve. The Fed remains in a hawkish posture — markets are pricing approximately 33 basis points of rate hikes across the final two meetings of the year, with roughly 50/50 odds of an increase at the October 28 meeting, and an additional 35 basis points of hikes priced for 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil, and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari echoed those hawkish sentiments Sunday, saying inflation is reaching all corners of the economy. The Treasury plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt — above its own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Diplomatic optimism around the UN General Assembly is the primary bull driver — Trump has signaled openness to meeting Iran's president, and China and Pakistan are reportedly pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting international pressure is building. If any credible diplomatic progress emerges, oil could drop sharply, which would cascade into lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump-Xi summit on Thursday could also produce modest tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion in goods, offering another incremental positive. Technically, futures are rallying despite a wall of bad news, suggesting the market may have already partially priced in the geopolitical risk.

BEAR CASE

Nearly all the weekend news flow was net negative — Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine launched its largest-ever drone strike on Russia hitting an oil refinery near Moscow, and the AI sector was hit with multiple headwinds including a delayed Anthropic IPO, weak 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. Monetary policy globally remains hawkish with the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiking this month and more increases expected, while core inflation is running hot independent of energy. The US-China trade truce extension was not agreed upon Sunday, and Washington is expected to raise the aggregate tariff rate on Chinese imports back to pre-Supreme Court IEEPA levels in coming weeks.

Market Outlook

Adam leans cautiously mixed with a diplomatic-hope-driven bull bias for today's session, but with significant downside risk lurking. The key catalyst to watch is any headline out of the UN General Assembly suggesting progress on Iran or Middle East diplomacy — even a modest signal could trigger an outsized oil selloff with broad bullish knock-on effects across the market.

Autos sector watching closely for any tariff developments out of the Trump-Xi summit on Thursday. Memory/semiconductors seeing some nuance — Chinese memory giant CXMT says its next-gen platform is in mass production, while Acer's CEO noted Chinese memory supply is helping ease component shortages and cap prices, a modest headwind for Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron pricing power.
