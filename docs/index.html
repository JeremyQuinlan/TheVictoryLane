VK · Earnings Whispers
◆ ANTHROPIC Anthropic Other

Anthropic's IPO was pushed to November from October, a slight delay that disappointed bulls. Year-end ARR projections came in at above 100-110 billion dollars versus some bulls hoping for 120 billion dollars or higher, and the company is considering launching a powerful new model ahead of the IPO to recapture momentum lost to OpenAI's Astra. The S1 has not yet been made public but could drop in late September or early October.

▼ ORCL Oracle Other

Oracle was the subject of a negative Financial Times article focusing on poor price action in debt tied to its massive Project Jupiter data center in New Mexico, with efforts to offload loans from the facility having 'hit a wall.' This raises concerns about Oracle's data center financing strategy and potential balance sheet risk at a time when AI infrastructure spending is under scrutiny.

▲ PSKY Paramount Global M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve the lawsuit against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery, and could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central. Progress on the settlement removes a key regulatory overhang on the deal closing.

▲ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Paramount's advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general over the state's lawsuit against the Warner deal represent meaningful progress toward deal closing. A settlement requiring CNN editorial independence preservation and some cable channel divestitures would be a relatively modest concession to clear the regulatory path.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure Friday after one of its largest franchisees, Meritage Hospitality Group, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Franchisee financial stress signals underlying weakness in the quick-service restaurant consumer environment and raises questions about Wendy's unit economics.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk M&A

Novo Nordisk is being pressured by shareholders to diversify away from GLP-1 exposure through M&A, reflecting investor concern about over-concentration in a single drug class. This shareholder pressure could accelerate deal-making activity but also signals unease about the company's long-term growth profile beyond obesity and diabetes.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner spacecraft to be used on ten or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, a potential meaningful contract win that would rehabilitate Starliner's reputation after its troubled crewed mission. This would directly compete with SpaceX for NASA commercial crew business.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture announced it will embed a team of evaluators to monitor safety and alignment at Anthropic, positioning the firm as a key enterprise partner in AI governance. The arrangement gives Accenture a front-row seat to Anthropic's technology ahead of its IPO and reinforces Accenture's AI services strategy.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores as the grocery giant pushes back against supplier price hikes, a meaningful escalation in retailer-supplier tension in the energy drink category. This is negative for Red Bull and signals that large grocers are increasingly willing to use shelf space as leverage against premium-priced brands.

▼ VOW Volkswagen Guidance Cut

Volkswagen issued a large downside pre-announcement Friday, now guiding for operating margins of up to 1% for 2026 versus the prior guidance of 4 to 5.5% and Street estimates of approximately 4.1%, driven by a goodwill impairment at Porsche, a challenging auto market especially in China, and additional restructuring charges. Adjusted for special items, margins would be roughly 4%, essentially in line with consensus, but the headline miss is significant.

Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by a sharp escalation in Middle East geopolitics and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically ratcheted up missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities, with Iran warning the US against any military response, while Ukraine launched a massive drone strike on Moscow including hitting an oil refinery. Markets head into the week of September 21 with elevated geopolitical risk premium, rising energy prices, and the Trump/Xi summit on Thursday as the key macro catalyst for potential tariff relief.

Global monetary policy remains firmly hawkish — the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiked this month, and markets are pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada, FOMC, ECB, and BOE over coming weeks. The BOJ's rate decision was slightly more dovish than expected but paradoxically weighed on the yen, rekindling fears that Japan could sell Treasuries to defend its currency. Kansas City Fed President Schmid made hawkish remarks Friday, warning that inflation is running too hot even excluding oil.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

A successful Trump/Xi summit Thursday could produce tangible tariff reductions on roughly 30 billion dollars worth of goods and potentially eliminate Chinese tariffs on US LNG, providing a meaningful relief valve for markets. Even a small improvement in Middle East geopolitics — possible given Iran's presence at the UN General Assembly — could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with favorable knock-on effects for yields, inflation, margins, and consumer confidence. Pentagon data showing Hormuz oil shipments at a six-month high suggests supply disruption fears may be overstated. Chinese memory supply is easing component shortages and capping prices, a positive for the PC and broader tech supply chain.

BEAR CASE

The Houthis dramatically escalated attacks on Saudi Arabian cities over the weekend, Iran is warning the US against military action, and the Pentagon is described as stretched too thin to respond to multiplying conflict fronts — the geopolitical backdrop is deteriorating fast. AI sentiment took multiple hits after Friday's close: Anthropic's IPO was pushed to November, its ARR projections disappointed the most bullish expectations, OpenAI's free cash flow burn is projected at negative 280 billion dollars through 2030, and Big Tech's off-balance sheet AI liability exposure surged roughly 300 billion dollars in just the last twelve months. Global central banks remain in synchronized tightening mode with the FOMC included, leaving little policy cushion. Rising energy prices from the Iran war — not Ukraine — are the primary driver of diesel and motor oil shortages, and that inflationary pressure has not yet fully worked through corporate earnings.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bearish into the new week. The weekend brought a net negative news flow — AI headline risk is mounting, Middle East geopolitics are deteriorating further, and monetary policy globally remains in a tightening posture. The Trump/Xi summit Thursday is the biggest near-term bullish catalyst and could produce modest tariff relief, while the UN General Assembly creates a slim chance for Middle East diplomatic progress. The key risk is further energy price escalation feeding through into inflation and demand destruction.

Sector Watch

AI and Big Tech face a broadening headwind from rising off-balance sheet liability exposure — up roughly 300 billion dollars in the last twelve months — alongside mounting regulatory scrutiny from California's new executive order mandating AI oversight panels and potential kill switch requirements. Energy is the key macro sector to watch given Iran-driven diesel and motor oil shortages, Houthi escalation threatening Saudi infrastructure, and rising refining capacity losses in Russia from Ukrainian drone strikes. Memory and PC components are seeing some relief as Chinese CXMT memory supply ramps and Acer's CEO notes easing component shortages. Auto sector faces dual pressure from a weak China market and the VW pre-announcement signaling broad industry margin stress.
