Company-specific news for Tues 9/22 (BMO) - AZO, CGNX, MLKN, PNR, THO, VKTX

Sep 22, 2026 · 07:15 AM ET

Good morning. This is a company-specific earnings note covering Tuesday before-the-open reporters — no broad macro or index commentary is included in today's edition, so let's go straight to the names.

AutoZone posted fiscal fourth quarter earnings of $56.05 per share against the Street's $54.03 estimate — a beat on the surface, but a low-quality one. About 145 basis points of gross margin help came from tariff refunds, and another 105 basis points from a non-cash LIFO adjustment. Strip those out and the picture gets murkier. Domestic comps came in at just plus 1.6% versus the Street's plus 3.1%, and the operating expense ratio worsened 100 basis points year-over-year. Management did note improving sales trends in the back half of the quarter and said they feel well-positioned for fiscal 2027 growth — but this is a mixed print at best.

Cognex is acquiring RealSense — the market leader in depth-sensing cameras for robotic perception and Physical AI — for roughly $500 million upfront plus another $106 million. RealSense is expected to generate $80 to $90 million in revenue, growing more than 50% year-over-year. This is a high-conviction move into the Physical AI and robotics theme and reads as a bullish strategic catalyst for Cognex.

MillerKnoll delivered a strong fiscal first quarter — 53 cents per share versus the Street's 36 cents. Importantly, even backing out an 11 cent tariff refund tailwind, clean earnings of 42 cents still beat consensus. Sales missed, down 3.3% organically, but orders rose 3.5% year-over-year. Full-year revenue guidance was trimmed slightly, though EPS guidance was left unchanged. A mixed but generally positive read.

THOR Industries missed fiscal fourth quarter estimates — 78 cents versus the Street's 87 cents — as gross margins fell 230 basis points year-over-year to 12.4%, well below expectations. Management's commentary was cautious, citing stubborn interest rates, elevated fuel costs, and inflation squeezing household budgets. North American towable RV order backlogs did surge roughly 75% year-over-year, which is a bright spot, and structural cost actions are underway — but no formal fiscal 2027 guidance was given. This is a bearish print in a macro-pressured space.

Viking Therapeutics is the standout mover this morning. Shares spiked in pre-market after the company reported a 22% weight loss result from its GLP-1 drug VK2735. In a competitive but enormously important obesity drug race, a 22% efficacy number is meaningful and the market is rewarding it.

Finally, Pentair named a new CFO — Bob Hau, previously chief financial officer at Fiserv, TE Connectivity, and Lennox — effective November first. Routine leadership transition, no financial results attached.

Bottom line for today: Viking is your high-energy mover on strong GLP-1 data. AutoZone and MillerKnoll are mixed quality beats worth watching for early price reactions. THOR is the clearest miss with cautious forward commentary. And Cognex's RealSense deal keeps the Physical AI M&A theme active. Stay nimble.
