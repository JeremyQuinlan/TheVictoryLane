Macro · Outlook · Key Names
Macro · Rates

US equity futures are rallying Monday morning with S&P +49 points (65 basis points), Nasdaq +329 points (110 basis points), and R2K +13.5 points (47 basis points). Asian and European markets are broadly higher, with Eurozone indices up 85-120 basis points led by industrials, basic resources, tech, and banks. The strength is counterintuitive given a weekend full of negative headlines — escalating Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, Ukraine's largest-ever drone strike on Russia, and a flood of troubling AI-sector news — with the primary driver appearing to be optimism around diplomatic progress at this week's UN General Assembly in New York.

Treasury yields are down 2-3 basis points across the curve, providing a modest tailwind for equities. Fed expectations remain hawkish — markets are pricing in roughly 33 basis points of additional rate hikes over the final two meetings of the year, with about 50/50 odds on a hike at the October 28th meeting, and another 35 basis points of increases expected in 2027. Kansas City Fed President Schmid and Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari both made hawkish remarks over the weekend, warning that elevated inflation is broad-based and not solely driven by energy prices. The Treasury plans to issue over $1 trillion in T-bills over the next 12 months, pushing short-term debt above 24% of marketable debt and above the Treasury's own 20% target.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

The primary bull argument is that nearly all of the market's macro problems trace back to oil, and even a small geopolitical improvement — such as a credible diplomatic opening with Iran at the UN General Assembly — could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices with powerful knock-on benefits: lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. Trump signaled openness to meeting Iran's president at the UN General Assembly, and reports indicate China and Pakistan are pressuring Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting meaningful international alignment toward de-escalation. Treasury yields are ticking lower this morning, providing additional support, and Asia and Europe are rallying broadly.

BEAR CASE

The weekend news flow was almost uniformly negative — Houthis launched a brazen missile and drone attack on Saudi Arabia's capital, Ukraine executed its largest-ever drone strike on Russia hitting a Moscow-area oil refinery, and a wave of troubling AI headlines hit after Friday's close including a delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, massive OpenAI cash burn, and Oracle data center debt problems. Geopolitical conditions remain extremely grim, with Iran tightening control over global energy supply via Hormuz, aligned Iraqi militias, and the Houthis, while Russia's refining capacity continues to be taken offline. Any UN General Assembly diplomatic optimism may prove to be wishful thinking, and the Fed remains on a hawkish trajectory even as energy-driven inflation continues to squeeze consumers and corporate margins.

Market Outlook

Adam's lean is cautiously bullish for Monday's session but with significant skepticism — the rally is being driven almost entirely by diplomatic hope around the UN General Assembly, which could easily disappoint. The key catalyst to watch is any signal from Trump-Iran interactions at the UN General Assembly, and whether oil prices respond meaningfully to the downside; a sustained oil drop would unlock a cascade of bullish macro effects including lower yields, reduced inflation pressure, and improved consumer confidence.

Sector Watch

AI and data center infrastructure is under notable pressure following a weekend wave of negative headlines: delayed Anthropic IPO, underwhelming Anthropic 2027 ARR projections, OpenAI projecting roughly $280 billion in negative free cash flow from 2026 to 2030, and Oracle data center debt stress. Despite this, data center pick-and-shovel stocks are outperforming in Europe this morning, suggesting the market is selectively differentiating. Energy stocks are underperforming in Europe as Brent slides 170 basis points to around $102.10, extending losses from late last week despite ongoing Middle East supply disruption. Chinese memory giant CXMT announced its next-generation platform has entered mass production, which combined with Acer's CEO commentary about Chinese memory easing component shortages, is a modest negative read-through for Western memory makers including Micron. Big Tech faces a growing off-balance-sheet liability concern, with AI-related commitments rising roughly $300 billion in just the last 12 months according to the Financial Times.
