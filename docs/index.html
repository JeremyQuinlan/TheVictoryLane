VK · Earnings Whispers
▼ ORCL Oracle Other

A negative Financial Times article highlighted poor price action in debt tied to Oracle's massive 'Project Jupiter' data center in New Mexico, with efforts to offload loans tied to the facility having 'hit a wall.' This raises concerns about Oracle's data center financing strategy and potential balance sheet risk at a critical moment for its AI infrastructure buildout.

◆ PSKY Paramount M&A

Paramount is in advanced settlement talks with California's attorney general to resolve a lawsuit against its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. As part of the settlement, Paramount could agree to preserve CNN's editorial independence and divest certain cable channels including Comedy Central, potentially clearing a key regulatory hurdle for the deal to close.

◆ WBD Warner Bros. Discovery M&A

Paramount's advanced settlement talks with California over the Warner deal represent a meaningful step toward regulatory clearance of the merger. A settlement requiring CNN editorial independence preservation and some cable channel divestitures could be a manageable price for deal completion.

▼ WEN Wendy's Other

Wendy's saw pressure after Meritage Hospitality Group, one of its largest franchisees, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. This raises concerns about franchisee health and potential sales weakness in the Wendy's system.

◆ NVO Novo Nordisk M&A

Shareholders are pressuring Novo Nordisk to diversify away from GLP-1 concentration risk by pursuing M&A. The pressure reflects growing investor concern about single-product dependency, though any deal could be strategically additive if executed well.

▲ BA Boeing Contract Win

Boeing is in talks with NASA for its Starliner capsule to be used on 10 or more new flights into low-Earth orbit, a potentially meaningful contract win that would rehabilitate the troubled program and provide a revenue stream from NASA after the high-profile crewed mission setbacks.

▲ ACN Accenture Partnership

Accenture will embed a team of evaluators to monitor safety and alignment at Anthropic, a consulting engagement that reflects the growing enterprise opportunity around AI governance. While a small deal, it signals Accenture's deepening relationship with frontier AI labs.

▼ KR Kroger Other

Kroger is dropping Red Bull from its stores nationwide as the grocery chain pushes back against supplier price hikes. The move signals increasing retailer leverage and pricing pressure on branded consumer goods suppliers.

▼ VW Volkswagen Guidance Cut

Volkswagen issued a large downside preannouncement for 2026 operating margins, now guiding to 'up to 1%' versus the Street's estimate of roughly 4.1% and prior guidance of 4 to 5.5%. The miss was driven by a goodwill impairment charge at Porsche, a challenging auto market particularly in China, and additional restructuring expenses; adjusted for special items, margins would be approximately 4%, roughly in line with consensus.

◆ NSCL Nscale Other

London-based neocloud firm Nscale filed its S1 publicly on Friday afternoon and aims to list on the NYSE under the ticker NSCL. The IPO adds to a busy AI infrastructure listing pipeline and will be watched as a read on investor appetite for neocloud and GPU-as-a-service business models.

Macro · Rates

Weekend news flow was net negative, driven by escalating Middle East tensions and a wave of concerning AI industry headlines. The Houthis dramatically ratcheted up missile and drone attacks on major Saudi Arabian cities, worsening the Iran war dynamic and raising fresh energy supply fears. Separately, a cluster of negative AI headlines dropped after Friday's close, covering Anthropic IPO delays, OpenAI's massive projected cash burn, and Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liability exposure surging ~$300B in the last 12 months.

The BOJ's rate hike came in slightly more dovish than expected, but rather than boosting risk sentiment it pressured the yen and rekindled fears about Japan selling Treasuries to defend its currency. Monetary policy globally remains firmly hawkish — the BOJ, ECB, and FOMC all hiked this month, and the market is pricing greater than 50% odds of additional hikes from the RBA, RBNZ, BoC, FOMC, ECB, and BOE in the coming weeks. Kansas City Fed President Schmid warned on Friday that elevated inflation is being driven by more than just oil, reinforcing the hawkish Fed posture.

Bull / Bear
BULL CASE

Even a small improvement in Middle East geopolitics could trigger an outsized drop in energy prices, with powerful knock-on effects including lower yields, reduced inflation, better corporate margins, and improved consumer confidence. The Trump/Xi summit could produce select tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion worth of goods, a modest but positive trade signal. China and Pakistan are reportedly urging Iran to rein in the Houthis, suggesting growing international pressure that could eventually produce a diplomatic outcome. Saudi Arabia is also moving to increase ship-to-ship oil transfers to help compensate for supply shortages, providing some near-term cushion.

BEAR CASE

The geopolitical landscape is genuinely deteriorating — the Houthis are escalating attacks on Saudi Arabian cities, Iran is activating proxy partners across the region, and the Pentagon lacks the bandwidth to respond to multiplying threats. Energy prices are spiking and beginning to work their way through the economic system, threatening demand destruction and margin pressure across corporate America. The AI sector faces a fresh set of headwinds including Anthropic's IPO delay, OpenAI's projected negative free cash flow of $280 billion between 2026 and 2030, debt stress tied to Oracle's New Mexico data center, and rapidly growing off-balance sheet AI liabilities at Big Tech firms. Globally hawkish central banks compound the pressure on multiples.

Market Outlook

Adam's leaning is cautious to bearish heading into the week. The weekend brought a pile of negative catalysts — escalating Middle East conflict threatening energy infrastructure, a hawkish global central bank backdrop, and a deteriorating AI narrative with Anthropic IPO delays and OpenAI's staggering cash burn figures. The key catalyst to watch this week is the Trump/Xi summit on Thursday September 24th, which could deliver modest tariff relief, and the UN General Assembly running September 22-28, which represents the best near-term hope for any Middle East diplomatic progress.

Sector Watch

AI sector faces a cluster of headwinds — Anthropic IPO pushed to November, OpenAI projecting negative $280 billion in free cash flow from 2026 to 2030, Big Tech off-balance sheet AI liabilities up roughly $300 billion in 12 months, and Oracle data center debt stress. Chinese memory giant CXMT entering mass production of its next-gen platform adds competitive pressure on Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Energy sector is in focus given Houthi escalation against Saudi Arabia, with motor oil prices surging and retailers rationing supplies in the US. Auto sector broadly weak with Volkswagen's large guidance cut and a challenging China demand backdrop. Consumer staples seeing supplier-retailer pricing tension as evidenced by Kroger dropping Red Bull.
